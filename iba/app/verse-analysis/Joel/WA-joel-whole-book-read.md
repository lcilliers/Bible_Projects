## Closing synthesis

**What recurred across the whole book.** Seven threads run the full length of Joel, confirmed only by reading all three chapters together: (1) **"the day of the LORD"** is named and characterized five times (seeded at the missing 1:15, then 2:1, 2:11, 2:31, 3:14) — the single most consistent structural spine in the book, framing alarm, repentance, restoration, and judgment alike as one continuous unfolding event; (2) **divine-possessive language** ("my"/"his" land/people/heritage) deepens across three registers — the prophet's own voice (1:6-7), the narrator's third person (2:18), and God's own directly quoted first-person speech (3:2); (3) **the locust/army identity** is a genuine three-chapter escalation, not a ch. 2 device alone — literal named locust species (1:4) become martial imagery (2:2-11) become the LORD's own explicitly claimed "army... which I sent" (2:25, reusing 1:4's own names); (4) **comprehensive-by-named-category address** recurs from ch. 1's occupational groups (drunkards, farmers, priests, 1:5/1:9/1:11/1:13) through ch. 2's fuller enumerations (elders/children/nursing-infants/bridegroom/bride, 2:16; sons/daughters/old/young/servants, 2:28-29) — universal scope consistently expressed through named particulars, never one undifferentiated mass; (5) **Q12 (divine mirroring) surfaced three real instances**, most via shared root: "return/turn" (2:12-14, extended 3:1/3:4/3:7 — the book's dominant thread), "calls" (2:32, self-contained), "sold" (3:3-8, the single clearest and most precisely talionic instance in the book); (6) **gladness/joy, withdrawn (1:12, 1:16) and then commanded restored** (2:21-26) — a complete emotional arc; (7) **"consecrate" (H6942G) reused with deliberate irony** — positive for Israel's own repentance (1:14, 2:15-16), repurposed for the nations' own doom-bound mobilization (3:9). The book's own final clause — "for the LORD dwells in Zion" (3:21) — closes exactly on the presence-theme that has structured it from "my holy mountain" onward.

**What never resolved.** Several questions the book itself never answers, at any point: the corporate-over-individual interior bias (real, but not absolute — Joel's own cry, 1:19, and the unidentified interjector, 3:11, punctuate it briefly without overturning it); the trafficked boy and girl's total silence (3:3), structurally mirrored but never answered by the nations' own children sold in reversal (3:8); Judah's own moral position as the LORD's instrument in that same reversal; the identity of 3:11's interjecting voice; whether "everyone who calls" (2:32) and "the survivors... whom the LORD calls" (2:32, same verse) name the same group or hold a real, unadjudicated tension; and whether the priestly intercession (2:17) is the text's own asserted cause of the LORD's pity (2:18) or only an invited inference from sequence.

**What's left genuinely open past the last chapter.** Three items are explicitly deferred beyond this single-book read: whether Q12's shared-vocabulary mirroring (three instances here, three in Jonah) is a device this corpus uses this densely elsewhere, or whether Joel and Jonah are unusual; the cross-corpus echo between Joel 3:10's "beat your plowshares into swords" and the inverse peace-oracle found elsewhere in the Prophets, not elaborated here per this method's own discipline against importing outside material; and whether the corporate-over-individual interior bias identified here (Joel 2's EQ3) holds up against genres less oracular than the Prophets, once the book-by-book campaign reaches them.
