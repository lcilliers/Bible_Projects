## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, unchanged from prior debates).** An operation bears on
the inner being when the text or span-data names or denotes an *interior* dimension of a human —
state, faculty, disposition, affect, or volition — or a movement whose stated source or target is
such an interior dimension. Applied interrogatively. **Per step-2 note (f)**
(`WA-passage-read-guidance-v1.2`), every human named in the passage is a presumptive candidate,
regardless of how outward or brief the mention. The scope fork (interior-named-only vs. also
non-human agency on human circumstance) remains **open and unsettled by the researcher**.

**Reading rule applied.** For `[AMBIGUOUS]` spans, the `STEP live` code-specific gloss is
authoritative over the base `meaning_tree`. **Not exercised in this range** — the base extract
carries no `[AMBIGUOUS]` flags; all 206 non-particle spans resolve cleanly.

**Corpus-continuity check (new this revision — the process gap the assessment identified).**
Before revising this debate, `WA-dan-2-1-16-debate-v1.1` was re-read in full specifically to

## Dan 2:17

> Then Daniel went to his house and made the matter known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions,

**Observation.** Daniel "went" (H0236) to his house and "made known" (H3046 Aphel, *yada* — to
know / cause to know) the matter (H4406, *millah* — word, thing, matter) to his three companions
(H2269, *chaver* — associate, companion).

**Operation 1 — Daniel's own interior in delivering the news (corrected this revision).**
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** `[INFERRED, not stated]` — no interior word is present in the verse; the acts
  ("went," "made known") are outward, communicative. But per step-2 note (f), a human performing
  even a bare communicative act is a presumptive candidate, and this is not a neutral disclosure:
  Daniel is bringing his three friends news of a death-decree that already names them as
  potential victims (established at Dan 2:12–13, `WA-dan-2-1-16-debate`). Some interior — urgency,
  dread, resolve to act rather than merely inform — is plausible, though not lexically supported.
  v1.0 of this debate raised this only to dismiss it in the same breath; this revision keeps the
  candidate open as a retained referential aspect rather than closing it.
- **Source:** self — Daniel's own initiative in going and disclosing.
- **Target:** the three companions (human, collective, Q8) — brought into shared knowledge of
  the crisis, which becomes the substrate for 2:18's joint petition.
- **Q9 (sufficiency):** insufficient to name the interior's content; recorded as inference, not
  asserted.

**Decision.** **Retain** as a referential aspect (Daniel's unstated but plausible urgency/resolve
in delivering the news), on the same footing as Dan 1:1's siege-resolve — **not** set aside, per
the correction. The communicative movement also stands, as before, as the substrate for 2:18.

## Dan 2:18

> and told them to seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions might not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

**Observation.** Daniel tells them "to seek" (H1156, *bĕʿah* — to ask, seek, request, desire,
pray, make petition) "mercy" (H7359, *rachamin* — compassion; Aramaic of *rachamim* H7356B) from
"the God of heaven," concerning "this mystery" (H7328, *raz* — secret), so that they "might not
be destroyed" (H0007, *abad* — perish/be destroyed) "with the rest" of Babylon's wise men.

**Operation 1 — the four's petition.**
- **Subject:** the four (Daniel and his three companions), collective (Q8).
- **Operation:** a petition — the content is stated (lexically present as "seek"); the
  disposition driving it (fear, faith-dependence) is inferred, named as inference.
- **Source:** the four themselves, self-initiated (at Daniel's prompting, 2:17) — the same
  pattern as Daniel's Dan 1:8 resolve and 1:12–13 devised test: an interior conviction/need
  seeking resolution through a deliberate, devised act, here prayer rather than a dietary
  experiment.
- **Target:** God (non-human), as recipient of the petition; **what is sought is itself an
  interior of God** ("mercy/compassion") — the mirror image of Dan 1:9, where a divine interior
  (favour/compassion, the same root H7356B/H7359) was *given* unsolicited; here it is *sought*.

**Cross-reference (new, not a new operation).** "The rest of the wise men of Babylon" names the
same condemned class whose own interior was, per `WA-dan-2-1-16-debate-v1.1` §2:13, retained as
an explicit recorded silence — they too are under this same death-sentence. Not re-litigated as
a fresh operation here; the existing silence at 2:13 covers them, and this verse simply confirms
Daniel's group shares their fate.

**Decision.** **Retain** a petition-operation: the four's self-sourced movement, Godward, seeking
a named divine interior (mercy/compassion) as the means of survival. Opens the thread closed at
2:19/2:23.

## Dan 2:19

> Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

**Observation.** The mystery "was revealed" (H1541, *gelah* — to reveal (secrets), Peal passive)
to Daniel "in a vision" (H2376, *chezev* — vision, appearance) "of the night." Daniel then
"blessed" (H1289, *berak* — to bless, praise) the God of heaven.

**Operation 1 — divine→human revelation.**
- **Subject:** God (implicit subject of the passive "was revealed," confirmed at 2:23 as "the God
  of heaven").
- **Operation:** revealing — the mystery delivered via a night vision. Directly parallel to Dan
  1:17 ("God gave them... understanding in all visions and dreams" for Daniel specifically).
- **Source:** God, explicitly.
- **Target:** Daniel, individuated (though the mystery concerns all four's survival, Q8). **Closes**
  the petition opened at 2:18.

**Operation 2 — Daniel's responsive blessing.**
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** a movement — self-sourced gratitude/reverence in immediate response to the
  granted revelation. Continuous with, and answering, his own Dan 1:8 resolve-and-devise pattern:
  here the response to answered prayer is praise, not further strategy.
- **Source:** Daniel himself — a human response to a divine act, distinct from the divine act
  itself (kept separate per Part B.5, the same discipline required at Dan 1:2).
- **Target:** God (relationally). Opens directly into the extended prayer of 2:20–23.

**Decision.** **Retain** both operations: the revelation (God → Daniel, closing 2:18) and
Daniel's responsive blessing (self-sourced, opening into 2:20–23).

## Dan 2:20

> Daniel answered and said: "Blessed be the name of God forever and ever, to whom belong wisdom and might.

**Observation.** Daniel predicates of God's name: "blessed" (H1289) forever, and states that
"wisdom" (H2452, *chokmah*) and "might" (H1370, *gevurah*) "belong" (H1932 + H9033, an
existential/possessive construction) to him.

**Step 2 applied directly (corrected this revision — no new fork needed).** This clause names no
human at all; it predicates possession of wisdom and might to God alone. Per read-guidance step 2
note (d) — "verses with only non-human beings in contexts, where the state/characteristics of the
non-human being is not related to a human being in the context of the passage, is not in scope...
the verse is set aside" — this clause is non-human content, and nothing in it is stated as acting
on, or being related to, a human within the clause itself (contrast 2:21 and 2:28–29, where God's
action explicitly reaches a human target, and which are retained below). **v1.0 of this debate
raised this as a new open fork (EQ-9) instead of applying note (d) directly — an unnecessary
escalation the assessment flagged. Corrected here: note (d) already answers it.**

**Decision.** **Set aside**, per step 2 note (d) — non-human content, not related to a human
within this clause. EQ-9 (as filed in v1.0) is withdrawn; the question it asked is answered by
the existing rule, not left open.

## Dan 2:21

> He changes times and seasons; he removes kings and sets up kings; he gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who have understanding;

**Observation.** God "changes" (H8133) times and seasons; "removes" (H5709, *avad*) and "sets up"
(H6966I, Aphel of *qum*) kings; "gives" (H3052, *yehav*) "wisdom" (H2452) to "the wise" (H2445)
and "knowledge" (H4486, *manda*) to "those who have understanding" (H3046 + prefixes).

**Operation 1 — kings removed/set up (circumstance, not interior).**
- **Subject:** God.
- **Operation:** removing and installing human rulers — concerns *standing/circumstance*,
  structurally parallel to Dan 1:2's "gave Jehoiakim into his hand," not stated as reaching the
  kings' *interior*.
- **Source:** God.
- **Target:** kings (human, individuated by office, not by name here).
- Under the conservative scope: outward/circumstantial. Bears on the passage-level fork (patterns
  with the "outcome/circumstance" side, not the "disposition" side) — a data point against, not
  for, the wider scope extending this far.

**Operation 2 — wisdom/knowledge given to a human class.**
- **Subject:** God.
- **Operation:** a giving — the general principle of which Dan 1:17 and 2:19/2:23 are specific
  narrated instances.
- **Source:** God, explicitly.
- **Target:** "the wise," "those who have understanding" — a human class (Q8), not restricted to
  Daniel's circle.
- **Q7 (major linkage):** the passage's clearest statement of the general theological principle
  behind the specific divine-giving events already narrated (1:17; 2:19; 2:23). **Bears on EQ-6**
  (filed against `WA-dan-1-7-21-debate` — not re-litigated here per researcher direction that
  emergent questions are resolved at the whole-book read, not synergised across passage debates;
  noted only as a data point for that later resolution).

**Decision.** **Set aside** the kings-removed/set-up clause as a stated IB operation (circumstance,
not interior). **Retain** the wisdom/knowledge-giving clause, feeding the wisdom-sourcing thread
(1:4 → 1:17 → 2:21 → 2:23 → 2:30).

## Dan 2:23

> To you, O God of my fathers, I give thanks and praise, for you have given me wisdom and might, and have now made known to me what we asked of you, for you have made known to us the king's matter."

**Observation.** Daniel "gives thanks" (H3029) and "praise" (H7624) to God, "for" God has "given"
(H3052) him "wisdom" (H2452) and "might" (H1370), and has "made known" (H3046) to him — and then,
in the closing clause, "to us" — "what we asked" (H1156, the same root as 2:18's "seek") and "the
king's matter."

**Operation 1 — Daniel's thanksgiving.**
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** a movement — gratitude/praise, self-sourced, responsive to Operation 2 below.
- **Source:** Daniel.
- **Target:** God.

**Operation 2 — divine giving, personally testified.**
- **Subject:** God.
- **Operation:** giving/making-known, with a durable resultant state (Daniel now possesses
  wisdom/might; the four now possess the answer). First-person, explicit: Daniel himself states
  God as source — the personal, testimonial counterpart to 2:21's general principle and 1:17's
  narrated fact.
- **Source:** God, explicitly, in Daniel's own voice ("you have given me").
- **Target:** a clean example for EQ-4 (filed at `WA-dan-1-1-7-debate`): the verse shifts from
  singular ("given **me**... made known to **me**") to plural ("made known to **us**") within
  three clauses — both an individuated target (Daniel) and a collective one (the four) recorded
  for the same divine act, by the text's own hand, not by an analyst's choice.
- **Q7:** this verse **explicitly closes** the petition opened at 2:18 — "what **we asked** of
  you" is the text's own cross-reference.

**Decision.** **Retain** an explicit divine→interior operation (God → Daniel individually and the
four collectively — both recorded), closing 2:18. Continues the wisdom-sourcing thread
(1:4 → 1:17 → 2:21 → **2:23**).

## Dan 2:24

> Therefore Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon. He went and said thus to him: "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon; bring me in before the king, and I will show the king the interpretation."

**Observation.** Daniel "went in" (H5954) to Arioch, "appointed" (H4483) by the king "to destroy"
(H0007) the wise men; Daniel says: do not destroy them, bring me before the king, "I will show"
(H2324, *chava*) "the interpretation" (H6591, *pishar*).

**Operation 1 — Daniel's intervention.**
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** `[INFERRED, not stated]` — confidence (from 2:19's revelation, 2:23's received
  answer) and an intervention extending mercy beyond his own circle to the wider guild of wise
  men (widening 2:18's "might not be destroyed... with the rest" from a shared-fate concern into
  an active rescue). Inferred with a stronger textual antecedent than Dan 1:1's parallel case
  (Nebuchadnezzar's siege-resolve): the immediately preceding revelation (2:19–23) supplies a
  concrete reason for Daniel's sudden confidence.
- **Source:** Daniel himself (self-sourced initiative), continuous with the resolve-and-devise
  pattern of Dan 1:8 and 1:12–13.
- **Target:** Arioch (immediate human recipient); behind him, the wise men of Babylon (collective,
  Q8) — the same class whose own interior is recorded as a silence at Dan 2:13
  (`WA-dan-2-1-16-debate-v1.1`), now to be *spared* rather than merely *outperformed* (cf. 1:20).

**Decision.** **Retain** an intervention-operation (Daniel's inferred confidence, antecedent-
supported by 2:19–23, → protective request), continuous with the 1:8/1:12–13 pattern.

## Dan 2:25

> Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste and said thus to him: "I have found among the exiles from Judah a man who will make known to the king the interpretation."

**Observation.** Arioch "brought in" (H5954) Daniel "before the king" "in haste" (H0927, *behal*
— Pual: to frighten, alarm, dismay; Ithpaal: to hurry, hasten). Arioch says: "I have found"
(H7912) "among the exiles from Judah a man who will make known... the interpretation."

**Operation 1 — Arioch's haste (interior-adjacent state).**
- **Subject:** Arioch.
- **Operation:** "in haste" carries, in its own registered sense-range, both *hurrying* and
  *alarm/dismay* — an interior-adjacent manner-word, not a clean seat-word. A fourth data point
  for the stated/inferred gradient (filed at `WA-dan-1-7-21-debate` EQ-1), sitting closer to that
  debate's Dan 1:14 *shama* case (interior carried in a verb's registered range, without a
  dedicated seat-word) than to its Dan 1:8 *leb* case (explicit seat-word).
- **Source:** situational — Arioch's own life was staked on the wise men's destruction (per 2:24's
  context, and per the class-wide silence recorded at 2:13); human/circumstantial, not divine.
- **Target:** how the haste shapes the action (bringing Daniel in quickly, and how Arioch frames
  his role).

**Referential note (not stated, inferred from narrative pragmatics).** Arioch reframes Daniel's
initiative as his own discovery ("**I** have found... a man who will...") — a self-interested
repositioning before the king. Not lexically marked as deceptive; surfaced as a possible reading,
not imported as fact, following the same discipline applied to the chief eunuch's unstated
interior at Dan 1:7.

**Decision.** **Retain** Arioch's haste as an interior-adjacent state (situationally sourced),
issuing in the presentation-movement; the self-positioning reframing recorded as referential.

## Dan 2:28–2:29 — the pivot: revelation reaching the pagan king

> [2:28] but there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will be in the latter days. Your dream and the visions of your head as you lay in bed are these: [2:29] To you, O king, as you lay in bed came thoughts of what would be after this, and he who reveals mysteries made known to you what is to be.

**Observation.** God "reveals" (H1541) "mysteries" (H7328) and "has made known" (H3046) to
Nebuchadnezzar "what will be in the latter days" (H0320, *acharith*). The king's "visions of
your head" (H2376 + H7217, *resh* — head, "as seat of visions") "as you lay in bed" are named.
Then: "thoughts" (H7476, *rayon* — thought) "came" (H5559, *seleq* — to ascend, come up) "to
you," and "he who reveals mysteries made known to you what is to be."

**Correction (this revision — see change-control).** v1.0 claimed this is the first time "since
Dan 1:1" that Nebuchadnezzar's interior is lexically stated, and framed it as closing "the
longest-open thread in the whole Daniel 1–2 debate series." This is false.
`WA-dan-2-1-16-debate-v1.1` records his interior as **stated**, repeatedly and explicitly, well
before this point: troubled spirit, stated (2:1, 2:3 — the king's own self-report); resolve,
partly stated (2:5, "the word is firm"); certainty and suspicion, stated of himself (2:8–9); and
fury, stated *and explicitly sourced* (2:12 — "the least inferential IB operation in the
passage"). What 2:29–30 actually gives, for the first time, is not a stated interior *simpliciter*
but a stated interior specifically about **content arising in him** — a thought, a vision — as
distinct from the emotional/volitional states (disturbance, resolve, suspicion, fury) already on
record. That narrower claim is what the operations below rest on.

**Operation 1 — a cognitive event arising in the king (stated).**
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** "thoughts" (*rayon*) explicitly names a content arising within him; "visions of
  your **head**" names the locus (head, as seat of visions) — an interior-seat reference,
  structurally parallel to Dan 1:8's *leb*/heart for Daniel, but here of Nebuchadnezzar, and here
  for *cognitive content* rather than *volition or emotion* — the category genuinely new at this
  point in the corpus, correcting the overstated "first stated interior" claim.
- **Source:** left unattributed by the text — "came thoughts... to you" is phrased as a
  natural/self-originating event; no source is named for the thought's *occurrence*.
- **Target:** n/a — reflexive.

**Operation 2 — God's making-known of the content's significance.**
- **Subject:** God ("he who reveals mysteries").
- **Operation:** making known what the arising thoughts mean/portend — kept textually distinct
  from Operation 1 within the same two verses. A clean in-text model of the source-of-state vs.
  source-of-content distinction the passage-level fork (Dan 1:2) and EQ-2
  (`WA-dan-1-1-7-debate`) have been circling: the king's raw cognitive event is not attributed to
  God; the revelatory *content/interpretation* of that event is.
- **Source:** God, explicitly.
- **Target:** Nebuchadnezzar — unambiguously (contrast the genuine target-ambiguity at Dan 1:9).

**Q4 (bears on EQ-6, filed at `WA-dan-1-7-21-debate` — not re-litigated here):** 2:28–29 shows God
revealing *cognitive content* to a non-covenant king — not the same as sourcing a *favourable
disposition* in him (nothing here gives Nebuchadnezzar goodwill, resolve, or character, as
1:9/1:17/2:21 do for Daniel's circle). Noted as a data point for the whole-book resolution of
EQ-6, per researcher direction that emergent questions are not synergised across passage debates.

**Decision.** **Retain** two linked operations: (i) a self-originating cognitive event in the
king (thoughts/visions — a *new category* of stated interior for him, correcting the overstated
"first time" claim), and (ii) an explicit divine movement making its significance known.

## Dan 2:30

> But as for me, this mystery has been revealed to me, not because of any wisdom that I have more than all the living, but in order that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and that you may know the thoughts of your mind.

**Observation.** Daniel: "this mystery has been revealed to me, **not because of** any wisdom
(H2452) that I have more than all the living, **but in order that** the interpretation may be
made known to the king, and that you may know the thoughts (H7476) of your **mind**" (H3825,
*levav* — heart, mind).

**Operation 1 — Daniel's self-denial of merit.**
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** a striking reversal — Daniel explicitly denies self as the operative source of
  the revelation ("not because of any wisdom that I have"), even though Dan 1:17, 1:20, 2:21, and
  2:23 have all attributed exactly this wisdom to him (as God-given). Disowns interior-based
  *merit* while implicitly retaining his role as *instrument*.
- **Source:** Daniel redirects source away from himself, toward purpose ("in order that... the
  king... may know") — a self-correcting move relative to his own testimony one section earlier
  (2:23).
- **Target:** the king again, and explicitly "the thoughts of your **mind**" (*levav*) — the
  clearest, most direct interior-seat naming of Nebuchadnezzar's mind in the whole range, using
  the **same root** (*levav*/*leb*) that named Daniel's own interior seat at Dan 1:8. The lexical
  echo (heart/mind, first for Daniel's resolve, now for the king's dream-content) is a
  deliberate-reading candidate, surfaced not asserted.
- **Q7:** **closes the passage's wisdom-sourcing arc** — unstated source (1:4) → God-given,
  narrated (1:17) → general principle (2:21) → personal testimony (2:23) → Daniel's own humble
  reframing, denying personal merit even in the moment of using the gift (2:30).

**Decision.** **Retain** Daniel's self-denial-of-merit as an interior/reflective operation
(source explicitly redirected from self to purpose/God), targeting the king's *levav* — closing
the wisdom-sourcing arc and echoing 1:8's *leb* lexically.

## Passage-level linkages (Q7)

1. **Petition → revelation → thanksgiving arc (2:18 → 2:19 → 2:23):** the cleanest, most
   explicitly self-closing arc in the corpus so far — the text names its own fulfillment ("what
   **we asked** of you," 2:23).
