## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, unchanged from prior debates).** An operation bears on
the inner being when the text or span-data names or denotes an *interior* dimension of a human —
state, faculty, disposition, affect, or volition — or a movement whose stated source or target is
such an interior dimension. Per step 2 note (f): every human mentioned — Nebuchadnezzar, the
herald, the enumerated officials, and the collective "peoples, nations, and languages" — is a
presumptive candidate, even where the verse's own grammar gives them only an outward,
administrative, or compliance-shaped act. A candidate that resolves to nothing is recorded as an
explicit silence, not omitted (Part B.4).

## Dan 3:1 — the image, wholly of gold

> King Nebuchadnezzar made an image of gold, whose height was sixty cubits and its breadth six
> cubits. He set it up on the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon.

**Observation.** Nebuchadnezzar "made" (H5648, *avad* — to make, do; also "to be done, be wrought,
be executed") "an image" (H6755, *tselem* — the same lexeme Daniel used at Dan 2:31/2:34-35/2:38
for the dream-image whose head was gold) "of gold" (H1722, *dehav*), sixty cubits high, six
cubits broad, and "set it up" (H6966I, *qum* Aphel — the same "set up/establish" lexeme used of
God's kingdom-founding at Dan 2:44) on the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon.

**Operation 1 — Nebuchadnezzar's act of construction and installation.**
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** per note (f), a purposive, administrative-looking act (commissioning and erecting
  a monumental image) that must still be carried through the interrogative rather than dismissed
  as merely logistical.
- **Q2 (implied interior).** What disposition underlies commissioning an image of this scale,
  wholly of gold? `[INFERRED, not stated]` — the text gives no stated motive (contrast Dan 1:8's
  explicit *leb*-resolve). Two referential readings are recorded, neither imported as fact
  (Part B.2): (a) an act continuous with the honour Nebuchadnezzar had already received and, per
  2:46-47, himself paid to Daniel/Daniel's God — a king building a monument in a culturally
  ordinary register; (b) a self-assertive reversal of the dream's own content — the dream (2:31-45)
  depicted his kingdom as merely the *first*, and golden, element of a declining succession, ended
  by a stone "cut by no human hand." An image built entirely of gold, on Nebuchadnezzar's own
  initiative, could be read as an implicit answer to that prophecy: not decline and succession, but
  a single, permanent, all-gold monument. This reading is well short of certain — the text never
  states the connection — and is flagged here as inference, per Part B.3, not asserted as the
  narrative's meaning.
- **Q4 (source).** Source of the *enablement* to act: Nebuchadnezzar's own kingship, itself
  circumstantially God-given per Dan 2:37-38 (`WA-dan-2-31-49-debate`). Source of the *disposition*
  behind the act (reading (a) or (b) above, or something else): not stated; `[INFERRED]` at most,
  and the two candidate readings are not reconciled.
- **Target:** the image itself (an object), and — prospectively, per 3:2-7 — every human subject of
  the empire, though that target is not yet named at 3:1 itself.
- **Q7 (major linkage).** Tests EQ-10 (2:31-49 debate): God's 2:37-38 giving to Nebuchadnezzar was
  found to be uniformly circumstantial (kingdom, power, might, glory, dominion), never
  dispositional. 3:1 is the first data point for how Nebuchadnezzar himself *exercises* that
  circumstantial dominion — building a monument — which is itself a further circumstantial act, not
  yet evidence either way on whether the *giving* ever reached his disposition. Filed as a
  continuing test, not a resolution.
- **Q9 (insufficiency).** No motive is stated; both referential readings above remain open. Named,
  not filled.

**Decision.** **Retain** as an inferred-interior operation — the act's disposition is genuinely
open (honour-continuous vs. prophecy-reversing), recorded as referential debate, not settled.

## Dan 3:2 — the summons of the whole administrative hierarchy

> Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent to gather the satraps, the prefects, and the governors, the
> counselors, the treasurers, the justices, the magistrates, and all the officials of the
> provinces to come to the dedication of the image that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**Observation.** Nebuchadnezzar "sent" (H7972, *shelach*) "to gather" (H3673, *knash* — **no entry
in STEP itself**, not a registration backlog; see insufficiencies) an eight-fold enumeration of
officials
(satraps H0324, prefects H5460, governors H6347, counselors H0148, treasurers H1411, justices
H1884, magistrates H8614, and "all the officials" H7984 "of the provinces" H4083) "to come" (H0858)
"to the dedication" (H2597, *chanukkah*) "of the image... King Nebuchadnezzar had set up" (H6966I,
repeating 3:1's verb).

**Operation 1 — Nebuchadnezzar's summons of the empire's administrative body.**
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** a further administrative act (summoning), again a presumptive candidate per note
  (f) despite its outward, procedural appearance.
- **Q2.** What interior motivates convening the *entire* administrative hierarchy — eight ranked
  classes, exhaustively named — for a dedication? `[INFERRED]`: a plausible reading is that
  universal attendance itself is the point — securing the whole apparatus of provincial governance
  as witness (and, per 3:4-7, as compelled participant) to the image's status. Not stated; named as
  inference.
- **Source:** Nebuchadnezzar, continuing 3:1's initiative.
- **Target:** the eight-fold official class, and — instrumentally — the image's dedication.
- **Q8 (collective).** The eight ranks are a professional/administrative collective, not a single
  individual; the nature of that collective (the empire's entire governing apparatus, enumerated
  exhaustively rather than summarized) reshapes this as a movement toward the whole class of
  imperial officialdom, not toward any named person.
- **Q7.** Referential note, not asserted as fact: Dan 2:49 placed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego
  "over the affairs of the province of Babylon," and Daniel as "chief prefect [H5460, the same
  *cagan* rendered "prefect" here] over all the wise men of Babylon." Whether the three (or Daniel)
  fall within this verse's enumerated classes is not stated here or in 2:49 with enough precision to
  say; flagged as an open referential question, not settled either way, and germane to a real
  narrative tension surfaced at 3:7 below.

**Decision.** **Retain** as an inferred-interior operation (universal-attendance motive), with the
overlap-with-2:49 question recorded as open, not resolved.

## Dan 3:3 — the hierarchy's compliant assembly

> Then the satraps, the prefects, and the governors, the counselors, the treasurers, the justices,
> the magistrates, and all the officials of the provinces gathered for the dedication of the image
> that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up. And they stood before the image that Nebuchadnezzar had set
> up.

**Observation.** The same eight-fold class "gathered" (H3673 again — no STEP entry) "for the
dedication," and "stood" (H6966I, the same "stand/set up" root now used intransitively of the
officials themselves, in contrast to its transitive use of Nebuchadnezzar at 3:1/3:2) "before"
(H6903G) the image.

**Operation 1 — the officials' compliant assembly (recorded as silence on interior).**
- **Subject:** the eight-fold official class (collective, Q8).
- **Operation:** compliance with the summons — gathering and standing before the image. Per note
  (f), this is a presumptive candidate: the officials *act* (travel, assemble, position themselves),
  and that act invites a question about the interior behind it, even though the verse's grammar
  gives them no verb of interior state.
- **Q2/Q3.** What disposition accompanies this compliance — duty, fear, ambition, genuine reverence
  for the image, indifference? `[not stated]` anywhere in this verse. Per Part B.4, this is recorded
  as an explicit silence, not filled; a forward-pointer is attached to 3:5-7, where the same
  question recurs for the wider population under an explicit death-penalty (3:6), which may bear
  retrospectively on how to read this compliance too, though the text does not make that link
  itself.
- **Source:** the royal summons (3:2) — an external compulsion/enablement to act; nothing is stated
  about a self-sourced disposition toward the image.
- **Target:** the image (positionally — "stood before").
- **Q9 (insufficiency, carried from 3:2).** H3673 ("gather/assemble") has no entry in STEP itself
  (not a registration backlog); its lexical range (e.g. whether it carries any connotation beyond
  neutral assembly) is simply not available from this source, permanently rather than pending.

**Decision.** **Retain** as a recorded silence — a human collective's compliant act, its interior
explicitly unstated, not assumed either way (contrast the explicit worship-verb reserved for 3:5-7).

## Dan 3:4 — the herald's proclaimed command

> And the herald proclaimed aloud, "You are commanded, O peoples, nations, and languages,

**Observation.** "the herald" (H3744, *karoz*) "proclaimed" (H7123, *qera* — to call, read aloud,
proclaim) "aloud" (H2429, *chayil* — lit. "strength/force," idiomatic for volume/forcefulness);
"You are commanded" (H0560, *amar* — to say, speak, **command**) addressed to "peoples" (H5972),
"nations" (H0524), and "languages" (H3961) — the same three-term formula used at Dan 2:38's
mankind-wide dominion grant, now redeployed as the addressees of a decree.

**Operation 1 — the herald's proclamation of the king's command.**
- **Subject:** the herald — a new human candidate, per note (f), not previously in the corpus.
- **Operation:** a speech-act (proclaiming a command). The herald's own interior (zeal, mere
  functionary neutrality, complicity) is not stated; `[not stated]`, recorded as silence — the verse
  gives the herald only a mediating role, voice for the king's decree, not an interior of his own.
- **Q4 (source, enablement vs. disposition).** Source of the herald's *authority/enablement* to
  proclaim: explicitly the king (implied by "You are commanded" being the content proclaimed, and
  by the office itself). Source of any *disposition* behind his proclaiming: not stated.
- **Target:** "peoples, nations, and languages" — the universal human collective (Q8), the same
  three-fold formula as Dan 2:38's "children of man," now not a dominion-grant's recipients but a
  decree's addressees.
- **Q7.** The reuse of the Dan 2:38 formula ("peoples/nations/languages" ≈ "children of man")
  strengthens the reading, raised at 3:1, that Nebuchadnezzar is here exercising — over the very
  scope of humanity God gave him dominion over — a *new* kind of demand (worship-compliance) not
  previously in view when that dominion was granted. Filed as a continuing test of EQ-10, not
  resolved.

**Decision.** **Retain** as a mediating speech-operation; the herald's own interior recorded as
silence. The command's addressee-scope (universal) is the operative finding, carried into 3:5-7.

## Dan 3:5 — the universal order: fall down and worship

> that when you hear the sound of the horn, pipe, lyre, trigon, harp, bagpipe, and every kind of
> music, you are to fall down and worship the golden image that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.

**Observation.** A conditional command: upon hearing (H8086, *shema*) the sound (H7032G) of six
named instruments and "every kind of music" (H2178 + H2170), the addressed "you" (plural,
continuing 3:4's peoples/nations/languages) are to "fall down" (H5308, *nephal*) and "worship"
(H5457, *seged* — the same lexeme Nebuchadnezzar himself received *and performed* at Dan 2:46,
where he "paid homage [*seged*]" to Daniel) before "the golden image... King Nebuchadnezzar has set
up" (H6966I again).

**Operation 1 — the universal command to worship the image.**
- **Subject:** "peoples, nations, and languages" (the same universal collective as 3:4), addressed
  as "you."
- **Operation:** a commanded movement — fall down, worship — conditioned on a stimulus (the music).
  This is the passage's central interior-bearing act: *seged* is not a neutral posture-verb but the
  same worship/homage lexeme carrying real theological weight at 2:46-47.
- **Q2/Q3 (the central question of this range).** Does the commanded act of "worship" entail, or
  even permit inference of, an interior disposition of reverence — or is it, as commanded conduct
  under a decree, silent on interior by construction? `[not stated]` either way in this verse: the
  text specifies the *act* required, not the state of mind required or presumed. Per Part B.5, the
  source of the *enablement/compulsion* to perform the act (the decree, backed by 3:6's penalty) is
  kept strictly separate from any source or presence of an *interior* disposition of devotion, which
  this verse does not address at all. Naming this gap explicitly, rather than assuming compliance
  implies belief (or that it doesn't), is the debate's own discipline here (Part B.1, B.4).
- **Source:** the king's decree, proclaimed via the herald (3:4) — an external compulsion. Not the
  addressees' own volition, so far as stated.
- **Target:** "the golden image... King Nebuchadnezzar has set up" — an object standing, per 3:1's
  referential debate, possibly for the king's own person/kingship.
- **Q7 (major linkage — the *seged* reversal).** The same *seged*-worship Nebuchadnezzar rendered to
  Daniel at 2:46 (itself an unresolved referential question there — worship of Daniel personally,
  of Daniel's God, or of the whole event) is now the exact term commanded, empire-wide, toward
  Nebuchadnezzar's own image. Whether this constitutes a deliberate narrative inversion (the one who
  worshipped now demands worship) or simply the ordinary term for prostration-before-royalty/deity
  reused without special weight is not determinable from the lexical data alone; both readings are
  recorded, neither asserted (Part B.2). This is the strongest single linkage in the passage.
- **Q9 (insufficiency).** This range (3:1-7) never reports any individual's or sub-group's interior
  response prior to compliance; only the act (3:7) is given for the general population. Whether any
  refused is not addressed here — the three companions' anticipated refusal is reported later in
  the chapter, outside this range (see Open decisions).

**Decision.** **Retain** as the passage's central operation. The compulsion-to-act is stated and
explicit; the disposition-question (does coerced *seged* ever constitute or reveal genuine
reverence?) is named as unresolved, not settled by assumption in either direction — carried forward
as a new emergent question (below), on the same B.9 footing as the tracked disposition/circumstance
fork already running through Dan 1-2.

## Dan 3:6 — the penalty: the furnace

> And whoever does not fall down and worship shall immediately be cast into a burning fiery
> furnace."

**Observation.** "whoever does not" (H4479 + H3809) "fall down and worship" (H5308, H5457 — the
same pair as 3:5) "shall immediately" (H8160, "in a moment") "be cast" (H7412) "into" (H1459,
*go* — "midst") "a burning" (H3345) "fiery" (H5135) "furnace" (H0861).

**Operation 1 — the king's stated penalty for non-compliance.**
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar (via the herald's continuing proclamation, 3:4-6 forming one
  speech-unit).
- **Operation:** a coercive operation — attaching a lethal consequence to the failure to perform
  3:5's commanded act. This makes explicit, for the first time in the corpus, that the source of
  *enablement* to comply (3:5) is not neutral: it is compulsion under threat of death, not merely an
  administrative summons (contrast 3:2's "sent to gather," which carried no stated penalty).
- **Source:** Nebuchadnezzar's decree.
- **Target:** any human ("whoever") who does not comply — a universally-scoped, hypothetical
  individual carved out of the collective addressed at 3:4-5.
- **Q4/Q7.** This verse sharpens, rather than resolves, 3:5's central question: it confirms the
  *source of enablement* (fear of death) but says nothing further about *disposition* — if anything,
  it makes the disposition-question harder to answer from the text alone, since any subsequent
  compliance (3:7) can no longer be read as spontaneous even provisionally; it must now be weighed
  against a live death-threat. Filed as sharpening the new emergent question raised at 3:5, not as
  new content.

**Decision.** **Retain** — a coercive operation whose target is the whole addressed population,
establishing the terms under which 3:7's compliance must now be read.

## Dan 3:7 — universal compliance

> Therefore, as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, pipe, lyre, trigon, harp,
> bagpipe, and every kind of music, all the peoples, nations, and languages fell down and worshiped
> the golden image that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**Observation.** "Therefore" (H6903H + H1836, *kol-qobel denah* — "because of this, therefore" —
an explicit causal connective) "as soon as" (H2166) "all the peoples... heard" (H8086) the same
instrument-list as 3:5, "all the peoples, nations, and languages fell down [H5308] and worshiped
[H5457]" the image "King Nebuchadnezzar had set up" (H6966I, closing the verb's fourth occurrence
in this range: 3:1, 3:2, 3:3, 3:7).

**Operation 1 — universal compliance, causally tied to the preceding decree and penalty.**
- **Subject:** "all the peoples, nations, and languages" — the universal collective (Q8), now
  reported as acting, not merely addressed.
- **Operation:** compliance — fell down, worshiped — with the explicit causal connective
  "therefore" tying this act directly back to 3:4-6 (the command and its penalty), not to 3:1-3
  (the image's construction/dedication-summons) in isolation.
- **Q3 (stated vs. inferred, sharpened by the connective).** The *proximate cause* of the act is
  stated, not merely inferred: the "therefore" explicitly links compliance to having heard the
  commanded signal, under the just-stated threat (3:6). This is a stronger textual warrant than a
  bare inference — the narrative itself supplies the causal chain. What remains unstated,
  genuinely, is any *further* interior beyond compliance-under-threat: assent, resentment, terror,
  indifference, or even genuine devotion coexisting with the coercion are all consistent with the
  text and none is named. Per Part B.4, this residual interior is recorded as silence, distinct from
  the stated proximate cause.
- **Source:** the decree and its penalty (3:4-6) — explicitly, via "therefore."
- **Target:** the golden image.
- **Q7 (the passage's open ending — a forward-pointer, not resolved here).** This range (3:1-7)
  reports universal compliance without exception stated. The eight-fold official class (3:2-3) and
  the wider population alike are said to comply. Per the referential note at 3:2, Shadrach,
  Meshach, and Abednego (Dan 2:49) are administratively part of "the province of Babylon" and thus,
  on the most natural reading, part of the population this verse describes as complying — yet the
  chapter is known (from outside this range) to report their refusal shortly after. **This range
  ends exactly at the point of maximal narrative tension**: universal compliance stated, with the
  three (never named in 3:1-7) apparently included in that universality, and their expected
  exception withheld until the next verses. This is flagged as an open passage-boundary observation,
  not resolved — the resolution belongs to the next debate in this book, not to this one.

**Decision.** **Retain** as the passage's closing operation. The stated cause (decree + penalty) is
solid; the residual disposition-question is recorded as silence, carried forward as an emergent
question; the passage's ending is deliberately left at its point of highest unresolved tension,
matching the base data's own range boundary (3:1-7).

## Passage-level linkages (Q7)

1. **The dream's gold, and Nebuchadnezzar's gold (Dan 2:38 → 3:1):** Daniel told Nebuchadnezzar "you
   are the head of gold" within a dream depicting decline and succession, ended by a
   not-by-human-hand stone. 3:1's all-gold, human-hand-built image is a candidate self-assertive
   answer to that prophecy — recorded as inference (Part B.3), not asserted as the text's own
   stated meaning.
2. **The *seged*-worship reversal (Dan 2:46 → 3:5-7):** the exact lexeme for Nebuchadnezzar's own
   worship of Daniel (or Daniel's God, or the whole event — unresolved at 2:46) becomes, four
   verses of narrative time later, the term for what the whole empire is compelled to render to
   Nebuchadnezzar's own image. The strongest single linkage in this range; recorded as an
   observation of lexical/narrative fact, with its theological significance left as open referential
   debate (Part B.2).
3. **A sharpened human-side mirror of the tracked disposition/circumstance fork (Dan 1:2 →
   2:37-38 → 3:5-6):** where Dan 1-2's fork asked whether *divine* sourcing reaches a human's
   disposition or only circumstance, this range raises the same structural question about *human*
   (royal) coercion: does a decree backed by death reach the compelled parties' disposition, or only
   their behaviour? Tracked as a new, related-but-distinct emergent question (EQ-12, below), per
   B.9's discipline — not settled here, not escalated to the researcher.
4. **EQ-10 tested, not resolved (2:31-49 debate → 3:1-7):** Nebuchadnezzar's own exercise of his
   God-given circumstantial dominion (building a monument, summoning officials, issuing a
   worship-decree) supplies new data on how that dominion is *used*, without bearing directly on
   whether the *giving itself* ever reached disposition. The two questions (how dominion is given;
   how it is used) are kept distinct.
5. **The overlap-with-2:49 question, raised not resolved (Dan 2:49 → 3:2-3, 3:7):** whether
   Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego (and Daniel) fall within the eight-fold official class enumerated in
   3:2-3, and are therefore among the compliant "all the peoples" of 3:7, is never stated in this
   range. This creates the passage's closing tension, left open deliberately (see 3:7 above and
   Open decisions).
6. **The herald, a new office not previously in the corpus (Dan 3:4):** unlike Daniel, the three, or
   the enumerated administrative ranks, the herald is a single, unnamed, non-recurring speech-office
   — noted for completeness (every human mentioned traced per note (f)), interior recorded as
   silence.

## Insufficiencies register

1. **Nebuchadnezzar's motive for building the image (3:1) is not stated.** Both referential
   readings offered above (honour-continuous with 2:46-47; or a self-assertive reversal of the
   dream's succession-prophecy) remain open; neither is filled from outside the text.
2. **H3673 ("to gather/assemble," 3:2 and 3:3) has no entry in STEP itself** — not a registration
   backlog item, a permanent gap in this source. Per Part B.7, named rather than supplied from
   memory or an external lexicon; the one non-particle span short of full coverage in this range
   (118/119).
3. **No interior is given for the eight-fold official class's compliance (3:3), nor for the wider
   population's compliance (3:7), beyond the stated proximate cause (decree + penalty, 3:4-6).**
   Whether compliance coexists with, masks, or is simply silent on genuine disposition is not
   determinable from this range alone.
4. **The time elapsed between Dan 2 and Dan 3 is not stated in this extract or range.** Whether
   Nebuchadnezzar's 2:46-47 reverence toward Daniel's God had "worn off," was never more than an
   episodic response, or coexists uneasily with 3:1-7's decree cannot be weighed without a stated
   interval — named as a gap, not filled by assumption.
5. **Whether Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego (or Daniel) are administratively included among the
   eight-fold official class of 3:2-3** is not stated in either 2:49 or 3:1-7. This bears directly on
   how to read 3:7's "all the peoples" and is left open pending the next range in this book.

## Emergent questions log (filed against this passage only — not merged across passages; resolved if at all at the whole-book read)

- **EQ-12 (new).** Does coerced compliance with a worship-command (backed by a death penalty) ever
  constitute, reveal, or say anything at all about a person's genuine interior disposition — or is
  compliance-under-threat definitionally silent on disposition, however it is later narrated? A
  human-coercion mirror of the divine-sourcing fork tracked since Dan 1:2 (per B.9: tracked, not a
  researcher decision — "it will either emerge from the broader study or not").
- **EQ-13 (new).** Is the reuse of *seged* (2:46 → 3:5-7) a deliberate narrative device (the
  worshipper becomes the one demanding worship) or simply the ordinary term for prostration reused
  without special theological freight? Worth testing against every future occurrence of *seged* in
  the book (it recurs later in ch. 3, outside this range).
- **EQ-14 (new, refines EQ-10 from `WA-dan-2-31-49-debate`).** EQ-10 asked whether "circumstantial
  dominion" is the right category for what God gave Nebuchadnezzar, as against enablement (Part
  B.5) generally. 3:1-7 shows that dominion, once given, can be *used* to compel a worship-act
  empire-wide — raising the further question of whether the text anywhere evaluates that *use*
