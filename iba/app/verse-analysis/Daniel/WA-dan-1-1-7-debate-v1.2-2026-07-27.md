## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, not fact).** An operation "bears on the inner being"
when the text or span-data names or denotes an *interior* dimension of a human — a state,
faculty, disposition, affect, or volition — **or** a movement whose stated source or target is
such an interior dimension. Following the Dan 1:1 correction, this is applied *interrogatively*:
a stated outward act does not close the question but opens it. **Revised this pass:** per
step-2 note (f), this now applies to *every* human named in the passage, not only the ones whose
acts read as obviously psychological — a human simply being acted upon (Jehoiakim), or performing
a routine-looking act (depositing vessels; receiving a new name), is carried into the interrogative
the same as a human whose disturbance is already lexically visible.

**Reading rule applied.** For `[AMBIGUOUS]` spans, the `STEP live` code-specific gloss is taken
as authoritative over the base `meaning_tree`, which can diverge (confirmed at Dan 1:4
"competent", where the base tree still shows an unrelated "lizard" sense while the code-specific
line reads *strength/power*).

**Passage-level open fork (raised at 1:2, never closed within this range).** Dan 1:2 states the
Lord "gave Jehoiakim into his hand." Whether divine sourcing reaches only the *outcome/enablement*
of the human acts in this passage, or also the *interior states* of the actors performing them,
is the recurring question. It is held open and flagged for source-level adjudication, not
resolved here.

## Dan 1:1

> In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it.

**Observation.** Temporal frame (third year of Jehoiakim). Nebuchadnezzar performs "came"
(H0935G, code-specific *come/go in*) and "besieged" (H6696A, code-specific *bind, besiege,
confine, shut in*) against Jerusalem, resumed by "it" (H9034, 3fs, agreeing with the city). All
named actors are human; no interior state is stated for any party.

**Operation 1 — Nebuchadnezzar's driving interior (referential).**
- **Action-type:** besieged.
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** an implied driving-interior behind the siege — resolve, will-to-subjugate,
  ambition, or hostility. `[INFERRED]` — content left unassigned; to name it would be to write
  an inner being the text does not give.
- **Source:** not stated at this verse (the source-of-enablement question is answered only at
  1:2, the Lord; source-of-state remains open — kept distinct, Part B.5).
- **Target:** the city Jerusalem, and behind it (Q8) its inhabitants / Judah as a collective —
  but the verse states action on the *city*, not on any interior.

**Interrogative — questions considered.**
- Q1/Q2: Nebuchadnezzar is a focused inner being. Besieging is not reflex; it carries an implied
  driving-interior. *(Inference — named, not asserted.)*
- Q3: the interior is inferred, not stated. Raised as referential; its content is left
  unassigned.
- Q4: source of that interior is unstated here; distinct from source-of-enablement (1:2).
- Q6: movement (external military act).
- Q7: links forward to 1:2 (the same campaign, now with the Lord as stated source of the
  outcome).
- Q9: the document is sufficient to state the act; it is silent on the interior, which is
  itself the finding.

**Decision.** No interior state is stated. Under the conservative working scope: **set aside as a
stated IB operation**, but the actor's implied driving-interior is **retained as a referential
aspect**.

## Dan 1:2

> And the Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, with some of the vessels of the house of God. And he brought them to the land of Shinar, to the house of his god, and placed the vessels in the treasury of his god.

**Observation.** Non-human subject, the Lord (H0136), is subject of "gave" (H5414G,
code-specific *give / deliver up / entrust*). Object: Jehoiakim, delivered "into his hand" —
"hand" is H3027H, whose code-specific line leans anatomical (*hand/arm*) while the base tree
carries the figurative *strength, power*. Nebuchadnezzar then "brought" and "placed" the vessels
(both H0935P) to Shinar, to the house of his god, into the treasury of his god.

**Observation — idiom note.** "Give into the hand of" is idiomatically a transfer of
*power/control* — the figurative sense the base tree carries — not the anatomical sense the
code-specific line foregrounds. Flagged as a case where the code-specific gloss may
under-capture the idiom. Not resolved here; referred to CC / source level.

**Operation 1 — the Lord's giving-over.**
- **Action-type:** gave.
- **Subject:** the Lord (non-human, in scope per step-2 note (b): related to human actors in
  context).
- **Operation:** a giving/delivering-over — Jehoiakim and temple vessels transferred into
  Nebuchadnezzar's power.
- **Source:** the Lord, explicitly stated as source of this *outcome*.
- **Target:** Jehoiakim and the temple vessels (co-object). Whether the Lord's sourcing also
  reaches Nebuchadnezzar's *disposition* (as opposed to his *success*) is the passage's central
  open fork — kept distinct per Part B.5, not resolved.

**Operation 2 — Jehoiakim's own interior (new this revision — previously never asked).**
- **Action-type:** (delivered into a hand) — acted upon, not acting.
- **Subject:** Jehoiakim — a named human, direct object of the verse's central action, and
  therefore a presumptive IB candidate under step-2 note (f), regardless of the fact that every
  verb in the verse is done *to* him rather than *by* him.
- **Operation:** `[INFERRED, not stated]` — being delivered into a conqueror's power is the kind
  of event that would ordinarily imply *some* interior — fear, humiliation, defiance, resignation
  — in the person it happens to. The text records only the transaction, not Jehoiakim's response
  to it.
- **Source:** n/a — the question here is Jehoiakim's own state, not what act produced it (that is
  Operation 1).
- **Target:** n/a — reflexive/internal, if anything is there at all.
- **Q9 (sufficiency):** insufficient. The extract gives no data — not even a further mention of
  Jehoiakim in this range — from which to weigh whether any interior should be attributed to him.
  Named as insufficiency, not filled. No forward-pointer is available either: Jehoiakim does not
  recur in 1:1–7.

**Decision.** **Retain as a recorded silence**, per Part B.4 — the candidate is raised and
explicitly not filled, rather than never having been raised at all (the prior gap this revision
corrects).

**Operation 3 — Nebuchadnezzar's own interior in the deposit-act (new this revision).**
- **Action-type:** brought, placed (deposited spoils).
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar — already established as a focused inner being at 1:1.
- **Operation:** `[INFERRED, not stated]` — bringing plundered vessels to "the house of his god"
  and placing them in its treasury is a triumphal, plausibly cultic act: a display of conquest,
  and/or an implicit assertion of his god's supremacy over the God whose house the vessels came
  from. Content left unassigned — devotional homage, political trophy-display, and simple
  administrative disposal-of-spoils are all consistent with the bare verbs "brought" / "placed";
  the text does not disambiguate.
- **Source:** self — this is Nebuchadnezzar's own choice of *where* to place the spoils, distinct
  from the Lord's enablement (Operation 1) that made the spoils his to place at all. Kept
  separate per Part B.5.
- **Target:** the vessels (object); relationally, "his god" (non-human, in scope per step-2 note
  (b) as related to the human actor); and, implicitly, the defeated party as an audience for the
  display — none of this stated, all inferred.
- **Q7 (linkage):** this act is the visible completion of Operation 1's enablement — the "giving
  into his hand" of 1:2a becomes concretely visible in the deposit-act of 1:2b. Also: temple
  vessels recur later in Daniel (beyond this range) — noted as a forward-looking observation
  only, not imported as a finding here.
- **Q9 (sufficiency):** sufficient to record the act; the specific motivational content
  (worship, rivalry, or mere trophy-taking) is not determinable from the extract as registered.

**Decision.** **Retain as a referential operation** — Nebuchadnezzar's own triumphal/religious
disposition in the deposit-act, inferred not stated, on the same footing as his siege-resolve at
1:1 (which the original v1.0 debate correctly retained; this revision brings the deposit-act up
to the same standard).

**Reflection.** This is the seam of the whole passage. If divine sourcing reaches only
circumstance, the inner beings of Daniel 1 act on their own interior fuel while God governs
outcomes; if it reaches disposition, the human interiors themselves become targets of divine
operation. 1:2 leaves this open. Recommendation: hold it open rather than resolve by import; it
is the richest question in the range.

## Dan 1:3

> Then the king commanded Ashpenaz, his chief eunuch, to bring some of the people of Israel, both of the royal family and of the nobility,

**Observation.** The king (Nebuchadnezzar) is subject of "commanded" — H0559, whose base gloss
range includes *to say in one's heart, to think, to intend*. So an interior dimension of
commanding is carried in the lemma's own range, not only in inference.

**Operation 1 — Nebuchadnezzar's intent.**
- **Action-type:** commanded.
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** a purposeful intent — a deliberate design to draw Judah's elite into his court.
  The verb's own semantic field (*think/intend*) brings this closer to the text than the
  siege-resolve of 1:1; still a strong referential inference, not an explicit predication —
  named as inference (Part B.3).
- **Source:** stated as his own (he commands); whether it also traces to the Lord's 1:2
  sourcing is the recurring fork.
- **Target:** Ashpenaz (immediate human recipient) and, beyond him, the collective to be
  selected (Q8).

**Operation 2 — Ashpenaz's interior (recorded as silence, already correctly done in v1.0).**
- **Action-type:** (command lands on him) — acted upon, not acting.
- **Subject:** Ashpenaz — the second focused inner being in the passage.
- **Operation:** a command lands on him; no interior response is stated — no assent, reluctance,
  or zeal.
- **Source / Target:** n/a — this is a recorded absence, not a positive operation.
- **Q9:** insufficient to assign any content; silence surfaced, not filled.

**Interrogative — questions considered (both operations).**
- Q8 (collective): "some of the people of Israel… royal family… nobility" — the target is the
  elite / lineage of Judah. Its *nature* (the flower of a nation's line, not anonymous captives)
  will bear on the formation in 1:4–1:5.

**Decision.** **Retain** the intent-operation (Nebuchadnezzar → selection of Judah's elite),
lexically supported, source-of-state open. **Retain** Ashpenaz's interior stance as an explicit
recorded silence.

## Dan 1:4

> youths without blemish, of good appearance and skillful in all wisdom, endowed with knowledge, understanding learning, and competent to stand in the king's palace, and to teach them the literature and language of the Chaldeans.

**Observation.** The youths are specified by a cluster of interior terms: skillful/prudent
(H7919A, *sakal* — be circumspect, have insight), wisdom (H2451, *chokmah*), knowledge (H3045 /
H1847), understanding (H0995, *bin* — discern), learning (H4093, *madda* — knowledge, *mind*,
thought), "competent" (H3581B, code-specific *strength/ability*), alongside outward qualities
(without blemish, good appearance). Stated purposes: "to stand in the king's palace" (H5975G,
*amad*) and "to teach them the literature and language of the Chaldeans" (H3925G, *lamad*).

**Operation 1 — possessed interior faculties (state).**
- **Action-type:** possess (wisdom/knowledge/discernment).
- **Subject:** the collective of youths (Q8).
- **Operation:** a possessed interior condition — prudence, wisdom, knowledge, discernment,
  mind.
- **Source:** unstated. The text predicates the qualities of them without naming an origin. Gap
  marked: are these God-given, native, or trained? The verse is silent — recorded, not filled.
- **Target:** n/a (a state, not a movement).

**Operation 2 — the faculties as selection-target (movement).**
- **Action-type:** selected (on the youths' faculties).
- **Subject:** the youths (whose interior is possessed) or the king (whose operation targets
  that interior) — subject-in-focus shifts by vantage.
- **Operation:** the king (via Ashpenaz) selects *on* the youths' possessed interior faculties —
  a human's inner being made the object of another human's assessment and appropriation.
- **Source:** the king's selecting agency.
- **Target:** the youths' interior faculties (Operation 1's state, now targeted).

**Operation 3 — formation targeting the interior (movement) — "to teach them."**
- **Action-type:** taught.
- **Subject:** the Babylonian instructional apparatus.
- **Operation:** instilling Chaldean literature and language into the youths' minds — the first
  operation in the passage whose target is unambiguously an inner being.
- **Source:** the Babylonian court/system.
- **Target:** the youths' interior (knowledge/formation).

**Reflection (tension surfaced — Q7).** The youths are chosen *for* an interior excellence
rooted in Judah, then subjected to a formation designed to *overwrite* the cultural furniture of
that same interior. The verse does not comment; but the linkage between "selected for their
wisdom" and "to be taught Chaldean learning" is real and is surfaced rather than passed over.

**Decision.** **Retain** all three operations. This is the density point of the passage.

## Dan 1:5

> The king assigned them a daily portion of the food that the king ate, and of the wine that he drank. They were to be educated for three years, and at the end of that time they were to stand before the king.

**Observation.** "Assigned" (H4487, *manah* — count/reckon/appoint) a daily portion of food and
wine; "educated for three years" (H1431, here glossed *to magnify / bring up*); "to stand
before the king" (H5975G).

**Operation 1 — formation over time (movement).**
- **Action-type:** educated/formed (over three years).
- **Subject:** the youths (collective).
- **Operation:** an intended transformation/qualification over time.
- **Source:** the king's design.
- **Target:** the youths' formed capability (interior). Q7: links explicitly back to 1:4's
  "teach them" — 1:5 sets the *duration and telos* of the formation 1:4 named.

**Operation 2 — the youths' interior stance toward the provisioning (recorded as silence).**
- **Action-type:** (provisioned) — acted upon, not acting.
- **Subject:** the youths.
- **Operation:** `[not stated]` — the text states no interior stance toward the provisioning; no
  acceptance, resistance, or ambivalence.
- **Q9 / forward-pointer:** silence surfaced deliberately, with a forward-pointer — the
  chapter's later movement is known to turn on exactly such an interior stance, but that content
  lies outside this range and is *not* imported. What is recorded is the pointer, not the
  content.

**Q9 (checked, not a new finding):** the king's own motive in choosing lavish royal
provisioning specifically (favour? assimilation strategy? simple custom?) is not asked
separately from "the king's design" as source of Operation 1 — noted here as a considered, not
missed, exclusion: the king's agency is already named as source; a *further* interior for the
king beyond that source-role is not textually supported enough to warrant a fourth operation,
and is recorded as such rather than silently skipped.

**Decision.** **Retain** the formation-operation, linked to 1:4. **Retain** the youths' interior
response as an explicit recorded silence with a forward-pointer.

## Dan 1:6

> Among these were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah of the tribe of Judah.

**Observation.** Four are named and located within a collective identity (tribe of Judah, Q8).
The span-data identifies the *persons* but does not gloss the *meaning* of their names.

**Operation 1 — identity/belonging status.**
- **Action-type:** belong (tribal identity).
- **Subject:** the four, named individually and as a collective.
- **Operation:** a status — who these four are and where they belong. Identity/belonging is
  arguably an inner-being dimension (the self located in a people), functioning here as the
  **substrate** on which 1:7 operates (Q7).
- **Source / Target:** n/a (a state, not a movement).
- **Q9 (insufficiency).** The theophoric sense of the Hebrew names — what would make the
  belonging *covenantal* rather than merely tribal — is **not in the document**; it lives in
  name-etymology data the extract does not carry. Named as insufficiency, not supplied from
  memory.

**Decision.** **Retain** an identity/belonging status as substrate for 1:7, with the covenantal
content flagged insufficiently evidenced.

## Dan 1:7

> And the chief of the eunuchs gave them names: Daniel he called Belteshazzar, Hananiah he called Shadrach, Mishael he called Meshach, and Azariah he called Abednego.

**Observation.** "Gave them names" and "called" each youth by a new name — H7760M,
code-specific *to set / appoint (a name)*. Four renamings: Daniel→Belteshazzar,
Hananiah→Shadrach, Mishael→Meshach, Azariah→Abednego. The span-data links each person across
both names but does not carry the *meaning* of either the Hebrew or the Babylonian names.

**Operation 1 — imposed relabeling of identity.**
- **Action-type:** renamed.
- **Subject:** each youth's identity (a collective of four, individuated — Q8).
- **Operation:** a movement — an imposed relabeling of the self by an external authority.
- **Source:** the chief of the eunuchs, acting as agent of the Babylonian court/system (human
  source standing for an institutional one).
- **Target:** the youths' identity — the substrate 1:6 established (Q7, explicit linkage).

**Operation 2 — the chief eunuch's own interior in doing this (already correctly raised in v1.0).**
- **Action-type:** renamed (the doing of it).
- **Subject:** the chief eunuch.
- **Operation:** `[INFERRED]` — his interior in doing this is unstated: administrative act, or
  deliberate assimilation? The category is raised; its content left unassigned.
- **Source / Target:** n/a.

**Operation 3 — the four youths' own response to being renamed (new this revision — previously
never asked, despite 1:5 modelling the identical move one verse earlier).**
- **Action-type:** (renamed) — acted upon, not acting.
- **Subject:** the four youths, as those undergoing the relabeling.
- **Operation:** `[not stated]` — the text records the renaming but no reaction from those
  renamed: no resistance, no grief, no compliance, no indifference. Per step-2 note (f), a human
  undergoing an act is exactly as much a candidate as a human performing one; this was missed in
  v1.0 while the structurally identical case one verse earlier (1:5, the youths' stance toward
  the provisioning) was correctly caught.
- **Source / Target:** n/a — the question is their own interior stance, not what produced the
  renaming (Operation 1 covers that).
- **Q9 / forward-pointer:** insufficient — no data on their felt response is present in this
  range. A forward-pointer is attached on the same basis as 1:5's: Daniel's resolve appears
  immediately next, at 1:8, and *may* retrospectively bear on how the renaming was received —
  but that content is outside 1:6–1:7 and is not imported here.

**Decisive insufficiency (Q9, unchanged from v1.0).** What would make renaming an operation on
*religious/covenantal* identity — the erasure of Yahwistic theophoric names and the imposition
of names invoking Babylonian deities — depends entirely on name-meaning data the document does
not contain. From this extract: *that* an external authority relabeled the four, and *whom* each
new name maps to, are establishable; the *interior significance* of the relabeling — both the
chief eunuch's intent (Operation 2) and the four's own reception of it (Operation 3) — is not
derivable from the document alone.

**Decision.** **Retain** Operation 1 (institutional source → youths' identity), resting on 1:6.
**Retain** Operation 2 (chief eunuch's unstated interior). **Retain** Operation 3 as an explicit
recorded silence with forward-pointer — the gap this revision closes.

## Passage-level linkage (stated once — Q7)

A single spine runs across the range: the Lord's sourcing in 1:2 stands behind a cascade of
human operations — siege (1:1), delivering-into-hand and deposit (1:2), command and selection
(1:3), formation and its telos (1:4–1:5), and relabeling of identity (1:7). Whether the divine
sourcing reaches only the *outcomes* of these acts or also the *interior states* of the actors
is the recurring open question, first raised at 1:2 and never closed by the text within this
range. Carried as a passage-level referential aspect, referred for source-level adjudication.

**New this revision:** the passage also now has a *complete* set of recorded-silence entries for
every human who is acted upon rather than acting — Jehoiakim (1:2), the youths toward
provisioning (1:5), and the youths toward renaming (1:7) — rather than a partial set that
happened to catch some and miss others. This is itself worth noting as a pattern: throughout
1:1–7, the party *doing* something to another human is far more likely to get an interrogated
interior than the party something is *done to*. Whether that asymmetry is a feature of the text
or an artefact of how the debate was first written is now testable, since all instances are
recorded on the same footing.

## Insufficiencies register

1. **Name etymologies absent (1:6, 1:7).** Hebrew and Babylonian name meanings are not in the
   base extract; the covenantal/religious weight of the identity and its relabeling cannot be
   established from this document. Additional data required.
2. **Youths' interior stance unstated (1:5, and now also 1:7).** No interior response to the
   king's provision (1:5) or to the renaming (1:7) is given in-range; recorded as silence with a
   forward-pointer (content not imported).
3. **Source of the youths' faculties unstated (1:4).** Whether the possessed wisdom/knowledge is
   God-given, native, or trained is not stated; recorded as a gap.
4. **Idiom vs code-specific gloss (1:2).** "Into his hand" (H3027H): the idiom of power/control
   sits in the base tree, while the code-specific line foregrounds the anatomical sense — a
   possible under-capture. Referred to CC / source level.
5. **Jehoiakim's own interior (1:2) — new.** No data at all, in this range, from which to weigh
   whether any interior should be attributed to Jehoiakim as recipient of the giving-over.
6. **Nebuchadnezzar's motive in the vessel-deposit (1:2) — new.** Whether the deposit-act is
   devotional homage, political display, or simple disposal-of-spoils is not determinable from
   the extract as registered.

## Emergent questions log (raised during this passage; carry forward within this passage's own
resolution — not merged with other passages' logs, per researcher direction: EQ items are
resolved at the whole-book read, not synergised across individual passage debates)

- **EQ-1.** When a verb's own lexical range carries an interior sense (e.g. H0559 *amar* = also
  *to think / say in one's heart / intend*), does that count as the interior being *stated*
  (text-grounded) or *inferred*? Dan 1:3 sits between the two. A rule may be needed for
  lexically-carried interiors.
- **EQ-2.** How should "source of state" vs "source of enablement" be recorded as *parts* when
  only one of the two is stated and the other is an open fork? (Recurs at 1:1–1:3.)
- **EQ-3.** For operations that *target* an inner being to reshape it (formation, 1:4–1:5;
  relabeling, 1:7), is the appropriate subject-in-focus the acting party or the affected
  interior — or both, recorded from each vantage?
- **EQ-4.** When a collective's individuated members are named (1:6–1:7), is the operation
  recorded once (to the collection) or per-member, or both?
- **EQ-5.** A standing sufficiency question: how often does visible operation outrun the base
  extract's data (as at 1:7)? If frequent, name-etymology coverage may need to enter the extract
  itself.
- **EQ-6 (new this revision).** This passage shows a recurring asymmetry: the party *acting*
  tends to get more interrogative attention than the party *acted upon* (Jehoiakim at 1:2; the
  youths at 1:5 and 1:7). Is this asymmetry inherent to what the text states, or a bias in how
  the debate is written? Worth testing against later passages now that this range records both
  sides on the same footing.

## Open decisions / next steps

1. **Scope fork — tracked, not a decision awaiting a ruling (corrected 2026-07-27, per
   `WA-interpretation-questions-v1.1` Part B.9).** Whether an operation bears on the inner being
   only when an interior is named, or also when a non-human being acts on a human's
   circumstance/standing, is not something to settle in the abstract. It governs 1:2 and the
   parallel acts in 1:3, 1:5, 1:7, and is carried forward like an emergent question — answered,
   if at all, by what the growing corpus shows.
2. **Divine-sourcing reach — the same fork, restated.** Whether God's sourcing of an outcome
   (1:2) ever reaches the actor's disposition is not a separate researcher call either; it is the
   same tracked question, weighed against each new data point as later passages are read.
3. **Data gap.** Decide whether name etymologies (and any other interior-weight data) should be
   added to the base extract to close the 1:6–1:7 insufficiency.
4. **Emergent questions.** These stay filed against this passage; they are resolved (or not)
   when the whole book is read, not folded into a shared cross-passage interrogative document.
