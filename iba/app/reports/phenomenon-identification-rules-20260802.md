**Requested by:** researcher, 2026-08-02, after finding phenomenon-identification errors in the
Amos 1-3 debate (God's own actions registered as if they were his own inner-being disposition;
phenomena force-fitted onto verses that have none). This file is a pull-together of every
instruction/config that governs "how do you identify an inner being phenomenon" — nothing here has
been edited or corrected; it is presented as-is for review.

## 2. `WA-passage-read-guidance-v1.5-2026-08-02.md` — the phenomenon-identification steps verbatim

**Phase 1 (the only phase that identifies phenomena):**

```
step 1 read the verse/passage
step 2 does the verse have any bearing on the IB - if no - exit (set the verse aside)
step 3 for EVERY inner being present in the verse (step 2 note (f): every human is a presumptive
       candidate; note (b)/(d): an in-scope non-human), isolate the inner-being phenomenon — a
       state, disposition, or characteristic of that party's inner life, which may be hidden
       behind a stated act or a refrained-from act (step 3 note (e))
step 3b (new) for each phenomenon isolated in step 3, record WHY it is regarded as an inner-being
        phenomenon: the specific textual warrant (the verb, clause, or stated silence) that
        grounds the identification, and whether it is stated or inferred. Record this per verse,
        per inner being, as its own entry in a phenomena register — this entry exists
        independently of, and is written before, any operation.
```

**Step 2 note (f)** — every human is a presumptive candidate:
> any human — named or collective, major or minor, however briefly mentioned — who performs an
> act, undergoes an act, thinks, speaks, refrains from acting, or is simply named as present, is a
> candidate operation... "no phenomenon found, silent" is a valid *result* of running step 3/3b on
> a human-bearing clause; it is not a valid *substitute* for running it.

**Step 3 notes:**
> a) isolate the inner being phenomenon. The verse may be silent about any phenomenon. Then set
> aside the verse (or that candidate within it).
> b) the phenomenon can underlie a state or status, or a movement (come from, go to, impact on,
> emerge, go away, becomes evident) — which of these it is, is determined when the operation is
> written in Phase 2, not in Phase 1; Phase 1 only isolates and justifies the phenomenon itself.
> c) there may be multiple phenomena in the same verse/passage... each must be isolated and
> justified in its own right.
> e) the inner being phenomenon could be expressed as, or inferred from, the state or status of
> the inner human, and this state may be hidden behind an act or a refrained-from act, with only
> the action recorded in the passage.

**What this text does NOT say anywhere:** it never states that a party performing a stated ACTION
(sending, declaring, punishing, speaking, knowing, revealing) is thereby only the *source* of that
action, not the phenomenon itself — nor does it caution against force-fitting a phenomenon onto
every verse. Both of those corrections were given verbally in this session; neither is yet written
into this document (see §4).

## 3. `WA-interpretation-questions-v1.4-2026-08-02.md` — the interrogative questions bearing on phenomenon vs. operation

**Q1 — Focused inner being.** Is this person a focused inner being in their own right, even where
only an outward act is stated?

**Q2 — Implied interior.** What interior state, disposition, intent, or volition could underlie or
be implied by the stated act or status?

**Q3 — Stated vs inferred.** Is that interior stated in the text or span-data, or inferred?

**Q4 — Source (state vs enablement).** What is the source of the interior state, and — separately
— the source of the enablement to act? Self / another human / a non-human being / an object or
situation.

**Q6 — State or movement.** Is the operation a state/status, or a movement?

**Part B.4 — Silence is a finding.** Where the text states no interior, the silence is recorded as
the result, not filled.

**Part B.5 — Source of state ≠ source of enablement.** A non-human being (e.g. the Lord) may be
stated as the source of an *outcome or enablement* without the text sourcing the actor's
*disposition*. Keep the two separate; extending sourcing from outcome to interior is an
interpretive step to be flagged, not assumed.

**Part B.12 — The phenomenon comes first... it is a failure mode to identify a GENERAL or TEXTUAL
phenomenon (a recurring literary formula...) and construct an operation around it.** This is the
passage that produced the Aug-2 restructure; it addresses cross-verse/cross-chapter literary
patterns being mistaken for phenomena. It does **not** address the separate error found in this
session: a *single verse's* stated divine action being mistaken for a phenomenon of God's own inner
being, rather than God being the operation's *source*.

The closest the current text comes to the researcher's correction is Part B.5 (source of state ≠
source of enablement) — but B.5 is framed as a caution about a *human's* disposition not being
sourced too readily to God's enablement; it is not framed the other direction (God's own stated
action is not, by itself, evidence of God's own disposition).

## 4. Provenance — this rule changed shape same-day, and the gap is old, not new

Checked against the archived predecessor, `iba/docs/archive/WA-passage-read-guidance-v1.3-2026-07-27.md`
(the version used for all six completed books — Daniel, Jonah, Joel, Obadiah, Micah, Hosea):

> step 3 isolate EVERY aspect that is IB related
> Step 1 note (a): the main aim... is to describe the activity of the inner being to capture the
> **operations** of different states and characteristics of the inner being. Each operation [has]
> ... the subject (the inner being in focus)...

**"Phenomenon" as a distinct, pre-operation concept did not exist before 2026-08-02.** Under v1.0
through v1.4, there was only ever one analytical unit — the "operation" — and its subject was
simply "the inner being in focus," with no rule anywhere distinguishing a party who *acts* (God
sending fire, declaring judgment) from a party in whom a *state* is *found*. The Aug-2 restructure
(v1.5/v1.4) introduced the phenomenon/operation split specifically to stop cross-verse literary
patterns being mistaken for phenomena (Part B.12) — but it did not introduce, and does not contain,
the finer distinction the researcher gave verbally in this session (God's action = source, never
itself the phenomenon; don't force a phenomenon onto a verse that doesn't have one).

**Consequence, stated as fact, not a recommendation:** since the old operation-first model never
drew this distinction at all, the same category of error is not unique to Amos — it is structurally
available in every one of the six already-completed books' debates (Daniel, Jonah, Joel, Obadiah,
Micah, Hosea), which were all produced under v1.0-v1.4. Whether that is in scope for review is the
researcher's call, not decided here.

## 5. What is written nowhere in `cfg_*`

Per `governance.rules_must_be_config_driven` (already invoked twice this session for other gaps):
the phenomenon-identification rule itself — including both the Aug-2 restructure and the two
corrections given verbally in this session — exists only as prose in `iba/docs/*.md`, read by
whichever AI applies it by hand. No `cfg_*` row encodes "a phenomenon requires X, not Y" as
checkable data; the only config-side presence is the two file-path pointers in §1. This mirrors
the exact pattern already found and fixed once this session for `cfg_report_section` — it is
flagged here, not fixed, pending direction.
