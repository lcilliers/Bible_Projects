# Nahum 1:1-15 — human beings in the verse, typed (training pass, draft v3)

Source: `verse` table, osisId `Nah.1.*`. Verse 1 has no row in the DB (verse-existence gap,
by-design per `governance.verse_gap_by_design` — noted, not treated as an error).

**Correction from v2:** scope is **human beings only**. God/Lord/Spirit/spirits/forces/animals are
excluded even though they act as grammatical subjects/objects in these verses (e.g. "the Lord" is
the subject of most of this chapter — excluded throughout). Each human being reference is typed:

- **(a) individual by name** — a specific named person
- **(b) unknown individual** — a specific single person, referenced but not named
- **(c) named collection** — a specific named group/people
- **(d) unknown collection** — a group referenced but not named
- **(e) implicit individual** — not directly referenced, but context implies a single person
- **(f) implicit collection** — not directly referenced, but context implies humans as a group

Where a referent is genuinely ambiguous between two of these types, both readings are given and
one is adopted explicitly, per this project's referent-crux convention — not silently resolved.

## Open cruxes carried across the passage

1. **The shifting "you" addressee (vv9, 11, 12, 13, 14):** reads as the enemy (implicit
   collection/individual, Nineveh-adjacent) in vv9/11, then shifts to God's own people in v12/13,
   then back to an individual enemy ruler in v14. None of this is named within 1:1-15 as read here
   (Nineveh's name sits in v1, which is absent from this DB range) — every "you" reading above is
   adopted for this pass and flagged, not settled.
2. **Nah 1:13 "his" (yoke)** and **Nah 1:15 "the worthless"** — left genuinely open between
   individual and collection; nothing in the verse itself decides it.
