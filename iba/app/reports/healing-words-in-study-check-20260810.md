# Healing-domain words — are they in the study? (Hebrew + Greek, checked against `word_strong`)

> Requested check: Hebrew roots (first batch) + Greek roots (this addition), each given as
> transliteration/count/gloss, checked against the IBA registry. Method: matched each
> transliteration to `strong.stepTransliteration` (language-scoped), cross-checked the gloss to
> pick the right homonym where the transliteration prefix matched more than one code, then checked
> `word_strong` for any active registry-word link to that exact code.

## Headline

**No registry word named "healing" or "health" exists** (checked directly — zero matches). Every
Hebrew "IN STUDY" hit is incidental (pulled in by an unrelated-sounding English word's own STEP
search). The Greek picture is more mixed: `salvation` IS a real, dedicated registry word, so
`sōzō`/`sōtēria`/`diasōzō` land there **on-topic**, not incidentally — but the core *physical
healing* family (`iaomai`, `iasis`, `iama` — ἰάομαι and its noun forms) has **zero** registry
coverage, under any word.

**Real, meaningful gap found**: `iaomai`/`iasis`/`iama` — the core ἰάομαι "to heal / healing"
family (the direct Greek equivalent of Hebrew `ra.pha`) — has **zero** registry coverage. Neither
does `hugiēs`/`therapeia` (the "healthy"/"service"-of-healing pair). `sōtēria etc.` genuinely IS
on-topic (salvation as a registry word is a real, deliberate entry, not an accident) — the
distinction from Part 1 is real: Hebrew coverage here is 100% incidental, Greek coverage is a mix
of incidental (`agathos`→`goodness` etc., `haplous`→`integrity`) and one genuinely on-topic cluster
(`sōzō`/`sōtēria`/`diasōzō`→`salvation`).

**Your `0x` counts for `euekteō`** — DB shows `count=1`, not 0. Given `strong.count` is confirmed
non-verse-scoped dictionary data (`BUILD.md` §88), this may not mean it actually occurs in the NT
text either way; not independently checked here against a live verse search.

## The four Greek words with no Strong's entry at all

`hugiazō`, `hugieia`, `iatreia`, `iatēs` — none matched by transliteration OR by gloss search
across the whole Greek `strong` table. Consistent with your own `0x` counts: these are classical/
LXX-only Greek words with **no occurrence in the New Testament text**, so Strong's own numbering
(which only covers the biblical corpus) never assigned them a number — this isn't a gap in this
app's onboarding, there is nothing to onboard.
