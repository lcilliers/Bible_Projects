# Action-word surfacing across the documented Daniel debates (2026-07-27)

**Purpose.** Not a method change. The researcher observed that reading verse-by-verse and
debating each one — rather than sorting into pre-defined characteristics — has surfaced actions
where the verse states neither the interior *source* of the action nor its *effect on* the inner
being, and asked whether the current reading already gives these enough visibility to be
correlated later against other verses in the Bible where the same action-type *is* tied to an
inner-being movement. This document rereads the seven passage debates now on file and pulls out
every action-word (Operation) recorded, tagged by how each was actually treated, so the pattern
can be looked at directly. Nothing in the method or the DB is changed here.

**Scope.** All seven debates currently on file, in book order:

1. `WA-dan-1-1-7-debate-v1.1`
2. `WA-dan-1-7-21-debate-v1.1`
3. `WA-dan-2-1-16-debate-v1.1`
4. `WA-dan-2-17-30-debate-v1.1`
5. `WA-dan-2-31-49-debate`
6. `WA-dan-3-1-7-debate`
7. `WA-dan-3-8-30-debate` (actual covered range 3:8-29)

**IB-status key** (the vocabulary the debates themselves already use):
- **Stated** — the interior is lexically present in the text (a seat-word like *leb*, an explicit
  emotion/cognition verb), not merely inferred.
- **Inferred** — the debate names a plausible interior but the text does not state it; recorded as
  inference, per Part B.3.
- **Silence** — a human is acted upon or performs an act, and the debate explicitly records that
  no interior is given, per Part B.4 (this is the category the researcher's observation bears on
  most directly).
- **Set aside** — non-human content unrelated to a human in the clause (step 2 note (d)), or an
  act whose interior was already covered by an adjacent operation.

## Clusters that emerge from the list itself

These are not new categories invented for this document — they are the same action-words already
sitting in the seven debates, grouped by recurring verb/action-type rather than by verse order.
Presented as observation, per Part B.6, not as a finding or a proposed rule.

**1. "Compliance with a command" — the heaviest silence cluster in the corpus.**
Ashpenaz commanded (1:3) → silence. Experts summoned (2:2) → silence, corrected only this
revision. Officials gathered for the dedication (3:2-3) → silence. The herald proclaiming (3:4) →
silence. Soldiers binding and casting the three in (3:20-21) → silence. Officials inspecting the
furnace (3:27) → silence. Counselors confirming (3:24) → silence. **Seven separate instances**,
spread across all three chapters, all sharing the same shape: a human is commanded, complies, and
the text (and, until the step-2 note (f) correction, the debate itself) gives nothing on the
interior behind the compliance. This is very likely the largest single body of "silent but
plausibly IB-bearing" material in the corpus so far.

**2. "Worship" (*seged*, H5457) — the same action-type recurring with sharply different
treatment each time.**
2:46 Nebuchadnezzar worships Daniel — **stated**, the clearest embodied interior in the corpus.
3:5-7 the empire is commanded to worship the image — compliance stated, disposition explicitly
**silent**. 3:12/3:16-18 the three refuse to worship — **stated** resolve, the corpus's richest
disposition/circumstance data point. 3:28-29 Nebuchadnezzar's own confession and decree protecting
worship of the true God. One lexeme, four appearances, four different IB outcomes — already
flagged inside the debates themselves (EQ-13) without any new machinery, purely because the same
word kept recurring across a short span. This is the clearest in-corpus demonstration of exactly
the correlation the researcher is describing: the action-word itself is the connective thread, and
tracing it surfaced a pattern (2:46 → 3:5-7 → 3:16-18 → 3:28-29) the individual passage debates
could only gesture at one link at a time.

**3. "Giving" (a non-human/divine subject, a human target) — the richest *stated* cluster,
functioning as the corpus's control case.**
1:9, 1:17, 2:19, 2:21, 2:23, 2:28-29, 2:37-38 all use a giving-verb with God as subject. Unlike
cluster 1, this family is almost entirely **stated**, not silent — because the text itself
supplies a seat-word or an explicit interior noun each time (favour, wisdom, understanding,
mercy). Useful as a contrast case: the same "gave" action-type is sometimes fully stated (favour
to Daniel, 1:9) and sometimes purely circumstantial (kingdoms to Nebuchadnezzar, 2:37-38) — a
demonstration that the action-word alone doesn't predict the IB-status; what varies is what
follows the verb.

**4. "Being acted upon, not acting" — the asymmetry already named as EQ-6 (Dan 1:1-7) and
partly re-tested since.**
Jehoiakim delivered (1:2), Ashpenaz commanded (1:3) and later payoff (1:18), the youths
provisioned (1:5) and renamed (1:7), the wise men condemned (2:13), Daniel receiving worship
(2:46) and promotion (2:48), the three being bound and cast in (3:20-21). Every one of these is
recorded as silence. The debates already flagged this pattern once (`WA-dan-1-1-7-debate`'s EQ-6)
and it recurs at nearly every subsequent passage — the party something is *done to* is
consistently the least likely to get a stated interior, even where (as with the wise men at 2:13,
or the three at 3:20-21) that party was extensively vocal immediately before or after.

**5. "Fear/alarm-adjacent" action-words with mixed treatment.**
Chief eunuch's stated "I fear" (1:10) vs. Arioch's interior-adjacent "in haste" (2:25, alarm +
hurry in one lexeme, no seat-word) vs. the wise men's unstated terror facing execution (2:13) vs.
the three's silence at the actual moment of being cast into the furnace (3:20-21). A single
emotional register (threat-response) that the text states directly in one case, half-states
lexically in another, and leaves wholly silent in two others of comparable or greater stakes.

**6. "Renaming/relabeling identity" — a single, thin, high-silence instance that a whole-Bible
correlation could genuinely inform.**
Dan 1:7's imposed renaming of the four is recorded with total silence on their own reception of
it (forward-pointer never closed within this range). This is the kind of action-type the
researcher's proposal is aimed at squarely: the Bible has other renaming-scenes (Abram/Abraham,
Jacob/Israel, Simon/Peter, Saul/Paul) where the text *does* tie the renaming to a stated identity
or covenant shift. Dan 1:7 alone gives no way to weigh whether the silence here is meaningful or
simply incidental — but the action-type itself ("imposed renaming") is a clean, nameable unit that
could be checked against those other instances once the whole-book/whole-corpus stage is reached.

## What this does and doesn't say

This is a rereading exercise, not a new rule. It confirms three things concretely:

1. The current method (step 2 note (f), Part B.4) already succeeds at *recording* these
   silent-but-plausibly-IB-bearing actions rather than skipping them — that correction (the
   corpus review, 2026-07-27) is why this list could be assembled at all from what's already
   written.
2. What the method does **not** yet do is connect one recorded silence to another recorded
   silence, or to a stated instance, *across* passages by action-type — each debate's forward-
   pointers currently point only within-book (Ashpenaz's fear at 1:10 → 1:18; the officials'
   compliance at 3:2-3 → 3:27), not toward a same-action instance in a different book entirely.
3. The clusters above emerged simply from grouping existing debate text by verb — no new field,
   tag, or table was needed to produce this document. Whether that's sufficient going forward, or
   whether a lighter structured layer (even just a consistent per-operation action-type label)
   would make this kind of grouping easier to redo at whole-book or whole-Bible scale, is the
   open question — left for you to weigh, not decided here.
