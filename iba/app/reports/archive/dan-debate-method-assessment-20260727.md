## Revision (second pass, same session) — the first pass graded the wrong layer

The first pass below only checked what happened to verses *after* they cleared the step-2 gate
("does the verse have any bearing on the IB — if no — exit"). It never checked the gate itself.
Re-reading step 3, note (e) — "the inner being operation could be expressed as, or **inferred
from the state or status of the inner human**, and this state **may be hidden behind an act or
refrain from an act with only the action being recorded in passage**" — the gate is not supposed
to be a verse-level or clause-level filter that outward-looking human action can fail. Any
mention of a human doing, thinking, refraining, or standing in some status is a **presumptive
candidate**; step 2 should almost never exit on a verse that names a human performing an act —
it should exit only where no human is present, or only a non-human is present unrelated to a
human (note 2d). "No operation found" is a legitimate *output* of running Q1–Q9 on a human-
bearing clause (silence, recorded per Part B.4); it is not a legitimate *substitute* for running
them.

Re-scanned on that basis, a real, recurring pattern shows up: clauses naming a human performing
an act were dismissed as "outward," "administrative," or "purely locational" **without Q1/Q2
ever being posed**, in a way inconsistent with how the same debates treat structurally identical
clauses elsewhere. Concrete instances, worst first:

- **Dan 2:13 (`dan-2-1-16`) — the strongest miss in the corpus.** "the wise men were about to be
  killed; and they sought Daniel and his companions, to kill them." The wise men facing
  imminent execution are named humans undergoing the single most existentially loaded moment in
  the range, and their interior — terror, despair, resignation — is **never even raised as a
  question**. The debate's Q-treatment of this verse covers only the executants' derivative
  intent-to-kill; the condemned themselves are not asked about at all. Compare 2:1's opening
  move (the king's *ruach* is the very first thing the passage establishes) — the same
  instrument that opens by centring an interior on the first page has no interior at all for the
  people about to die three verses from the end of this range.
- **Dan 1:7 (`dan-1-1-7`) — the sharpest internal inconsistency.** The four youths are renamed
  (Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego) — their very identity is reassigned by an external
  authority. The debate asks Q1 for the chief eunuch (his interior in doing this is raised,
  "administrative act, or deliberate assimilation?") but **never asks Q1 for the four
  themselves** — how they respond to having their names, and by the debate's own reading,
  covenantal identity, overwritten. This is the *exact* move the debate makes one verse earlier,
  correctly, at 1:5 — "the youths' interior stance... recorded as unstated and referable
  forward" — a silence explicitly named and forward-pointed. At 1:7, the structurally identical
  move (a human undergoing something done to them, with no stated reaction) is not made at all;
  the youths simply aren't asked about.
- **Dan 1:2 (`dan-1-1-7`) — Jehoiakim.** "the Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand."
  Jehoiakim is a named human being who is the direct object of the verse's central action — and
  Q1 ("is this person a focused inner being in their own right, even where only an outward act
  is stated?") is never asked of him at all. The debate's Q1/Q2 treatment in this verse goes to
  Nebuchadnezzar only.
- **Dan 1:2 (`dan-1-1-7`) — Nebuchadnezzar's own act of deposit.** The same verse has
  Nebuchadnezzar "bringing" and "placing" the temple vessels in his god's treasury — a
  triumphal, plausibly homage- or dominance-laden act. The debate discusses only the
  *enablement* he received from the Lord; his own driving interior in performing this specific
  placement is not asked about, though Q1/Q2 was applied to him one verse earlier (1:1) for a
  structurally similar case (the siege).
- **Dan 1:15 (`dan-1-7-21`).** "it was seen that they were better in appearance..." — set aside
  outright ("no interior stated → not an IB operation in its own right"). But *someone* did the
  seeing/judging here, and the same debate treats a nearly identical act — the king "finding
  none like them" at 1:18–19 — as an interior assessment operation, inferred and retained. The
  two clauses are not distinguished on any stated ground; one gets Q1/Q2, the other doesn't.
- **Dan 1:18–19 (`dan-1-7-21`) — the chief eunuch/Ashpenaz.** He personally presents the youths
  to the king, at the exact moment his own life-stake (explicit since 1:10 — "you would endanger
  my head with the king") is realised or averted. His interior at this moment is never
  revisited; the debate's Q1/Q2 apparatus, which it used carefully for him at 1:10, is simply
  not brought back.
- **Dan 2:2 (`dan-2-1-16`).** The summoned magicians/enchanters "came in and stood before the
  king" is dismissed as "purely locational... no IB work done by those terms here." Given the
  king is already established (2:1) as disturbed and the summons is not obviously benign, prompt
  compliance under an unstated but plausible duress is at least a candidate for Q1/Q2 — not
  raised.
- **Dan 2:17 (`dan-2-17-30`, mine).** Daniel breaking the death-decree news to his three
  companions is set aside with only a brief, undeveloped inference note, rather than explicitly
  retained as a referential aspect the way structurally similar cases are handled elsewhere in
  the corpus (e.g. 1:1's siege-resolve).

**This changes the verdict on (a) and, downstream, on (c).** Both are revised below; (b) and (d)
are unchanged from the first pass.

---

## Bottom line

The core interrogative discipline — surfacing hidden interiors, naming inference as inference,
not force-resolving — is working *where it is actually applied*, and is the strongest thing
across all four documents. But per the revision above, it is not being applied to every human-
bearing clause — a real subset never reach Q1/Q2 at all, dismissed instead at something
functioning as an over-broad step-2 gate. That is the most important finding in this review and
supersedes the two defects the first pass led with, which still stand as secondary findings:

1. **The four documents do not use the same output shape.** Only `dan-2-1-16` implements the
   read-guidance's step 5 ("record the parts of each operation") as an explicit, extractable
   Subject/Operation/Source/Target block. The other three (mine) fold source/target into
   Q4/Q5 prose inside "Interpretation — questions considered" — substantively present, but not
   isolated as a discrete, scannable record. This is a literal shortfall against Part C of the
   interpretation-questions doc, which lists "operations and their parts (subject, operation,
   source, target)... where determinable" as a required output element, separate from the
   questions-considered narrative.
2. **The emergent-questions log is not actually continuous across the corpus, despite the rule
   that it must be.** `dan-2-1-16` runs its own EQ-1…EQ-4; `dan-1-7-21` and `dan-2-17-30` run a
   separate EQ-1…EQ-9. These are different questions sharing the same numbers. Worse, my
   `dan-2-17-30` debate makes a factual claim — "the king's interior is stated for the first
   time since Dan 1:1" (at 2:29) — that `dan-2-1-16` directly contradicts: that document shows
   Nebuchadnezzar's interior stated repeatedly and explicitly (troubled spirit 2:1/2:3, resolve
   2:5, certainty/suspicion 2:8–9, and fury 2:12, which it calls "the least inferential IB
   operation in the passage"). I wrote 2:17–30 without having read 2:1–16 first. That is a
   corpus-discipline failure, not a debate-instrument failure — the instrument's own rule (Part
   B.8, "emergent questions are carried forward... to later passages") implies the same for
   findings generally, and I didn't check.

Everything below expands the four assessment criteria in the order given.

## a) Isolate all the operations

**Read-guidance basis:** step 3 ("isolate EVERY aspect that is IB related... there may be
multiple operations in the same verse... each operation must be described in its own right");
step 2 ("does the verse have any bearing on the IB — if no — exit"); step 3, note (e) (interior
"may be hidden behind an act or refrain from an act with only the action being recorded").

**Verdict, revised: incomplete, not strong.** At the *verse* level, coverage is complete — no
verse is skipped, and every verse gets at least a Step-2 ruling. But verse-level completeness
was the wrong thing to check. At the *clause*/*human-mentioned* level — the actual grain step 3
demands ("isolate EVERY aspect," "multiple operations... each described in its own right") —
coverage is not complete. The seven instances listed in the Revision above are cases where a
named or collective human is stated as performing, undergoing, or refraining from an act, and
Q1/Q2 is simply never posed for them — not resolved as silence, not raised and set aside, just
absent from the debate's attention. That is a different and more serious kind of gap than a
verse correctly ruled "no bearing": it means step 3's "isolate EVERY aspect" was not actually
attempted for those humans, so there is no way to know from the document whether their silence
is a finding (Part B.4) or an oversight. The worst instance (Dan 2:13, the condemned wise men)
sits in the range's most dramatically loaded verse, which makes the omission harder to explain
as a considered judgement rather than a miss.

The 2:1–16 debate remains the most disciplined of the four on grain generally — it takes more
borderline verses to an explicit, labelled record than the other three do — but it is not
immune to the pattern (2:2, 2:13 are its own). This is not a single document's failing; it
recurs across documents from three different points in the series, which is what makes it look
like a property of how "step 2" is being *used* in practice (as a fast administrative-act
dismissal) rather than a one-off lapse.

## b) Isolate all the parts of the operations

**Read-guidance basis:** step 1(a) defines the four parts explicitly (subject, operation,
source, target); step 5 ("record the parts of each operation in the md"). **Interpretation-
questions basis:** Part C lists "operations and their parts (subject, operation, source,
target) where determinable, and marked where silent, inferred, or insufficiently evidenced" as
a required, distinct output element.

**Verdict: inconsistent, and this is the main finding.**

- `dan-2-1-16` complies literally: every operation gets an explicit block —
  **Subject:** / **Operation:** / **Source (of state):** / **Target:** — with `[INFERRED]`
  tags inline where a part is not stated. This is auditable at a glance; you can scan the
  document and extract a parts-table without re-reading the prose.
- `dan-1-1-7`, `dan-1-7-21`, and `dan-2-17-30` (mine) do not. Source and target are answered,
  but as prose sentences attached to "Q4" and "Q5" bullets inside "Interpretation — questions
  considered," interleaved with inference-status commentary, reflection, and cross-references.
  The information is present but not *isolated* — which is exactly what the criterion (and the
  read-guidance's word "isolate") asks for. Example: Dan 2:23 in my debate states the source
  ("God — explicitly, first person") and the target (Daniel individually **and** the four
  collectively) correctly and richly, but a reader has to parse two paragraphs to reconstruct
  the four-part record; `dan-2-1-16`'s format would have made the same content four labelled
  lines.

This is not a matter of one style being wrong — both documents *ask the right questions*
(criterion c, below, holds up in all four) — but only one of the four actually *produces the
required output object* (a parts record) as its own artifact rather than as a by-product of
narrative. Recommend: retrofit `dan-1-1-7`, `dan-1-7-21`, and `dan-2-17-30` with an explicit
Subject/Operation/Source/Target line per retained operation, or adopt a documented decision that
the narrative form satisfies Part C (in which case Part C's wording should be loosened to match,
since as written it calls for the parts as a separate element).

## c) Ask the right questions to synergise what is not evident

**Interpretation-questions basis:** Part A, Q1–Q10, and the stated purpose — "not 'is an inner
state stated?' but 'what inner state must be asked after here, and what is its source and
target?'"

**Verdict, revised: strong wherever a clause actually reaches the interrogative, but that can no
longer be called "strong within each document" without qualification — (a)'s finding means the
interrogative's coverage is gated by an over-broad step-2/step-3 filter, not just by
cross-document synergy.** The quality of the questioning, once a clause is in front of it, is
not in doubt (the four examples below still stand as genuine strengths). The problem is upstream
of the questions: a clause has to survive an implicit "is this worth asking about" judgement
before Q1–Q9 ever get applied to it, and the Dan 2:13 / Dan 1:7 / Dan 1:2 (Jehoiakim) instances
show that judgement is sometimes made on administrative-act grounds that note 3(e) explicitly
rules out as sufficient grounds for skipping the interrogative. Fixing (a) — running Q1/Q2 on
every human-bearing clause before any dismissal — will very likely also fix most of what looked
like a synergy problem in this section, since several of the "missed" cross-references below are
places where a later verse silently answers a question an earlier verse never asked.

Within-document strengths (unaffected by the revision):

Within a single document, the interrogative is doing real work in all four:
- 1:1–7 corrects its own first-pass miss (Nebuchadnezzar's siege-resolve) using exactly this
  discipline — the debate's origin story.
- 1:7–21 introduces the stated/inferred *gradient* (EQ-1: seat-word present vs. verb-carried
  vs. purely inferred) — a genuine refinement the questions produced, not one imported.
- 2:1–16 goes furthest: it distinguishes an operation *impacting* another interior from an
  operation *reading/attributing* another interior (its own EQ-1, Dan 2:8–9 — the king
  construing the experts' intent) — a real gap in the original Q5 target-taxonomy, surfaced by
  applying the questions honestly rather than by design.
- 2:17–30 extends the source-of-state-vs-source-of-content distinction using Dan 2:28–29's own
  textual structure (the king's thought *arising* is left unsourced; its *interpretation* is
  explicitly God-sourced, in the same two verses) — a genuine in-text model, not an imposed one.

**But the "synergise" half is not happening across documents**, and it should, per Part B.8
("Emergent questions are carried forward, so they can be applied to or tested against later
passages") and the whole premise of a growing instrument. Concretely:

- **Correction (researcher, 2026-07-27): the EQ logs are not supposed to be cross-document.**
  My original reading of this as a defect was wrong. Emergent questions are filed *per passage*
  by design and are resolved, if at all, when the whole book is read — not synergised across
  individual passage debates as they're written. On that basis, `dan-2-1-16`'s EQ-1…EQ-4 and
  `dan-1-7-21`'s EQ-1…EQ-7 sharing numbers for different questions is not a collision to fix; it
  is two passages each keeping their own register, correctly. This finding is withdrawn. What
  the debates *do* need to check against each other is factual continuity (see next point) — that
  is a different requirement from merging the EQ logs, and the two should not have been
  conflated.
- **A factual claim in my `dan-2-17-30` debate is wrong because I didn't read `dan-2-1-16`
  first.** I wrote: "for the first time since Dan 1:1... the king's interior is lexically
  stated" (at 2:29), and framed this as closing "the longest-open thread in the whole Daniel 1–2
  debate series." `dan-2-1-16` — which existed before I wrote this, though I had not read it —
  already records Nebuchadnezzar's interior as *stated*, repeatedly: 2:1 ("the state is stated;
  its source is inferred"), 2:3 ("stated here — a within-passage resolution of the earlier
  inference"), 2:5 (resolve, "partly stated"), 2:8–9 (certainty and suspicion, stated of
  himself), and most sharply 2:12 ("both the state (fury) and its source... are **stated** —
  the least inferential IB operation in the passage"). My "first time" claim needs to be struck
  or corrected in `dan-2-17-30`; what actually happens at 2:29–30 is not the *first* stated
  interior for Nebuchadnezzar but the first stated interior *specifically about future/hidden
  content arising in him (a thought, a vision)*, as opposed to the emotional/volitional states
  (trouble, resolve, suspicion, fury) already stated in 2:1–16. That is still a real and
  worthwhile distinction — it is just not the distinction I wrote down.
- **This is a process gap, not an instrument gap.** The interrogative and the guidance are
  sound; what's missing is a step, before writing a new debate, of reading the *existing*
  debates that cover the immediately preceding range in the same chapter. `dan-2-17-30`'s own
  "Previous outputs referenced" line lists the two method docs and the two Daniel-1 debates, but
  not `dan-2-1-16` — the one document it most needed.

## d) Avoid drifting off into areas that is not part of the brief

**Read-guidance basis:** step 2, note (d) — "verses with only non-human beings in contexts,
where the state/characteristics of the non-human being is not related to a human being in the
context of the passage, is not in scope... the verse is set aside."

**Verdict: mostly disciplined; one clear instance of scope drift, in my own document.**

- `dan-1-1-7`, `dan-1-7-21`, and `dan-2-1-16` stay inside the brief throughout. Reflection
  sections speculate (e.g. 1:7–21's EQ-7 on "externalisation of the interior... worth watching
  across the corpus," 2:1–16's closing cross-verse reflection on the three-way contrast of
  interiors) but every such move is explicitly labelled as observation/reflection, not asserted
  as a finding, per Part B.6 — that is the guidance's own escape valve for productive
  speculation, used correctly.
- **My `dan-2-17-30` debate drifts at Dan 2:20 and 2:22.** These verses predicate wisdom, might,
  knowledge, and revealing directly of God as possessor, with no human party acted on in the
  clause itself. Step 2, note (d) already has a rule for exactly this case: non-human-only
  content, not related to a human in the passage's context, is out of scope and the verse (or
  clause) is set aside. Instead of applying that existing rule, I manufactured a new open fork
  ("EQ-9 — should divine self-attributes be tracked by an instrument built for the human inner
  being?") and left it "flagged, not resolved." That is process drift: inventing a new
  scope question the method already answers, rather than applying the rule that's already
  there. The correct move, on the read-guidance as written, is: set 2:20 and 2:22 aside as
  non-human-only content (their relation to the human petitioners is only indirect — Daniel
  addressing God, not God acting on a human interior within those specific clauses), and drop
  EQ-9, or at minimum re-file it as a question about the *rule's* clarity rather than a live
  open fork.
- No other instance of drift was found — no document imports outside theological content, no
  document treats an inferred reading as fact, and insufficiencies (dream content absent, name
  etymologies absent, thin lexeme trees) are named and left unfilled throughout, per Part B.7.

## Recommendations, in priority order

0. **Re-pass all four debates at clause grain, treating every human mention as a presumptive
   candidate.** For each verse already in the corpus, re-check every clause naming a human
   (named or collective) performing, undergoing, or refraining from an act, and confirm Q1/Q2
   was posed for them — even if the answer is "silent, not filled." Start with the seven
   instances listed in the Revision section, worst first: Dan 2:13 (the condemned wise men,
   `dan-2-1-16`), Dan 1:7 (the four youths' own response to renaming, `dan-1-1-7`), Dan 1:2
   (Jehoiakim; and Nebuchadnezzar's own interior in depositing the vessels, `dan-1-1-7`), Dan
   1:15 and 1:18–19 (the unnamed observers and Ashpenaz at his moment of consequence,
   `dan-1-7-21`), Dan 2:2 (the summoned experts' compliance, `dan-2-1-16`), Dan 2:17 (Daniel's
   own interior in breaking the news, `dan-2-17-30`). This is more consequential than items 1–5
   below and should be done first, since some of those items may partly resolve once this pass
   is complete.
1. **Fix the `dan-2-17-30` "first stated interior" claim.** Re-open that debate's 2:29–30
   section and Closed-items entry; replace "first time the king's interior is stated" with the
   accurate claim (first time *cognitive content*, as opposed to emotional/volitional state, is
   stated for him), citing `dan-2-1-16` 2:1/2:3/2:5/2:8–9/2:12 as the prior stated instances.
2. ~~Merge the two EQ logs into one running register.~~ **Withdrawn (researcher correction,
   2026-07-27): EQ logs are per-passage by design and resolve at the whole-book read, not by
   cross-document merging.** No action needed here.
3. **Decide the parts-record format question**, one way or the other: either retrofit
   `dan-1-1-7`, `dan-1-7-21`, and `dan-2-17-30` with `dan-2-1-16`'s explicit
   Subject/Operation/Source/Target blocks, or formally amend Part C to state that the narrative
   Q4/Q5 form satisfies the "parts" requirement. Leaving the mismatch undecided means the next
   debate will pick one at random.
4. **Add a standing step to the read-guidance or the interpretation-questions doc**: before
   debating a new range, read the debate(s) covering the immediately adjacent prior range in the
   same book, specifically to check for claims like "first time," "closes the open thread," or
   "never before stated," which are exactly the claims that go stale fastest.
5. **Apply the existing step-2(d) rule before minting a new EQ.** Reserve emergent questions for
   cases the guidance genuinely doesn't cover — 2:20/2:22 was not one of those cases.
