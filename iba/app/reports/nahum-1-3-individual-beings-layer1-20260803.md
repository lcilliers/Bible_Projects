# Nahum 1-3 — individual-named and individual-unknown beings (Layer 1 pass)

Source: `verse` table, osisId `Nah.%` — all 46 rows currently in DB (Nah 1: 14/15, missing v1
gap-by-design; Nah 2: 13/13 complete; Nah 3: 19/19 complete).

**Method, per the layering agreed this session:** mark only what the text itself decides on the
spot — a human being that is either (a) given a personal name, or (b) singular, unnamed, but
explicitly described as one person (a title, a role, an apposition). Bare pronouns ("he", "you",
"they", "who") with no such marker are **not** forced into a type here — they stay in an "open"
bucket for the separate referent-resolution layer, not resolved in this pass. Collections (c/d/e/f)
are out of scope for this file; God/Lord/Spirit/animals are excluded throughout (lions in ch.2 are
literal animal imagery, excluded even where read as a royal metaphor — that reading belongs to a
later interpretive layer, not this one).

---

## (a) individual by name

**None found in Nah 1-3.** No human person is referred to by a personal name anywhere in this
book, as read here. Every specific human party is either unnamed-but-described (b, below) or
introduced under a place/nation name (Judah, Jacob, Israel, Nineveh, Assyria, Thebes, Cush, Egypt,
Put, the Libyans) — all of which name a people/place collectively, not an individual. This absence
is itself a finding worth carrying forward, not a null result to discard.

## Candidates — genuinely ambiguous individual-vs-collection (not resolved here)

These read as possibly a single described person, but the text also supports a collective/
personified reading (an army, a city). Left open rather than forced into (b), per the "mark, don't
resolve" rule — a referent-resolution pass would need to look at these specifically.

| Verse | Text | Candidate | Why open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nah 1:6 | "Who can stand... Who can endure" | rhetorical "who" | conventional rhetorical negation ("no one"), stands for people generally, not necessarily one person |
| Nah 2:1 | "The scatterer has come up against you" | the scatterer | could be a specific enemy commander or the invading force personified as one |
| Nah 2:3 | "the day he musters them" | he (commander?) | singular verb distinct from the surrounding collective nouns ("his mighty men", "his soldiers"), but referent not named |
| Nah 2:5 | "He remembers his officers" | he (same referent as 2:3?) | same issue — singular actor, unnamed, possibly the same being as 2:3 |
| Nah 3:4 | "the countless whorings of the prostitute... who betrays nations" | the prostitute | grammatically singular and described, but conventionally read as personification of the city/nation collectively |
| Nah 3:7 | "who will grieve for her?" | rhetorical "who" | same pattern as 1:6 |
| Nah 3:12 | "if shaken they fall into the mouth of the eater" | the eater | generic singular, could be rhetorical/collective ("whoever eats") rather than one specific person |

## Observation

Confident (b) hits cluster where the text supplies an explicit **role or action description**
attached to a singular verb/pronoun (counselor, messenger, mistress, king). Every open candidate
lacks that explicit marker and instead relies on a bare singular pronoun or a personification
convention — which is exactly the class of case flagged earlier as needing its own referent-
resolution layer rather than a same-pass guess.
