**4 open question(s)**

- **`open.pipeline.base-layer-carve`** — The base layer (spans · passages · stems · seeds) has one process for passages, none for spans or stems, and its seeds sit inside an interpretation process. The `governs` vocabulary has no value for spans or stems, so a module for them cannot even declare what it governs.
  - _recommendation:_ B. Spans and stems have nothing in common except being derived: a span is an ADDRESS (the join key for everything above), a stem is a LINGUISTIC DERIVATION. Bundling them under one `base` process would produce exactly the kind of catch-all that the failure record calls over-structuring. C is how it works today and is precisely what hides the step.
  - _resolve by:_ researcher decision — BLOCKS a complete pipeline; modules 4 and the span module cannot declare `governs` until it is ruled
- **`open.pipeline.seed-layer-home`** — The candidate seed is BASE per plan §3.4.1 ('Base — derived: passages, stems, seed/candidate stamping \| Regenerate'), but char.seed-layers and ent.char.candidate live in process/characteristics.json, an interpretation process.
  - _recommendation:_ Split by layer, not by topic: the mechanical seed (which spans are candidates) is BASE; the Screen-0 verdict and the role (what a candidate IS) are INTERPRETATION. They are already two facts on one row, which is the actual defect — the live DB stamps role/char_candidate onto verse_span_index, and the migration strips exactly that (plan 3.4.1).
  - _resolve by:_ researcher decision — follows open.pipeline.base-layer-carve
- **`open.pipeline.module-8-and-prose`** — Module 8 (Prepare data for analysis) governs no process. Prose (layer 4) has neither a process nor a module — the nine end at findings.
  - _recommendation:_ Defer both, deliberately and on the record. The plan itself parks them ('Analysis & findings (least-defined): the study's higher-order outputs sit beyond segment 9'). Not a gap to close now — but it should be a KNOWN debt, not a silence, because it is half the stated end point.
  - _resolve by:_ researcher decision — not urgent
- **`open.pipeline.raw-fetch-coupling`** — The researcher observed that raw and fetch are 'in the same pipeline'. They are: module 1 (Get data from STEP) performs both, and they are separate PROCESSES but not separate MODULES.
  - _recommendation:_ Keep them as one module and two processes, and let this be the worked example that process != module. Splitting them would gain nothing: a fetch that does not store is useless, and a raw write with no fetch cannot happen. The rules differ; the execution does not.
  - _resolve by:_ no decision needed — recorded so the asymmetry is deliberate
