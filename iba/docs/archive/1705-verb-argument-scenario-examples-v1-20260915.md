# `verb_argument` scenario examples — real verses, pulled live

**Escalation:** #1705 · **Purpose:** raw research material — real verses illustrating the range of
T3-triggered discovery scenarios named in
[1705-verb-argument-what-exists-reference-v1-20260915.md](1705-verb-argument-what-exists-reference-v1-20260915.md)
§7, so the pattern-finding work has genuine examples to read, not a description of a mechanism with
nothing to point at. **Nothing designed here** — this is the "go dig up examples" step, not a
proposal.

## Method, and an honest caveat before reading any of these

Every example below is a **live verse where an M-code-tagged word and a T3-tagged word co-occur**,
further filtered to also carry a word tagged with whichever T-code the scenario names (T4/T7/T8/T9
for party-kind, T10–T15 for referent-identity, T5/T6 for negation/connection). Query: `verse_lexical`
joined to `cluster_strong` (live, `deleted=0` both sides), grouped by verse.

**What this does NOT establish**: that the T3 verb's actual grammatical trigger or impact *is* the
other tagged word. These are **co-occurrence candidates** — real verses where the right ingredients
are present in the same verse — not confirmed argument-structure instances. Working out whether the
T3 word and the other tagged word are actually linked (trigger→verb, verb→impact) is exactly the
reading work `verb_argument`'s own trigger condition still needs, per
[the reference doc](1705-verb-argument-what-exists-reference-v1-20260915.md) §4 item 1. Treat every
example below as "read this and see," not "this is a settled instance."

## Divine (T7) → verb (T3) → M-code → Human (T8)

**Num 31:3** — *"So Moses spoke to the people, saying, 'Arm men from among you for the war, that
they may go against Midian to execute the LORD's vengeance on Midian.'"*
- `[T8]` H4872 Moses — `[T3]` H0559 "saying", H2502B "Arm", H5414H "execute" — `[T7]` H3068G "LORD's"
  — `[M26]` H5360 "vengeance" (Judgment & Condemnation) — `[T10]` H4080H Midian (target place)
- Candidate reading: a human agent (Moses, instructing the people) executes an action whose object
  is explicitly possessed by God ("the LORD's vengeance") — M26 (Judgment) is the M-code the T3 verb
  (H5414H "execute/give") most plausibly relates to.

**Psa 57:1** — *"Be merciful to me, O God... in the shadow of your wings I will take refuge..."*
- `[T7]` H0430G God — `[M50]` H2603A "merciful" (Grace & Mercy) — `[T8]` H1732 David (implicit
  speaker) — `[T3]` H1272 "fled", H5674A "pass by" — `[M19]` H1942/H2620 refuge-related (Trust & Refuge)
- Looser fit: the T3 verbs here (fled/pass by) describe David's own flight, not a direct
  divine→human action — worth reading as an example of why co-occurrence alone isn't sufficient
  (§ caveat above).

## Adversarial (T4) → verb (T3) → M-code

**2Ki 23:24** — *"Josiah put away the mediums and the necromancers... that he might establish the
words of the law."*
- `[T8]` H2977G Josiah (human agent) — `[T3]` H1197I "put away", H6965I "establish" —
  `[T4]` H3049 "mediums" (spiritist, the adversarial-class object being acted on) —
  `[M65]`/`[M54]` "words"/"law" (Speech & Tongue / Torah & Obedience)
- Here the adversarial party is the **object/target** of the T3 verb (Josiah purges the mediums),
  not the source — a genuinely different direction from the divine/human examples above, worth
  naming explicitly when the discovery-question set (source/target) gets built.

**Rev 12:12** — *"But woe to you, O earth and sea, for the devil has come down to you in great
wrath, because he knows that his time is short!"*
- `[T4]` G1228G "devil" — `[T3]` G2597 "come down", G2192 "have/be [in wrath]" — `[M02]` G2372
  "wrath" (Anger & Wrath) — `[T10]` G3772 "heavens" — here the adversarial party is the **source**
  of the T3 action, the opposite direction from 2Ki 23:24 above. Both directions are real and both
  matter.

## What this is for

Raw material for the researcher's own reading pass — not a conclusion. Patterns worth noticing on a
first read, flagged not asserted: (1) the adversarial/angelic party can be either source OR target
of a T3 verb (2Ki 23:24 vs. Rev 12:12) — direction isn't fixed by which T-code is present; (2)
several verses carry 3+ scenario categories at once (Job 11:14: body-part + place + negation +
M-code), meaning real verses rarely isolate one clean scenario type — the eventual mechanism will
need to handle multiple simultaneous arguments, not one per verse; (3) the Calendar category (T15)
consistently plays a temporal-anchor role, never a participant — possibly not a `verb_argument`
"argument" at all, worth deciding explicitly rather than forcing it into the same slot as party/place/
object arguments.
