scratch on the next book.

## Why this exists

`WA-passage-read-guidance` and `WA-interpretation-questions` answer *what* to isolate (a
phenomenon, per verse, per inner being, with its warrant) and *what to ask* of each candidate
(Q1-Q12). Neither says *how* to actually extract the warrant from the text. In practice that gap
was filled, on the interim check, by reading the English translation and checking it against a
mental list of disposition-words — which produced a plausible-looking table that was nonetheless
wrong in specific, checkable ways once the same verses were re-read from the underlying lexical
data. The corrective is not a new phenomenon rule; it is a reading discipline. This document is
that discipline, stated as steps.

**Scope.** Applies wherever Phase 1 step 3/3b (phenomenon isolation + warrant) and the Q1-Q4
interrogative are executed against a verse-span-meaning extract — every book, not just Obadiah.

**Input this technique assumes.** The verse-span-meaning extract's per-clause row: `# | surface |
strong | morph | particle | meaning` — where `meaning` carries the stepGloss and the full
meaning_tree (the word's entire lexical range, not a single contextual gloss). See
`obad-1-verse-span-meaning.md` for the exact shape.

---

## The technique, step by step

### T1 — Work from the row, not the gloss
The unit of analysis is the row (surface + strong + morph + full meaning_tree/stepGloss), not the
English translation printed above the table. The translation orients the reader; it is not the
evidence. Do not extract English clauses from it and match them against a checklist of
disposition-words — that is the exact method that produced the interim check's misses.

### T2 — Pull the full lexical range before assigning a sense
Read the word's entire meaning_tree entry for its Strong's code(s), not just the stepGloss or the
one sense the English translation happens to use. Record explicitly when:
- a "bad"/disposition sense (arrogant, contemptuous, etc.) is already a standing member of the
  word's own lexical range — this is **stated via the lexicon**, not inferred from metaphor or
  surrounding imagery; say so ("this verb's own semantic range already contains X as a live
  sense, independent of the imagery around it") rather than treating it as an inference;
- the range is genuinely ambiguous across senses that matter for this verse (e.g. "vision"
  spanning ecstatic-state / night-vision / oracle-as-divine-communication / book-title) — name the
  live senses and reason which is operative here, rather than silently picking one.

### T3 — Let morph decide voice, person, and aspect — never the English word order or tense
Read the morph code for every verb before deciding stated/inferred, tense, or voice:
- perfect vs. imperfect vs. participle carry different aspectual force (a passive **participle**
  is a durative/ongoing condition — "is, continually" — not a future event); check this against
  the tense the English translation happens to use, and correct the reading where they diverge
  (record the correction explicitly, per T9 — don't just quietly swap the answer);
- person/number (1cp, 3ms, 2mp, etc.) decides who the grammatical subject actually is — do not
  infer this from an English pronoun alone;
- voice (Qal active / Niphal / Pual / Hiphil / passive participle) decides whether the clause's
  subject is agent or patient, which in turn decides whether that party is the phenomenon's
  **source** or its **bearer**.

### T4 — Separate the causing action (source) from the resulting state (the phenomenon)
Where a verse supplies both an action that causes a condition and the condition itself (e.g. "I
will make you small" → "you are despised"), record both halves: the acting party as source, and
the party whose actual state is described as the phenomenon-bearer. Report the resultant state on
its own terms — do not credit the causing party's own disposition merely because they caused it
(`WA-interpretation-questions` Part B.5), and do not drop the causal half either; both belong in
the record.

### T5 — Referent cruxes: name every grammatically live reading, adopt one explicitly, keep the rest on record
When a pronoun or unnamed party is genuinely ambiguous (e.g. "we" in Obad 1 — prophet+divine
council, prophet+Israel/Judah, or Edom overhearing its own doom are all grammatically live), do
not silently resolve it and do not default to the most obvious English reading:
1. enumerate every reading that is grammatically or contextually live;
2. give the textual grounds for each;
3. adopt one explicitly, stating why, and flag whether the choice is a directed/researcher call or
   this pass's own default;
4. keep the rejected alternatives on record, not just the winner — a later verse or a researcher
   review may overturn the choice.

This is `WA-interpretation-questions` Part B.2 ("referential debate is legitimate") executed at the
clause level, not skipped past.

### T6 — Unstated agents: record as genuinely underdetermined, not resolved either way
A passive verb with no stated agent (e.g. "a messenger has been sent" — Pual, agent unstated)
raises exactly the question Q4 (source of enablement) asks. Where the text itself declines to
answer it, the correct record is **"expected an agent, none given — genuinely underdetermined,"**
not a guess in either direction, and not silence as if the question were never asked.

### T7 — Record genre-conventional elements that are expected but textually absent
Where the verse's form (a vision report, a call narrative, a lament, etc.) conventionally carries
an accompanying element elsewhere in the corpus (e.g. awe, prostration, or fear alongside a vision
report — cf. Daniel, Ezekiel), and that element is absent here, record the absence explicitly as an
observation. Do not fill it in from the convention, and do not pass over the comparison silently.
This is a stronger, comparison-anchored form of "silence is a finding"
(`WA-interpretation-questions` Part B.4): the absence is worth naming because it was actively
checked against what the form usually carries, not merely because nothing was stated.

### T8 — Split distinct phenomena even where one is evidence for another
Where a verse holds more than one phenomenon for the same inner being — one an underlying
disposition, another its own separately-expressed effect (e.g. Edom's pride as an internal agent
of self-deception in v3, and Edom's own interior speech "who will bring me down?" as a distinct
boast/false-security phenomenon) — register both separately, per `WA-passage-read-guidance` step 3
note (c). Do not merge the second into the first just because it is evidence of it.

### T9 — Carry corrections forward explicitly, never silently
Where a re-read corrects an earlier pass (a referent, a tense, a missed phenomenon), state the
correction in the record itself — what changed and why — rather than quietly replacing the earlier
conclusion. This keeps the trail available for researcher review instead of erasing it.

### T10 — Self-check before closing a verse
Before moving to the next verse, confirm every phenomenon (and every recorded silence,
underdetermined case, or genre-absence) is traceable to a specific lexical or morphological fact —
the Strong's code, the morph code, or the meaning_tree entry that grounds it — not to an English
keyword match. If a finding cannot be pointed at a specific row/field, it has not been established
under this technique yet.

## Relationship to the existing guidance

This document sits underneath `WA-passage-read-guidance-v1.5` Phase 1 (steps 1-3b) and
`WA-interpretation-questions-v1.4` (Q1-Q6; Part B.2, B.4, B.5). It adds no new phenomenon-
identification rule and changes nothing about what counts as a phenomenon or how Phases 2/3 work.
It specifies the mechanics of executing Phase 1 against the actual verse-span-meaning extract, so
that the same verse read twice — by this instrument, or checked by the researcher — converges on
the same reading and the same warrant, rather than on whatever the English translation happens to
suggest.
