## Purpose

The read guidance answers *what* to isolate: operations and their parts. This document answers *how to interrogate* each verse so that potential and hidden parts of a human's operation or status are surfaced rather than missed.

It exists because of a specific failure observed in reading: a stated outward act was treated as having no inner-being bearing, when in fact every human act invites a question about the interior behind it and about the source of that interior. The corrective is interrogative — not "is an inner state stated?" but "what inner state must be asked after here, and what is its source and target?"

---

## Part A — The interrogative

Apply to every human, and every human act, in a qualifying verse. **Every question is considered for every candidate; a question need not resolve, but it must be considered.** Considering a question may itself raise a new one (see Q10).

- **Q1 — Focused inner being.** Is this person a focused inner being in their own right, even where only an outward act is stated? (Do not let an unstated interior collapse into "no bearing.")
- **Q2 — Implied interior.** What interior state, disposition, intent, or volition could underlie or be implied by the stated act or status?
- **Q3 — Stated vs inferred.** Is that interior stated in the text or span-data, or inferred? If inferred, raise and name it as referential; never assert it as fact.
- **Q4 — Source (state vs enablement).** What is the source of the interior state, and — separately — the source of the enablement to act? Self / another human / a non-human being / an object or situation. Are the two sources the same? Is either stated or inferred?
- **Q5 — Target.** What does the operation impact — another inner being, a human, a collective, a non-human being, or an object/situation?
- **Q6 — State or movement.** Is the operation a state/status, or a movement (come from, go to, impact on, emerge, go away, become evident)?
- **Q7 — Linkage.** What linkages run to other operations in the passage? Where a linkage is absent, surface the absence; do not pass over it silently.
- **Q8 — Collective.** If the human is a collective (tribe, youths, nation, gentiles…), what is the nature of that collective, and how does that reshape the operation as a movement to a collection rather than to an individual?
- **Q9 — Sufficiency.** Does the document contain the data needed to weigh this operation's inner-being significance? If not, name the missing data as an insufficiency; do not supply it from outside.
- **Q10 — Emergent questions.** After Q1–Q9, what new question has this verse raised that the instrument does not yet hold, and that may matter for future analysis? Log it and carry it forward.

---

## Part B — Guidance of interpretation

1. **Consider, don't force-resolve.** Leaving a question open is acceptable; not considering it is not.
2. **Referential debate is legitimate.** Where parties or interiors are unnamed but implied, the debate is conducted and recorded — provided nothing inferred is imported as fact.
3. **Inference is named as inference,** at the point it is made.
4. **Silence is a finding.** Where the text states no interior, the silence is recorded as the result, not filled. A forward-pointer may be attached where a later verse or passage is expected to bear on it.
5. **Source of state ≠ source of enablement.** A non-human being (e.g. the Lord) may be stated as the source of an *outcome or enablement* without the text sourcing the actor's *disposition*. Keep the two separate; extending sourcing from outcome to interior is an interpretive step to be flagged, not assumed.
6. **Observation / interpretation / reflection are distinguished and labelled.** Observation = what the text or data states. Interpretation = what is reasoned from it, including referential inference. Reflection = consequence, tension, significance.
7. **Insufficiency is named, not papered over.** Where required data (e.g. name etymologies) is absent from the base extract, say so; do not substitute remembered or external knowledge.
8. **Emergent questions are carried forward,** so they can be applied to or tested against later passages.

## Part C — Output directive (supersedes read-guidance step 4–5 output)

Per passage, the output is a **single debate document** — `WA-[passage]-debate-[version]-[date].md` — that captures the whole consideration:

- the working scope, declared as an assumption;
- per-verse observation / interpretation / reflection;
- every interrogative question considered, whether resolved or not;
- referential debates recorded (never imported as fact);
- operations and their parts (subject, operation, source, target) where determinable, and marked where silent, inferred, or insufficiently evidenced;
- linkages and surfaced non-linkages;
- an insufficiencies register;
- an emergent-questions log.

This replaces the earlier instruction to produce a separate operations `.md` with one paragraph plus a parts breakdown per operation. See `WA-passage-read-guidance-v1.1-2026-07-26.md`, change-control note.
