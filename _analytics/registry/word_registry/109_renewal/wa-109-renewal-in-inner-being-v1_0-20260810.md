## Preamble — the field and its spine

"Renewal" gathers several ways of making something new again. Registry 109 links sixteen Strong's across families that circle one idea from different sides: the Greek renew-and-rebirth group (*anathallō*, *anakainizō*, *anakainoō*, *anakainōsis*, *ananeoomai*, *palingenesia*), the Hebrew renew-and-repair verb *chadash*, the change-and-pass pair *chalaph* / *chalifah*, the heal-and-restore family *rapha* / *arukhah* (with *riphut*, *teruphah*), the waking verb *qits*, and the single "grow fresh" verb *rutaphash*.

Read as movements of the inner being, the field does not split into opposing poles. It presents an **axis**: at one end **restoration**, a return to a former and better state; at the other end **re-creation**, a passage into a state genuinely new. Between and around that axis run two edges the evidence keeps close to renewal — a **healing** edge, where an inner wound is mended, and an **awakening** edge, where the dormant is roused, reaching as far as resurrection.

The spine carried through every movement is the method's: *how does renewal operate in the inner being* — its source or cause, its inner operation, and where the effect lands: mind, spirit, strength, or heart.

*(Evidence note: the report samples one example verse per span. Every incidence below is grounded in that report; the reading is extract-level, and a verse-exhaustive pass remains outstanding. The registry also bundles physical repair, object-healing, and spatial "passing" that fall outside inner-being renewal; those are set out in the register, not worked into the movements.)*

## Part I — The inner self made new: renewal as re-creation

At one end of the axis, renewal is not a return but a remaking. The clearest incidence names the object directly: though "our outer self is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day" (*2 Corinthians 4:16*). The operation is continuous and interior, set against bodily decline; its effect is that "we do not lose heart" — the inner being held up while the outer fails.

The seat of this remaking is the mind. Renewal here is the mechanism of transformation: "be transformed by the renewal of your mind" (*Romans 12:2*), whose stated effect is discernment — that one "may discern what is the will of God." Paul locates the same movement in the mind's spirit — "to be renewed in the spirit of your minds" (*Ephesians 4:23*). At its fullest the movement is rebirth, and its source is named: the "washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit" (*Titus 3:5*), grounded not in works but in mercy. Across these, the source is God or the Spirit, the operation is an inward re-creation, and the effect reaches mind, spirit, and heart.

---

## Part II — Restoration: the depleted brought back

At the other end of the axis, renewal is a return — a worn or spent state replenished to what it was. The plea form asks for it directly: "Renew our days as of old" (*Lamentations 5:21*), a restoration of a former condition and relationship. The vigour form describes it: "your youth is renewed like the eagle's" (*Psalm 103:5*), and, of the body, "let his flesh become fresh with youth" so that a man may "return to the days of his youthful vigor" (*Job 33:25*). The strength form is corporate: "let the peoples renew their strength" (*Isaiah 41:1*) — strength drawn back up in those who wait.

Restoration can also be of an inner posture, and the field marks where it is barred. Of those who have fallen away, it is "impossible... to restore them again to repentance" (*Hebrews 6:6*) — repentance is treated as a state that renewal would return, and its non-return is the point. And restoration can be of an affection: Paul rejoices that "you have revived your concern for me" (*Philippians 4:10*), the verb carrying the sense of blooming again — a care that had lapsed brought back into leaf. The source is variously God, time, or another's turning; the effect lands on vigour, strength, and the affections.

---

## Part III — Healing as renewal: the inner wound mended

Where the hurt is inward, healing and renewal become the same movement. The condition healed is often not a body but a disposition: "I will heal their apostasy" (*Hosea 14:4*) — the wound is turning-away, and the healing is its reversal, joined to freely-given love. The personal plea makes healer and refuge one: "Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed... for you are my praise" (*Jeremiah 17:14*). Healing is set beside forgiveness as a paired inner work — the One "who forgives all your iniquity, who heals all your diseases" (*Psalm 103:3*).

The effect is restored wholeness. The Lord promises, "I will restore health to you, and your wounds I will heal" (*Jeremiah 30:17*), to those called outcast; the healing "shall spring up speedily" like dawn (*Isaiah 58:8*); and it reaches the body's core — "healing to your flesh and refreshment to your bones" (*Proverbs 3:8*), where the refreshment is felt inwardly. Behind all of it stands the named source: "I am the Lord, your healer" (*Exodus 15:26*). The operation is a mending of what is broken in the person; the effect is wholeness and restored favour.

*(Only the inner and figurative healing spans are drawn on here; the family's physical and object senses — healed water, mended pottery, repaired walls — are registered below as outside inner-being renewal.)*

## Part IV — Awakening and the reach past death

The last edge rouses what has gone dormant. Waking is renewal from sleep: "I lay down and slept; I woke again, for the Lord sustained me" (*Psalm 3:5*) — the waking held within the Lord's keeping. It can dispel an illusion — "like a dream when one awakes" (*Psalm 73:20*). At its furthest the waking is from death itself: "your dead shall live... awake and sing for joy" (*Isaiah 26:19*), where waking and resurrection and song run together.

At this edge renewal reaches past the grave, and the field frames it as a question and a hope. Job asks it plainly — "If a man dies, shall he live again?" — and waits "till my renewal should come" (*Job 14:14*), the noun for a change of state set against death. The image beneath it is a felled tree: "there is hope for a tree, if it be cut down, that it will sprout again" (*Job 14:7*), the question being whether a man may. At its widest the same word for rebirth names a remade order — "in the new world, when the Son of Man will sit on his glorious throne" (*Matthew 19:28*). *(This cosmic reach is the boundary of the inner-being scope and is noted in the register.)* The source is the Lord who sustains and raises; the operation is the dormant or dead roused; the effect is life, song, and hope held against death.

- **Physical repair / object-healing (outside inner-being renewal):** repair of walls, altar, and house (*Nehemiah 4:7; 2 Chronicles 24:12–13; 2 Chronicles 15:8; Isaiah 61:4; 1 Kings 18:30*); breaches repaired (*Psalm 60:2*); water healed / made fresh (*2 Kings 2:22; Ezekiel 47:9*); pottery that can never be mended (*Jeremiah 19:11*); physicians embalming (*Genesis 50:2*); "thoroughly healed" of an injury (*Exodus 21:19*); leaves for healing (*Ezekiel 47:12*). Used only the figurative/inner spans of *rapha*, *chadash*, *arukhah*.
- **Change / passing senses (outside the concept):** *chalaph*'s spatial and temporal uses — glided, go by, go on, moves on, sweep, passes through, through, over, place, exchange — and the opposite-sense "violated / transgressed" (*Isaiah 24:5*).
- **Clothing changes:** *chalifah* / *chalaph* — changes of clothing (*2 Kings 5:23*), garments (*Judges 14:19*), shifts (*1 Kings 5:14*), and David changing clothes (*2 Samuel 12:20*, flagged as a boundary case: the literal change marks a grief-to-worship turn).
- **Rousing God, not a human inner being:** *qits* — "Rouse yourself" / "Awake" addressed to the Lord (*Psalm 35:23; Psalm 59:5*).
- **Nature-transience imagery (boundary):** grass "renewed in the morning" then swept away (*Psalm 90:5*).
- **Cosmic scope (boundary):** the "new world" / regeneration of the order (*Matthew 19:28*); the inner-being span of *palingenesia* used here is the rebirth of the person (*Titus 3:5*).
- **Zero-occurrence Strong's:** G7361 *epanakainizō* — not built in any processed book; nothing to synthesise.

## Interpretation — labelled, kept inside the document (not conclusions)

Read as movements, renewal in this field runs along a single **axis** with two edges. The axis stretches from **restoration** — a return to a former, better state ("renew our days as of old"; youth renewed; strength renewed) — to **re-creation** — a passage into a genuinely new state (the inner self renewed, the mind renewed, regeneration). The **healing edge** treats an inner wound (apostasy, distress) as the thing renewal mends; the **awakening edge** carries the movement from ordinary waking to resurrection ("till my renewal should come"). Across every movement the source is consistently God or the Spirit, and the effect lands inwardly — on mind, spirit, strength, and heart. This is the structure the spans themselves present; nothing beyond the document is imported.
