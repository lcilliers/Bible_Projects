# 3. The heart: the centre

If I had to name the one word Scripture leans on most for the inside of a person, it would be the heart: *lēv* and *lēvāv* in Hebrew, *kardia* in Greek. It is also the word with the widest reach. I came to it last and it turned out to be first.

## The middle of things

The Hebrew word for heart is also the ordinary word for the *middle* of something. Ships sink "in the heart of the seas" (Ezekiel 27:26). The mountain burned "to the heart of heaven" (Deuteronomy 4:11). The Son of Man will be "three days and three nights in the heart of the earth" (Matthew 12:40). A person's heart is, quite simply, their middle, the centre from which everything else is reached.

It is also the physical heart in the chest. An arrow can go through it (2 Kings 9:24). And when Aaron goes before the Lord, he wears the names of the tribes "on his heart" on the breastpiece (Exodus 28:29–30). The physical place and the inner centre are one word. Aaron carries his people on his heart in both senses at once.

## The heart speaks to itself

The heart is the place where a person talks without a voice. Scripture says, over and over, that someone "said in his heart":

- Abraham laughed and "said to himself" (Genesis 17:17).
- Esau planned murder in his heart (Genesis 27:41).
- The fool "says in his heart, 'There is no God'" (Psalm 14:1).
- "Beware lest you say in your heart, 'My power and the might of my hand have gotten me this wealth'" (Deuteronomy 8:17).
- Babylon "said in her heart, 'I am, and there is no one besides me'" (Isaiah 47:8).

What the heart says to itself is very often a verdict on itself, and very often a false one. "I shall not be moved" (Psalm 10:6). The heart's private speech is where self-deception lives.

The same inner speech can be turned toward God. Hannah "was speaking in her heart; only her lips moved, and her voice was not heard" (1 Samuel 1:13). Abraham's servant says, "Before I had finished speaking in my heart, behold, Rebekah came out" (Genesis 24:45). The prayer was complete, and answered, before a word was spoken aloud. The opposite is true as well: "They do not cry to me from the heart, but they wail upon their beds" (Hosea 7:14). A loud cry that does not come from the heart does not count.

## The heart thinks, attends and remembers

Much of what we would call the mind, Scripture calls the heart.

- **It plans.** "The heart of man plans his way, but the Lord establishes his steps" (Proverbs 16:9). "Many are the plans in the mind [heart] of a man, but it is the purpose of the Lord that will stand" (Proverbs 19:21).
- **It understands.** Solomon asks for "an understanding mind" (1 Kings 3:9). The Hebrew is a *hearing heart*. The prophets speak of people who will not "understand with their hearts" (Isaiah 6:10; Matthew 13:15).
- **It pays attention.** To pay attention is literally to *set the heart*. Haggai's "Consider your ways" is "set your heart on your ways" (Haggai 1:5). The Preacher "applied my heart to seek and to search out" (Ecclesiastes 1:13). The failure is its opposite: "it burned him up, but he did not take it to heart" (Isaiah 42:25).
- **It remembers and holds.** "I have stored up your word in my heart" (Psalm 119:11). Mary "treasured up all these things, pondering them in her heart" (Luke 2:19). Mary's second treasuring comes straight after a saying she "did not understand" (2:50–51). Daniel, alarmed by his vision, "kept the matter in my heart" (Daniel 7:28). **The heart holds what the mind cannot yet resolve.**

## The heart decides and is moved

- "Daniel resolved [set on his heart] that he would not defile himself with the king's food" (Daniel 1:8).
- "Each one must give as he has decided in his heart" (2 Corinthians 9:7).
- David had it "in my heart to build a house" (1 Chronicles 28:2).
- When the tabernacle was built, "everyone whose heart stirred him" brought an offering (Exodus 35:21).
- The heart's purposes run deep: "The purpose in a man's heart is like deep water, but a man of understanding will draw it out" (Proverbs 20:5).

## The heart feels

The heart carries the widest range of feeling of any of the inner words. It rejoices (Psalm 16:9), grieves (Nehemiah 2:2), melts in fear (Joshua 7:5), takes courage ("be strong, and let your heart take courage", Psalm 27:14), desires ("the desires of your heart", Psalm 37:4), loves ("the king's heart went out to Absalom", 2 Samuel 14:1) and despises ("she despised him in her heart", 2 Samuel 6:16). Grief is felt there like physical pain: "My anguish, my anguish! I writhe in pain! Oh the walls of my heart! My heart is beating wildly" (Jeremiah 4:19).

Scripture reaches for materials to describe what state a heart is in. It can be stone (Ezekiel 36:26), or so hard it is "diamond-hard" (Zechariah 7:12). It can be "like wax … melted within my breast" (Psalm 22:14), or "unfeeling like fat" (Psalm 119:70). It can be like an oven (Hosea 7:6), or "struck down like grass and has withered" (Psalm 102:4). It is "a stream of water in the hand of the Lord" (Proverbs 21:1). And it is a mirror: "As in water face reflects face, so the heart of man reflects the man" (Proverbs 27:19).

## The heart is the source

Jesus is direct about this: "out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks" (Matthew 12:34). "Out of the heart come evil thoughts, murder, adultery …" (Matthew 15:19). Proverbs says the same in its own way: "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life" (Proverbs 4:23). In the verses around it, the guarding moves outward from the heart to the mouth, then the eyes, then the feet (4:20–27).

Mouth and heart can be in step: "if you confess with your mouth … and believe in your heart" (Romans 10:9). Or they can be out of step: "This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me" (Isaiah 29:13). "His speech was smooth as butter, yet war was in his heart" (Psalm 55:21).

## The heart holds what is put into it

The heart is the place into which things are put. God writes on it: "I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts" (Jeremiah 31:33). His Spirit is given into it (Galatians 4:6), and his love is poured into it (Romans 5:5). Wisdom comes into it (Proverbs 2:10). God "has put eternity into man's heart" (Ecclesiastes 3:11). But people also take "idols into their hearts" (Ezekiel 14:3), and Satan filled Ananias's heart (Acts 5:3). It is soil in which the word is sown (Luke 8:15), and a treasury: "the good person out of the good treasure of his heart produces good" (Luke 6:45). "Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also" (Matthew 6:21).

## The heart has its own sight

A few verses give the heart its own senses. "The eyes of your hearts enlightened" (Ephesians 1:18). God "has shone in our hearts" (2 Corinthians 4:6). "A veil lies over their hearts" (2 Corinthians 3:15). And the lover in the Song says, "I slept, but my heart was awake" (Song 5:2).

## The heart judges itself

Before the New Testament has a word for conscience, the heart does the work. "David's heart struck him" (1 Samuel 24:5). "My heart does not reproach me" (Job 27:6). "Your heart knows that many times you yourself have cursed others" (Ecclesiastes 7:22). The New Testament keeps this: "whenever our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart" (1 John 3:20).

## The heart is hidden, and God sees it

"The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick; who can understand it?" (Jeremiah 17:9). "The heart knows its own bitterness, and no stranger shares its joy" (Proverbs 14:10). Other people cannot fully see into it, and often its owner cannot either. But "man looks on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7). God is the one who "knows the secrets of the heart" (Psalm 44:21). He tests it and weighs it: "the Lord weighs the heart" (Proverbs 21:2).

## Is the heart the centre of it all?

It is sometimes said that the heart is where soul and spirit meet. No verse says that. What the verses do show is that the heart is the **middle of the person**, as it is the middle of the sea. It is the place where a person thinks, speaks inwardly, decides, feels, remembers, keeps and judges. It is the place into which God, other people and other powers put things. And it is the part God examines. Whether that makes the heart *the* integrating centre of the inner being, I leave open. The evidence leans that way, but I have not yet read enough of the other inner-being words to say it.
