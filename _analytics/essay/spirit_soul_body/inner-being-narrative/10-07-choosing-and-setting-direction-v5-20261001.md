# 10.7 Choosing and setting direction

Scripture speaks of setting, inclining, turning and directing the heart.

- "Incline your heart to the Lord, the God of Israel" (Joshua 24:23).
- "Incline my heart to your testimonies, and not to selfish gain!" (Psalm 119:36).
- "Direct your heart to the Lord and serve him only" (1 Samuel 7:3).
- The heart can be whole or divided: "unite my heart to fear your name" (Psalm 86:11), against "a double heart" (Psalm 12:2). Solomon's "heart was not wholly true to the Lord his God" (1 Kings 11:4).
- It can be stubborn. "The stubbornness of their evil hearts" runs through Jeremiah (Jeremiah 7:24).

"Everyone whose heart stirred him, and everyone whose spirit moved him" (Exodus 35:21) puts heart and spirit side by side.

Fear can stop a choice from being carried out.
- Gideon told his son to kill the captive kings, "But the young man did not draw his sword, for he was afraid, because he was still a young man" (Judges 8:20).
- Saul told his armor-bearer to kill him, "But his armor-bearer would not, for he feared greatly" (1 Samuel 31:4).
- The army found honey in the forest, "but no one put his hand to his mouth, for the people feared the oath" (1 Samuel 14:26).

Fear can also set a direction. Uriah "was afraid and fled and escaped to Egypt" (Jeremiah 26:21). Fear of God's word set some of Pharaoh's servants moving: "whoever feared the word of the Lord among the servants of Pharaoh hurried his slaves and his livestock into the houses" (Exodus 9:20). "One who is wise is cautious and turns away from evil, but a fool is reckless and careless" (Proverbs 14:16). There, "cautious" renders the fear word.

When fear is spoken to, other commands come with it.
- "Be strong and courageous. Do not fear or be in dread of them, for it is the Lord your God who goes with you" (Deuteronomy 31:6).
- "Fear not, stand firm, and see the salvation of the Lord" (Exodus 14:13).
- "Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not be frightened, and do not be dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go" (Joshua 1:9).
- Before battle: "let not your heart faint. Do not fear or panic or be in dread of them" (Deuteronomy 20:3).
- "Be careful, be quiet, do not fear, and do not let your heart be faint" (Isaiah 7:4).

Choosing grows out of thinking, and God reads the intent underneath the plan. Of the king of Assyria: "he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy" (Isaiah 10:7). The heart's leaning can also be held and directed. David prays: "keep forever such purposes and thoughts in the hearts of your people, and direct their hearts toward you" (1 Chronicles 29:18). A leaning that is set on God brings peace: "You keep him in perfect peace whose mind is stayed on you, because he trusts in you" (Isaiah 26:3).

Turning is the great choice Scripture asks for under God's anger, and it is asked of the heart: "rend your hearts and not your garments" (Joel 2:13). It can be done halfway. Judah "did not return to me with her whole heart, but in pretense" (Jeremiah 3:10). And it can be refused by a heart that does not attend to what it is doing. Babylon "did not lay these things to heart or remember their end" (Isaiah 47:7). Chapter 13 follows this.

Jehoshaphat set the fear of the Lord before the judges: "let the fear of the Lord be upon you. Be careful what you do, for there is no injustice with the Lord our God, or partiality or taking bribes" (2 Chronicles 19:7). And when "the dread of the Lord fell upon the people", "they came out as one man" (1 Samuel 11:7).
