# 10.2 Knowing and hearing

Knowing belongs chiefly to the heart, as Chapter 3 showed. To pay attention is to set the heart. To understand is to have a *hearing heart*. To remember is to keep a thing in the heart. The spirit knows the self from within: "who knows a person's thoughts except the spirit of that person, which is in him?" (1 Corinthians 2:11). The soul receives what is known as something good to taste: "knowledge will be pleasant to your soul" (Proverbs 2:10).

**Hearing is the main way in.** The ear receives, and the heart keeps it or refuses it. "All my words that I shall speak to you receive in your heart, and hear with your ears" (Ezekiel 3:10). "Making your ear attentive to wisdom and inclining your heart to understanding" (Proverbs 2:2). When the heart is hard, hearing stops: "Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he would not listen" (Exodus 7:13). "They made their hearts diamond-hard lest they should hear the law" (Zechariah 7:12). A crushed spirit can block hearing as well. Israel "did not listen to Moses, because of their broken spirit and harsh slavery" (Exodus 6:9).

What is heard moves everything inside. Rahab says, "As soon as we heard it, our hearts melted, and there was no spirit left in any man because of you" (Joshua 2:11). Good news works the other way: "Like cold water to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country" (Proverbs 25:25). And hearing is a kind of eating: "Listen diligently to me, and eat what is good, and delight yourselves in rich food. Incline your ear, and come to me; hear, that your soul may live" (Isaiah 55:2–3).

Sometimes hearing is not enough, and the inner being needs to *see*. Jacob's heart went numb at the news that Joseph was alive. His spirit revived only when he saw the wagons (Genesis 45:26–27).

**Hearing goes both ways.** The ear is the way in, and it is also where what goes out arrives. God hears what people say: "You have heard their taunts, O Lord, all their plots against me" (Lamentations 3:61). "Then those who feared the Lord spoke with one another. The Lord paid attention and heard them" (Malachi 3:16). And refusing to hear goes with devising. "I am bringing disaster upon this people, the fruit of their devices, because they have not paid attention to my words" (Jeremiah 6:19). Jeremiah's enemies plot against him with these words: "let us not pay attention to any of his words" (Jeremiah 18:18).

What is heard can also kindle. "I was very angry when I heard their outcry and these words" (Nehemiah 5:6). And when what is heard lowers the hearers' standing or exposes their guilt, rage can refuse to hear any more. Stephen's hearers "were enraged, and they ground their teeth at him … But they cried out with a loud voice and stopped their ears and rushed together at him" (Acts 7:54, 57). James puts hearing first for this very reason: "let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God" (James 1:19–20).

Some things are only understood later. Of God's anger, Jeremiah says, "In the latter days you will understand it clearly" (Jeremiah 23:20). Chapter 13 follows how people read, and misread, what has happened to them.

What is known is also kept and returned to. Remembering feeds thinking: "I remember the days of old; I meditate on all that you have done; I ponder the work of your hands" (Psalm 143:5).

**Connections:** into thinking (10.4); into feeling (10.1), since what is heard kindles anger; with remembering (10.3); from speaking (10.5), since what is said is heard; with feeling (10.1), since what is heard melts or revives the heart; with God (10.9).
