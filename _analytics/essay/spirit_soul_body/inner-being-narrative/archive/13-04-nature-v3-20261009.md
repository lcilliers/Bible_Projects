# 13.4 Nature

Nature here means the creatures and the world in their own right, as the verses speak of them before God.

**God's look on what he made.** The first seeing in Scripture is God's. After each work, "God saw that it was good": the sea creatures and the birds (Genesis 1:21), the beasts of the earth (1:25). At the end, "God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good" (1:31). His look approves what he made before anything in it has done anything. The next look in the story, the woman's at the tree, uses the same words and goes another way (10.6, *Through the eye*).

**The creatures' thirst and hunger met.** "You make springs gush forth in the valleys; they flow between the hills; they give drink to every beast of the field; the wild donkeys quench their thirst" (Psalm 104:10–11). The same list runs on without a break to people: "wine to gladden the heart of man, oil to make his face shine and bread to strengthen man's heart" (104:15). Then it gathers them together: "These all look to you, to give them their food in due season. When you give it to them, they gather it up; when you open your hand, they are filled with good things" (104:27–28). The creatures' need and a man's heart are met by one open hand. Psalm 145:16 says the same: "you satisfy the desire of every living thing" (10.6).

When the water fails, the creatures are pictured turning to God: *"Even the beasts of the field pant for you because the water brooks are dried up"* (Joel 1:20). The same verb is used of the sun: it *"hastens to the place where it rises"* (Ecclesiastes 1:5), and of a person panting for God's commandments (Psalm 119:131; 10.6).

The psalmist also takes the creature's thirst as the picture of his own: "As a deer pants for flowing streams, so pants my soul for you, O God" (Psalm 42:1; Chapter 5). The difference is in what is thirsted for. The donkey's thirst is met by water. The psalmist's thirst is for God himself.

**A creature without understanding.** God shows Job the ostrich: "For she leaves her eggs to the earth and lets them be warmed on the ground, forgetting that a foot may crush them … She deals cruelly with her young, as if they were not hers … because God has made her forget wisdom and given her no share in understanding" (Job 39:14–17). Wisdom and understanding, which are inner words when they are used of people, are named here as things God gave or withheld in a creature.

**Willing.** "Is the wild ox willing to serve you?" (Job 39:9; 13.1, *Willing*).
