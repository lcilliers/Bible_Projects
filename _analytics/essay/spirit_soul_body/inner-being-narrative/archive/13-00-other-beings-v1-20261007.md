# 13. Other beings

This book is about the human inner being. But the words Scripture uses for the inner life are not used of people alone. God chooses, loves, is angry and is grieved. Angels "long to look" into what God has done. Spirits trouble, deceive and are driven out. The earth mourns, and the trees of the field clap their hands. The same words describe them all.

So I have to ask what a word means when it is used of someone, or something, other than a person. I take these other beings in this chapter, each in its own section. I do not read them as a picture of the human inner being, and I do not read the human inner being into them. I let the verses say what each is and does.

There are two reasons they belong in this book:

- They act on the inner being. God speaks to it, searches it and makes it new. Spirits come upon a person. The account of what happens in the person stays in the earlier chapters, where it belongs.
- God made the human person in his image. What the verses say of God's own character is therefore a key to what the inner being was made to be. I keep that question open. I do not answer it before the verses do.

## What I have found so far

This chapter is new, and it fills as the study goes on. The other beings already come up in earlier chapters:

- **God.** The fullest account so far is of his anger, in 10.13: what kindles it, that he is slow to anger, why he holds it back, and how it ends. His Spirit with the human spirit is in Chapter 6.
- **Angels.** They appear in Chapters 2, 4 and 11, and in 10.12 and 10.13.
- **Other spirits.** "Spirit-beings are real in Scripture, and they act on people" (Chapter 6, "Other spirits").
- **Nature.** The earth, the sea and the beasts come up in 17 of the chapter files.

As each part of the study is taken up again, what the verses say of these beings in their own right will be gathered here.
