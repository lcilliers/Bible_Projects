# 10.10 Relating to others

The inner being is not private. It reaches out, is reached, and is bound to other people.

- **Love lives in the soul.** "The soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul" (1 Samuel 18:1). The lover in the Song seeks "him whom my soul loves" (Song 3:1).
- **Hatred starts in the heart.** "You shall not hate your brother in your heart" (Leviticus 19:17). The law names the heart as the place of hatred. Esau's hatred began as heart-speech: "Esau said to himself, 'The days of mourning for my father are approaching; then I will kill my brother Jacob'" (Genesis 27:41). The next verse says Rebekah was told.
- **The spirit registers the presence and absence of friends.** "My spirit was not at rest because I did not find my brother Titus there" (2 Corinthians 2:13). Later, "his spirit has been refreshed by you all" (2 Corinthians 7:13). Friends "refreshed my spirit as well as yours" (1 Corinthians 16:18).
- **The spirit is the manner of approach.** "Shall I come to you with a rod, or with love in a spirit of gentleness?" (1 Corinthians 4:21).
- **Inner states spread.** The fainthearted soldier is sent home before battle "lest he make the heart of his fellows melt like his own" (Deuteronomy 20:8). "Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return home" (Judges 7:3).
- **Joy at another's fall is watched.** "Do not rejoice when your enemy falls, and let not your heart be glad when he stumbles" (Proverbs 24:17).

- **Thinking happens together.** The disciples "had argued with one another about who was the greatest" (Mark 9:34). Shared reasoning can become a shared deed: "they said to themselves, 'This is the heir. Let us kill him'" (Luke 20:14).
- **Reasoning together can become a quarrel.** The disciples' reasoning about greatness became "an argument … as to which of them was the greatest" (Luke 9:46). Paul tells the church to welcome the one who is weak in faith, "but not to quarrel over opinions" (Romans 14:1), and to "do all things without grumbling or disputing" (Philippians 2:14).
- **Thinking can make distinctions it should not.** When the rich man is given the good seat and the poor man told to stand: "have you not then made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts?" (James 2:4).
- **The heart can be absent from the words.** "'Eat and drink!' he says to you, but his heart is not with you" (Proverbs 23:7).
- **Love does not keep the count.** Love "is not irritable or resentful" (1 Corinthians 13:5). The Greek word behind "resentful" is the reckoning word: love does not reckon up wrong. It is the same word used of God, who in Christ was "not counting their trespasses against them" (2 Corinthians 5:19). Paul, deserted at his trial, prays: "May it not be charged against them!" (2 Timothy 4:16). The one who has done wrong asks for the same thing: "Let not my lord hold me guilty or remember how your servant did wrong" (2 Samuel 19:19). And where there is trust, the count is not even asked for: "they did not ask for an accounting from the men into whose hand they delivered the money … for they dealt honestly" (2 Kings 12:15).

## Fear between people

The fear of God is named in how others are to be treated. In the law it often stands as the "but" after a wrong that is forbidden.
- "You shall not curse the deaf or put a stumbling block before the blind, but you shall fear your God" (Leviticus 19:14).
- "You shall stand up before the gray head and honor the face of an old man, and you shall fear your God" (Leviticus 19:32).
- "You shall not wrong one another, but you shall fear your God" (Leviticus 25:17).
- "You shall not rule over him ruthlessly but shall fear your God" (Leviticus 25:43).

People act on it, or say they will.
- "The midwives feared God and did not do as the king of Egypt commanded them, but let the male children live" (Exodus 1:17).
- Nehemiah would not burden the people as earlier governors had: "But I did not do so, because of the fear of God" (Nehemiah 5:15).
- Joseph to his brothers: "Do this and you will live, for I fear God" (Genesis 42:18).
- Judges are to be "men who fear God, who are trustworthy and hate a bribe" (Exodus 18:21).

Its absence is named where people are harmed.
- Abraham: "There is no fear of God at all in this place, and they will kill me because of my wife" (Genesis 20:11).
- Amalek "attacked you on the way when you were faint and weary, and cut off your tail, those who were lagging behind you, and he did not fear God" (Deuteronomy 25:18).
- "He who withholds kindness from a friend forsakes the fear of the Almighty" (Job 6:14).

The same word is used of reverence for people. "Every one of you shall revere his mother and his father" (Leviticus 19:3). When "the Lord exalted Joshua in the sight of all Israel", "they stood in awe of him just as they had stood in awe of Moses" (Joshua 4:14). Israel "stood in awe of the king, because they perceived that the wisdom of God was in him to do justice" (1 Kings 3:28). "My son, fear the Lord and the king" (Proverbs 24:21).

People also speak to one another's fear, and give a reason.
- The midwife, to Rachel in labour: "Do not fear, for you have another son" (Genesis 35:17).
- Boaz: "do not fear. I will do for you all that you ask" (Ruth 3:11).
- Joseph: "Do not fear, for am I in the place of God? … So do not fear; I will provide for you and your little ones" (Genesis 50:19, 21).
- David to Mephibosheth: "Do not fear, for I will show you kindness for the sake of your father Jonathan" (2 Samuel 9:7).
- Elisha to his servant: "Do not be afraid, for those who are with us are more than those who are with them" (2 Kings 6:16).

The same words are also spoken to lead someone into harm (10.5). Fear is used as a weapon (Nehemiah 6:9, 13). And fear of a person can harden: "Saul was even more afraid of David. So Saul was David's enemy continually" (1 Samuel 18:29).

Trembling meets an arrival. The elders of Bethlehem came to meet Samuel "trembling and said, 'Do you come peaceably?'" (1 Samuel 16:4). "Ahimelech came to meet David, trembling" (1 Samuel 21:1).

Fear is also made.
- Ahithophel's counsel: "I will come upon him while he is weary and discouraged and throw him into a panic, and all the people who are with him will flee" (2 Samuel 17:2).
- Gideon "threw all the army into a panic" (Judges 8:12).
- To Babylon: "Stand fast in your enchantments … perhaps you may inspire terror" (Isaiah 47:12).
- God does justice "so that man who is of the earth may strike terror no more" (Psalm 10:18).

A person can become an object of dread: "an object of dread to my acquaintances; those who see me in the street flee from me" (Psalm 31:11).

The fear of one person, or of one people, can fall on many.
- "Many from the peoples of the country declared themselves Jews, for fear of the Jews had fallen on them" (Esther 8:17). The officials "also helped the Jews, for the fear of Mordecai had fallen on them" (9:3).
- Of Nebuchadnezzar: "because of the greatness that he gave him, all peoples, nations, and languages trembled and feared before him. Whom he would, he killed" (Daniel 5:19).
- "When Ephraim spoke, there was trembling; he was exalted in Israel, but he incurred guilt through Baal and died" (Hosea 13:1).
- One man would not tremble. Mordecai "neither rose nor trembled before him", and Haman "was filled with wrath against Mordecai" (Esther 5:9).

A heart also trembles for another. Eli's "heart trembled for the ark of God" (1 Samuel 4:13). Elisha uses the same root to the Shunammite: "See, you have taken all this trouble for us" (2 Kings 4:13). When Esther was told of Mordecai in sackcloth, "the queen was deeply distressed" (Esther 4:4).

Those who fear God are named together. "I am a companion of all who fear you, of those who keep your precepts" (Psalm 119:63). "Then those who feared the Lord spoke with one another" (Malachi 3:16).

## Anger between people

Much of what Scripture says about anger is about anger between people.
- **Kinship stops strife, or feeds it.** Abram: "Let there be no strife between you and me … for we are kinsmen" (Genesis 13:8). He gave Lot the choice. In another quarrel, kinship is the weapon: "Because the king is our close relative. Why then are you angry over this matter?" (2 Samuel 19:42). And "a brother offended is more unyielding than a strong city" (Proverbs 18:19).
- **Anger at a brother is judged like murder, and worship waits on peace.** "Everyone who is angry with his brother will be liable to judgment … First be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift" (Matthew 5:22, 24).
- **The anger of the powerful decides others' fate.** "A king's wrath is like the growling of a lion" (Proverbs 19:12). Those under it plead (Genesis 44:18), read it as a sign ("if he is angry, then know that harm is determined by him", 1 Samuel 20:7), or reach it through a go-between (Acts 12:20).
- **The oppressor's wrath is shown with its end in view.** The king of Babylon "ruled the nations in anger with unrelenting persecution" (Isaiah 14:6). The taunt over him begins "How the oppressor has ceased, the insolent fury ceased!" (14:4), and ends "The whole earth is at rest and quiet" (14:7).
- **A leader's anger can yield to a reason.** Moses was angry at Aaron's sons. When Aaron explained, "when Moses heard that, he approved" (Leviticus 10:20).
- **Anger is learned.** "Make no friendship with a man given to anger, nor go with a wrathful man, lest you learn his ways and entangle yourself in a snare" (Proverbs 22:24–25).
- **Strife has a source inside.** "Your passions are at war within you" (James 4:1). Chapter 10.6 follows that.
- **It wears a household down.** "It is better to live in a corner of the housetop than in a house shared with a quarrelsome wife" (Proverbs 21:9). The image is "a continual dripping on a rainy day" (27:15): duration, not a storm.
- **The one attacked feels it inside.** "In anger they bear a grudge against me. My heart is in anguish within me" (Psalm 55:3–4).
- **The trembling word is used of quarrel and rage.** Joseph sends his brothers home with "Do not quarrel on the way" (Genesis 45:24), and the word is *rāgaz*, "to tremble". "If a wise man has an argument with a fool, the fool only rages and laughs, and there is no quiet" (Proverbs 29:9).
- **Love does not get provoked.** Love "is not irritable" (1 Corinthians 13:5).
- **One person's sin can bring trouble on many.** David, under the plague, asks to bear it alone: "these sheep, what have they done? Please let your hand be against me and against my father's house" (2 Samuel 24:17).
- **Vengeance is left to God.** "Never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God … if your enemy is hungry, feed him" (Romans 12:19–20).

Several times Scripture answers human anger not with a rebuke but with a question, or a new way of seeing.
- Joseph to his brothers: "it was not you who sent me here, but God" (Genesis 45:8).
- The father to his angry elder son: "this your brother was dead, and is alive" (Luke 15:32). The son had said "this son of yours" (15:30).
- Jesus to the disciples, indignant at two of their number: "whoever would be great among you must be your servant" (Matthew 20:26).

Twice the text ends before the angry one answers: Jonah, and the elder brother.

**Connections:** with feeling (10.1), since strife is anger between people and fear spreads; with God (10.9), whose fear governs the treatment of others; with speaking (10.5) and thinking (10.4); with feeling, since inner states spread (10.1; Chapter 11).

Chapter 11 draws together the patterns that run through all of this.
