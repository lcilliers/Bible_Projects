# 10.14 Humility

Humility is not a feeling in the verses. In none that I read is a person said to feel humble. They speak of it in other ways:
- **as something a person does.** He humbles himself, before God or before God's word.
- **as a standing.** Some are called the humble, the lowly or the meek.
- **as something sought and walked in.** "Seek humility" (Zephaniah 2:3). "Walk humbly with your God" (Micah 6:8).
- **as a state of the spirit.** A lowly and contrite spirit; a broken heart.
- **as a place taken among people.** The lower seat; counting others more significant than oneself.
- **as something done to a person.** God humbles to test and to teach, and he brings the proud low.
- **as God's own way.** He "looks far down" (Psalm 113:6), and in his Son he "humbled himself" (Philippians 2:8).

In every one of these the verses set humility against a rising: the proud heart, the lifted-up self, the stiff neck, the haughty eyes. I cannot read humility in Scripture without reading what it stands against. Pride is in 10.15, and where it goes wrong in Chapter 12. This section holds its counterpart.

## The words

Hebrew has a verb for humbling oneself or being humbled, used also when a nation is "subdued" in war. It has another for bringing low, used of the proud, of mountains and of a city. It has a word for the lowly, and one word that is translated "humble", "afflicted", "meek" and "poor" by turns. The same Hebrew word stands behind "the humble" whom God teaches (Psalm 25:9), "the afflicted" whose cry he does not forget (Psalm 9:12), "the meek" who inherit the land (Psalm 37:11), and Moses, "very meek" (Numbers 12:3). The English changes with what the verse is about. The Hebrew does not.

Greek has a family of words for being low, for humbling and for humility, and another for meekness and gentleness. The noun for humility toward one another (Philippians 2:3) is also the word for a self-made "asceticism" that puffs a person up (Colossians 2:18, 23). It has a verb for submitting, placing oneself under another, used of submitting to God and to one another.

The broken and contrite heart has words of its own, for breaking and crushing. Contrition is told in those words, not in the words for humility.

## Humbling oneself before God

### It faces someone

Humbling oneself is never done in the air. It is done "before me" (1 Kings 21:29), "before the Lord" (2 Kings 22:19), "before the God of his fathers" (2 Chronicles 33:12), "before your God" (Daniel 10:12). Zedekiah's failure is put this way: "He did not humble himself before Jeremiah the prophet, who spoke from the mouth of the Lord" (2 Chronicles 36:12). To refuse the prophet was to refuse the word he carried.

### It answers a word heard

Most often a person humbles himself when a word reaches him.
- Rehoboam and his princes heard "You abandoned me, so I have abandoned you". They "humbled themselves and said, 'The Lord is righteous'" (2 Chronicles 12:5–6). Their humbling was saying that God was right.
- Josiah humbled himself "when you heard how I spoke against this place" (2 Kings 22:19).
- Ahab, "when Ahab heard those words, … tore his clothes and put sackcloth on his flesh and fasted" (1 Kings 21:27).
- Belshazzar had the word in his father's story and did not bow to it: "you … have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this" (Daniel 5:22).

### Where it happens

The verses place it in the heart.
- "Because your heart was penitent, and you humbled yourself before the Lord" (2 Kings 22:19), or, in the parallel account, "your heart was tender" (2 Chronicles 34:27).
- "If then their uncircumcised heart is humbled and they make amends for their iniquity" (Leviticus 26:41), after they "confess their iniquity" (26:40).
- "Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart" (2 Chronicles 32:26). The verse before says, "his heart was proud" (32:25). I read this as the same heart turning back from itself. The pride and the humbling are in one place.
- Daniel's is not a response to rebuke at all: "from the first day that you set your heart to understand and humbled yourself before your God, your words have been heard" (Daniel 10:12). Here humbling oneself stands beside setting the heart to understand.

### What goes with it

The humbling is told with what it does:
- saying "The Lord is righteous" (2 Chronicles 12:6)
- tearing clothes, sackcloth, fasting, going about "dejectedly" (1 Kings 21:27)
- weeping (2 Kings 22:19)
- entreating and praying (2 Chronicles 33:12–13)
- fasting and seeking, after choosing not to lean on the king's soldiers: "since we had told the king, 'The hand of our God is for good on all who seek him'" (Ezra 8:21–22)
- confessing and making amends (Leviticus 26:40–41)

One sentence gathers it: "if my people who are called by my name humble themselves, and pray and seek my face and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven and will forgive their sin and heal their land" (2 Chronicles 7:14).

Jesus gives it a face. The tax collector, "standing far off, would not even lift up his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast, saying, 'God, be merciful to me, a sinner!'" The Pharisee beside him prayed, "God, I thank you that I am not like other men … I fast twice a week; I give tithes of all that I get." Jesus: "this man went down to his house justified, rather than the other. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but the one who humbles himself will be exalted" (Luke 18:11–14). The one who lifts himself measures himself against others and lists what he does. The one who humbles himself owns that he is a sinner and asks for mercy.

James and Peter say it as a run of commands. "Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil … Draw near to God … Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded. Be wretched and mourn and weep … Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you" (James 4:7–10). "Humble yourselves, therefore, under the mighty hand of God so that at the proper time he may exalt you, casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:6–7). In Peter, to humble oneself under God's hand is also to put down what one carries, because he cares. The lifting is "at the proper time". It is his to time.

### What it is set against

- pride of heart (2 Chronicles 32:25–26)
- "you have lifted up yourself against the Lord" (Daniel 5:23), as with his father: "when his heart was lifted up and his spirit was hardened so that he dealt proudly" (5:20)
- "He stiffened his neck and hardened his heart against turning to the Lord" (2 Chronicles 36:13)
- "Do not now be stiff-necked as your fathers were, but yield yourselves to the Lord" (2 Chronicles 30:8)
- "How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me?" (Exodus 10:3), to Pharaoh, whose heart was hardened

When Hezekiah's couriers carried the call, the cities "laughed them to scorn and mocked them. However, some men of Asher, of Manasseh, and of Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem" (2 Chronicles 30:10–11). The same call met two hearts. The verses do not say why.

### Distress does not decide it

Manasseh, captured and bound, "when he was in distress, … entreated the favor of the Lord his God and humbled himself greatly" (2 Chronicles 33:12). Ahaz, under the same kind of pressure, "In the time of his distress … became yet more faithless to the Lord" (2 Chronicles 28:22). Distress is not what humbles a person. It is met by the heart either way (Chapter 11, *What changes the character*).

Nor does it pass from father to son. Amon "did not humble himself before the Lord, as Manasseh his father had humbled himself" (2 Chronicles 33:23).

### God sees it, and answers in measure

- "When the Lord saw that they humbled themselves" (2 Chronicles 12:7)
- "Have you seen how Ahab has humbled himself before me?" (1 Kings 21:29)
- "I also have heard you" (2 Kings 22:19)
- "God was moved by his entreaty" (2 Chronicles 33:13)
- "your words have been heard" (Daniel 10:12)

The answer is real, and it is measured.
- "I will grant them some deliverance … Nevertheless, they shall be servants to him" (2 Chronicles 12:7–8).
- "I will not bring the disaster in his days; but in his son's days" (1 Kings 21:29).
- The wrath turned "so as not to make a complete destruction" (2 Chronicles 12:12).

Humbling oneself is how God's anger is turned in many of these accounts (10.13, *The heart that turns, and the heart that does not*).

It does not hold by itself. Two verses after Rehoboam humbled himself: "he did evil, for he did not set his heart to seek the Lord" (2 Chronicles 12:14). And in Manasseh, knowing comes after: "Then Manasseh knew that the Lord was God" (2 Chronicles 33:13).

### A humbling God does not take knowledge of

The people asked, "Why have we fasted, and you see it not? Why have we humbled ourselves, and you take no knowledge of it?" The answer: "in the day of your fast you seek your own pleasure, and oppress all your workers … Is it to bow down his head like a reed, and to spread sackcloth and ashes under him?" The fast God chooses is turned outward: "to share your bread with the hungry and bring the homeless poor into your house; when you see the naked, to cover him" (Isaiah 58:3–7).

Ahab's sackcloth and fasting were seen and answered (1 Kings 21:27–29). Israel's were not. I read the difference in what went with them. Ahab acted when the word came against him. Israel fasted while seeking its own pleasure, and left the naked it saw uncovered.

The New Testament names the same thing. The noun for humility appears as "asceticism": "Let no one disqualify you, insisting on asceticism … puffed up without reason by his sensuous mind", and "self-made religion and asceticism and severity to the body, but they are of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh" (Colossians 2:18, 23). What looks like lowering puffs the person up. The Pharisee's "I fast twice a week" stands on the same side (Luke 18:12).

## Being humbled by God

God humbles people, and the verses give his reasons.

**To show the heart, and to teach.** "that he might humble you, testing you to know what was in your heart, whether you would keep his commandments or not. And he humbled you and let you hunger and fed you with manna … that he might make you know that man does not live by bread alone" (Deuteronomy 8:2–3). The end is named: "to do you good in the end" (8:16). The danger it guards against comes in the same chapter. When plenty comes, "your heart be lifted up, and you forget the Lord", and you "say in your heart, 'My power and the might of my hand have gotten me this wealth'" (8:14, 17). The next chapter goes on: "Do not say in your heart … 'It is because of my righteousness'" (9:4).

**After rebellion.** "for they had rebelled against the words of God … So he bowed their hearts down with hard labor; they fell down, with none to help. Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and he delivered them" (Psalm 107:11–13). What he bowed was the heart.

**As a father.** "Shall we not much more be subject to the Father of spirits and live? … he disciplines us for our good, that we may share his holiness. For the moment all discipline seems painful rather than pleasant, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness" (Hebrews 12:9–11).

**Against pride.** "those who walk in pride he is able to humble", said by Nebuchadnezzar after his reason returned (Daniel 4:37). The humbled man says it of what was done to him.

**Felt from the inside.** "Because God has loosed my cord and humbled me, they have cast off restraint in my presence" (Job 30:11), after "They abhor me … they do not hesitate to spit at the sight of me" (30:10). To be humbled by God left Job open to the contempt of others, and he knew it as God's doing.

**Over others' sin.** "I fear that when I come again my God may humble me before you, and I may have to mourn over many of those who sinned earlier and have not repented" (2 Corinthians 12:21). Paul expected to be humbled by what he would find in others, and to mourn.

**Being humbled can turn a person, or harden him.** "The Lord humbled Judah because of Ahaz", and Ahaz grew "yet more faithless" (2 Chronicles 28:19, 22). Manasseh, brought low, humbled himself (33:12).

## The proud brought low, the lowly raised

This is said again and again: in the histories, the psalms, the proverbs, the prophets and the Gospels.
- "The Lord makes poor and makes rich; he brings low and he exalts. He raises up the poor from the dust" (1 Samuel 2:7–8).
- "One's pride will bring him low, but he who is lowly in spirit will obtain honor" (Proverbs 29:23).
- "Exalt that which is low, and bring low that which is exalted" (Ezekiel 21:26).
- "You save a humble people, but your eyes are on the haughty to bring them down" (2 Samuel 22:28).
- "he has brought down the mighty from their thrones and exalted those of humble estate" (Luke 1:52).
- "Whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted" (Matthew 23:12).

**What is brought low is a bearing, not only a rank.**
- "The haughty looks of man shall be brought low, and the lofty pride of men shall be humbled, and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day" (Isaiah 2:11, 17).
- "the eyes of the haughty are brought low" (Isaiah 5:15).
- "I will put an end to the pomp of the arrogant, and lay low the pompous pride of the ruthless" (Isaiah 13:11).

In Isaiah the setting is a land "filled with silver and gold … filled with idols; they bow down to the work of their hands" (2:7–8). I read being brought low there as the lifted-up thing made to give way to the One who is high. The verse gives the end: "the Lord alone will be exalted".

**Only God does it.** Job is challenged: "look on everyone who is proud and abase him … Then will I also acknowledge to you that your own right hand can save you" (Job 40:11–14). He cannot. "Not from the east or from the west … comes lifting up, but it is God who executes judgment, putting down one and lifting up another" (Psalm 75:6–7).

**Pride brings its own fall.** In the proverbs no other hand is named: "One's pride will bring him low" (Proverbs 29:23). "When pride comes, then comes disgrace" (Proverbs 11:2). "Before destruction a man's heart is haughty" (Proverbs 18:12). "It is better to be of a lowly spirit with the poor than to divide the spoil with the proud" (Proverbs 16:19). That last is a choice of company.

**Where the boast goes.** "Let the lowly brother boast in his exaltation, and the rich in his humiliation, because like a flower of the grass he will pass away" (James 1:9–10). "God chose what is low and despised in the world … so that no human being might boast in the presence of God … 'Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord'" (1 Corinthians 1:28–31). I read both as moving the boast from what a person has to what God does. The rich man may boast in being brought low, because it tells him the truth about himself.

**Reading another's fall.** "For when they are humbled you say, 'It is because of pride'; but he saves the lowly" (Job 22:29). Eliphaz explains a man brought low as pride. The book shows how far that explanation can be wrong.

## The humble

Some are simply called the humble, the lowly or the meek. The verses tell what they do and what God does for them.

**They seek.** "Seek the Lord, all you humble of the land, who do his just commands; seek righteousness; seek humility; perhaps you may be hidden on the day of the anger of the Lord" (Zephaniah 2:3). Those already called humble are told to seek humility. I read from this that humility is not held once for all. Of the remnant God leaves: "a people humble and lowly. They shall seek refuge in the name of the Lord … they shall do no injustice and speak no lies" (Zephaniah 3:12–13).

**They are glad at what God did for another.** "My soul makes its boast in the Lord; let the humble hear and be glad" (Psalm 34:2). "When the humble see it they will be glad; you who seek God, let your hearts revive" (Psalm 69:32). In both psalms one man's rescue is the gladness of others.

**They are taught.** "He leads the humble in what is right, and teaches the humble his way" (Psalm 25:9). The man praying it says, "Remember not the sins of my youth" and "pardon my guilt, for it is great" (25:7, 11). I read the humble here as the teachable, and the teachable as those who own their guilt.

**God is near them.**
- "though the Lord is high, he regards the lowly, but the haughty he knows from afar" (Psalm 138:6)
- "to the humble he gives favor" (Proverbs 3:34), quoted twice in the New Testament as "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5)
- "he adorns the humble with salvation" (Psalm 149:4)
- "God arose to establish judgment, to save all the humble of the earth" (Psalm 76:9)
- "The meek shall obtain fresh joy in the Lord" (Isaiah 29:19)

**The meek, where another provokes.** "Refrain from anger, and forsake wrath! Fret not yourself; it tends only to evil … But the meek shall inherit the land and delight themselves in abundant peace" (Psalm 37:8–11). "Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth" (Matthew 5:5). Moses was "very meek, more than all people who were on the face of the earth" (Numbers 12:3). That is said at the moment Miriam and Aaron spoke against him, and it is the Lord, not Moses, who answers them. I read meekness, in both places, as standing where another provokes, and leaving the answer to God.

**The humble and the afflicted.** The one Hebrew word is "humble" where the verse speaks of God's favour and teaching. It is "afflicted" where the verse speaks of a cry and of oppression: "he does not forget the cry of the afflicted" (Psalm 9:12); "you hear the desire of the afflicted; you will strengthen their heart" (Psalm 10:17). Whether the humble and the afflicted are one people seen from two sides, the verses raise but do not settle. Being poor and needy, and what the sight of the poor moves in a person and in God, are still to be read for themselves.

## Humility as a way of walking

**Before honour.** "humility comes before honor" is said twice (Proverbs 15:33; 18:12). The verses join it to the fear of the Lord: "The fear of the Lord is instruction in wisdom, and humility comes before honor" (15:33); "The reward for humility and fear of the Lord is riches and honor and life" (22:4). And to wisdom: "with the humble is wisdom" (11:2).

**With hearing.** In both places where "humility comes before honor", the verse beside it is about hearing.
- "The ear that listens to life-giving reproof will dwell among the wise. Whoever ignores instruction despises himself, but he who listens to reproof gains intelligence" (Proverbs 15:31–32).
- "If one gives an answer before he hears, it is his folly and shame" (Proverbs 18:13).
- James says it again: "let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger … receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls" (James 1:19–21).
- God looks to "he who is humble and contrite in spirit and trembles at my word" (Isaiah 66:2).

I read humility, in these verses, as the ear that can take a reproof and a word, and does not answer before it hears (10.2).

**Walking with God.** "With what shall I come before the Lord … Shall I come before him with burnt offerings …? He has told you, O man, what is good; and what does the Lord require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?" (Micah 6:6–8). The question is what to bring, and the answer is a way to walk. "Humbly" is "with your God": a walk in his company, not a gift handed over.

**Instead of the offering.** Three more passages set what God looks to against a religious act:
- the fast (Isaiah 58)
- the house and the sacrifices: "But this is the one to whom I will look: he who is humble and contrite in spirit" (Isaiah 66:1–3)
- "For you will not delight in sacrifice … The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise" (Psalm 51:16–17)

The psalm goes on, "then will you delight in right sacrifices" (51:19). The offering is not done away with. It follows the broken heart.

## A lowly and contrite spirit

"For thus says the One who is high and lifted up, who inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy: 'I dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit, to revive the spirit of the lowly, and to revive the heart of the contrite'" (Isaiah 57:15). God names two dwellings, and the second is a lowly spirit. What he does there is revive. The reason follows: "For I will not contend forever … for the spirit would grow faint before me" (57:16).

Where God and a broken heart meet in these verses, he is near and he heals:
- "The Lord is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit" (Psalm 34:18)
- "He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds" (Psalm 147:3)
- the anointed one is sent "to bind up the brokenhearted" (Isaiah 61:1)

**The breaking has more than one cause.**
- **Over one's own sin**, and brought to God: Psalm 51, and "I am feeble and crushed; I groan because of the tumult of my heart" because "my iniquities have gone over my head", with "all my longing is before you" (Psalm 38:4, 8–9).
- **By what others do:** "Reproaches have broken my heart, so that I am in despair. I looked for pity, but there was none" (Psalm 69:20). "For I am poor and needy, and my heart is stricken within me" (Psalm 109:22).
- **By slavery:** "they did not listen to Moses, because of their broken spirit and harsh slavery" (Exodus 6:9). That broken spirit could not hear the promise of rescue.
- **By God's word against what is seen:** "My heart is broken within me … because of the Lord and because of his holy words" (Jeremiah 23:9).
- **By sorrow:** "by sorrow of heart the spirit is crushed" (Proverbs 15:13), and "a crushed spirit who can bear?" (Proverbs 18:14).

The verses do not divide contrition from despair by name. They give the cause of the breaking, and whether the broken heart is brought to God. Where it is, God is near, heals and revives. The spirit's own part is in Chapter 6.

## Toward one another

The New Testament says much more than the Old about humility between people.

**Counting others.** "Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves. Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others" (Philippians 2:3–4). "Do not be haughty, but associate with the lowly. Never be wise in your own sight" (Romans 12:16).

**Worn.** "Put on then … compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience, bearing with one another and … forgiving each other" (Colossians 3:12–13). "Clothe yourselves, all of you, with humility toward one another" (1 Peter 5:5). It is always named with patience, bearing with, forgiving, sympathy and a tender heart (Ephesians 4:2; 1 Peter 3:8). It keeps "the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace" (Ephesians 4:3).

**The seat and the title.** "do not sit down in a place of honor … go and sit in the lowest place, so that when your host comes he may say to you, 'Friend, move up higher'" (Luke 14:8–10). The proverb had said it: "Do not put yourself forward in the king's presence … it is better to be told, 'Come up here,' than to be put lower in the presence of a noble" (Proverbs 25:6–7). To the question of titles: "you are not to be called rabbi … The greatest among you shall be your servant" (Matthew 23:8, 11). And: "Whoever humbles himself like this child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 18:4). The lower place is taken before anyone gives it, and the honour is given, not taken.

**In meeting someone who is wrong.** Gentleness shows in how one meets a wrong:
- "restore him in a spirit of gentleness. Keep watch on yourself, lest you too be tempted" (Galatians 6:1); "if anyone thinks he is something, when he is nothing, he deceives himself" (6:3)
- "correcting his opponents with gentleness. God may perhaps grant them repentance" (2 Timothy 2:25)
- "make a defense … with gentleness and respect" (1 Peter 3:15)
- "to be gentle, and to show perfect courtesy toward all people. For we ourselves were once foolish" (Titus 3:2–3)

What these verses join to gentleness is watching oneself, remembering what one was, and leaving the outcome to God.

**Where it comes from.**
- It is given: "the fruit of the Spirit is … gentleness" (Galatians 5:22–23).
- It is pursued: "Pursue … gentleness" (1 Timothy 6:11).
- It is put on (Colossians 3:12).
- It lives in "the hidden person of the heart with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which in God's sight is very precious" (1 Peter 3:4).

**Lowering oneself for another.** "did I commit a sin in humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I preached God's gospel to you free of charge?" (2 Corinthians 11:7). Paul also learned to be brought low and to be content: "I know how to be brought low, and I know how to abound … I can do all things through him who strengthens me" (Philippians 4:12–13).

## Submitting

**To God.** "Submit yourselves therefore to God" (James 4:7), in the same breath as "Humble yourselves before the Lord" (4:10). James draws no line between them.

Not submitting is told in two ways:
- **Building one's own righteousness:** "seeking to establish their own, they did not submit to God's righteousness" (Romans 10:3).
- **Inability:** "the mind that is set on the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to God's law; indeed, it cannot" (Romans 8:7). The way out named is the Spirit (8:9).

**To one another, for a reason that faces God.** Submission among people is almost never told without its ground:

| Verse | The ground given |
| --- | --- |
| Ephesians 5:21 | *"out of reverence for Christ"* |
| 1 Peter 2:13 | *"for the Lord's sake"* |
| Romans 13:5 | *"for the sake of conscience"* |
| 1 Peter 2:19 | *"mindful of God"* |
| 1 Peter 3:5 | *"the holy women who hoped in God"* |
| Hebrews 13:17 | the leaders *"are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account"* |

Where the passage goes on, it lays a duty on the other side:
- "Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her" (Ephesians 5:25)
- "do not be harsh with them" (Colossians 3:19)
- the elders are "not domineering over those in your charge" (1 Peter 5:3)

**Under wrong.** "be subject to your masters with all respect, not only to the good and gentle but also to the unjust. For this is a gracious thing, when, mindful of God, one endures sorrows while suffering unjustly … because Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example" (1 Peter 2:18–21). Hagar was sent back to a mistress who had dealt harshly with her: "Return to your mistress and submit to her", with "the Lord has listened to your affliction" (Genesis 16:6–11).

**One's own spirit.** "the spirits of prophets are subject to prophets. For God is not a God of confusion but of peace" (1 Corinthians 14:32–33). The one who speaks governs what moves in him.

**The limit.** "to them we did not yield in submission even for a moment, so that the truth of the gospel might be preserved for you" (Galatians 2:5). To false brothers who would bring others into slavery, refusing is the right act. Its ground is given too.

## Jesus

In the New Testament, humility rests on what the verses say of Jesus. God's own part is in 13.1, *Lowering himself*. Here is what it means for the one who humbles himself.

**He is lowly.** "learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls" (Matthew 11:29). His own word on his own heart. Rest is found by learning from it.

**He came humble.** "your king is coming to you, humble, and mounted on a donkey" (Matthew 21:5; Zechariah 9:9). As a boy who knew "I must be in my Father's house", "he went down with them and came to Nazareth and was submissive to them" (Luke 2:49, 51). "In his humiliation justice was denied him", and like a lamb he "opens not his mouth" (Acts 8:32–33).

**He humbled himself, and was exalted.** "who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross. Therefore God has highly exalted him" (Philippians 2:6–9). "we see him who for a little while was made lower than the angels, namely Jesus, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death" (Hebrews 2:9). I read the saying he taught, "whoever humbles himself will be exalted", as what is told of him.

**He is the ground of humility toward others.**
- "Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 2:5) is the reason for "in humility count others more significant than yourselves" (2:3).
- "leaving you an example, so that you might follow in his steps" (1 Peter 2:21).
- "out of reverence for Christ" (Ephesians 5:21).
- Paul appeals "by the meekness and gentleness of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:1).

**Others lower themselves before him.** John the Baptist: "He must increase, but I must decrease" (John 3:30). He says it after "I am not the Christ", and with "this joy of mine is now complete" (3:28–29). His lowering is said with joy.

**At his coming, a low estate is looked on.** Mary: "he has looked on the humble estate of his servant" (Luke 1:48). Her song goes on as Hannah's had: he scatters "the proud in the thoughts of their hearts", brings down the mighty and exalts the humble, fills the hungry and sends the rich away (Luke 1:51–53; 1 Samuel 2:7–8).

## The Old Testament and the New

Everything above is in both Testaments:
- humbling oneself before God, and God's answer
- the proud brought low and the humble raised
- favour to the humble
- the lower seat
- the meek inheriting
- God humbling for good
- a humbling he does not accept
- humility beside hearing
- the humble king
- God lowering himself

The New Testament quotes the Old on three of them (Proverbs 3:34; Zechariah 9:9; Psalm 8:5–6).

What differs is emphasis, and one thing that is new.

- **Before God, then also toward one another.** In the Old Testament humbling is almost always before God. Kings and the people humble themselves, and the answer is wrath turned or disaster delayed. In the New, much of what is said is about humility toward one another. It always has a reason that faces God, and the answer is named as being justified, grace, and rest for the soul.
- **The new thing: Christ.** He is the one who is lowly in heart, who humbled himself to death, and in whose mind the humble live. What God did in looking down, in his gentleness and in his breaking over a heart, the Son did in a human life. What the Old Testament said of man, "you have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings … you have put all things under his feet" (Psalm 8:5–6), the letter to the Hebrews reads as "namely Jesus" (Hebrews 2:6–9).

## When humility changes character

The same words wear different faces.
- **One Hebrew verb** is a king humbling himself before God, God humbling a people, and an enemy "subdued" in war. When a person humbles himself, the heart is named and God answers. When an enemy is beaten, no inner act is named.
- **One Greek noun** is humility toward one another, and a self-made asceticism that puffs up (Colossians 2:18, 23).
- **The word "humble" itself** is a charge flung at Paul: "I who am humble when face to face with you, but bold toward you when I am away!" (2 Corinthians 10:1).
- **Being brought low** is God's judgment on the proud (Isaiah 2:11), a test for good (Deuteronomy 8:16), a circumstance met with contentment (Philippians 4:12), and the place Christ went (Philippians 2:8).
- **Submitting** is right before God, right for the Lord's sake among people, and wrong to those who would enslave the gospel (Galatians 2:5).
