# 10. The inner being at work

So far I have taken the inner words one at a time. Now I want to turn it round and ask what the inner being actually *does*: how it feels, knows, wants, chooses, relates to God and relates to other people. And in each of these, which of the inner words Scripture reaches for.

## Feeling

Every one of the inner words feels, but not all in the same way.

- **The heart** has the widest range: joy, grief, fear, courage, anxiety, love, contempt, despair.
- **The soul** is above all bitter, longing, weary and troubled. "Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you in turmoil within me?" (Psalm 42:5).
- **The spirit** is troubled, crushed, faint, provoked, and hot or cool in temper. "My spirit faints within me" (Psalm 143:4). "He who has a cool spirit is a man of understanding" (Proverbs 17:27).
- **The flesh** trembles, faints and sings (Chapter 6).

The state of the heart decides whether a feeling takes hold. A settled heart does not panic: "He is not afraid of bad news; his heart is firm, trusting in the Lord" (Psalm 112:7). A hardened heart does not fear God: "why do you … harden our heart, so that we fear you not?" (Isaiah 63:17).

Feelings pass between the inner being and the body in both directions. "A glad heart makes a cheerful face" (Proverbs 15:13). "A joyful heart is good medicine" (Proverbs 17:22). Going the other way: "wine to gladden the heart of man, oil to make his face shine and bread to strengthen man's heart" (Psalm 104:15). "Oil and perfume make the heart glad" (Proverbs 27:9).

And God feels in heart and soul too. "The Lord regretted that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him to his heart" (Genesis 6:6). "I will rejoice in doing them good … with all my heart and all my soul" (Jeremiah 32:41).

## Knowing and hearing

Knowing belongs chiefly to the heart, as Chapter 3 showed. To pay attention is to set the heart. To understand is to have "a hearing heart". To remember is to keep a thing in the heart. The spirit knows the self from within: "who knows a person's thoughts except the spirit of that person, which is in him?" (1 Corinthians 2:11). The soul receives what is known as something good to taste: "knowledge will be pleasant to your soul" (Proverbs 2:10).

**Hearing is the main way in.** The ear receives, and the heart keeps it or refuses it. "All my words that I shall speak to you receive in your heart, and hear with your ears" (Ezekiel 3:10). "Making your ear attentive to wisdom and inclining your heart to understanding" (Proverbs 2:2). When the heart is hard, hearing stops: "Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he would not listen" (Exodus 7:13). "They made their hearts diamond-hard lest they should hear the law" (Zechariah 7:12). A crushed spirit can block hearing as well. Israel "did not listen to Moses, because of their broken spirit and harsh slavery" (Exodus 6:9).

What is heard moves everything inside. Rahab says, "As soon as we heard it, our hearts melted, and there was no spirit left in any man because of you" (Joshua 2:11). Good news works the other way: "Like cold water to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country" (Proverbs 25:25). And hearing is a kind of eating: "Listen diligently to me, and eat what is good, and delight yourselves in rich food. Incline your ear, and come to me; hear, that your soul may live" (Isaiah 55:2–3).

Sometimes hearing is not enough, and the inner being needs to *see*. Jacob's heart went numb at the news that Joseph was alive. His spirit revived only when he saw the wagons (Genesis 45:26–27).

## Wanting

The soul is the wanting self (Chapter 4). In Hebrew, "your soul desires" is simply how you say "you want". The heart has desires too: "Delight yourself in the Lord, and he will give you the desires of your heart" (Psalm 37:4). And in the New Testament the flesh becomes the great subject of wrong desire (Chapter 6).

Desire can be aimed at nothing. The Preacher tried everything his heart wanted, and it was "vanity and a striving after wind" (Ecclesiastes 2:11). "Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the appetite [soul]: this also is vanity and a striving after wind" (Ecclesiastes 6:9). A soul that wants and a wind that cannot be caught: the image is exact.

## Choosing and setting direction

If the soul wants, the heart **sets direction**. Scripture speaks of setting, inclining, turning and directing the heart.

- "Incline your heart to the Lord, the God of Israel" (Joshua 24:23).
- "Incline my heart to your testimonies, and not to selfish gain!" (Psalm 119:36).
- "Direct your heart to the Lord and serve him only" (1 Samuel 7:3).
- The heart can be whole or divided: "unite my heart to fear your name" (Psalm 86:11), against "a double heart" (Psalm 12:2). Solomon's "heart was not wholly true to the Lord his God" (1 Kings 11:4).
- It can be stubborn. "The stubbornness of their evil hearts" runs through Jeremiah (Jeremiah 7:24).

The spirit supplies **energy** rather than direction. It is moved, stirred, fervent and willing. "Everyone whose heart stirred him, and everyone whose spirit moved him" (Exodus 35:21) puts the two side by side. The heart is stirred to the choice, and the spirit is moved with the energy to act on it.

## Right and wrong

When Scripture judges a person, it looks first at the heart. The heart is upright or crooked, pure or deceitful, whole or double, proud or humble. God "weighs the heart" (Proverbs 21:2). The soul is the one who answers for what is done: "the soul who sins shall die". The spirit is judged mainly on pride and humility: haughty or lowly, crushed or contrite. Chapter 12 takes this further.

## Relating to God

Relating to God engages the whole inner being, and Scripture says so by formula: "with all your heart and with all your soul". That formula governs loving God (Deuteronomy 6:5), serving him (Deuteronomy 10:12), seeking him (Deuteronomy 4:29), returning to him (Deuteronomy 30:10) and keeping covenant (2 Kings 23:3).

- **Prayer lives in the heart.** It can be complete there without a voice (1 Samuel 1:13). "Pour out your heart like water before the presence of the Lord" (Lamentations 2:19). "With my whole heart I cry; answer me, O Lord!" (Psalm 119:145).
- **God speaks to the heart.** To comfort someone, in Hebrew, is to "speak to their heart". Joseph "comforted them and spoke kindly to them" (Genesis 50:21), literally to their hearts. God says of his people, "I will allure her, and bring her into the wilderness, and speak tenderly to her" (Hosea 2:14), again to her heart. "Speak tenderly to Jerusalem" (Isaiah 40:2).
- **God tests the heart.** "The Lord tests hearts" (Proverbs 17:3). And he forgives according to what he knows there: "render to each whose heart you know, according to all his ways, for you, you only, know the hearts of all the children of mankind" (1 Kings 8:39).
- **The soul is the self in address.** It is told to bless, to hope and to rest (Chapter 4).
- **The human spirit worships.** "God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth" (John 4:24). "My spirit rejoices in God my Savior" (Luke 1:47). Paul serves God "with my spirit" (Romans 1:9). At death the spirit is what is committed into God's hands.
- **God's Spirit prays within.** "God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, 'Abba! Father!'" (Galatians 4:6). The Spirit "intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words. And he who searches hearts knows what is the mind of the Spirit" (Romans 8:26–27). The cry to God rises from inside the heart, and it is God's own Spirit crying.

## Relating to others

The inner being is not private. It reaches out, is reached, and is bound to other people.

- **Love lives in the soul.** "The soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul" (1 Samuel 18:1). The lover in the Song seeks "him whom my soul loves" (Song 3:1).
- **Hatred starts in the heart.** "You shall not hate your brother in your heart" (Leviticus 19:17). The law stops hatred at its source, before it becomes a deed. Esau's hatred began as heart-speech: "Esau said to himself, 'The days of mourning for my father are approaching; then I will kill my brother Jacob'" (Genesis 27:41). The next verse says Rebekah was told. What the heart says in private gets out.
- **The spirit registers the presence and absence of friends.** "My spirit was not at rest because I did not find my brother Titus there" (2 Corinthians 2:13). Later, "his spirit has been refreshed by you all" (2 Corinthians 7:13). Friends "refreshed my spirit as well as yours" (1 Corinthians 16:18).
- **The spirit is the manner of approach.** "Shall I come to you with a rod, or with love in a spirit of gentleness?" (1 Corinthians 4:21).
- **Inner states spread.** The fainthearted soldier is sent home before battle "lest he make the heart of his fellows melt like his own" (Deuteronomy 20:8).
- **Joy at another's fall is watched.** "Do not rejoice when your enemy falls, and let not your heart be glad when he stumbles" (Proverbs 24:17).

Chapter 11 draws together the patterns that run through all of this.
