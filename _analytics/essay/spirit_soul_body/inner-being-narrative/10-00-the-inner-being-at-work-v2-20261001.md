# 10. The inner being at work

So far I have taken the inner words one at a time. Now I want to turn it round and ask what the inner being actually *does*: how it feels, knows and hears, thinks, speaks, wants, chooses, weighs right and wrong, relates to God and relates to other people. And in each of these, which of the inner words Scripture reaches for.

I take these activities one at a time in the sections that follow (10.1 to 10.9). But in life they do not happen one at a time. A feeling becomes a thought, a thought becomes a word, and a word becomes a deed. So before I take them apart, I want to follow one path along which Scripture traces them working together.

## From a thought to a deed

Read side by side, the verses trace a path.

1. **Thoughts arise.** "To you, O king, as you lay in bed came thoughts of what would be after this" (Daniel 2:29). "Why do doubts arise in your hearts?" (Luke 24:38).
2. **They lodge.** "How long shall your wicked thoughts lodge within you?" (Jeremiah 4:14).
3. **They weigh and judge.** "We esteemed him stricken, smitten by God, and afflicted" (Isaiah 53:4).
4. **They take the shape of a plan.** "He plots trouble while on his bed" (Psalm 36:4).
5. **They reach the tongue, the lips and the eyes.** "Your tongue plots destruction" (Psalm 52:2). "Whoever winks his eyes plans dishonest things; he who purses his lips brings evil to pass" (Proverbs 16:30).
6. **They reach the hand and the feet.** "Woe to those who devise wickedness and work evil on their beds! When the morning dawns, they perform it, because it is in the power of their hand" (Micah 2:1). "A heart that devises wicked plans, feet that make haste to run to evil" (Proverbs 6:18). "Their feet run to evil … their thoughts are thoughts of iniquity" (Isaiah 59:7).

No single verse lists these six steps. The order is my reading of many verses together. But the stories show the path being walked.
- David "said in his heart, 'Now I shall perish one day by the hand of Saul. There is nothing better for me than that I should escape to the land of the Philistines'" (1 Samuel 27:1). And he went.
- Jeroboam "said in his heart, 'Now the kingdom will turn back to the house of David'" (1 Kings 12:26). Two verses later, "the king took counsel and made two calves of gold" (12:28). A private sentence became a nation's idolatry.
- The tenants in the parable reason it through together: "they said to themselves, 'This is the heir. Let us kill him, so that the inheritance may be ours'" (Luke 20:14).

The same path runs toward good.
- "When I think on my ways, I turn my feet to your testimonies" (Psalm 119:59). The thinking reaches the feet.
- Joshua is told to meditate on the law day and night "so that you may be careful to do according to all that is written in it" (Joshua 1:8).

There can be a pause on the path, and the pause matters: "The heart of the righteous ponders how to answer, but the mouth of the wicked pours out evil things" (Proverbs 15:28).

## Where I am answerable

The commands meet this path from the second step onward.
- "Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts" (Isaiah 55:7).
- "Let none of you devise evil against another in your heart" (Zechariah 7:10).
- "Do not say in your heart … 'It is because of my righteousness'" (Deuteronomy 9:4).
- "Take every thought captive to obey Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:5).
- "Wash your heart from evil" (Jeremiah 4:14).

I have found no command about what first arises. But what arises is not neutral. "Every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (Genesis 6:5). "Out of the heart of man, come evil thoughts" (Mark 7:21). I read it this way: what arises shows me the state of my heart, and what I let lodge, plan, say and do is where I am answerable.

Scripture does more than command. It also answers the sentence the heart speaks. "If you say in your heart, 'These nations are greater than I. How can I dispossess them?' you shall not be afraid of them but you shall remember what the Lord your God did to Pharaoh and to all Egypt" (Deuteronomy 7:17–18). The answer to a fearful inner sentence is a remembered act of God.

## How the activities connect

Each section that follows ends by naming the activities it touches. Taken together so far, they look like this:

- **Hearing feeds thinking.** What is heard and remembered is what the heart then ponders (10.2 → 10.3).
- **Thinking passes into speaking**, and speaking is heard, by people and by God (10.3 → 10.4 → 10.2, 10.8).
- **Thinking passes into choosing and into the deed** (10.3 → 10.6, and the path above).
- **Thinking and feeling move each other.** "His thoughts alarmed him; his limbs gave way, and his knees knocked together" (Daniel 5:6) (10.3 ↔ 10.1).
- **Wanting drives thinking.** The rich man's planning serves his wanting (Luke 12:17–19) (10.5 → 10.3).
- **Thinking stands under right and wrong**, and its own thoughts accuse or excuse it (Romans 2:15) (10.3 → 10.7).
- **Thinking happens together**, and shared reasoning can become a shared deed (10.3 → 10.9).

This picture is not finished. As I read more widely, I expect it to grow, and to correct itself.
