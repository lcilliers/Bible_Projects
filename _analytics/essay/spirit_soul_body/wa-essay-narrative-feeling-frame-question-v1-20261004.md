## 1. What 10.1 assumes

10.1 opens: "Every one of the inner words feels, but not all in the same way". It lists what the heart, soul, spirit and flesh feel. It then says: "Anger, fear and anxiety are the feelings I have followed through their verses so far". So **"feeling" is the container, and fear, anger and anxiety are its contents.** 10.1 never says what feeling is. It takes the category as given.

## 2. Does Scripture have this category? A check of the data

I searched iba.db `verse_lexical` for every English surface containing "feel".
- **There are 25 hits in 16 verses**, spread over more than a dozen different roots. None of these roots means "feeling" as a category. The ESV uses "feel" for:
  - touching: Isaac feels Jacob (Gen 27:21); Samson feels the pillars (Judg 16:26); idols "have hands, but do not feel" (Psa 115:7)
  - knowing: "they beat me, but I did not feel it" (Pro 23:35)
  - groping: "feel their way toward him" (Act 17:27)
  - trusting: "feel secure" (Amo 6:1; Job 11:18)
  - specific movements: "a God who feels indignation" (Psa 7:11); "I feel a divine jealousy" (2Cor 11:2); "the joy that we feel" (1Th 3:9); "the dread that your heart shall feel" (Deu 28:67)
  - one negative: "their heart is unfeeling like fat" (Psa 119:70)
- **So "feeling" is an English category, not a biblical word.** The verses name two things:
  - **the seats:** heart, soul, spirit, bowels, flesh, bones
  - **the particular movements:** fear, anger, joy, grief, dread, and others

  The verses do not name a class that gathers the movements together.

**What this means (R):** the heading "Feeling" sets a division no verse states. Under rule 69 (no imputed divisions) that is the same kind of problem as the "five distinctions" the claim register withdrew. It does not make the heading wrong for a reader. But the narrative cannot treat "feeling" as a thing Scripture describes, and then sort phenomena into it.

## 3. Why fear does not fit inside "feeling" (from the overview)

The fear overview (§A) found that the verses speak of fear in **two ways that do not reduce to each other**:
- **something that comes upon a person** and is felt in the heart and body (this is the "feeling" side)
- **a standing toward God**, which is commanded, learned, given, owed, and lived as a way of walking ("walking in the fear of the Lord", Act 9:31; "where is my fear?", Mal 1:6)

The overview also shows the fear words used for:
- **the one feared:** "awesome", "terrifying", "great deeds of terror"
- **an act:** "who spread terror"
- **respect owed to people**
- **rage and grief**, through *rāgaz* (to tremble)

Only the first of these is a feeling in the English sense. Putting the whole of fear under "Feeling" therefore has to split it, as 10.1 now does: the fear of God goes to 10.9, and fear between people goes to 10.10. **That split is the place where 10.1 unravels.** Anger is likely to show the same thing (God's anger; anger as provoking, an act), and so is any other phenomenon read this deeply.

## 4. Does it matter to define feeling?

**My view (R):**
- It matters only because the narrative uses "feeling" as a container.
- The verses do not define feeling, because they do not name it. Any definition I wrote would be mine, not Scripture's.
- So the useful question is not "what is feeling?" but **"how do the verses speak of what moves within a person?"** The verses do answer that:
  - through the seats (what the heart, soul and spirit undergo)
  - through each phenomenon's own words, its forms and what changes its character
- That second question can be answered without a definition of feeling.
