## 1. What went wrong

The v1 method has two passes: weave the new into the narrative, then adjust the narrative to the new. In practice the first pass worked like this:
1. read a verse
2. find the existing heading it best matches
3. put it under that heading, often as a bullet

Each observation's "touches" column was filled with **existing** sections, so the structure was fixed before the meaning was read. The adjust pass rarely changed a heading.

**The example the researcher named: Ch 2 v11 "Life set before a person".** It quotes Deuteronomy 30:19 and 30:6. A bullet list of six "do this and live" verses follows, introduced as "The choice is made by doing, seeking and holding on". The section never says:
- what the life is that is set before the person
- why the choice is possible at all
- who witnesses it
- what turning away starts from

Deuteronomy 30 itself says all four (§3). The section has the verses and has lost their meaning. That introductory line also joins six verses from five books into a statement none of them makes (rule 69).

## 2. The method going forward

The order changes. **Meaning comes first and structure comes last.** Placement is the final step, not the first.

### Step 1. Read the passage, not the verse

For each key statement, read the whole unit it stands in: the speech, the psalm, the oracle, the story. The verse a hit lands on is where the reading starts, not its boundary.

A **key statement** is a verse or phrase that says something in its own right about the inner being, or about the strand's subject. A hit that only mentions the word, and says nothing about either, stays in the data and gets no meaning pass. This is the #1930 filter, applied before the reading rather than after the writing.

### Step 2. Meaning pass: one row per key statement

The ledger row is built around five questions. Unit 4 (LD-143 onward) already uses columns 1–3. This makes them standard and adds 4–5.

| # | Question | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Setting.** Who speaks, to whom, in what situation, and in what kind of text (law, oracle, taunt, prayer, proverb, story)? | Genre is stated where it changes how the verse can be read (e.g. LD-144, a taunt) |
| 2 | **What it means there.** In plain words, what the statement says in its own setting | This is required interpretation (#1930), not synthesis |
| 3 | **What it implies.** What follows from it for the inner being, and for the strand's subject | Anything beyond what the verse states is marked **R** ("I read") |
| 4 | **Same words, other settings.** Where the same phrase or word appears elsewhere, does its character change? What differs, and why, as the verses give it? | Feeds the change-of-character record (#1919) |
| 5 | **Who else is in it.** Each external party, and its role as the verse states it | Style guide §7a |

A row is not complete until 2 and 3 are answered. A bare quotation in the "meaning" column is a failed row.

### Step 3. Name the concepts from the rows

When the rows are complete, group them by **the concept the statements themselves name**, in the verses' own words, for example:
- "set before you"
- "sleep … awake"
- "he is your life"

Existing chapter headings are not used to group them. This is a working list in the ledger, not chapter structure.

### Step 4. Decide the structure: sections emerge

For each concept, ask one question: **does an existing section already give it full attention, without cutting its implications?**
- **Yes:** it is woven there, and the section is re-read and rewritten around it (v1 §2.2, the adjust pass, unchanged).
- **No:** it is proposed as an **emerging section** (disposition **NS**) in the ledger's decisions section, with:
  - the concept
  - its key statements
  - what would be lost if it were split across existing sections

  Unit 4's "Sleep and waking" (#1949, decision 1) is the worked case: the image runs through all of its verses and would be lost if they were split.

- **Test for the "no" answer:** if placing it would need its implications cut, or its verses scattered under several headings, it is an emerging section.
- **The bias is set deliberately:** when unsure, propose the section and let the researcher fold it back. Folding back is cheap. A lost implication is not seen again.

Each emerging section gets one coherent account (the 10.1 "Fear" model, #1930). The questions come from the concept, not from a template. Other sections carry only their own part, in prose, and point back to it.

### Step 5. Carry implications through

Each implication in column 3 records every place it **changes** something, not only where it fits. This is what #1945 calls "impacts". Where a verse itself states a cause, for example with "therefore", "because", "so that" or "if … then", that link is recorded as the verse gives it. Links of this kind are what #1946 calls chain operations. A cause the verses do not state is not recorded (rule 69).

### Step 6. Write, then check

The style guide §8 checks still apply. Two are added:
- **Meaning check.** Every key verse quoted in chapter text has its meaning in setting said in prose, either beside it or in the account it points to. A bullet list of verses whose prose introduction does not say what they mean fails.
- **List-drift warning.** The quoted share of each changed section is measured before and after. A rise is flagged in the escalation, as in #1930 (10.9: 49% → 60%). It is a warning, not a limit.

## 3. Worked example: "life set before you"

This is an illustration of steps 1–4 only. Ch 2 is not changed; that is #1948. Quotes were checked against the ESV in iba.db `verse` (2026-10-04).

**Deuteronomy 30:15–20** (passage read: 30:1–20)

| Question | Reading |
|---|---|
| Setting | Moses, at the end of the covenant speech. The chapter opens on a people already scattered under the curse, who "call them to mind" and return "with all your heart and with all your soul" (30:1–2) |
| What it means there | The life set before them is named: "loving the Lord your God, obeying his voice and holding fast to him, **for he is your life** and length of days" (30:20). The choice is between holding to God and a heart that "turns away" (30:17) |
| What it implies | (a) **The life offered is not only long days in the land; it is God himself** ("he is your life"). Length of days and the land follow from it in the same verse. (b) **Choosing is possible because the word is near:** "It is in your mouth and in your heart, so that you can do it" (30:14). (c) **Death starts in the heart:** "if your heart turns away, and you will not hear, but are drawn away" (30:17). (d) **R:** I read 30:6 ("the Lord your God will circumcise your heart … that you may live") and 30:19 ("choose life") as standing side by side in one chapter. God's work on the heart and the person's choice are both stated, and the text does not reconcile them. |
| Who else is in it | God, who sets the choice and circumcises the heart. "Heaven and earth", called "to witness against you" (30:19). The offspring, whose life is bound up in the choice (30:6, 19). Other gods, which a heart is "drawn away to worship" (30:17) |

**Jeremiah 21:8–10.** Same words, another setting (question 4).

| Question | Reading |
|---|---|
| Setting | Jerusalem under siege. The king has asked whether the Lord will turn Babylon back (21:2). This is the answer |
| What it means there | "Behold, I set before you the way of life and the way of death." The way of life is to go out and surrender to the Chaldeans, and so "have his life as a prize of war". The way of death is to stay in the city. The reason: "I have set my face against this city for harm and not for good" (21:10) |
| What it implies | The same words set out a different choice. In Deuteronomy, life is holding fast to God. Here it is bodily survival, gained by yielding to the judgment God has sent, not by resisting it |

**What the meaning pass changes (step 4, illustration only):**
- The existing section's line, "The choice is made by doing, seeking and holding on", joins six verses and loses "he is your life". The concept the verses themselves name is **life as God himself, set before a heart that can turn**. The "do this and live" verses then become applications of that concept, not the concept itself.
- Deuteronomy 30 and Jeremiah 21 use the same words with a **change of character**. That belongs in the account and in Ch 11 §4, not as two quotations side by side.
- Under step 4, this concept probably needs its own account. Whether that account is a reshaped "Life set before a person" or something else is for #1948 to propose. It is not decided here.
