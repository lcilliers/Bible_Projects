# How call operates in the inner being: draft claims from the observations

A working draft for the researcher's reading. Every claim is inferential (B:4) until confirmed. Nothing here is entered in the claim register or the narrative. The verse wording is the ESV text in iba.db `verse`; the observation ids are the engagement observations of collection `call` (`engagement-call-observations-20261109-v1.md`).

## The shape of the story

Of the 832 occurrences of `call`, `called`, `calling`, `calls` and `invoke`: 308 (37%) are plain labels or have no bearing; 311 (37%) are an outward act (a summons, God's own call, a proclamation) that bears on someone's response; 48 (6%) are the standing a call gives, as the object of effort; 165 (20%) are the word itself carrying an inner operation (161 characteristic, 4 qualifier).

So call is mostly not an inner operation. It is a threshold word: it marks where the inner being is voiced outward, or addressed from outside, and the inner operation (need, trust, grief, humility, obedience) is usually named by its neighbours. The claims below say where the word itself does the inner work, and where it only sets the inner being in motion.

## Part 1. The human calling: the inner being voiced

**C1. Calling on the Lord is a purposeful act of the inner being turned to God, in need, trust or praise; its absence is told as a fault.**
Obs 21301, 21312, 21326, 21333 (91 verses).
- "In my distress I called upon the Lord; to my God I called" (2 Sam 22:7)
- "Jabez called upon the God of Israel" (1 Chr 4:10)
- "everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved" (Joel 2:32)
- Absence: "There is no one who calls upon your name, who rouses himself to take hold of you" (Isa 64:7); "none of them calls upon me" (Hos 7:7).
- Isa 64:7 joins the call to an effort of the self (to rouse oneself); the failure to call is a condition of the inner being, not only a lapse of speech.

**C2. A call to another person voices the caller's own need, and it is told as awaiting an answer.**
Obs 21310, 21318, 21323, 21332 (10 verses).
- "I call to my servant, but he gives me no answer" (Job 19:16)
- "I called him, but he gave no answer" (Song 5:6); "I called to my lovers, but they deceived me" (Lam 1:19)
- "will himself call out and not be answered" (Prov 21:13)
- Reading, not an observation: the answer is part of what is told. Against this, "When he calls to me, I will answer him" (Ps 91:15) gives the same shape from God's side. This needs a count of answered and unanswered calls before it stands as a claim.

**C3. A naming voices the namer's inner state, when the verse states the reason.**
Obs 21337, 21339 (31 verses). Most naming verses are bare labels (obs 21306, 21311, 21325, 21330: about 270 verses) and are not claimed.
- Grief: "call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me" (Ruth 1:20)
- Thanks and trust: "she called his name Samuel, for she said" (1 Sam 1:20); "called its name Ebenezer" (1 Sam 7:12)
- Sojourning: "he called his name Gershom" (Exod 2:22)
- Hope in a relation: "I thought you would call me, My Father" (Jer 3:19)

**C4. To call a thing by a name is a judgement of the heart.**
Obs 21308, 21317, 21329 (10 verses).
- "Woe to those who call evil good and good evil" (Isa 5:20)
- "call the Sabbath a delight" (Isa 58:13); "Will you call this a fast, and a day acceptable to the Lord?" (Isa 58:5)
- "Why do you call me" (Luke 6:46)
- "called fine gold my confidence" (Job 31:24)

**C5. To call to mind is an inner operation of memory, tied to hope.**
Obs 21307 (2 verses): "But this I call to mind, and therefore I have hope" (Lam 3:21); also Deut 30:1. Small, but it is the one place where call is wholly inward.

## Part 2. God's calling: the inner being addressed

**C6. God's call is an outward act upon a person; the inner response it asks for is named by other words of the verse.**
Obs 21304, 21315, 21321, 21328 (84 verses).
- Holiness: "as he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct" (1 Pet 1:15)
- Worthy walking: "walk in a manner worthy of God, who calls you into his own kingdom and glory" (1 Thess 2:12)
- Peace: "God has called you to peace" (1 Cor 7:15)
- Proclamation: "proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light" (1 Pet 2:9)
- Creation of what is not: "calls into existence the things that do not exist" (Rom 4:17)
- Hearing: "Speak, for your servant hears" (1 Sam 3:10), the answer to a call that names no inner operation of its own.

**C7. The standing the call gives becomes the object of the inner being's effort.**
Obs 21302, 21314, 21319 (43 verses).
- "walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called" (Eph 4:1)
- "be all the more diligent to confirm your calling and election" (2 Pet 1:10)
- "I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God" (Phil 3:14)
- "make you worthy of his calling" (2 Thess 1:11)

**C8. The standing is felt by the inner being from both sides: humility in the one called, no shame in the one who calls.**
Obs 21340, 21342, 21336, 21341, 21320 (10 verses).
- "I am no longer worthy to be called your son" (Luke 15:19); "unworthy to be called an apostle" (1 Cor 15:9)
- "refused to be called" the son of Pharaoh's daughter, by faith (Heb 11:24)
- God is "not ashamed to be called their God" (Heb 11:16); "not ashamed to call them brothers" (Heb 2:11)

## Part 3. The boundary: where call carries no inner operation

**C9. A public summons gathers people to an inner act that other words carry.**
Obs 21309, 21316, 21324 (16 verses).
- "the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth" (Jonah 3:5): the belief is named by "believed", the call only brings them together.
- "call a solemn assembly ... and cry out to the Lord" (Joel 1:14)

Plain summons of a person (obs 21303, 21313, 21322, 21327: about 180 verses) and labels (about 270 verses) are left out of the story on purpose.

## What the draft does not settle

- The 832 roles were assigned by me and are draft. The group `behaviour` for call is mine too.
- C2's pattern of the answer is a reading of the verses, not an observation, and needs a count.
- The story covers only the five member surfaces. The words that speak or cry (`cry`, `speak`, `appeal`, `entreat`, `shout`) are associated, not members, so the part of C1 and C2 that is "voicing" is only half told here.
- The 41 engagement observations are generic by surface: one observation covers many verses. A claim that needs the verse's own circumstance (who, why, with what result) would go back through the reading process, not stop at these observations.
