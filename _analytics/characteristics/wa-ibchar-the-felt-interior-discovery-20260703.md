# The felt interior / the heart's states — a discovery document (developing)

> **Mode: DISCOVERY, not summary.** Gather the *felt* states of the inner being; trace the colours; hold open whether the felt interior is a distinct characteristic or the *experiential face* of all of them, and why Proverbs — terse and impersonal — maps it more finely than expected. Registry: `ib_characteristic.code = the-felt-interior`.

## Working sense (held open)
The inner being as a *felt* thing — its states (glad, crushed, heavy, tranquil, anxious, bitter), their register on the body, and their privacy. This is where the inner being is most obviously *experienced* rather than *acted*.

## The evidence, gathered by colour

**Colour 1 — the incommunicable / private heart.** "**The heart knows its own bitterness, and no stranger shares its joy**" (Pro 14:10) — the deepest word in the corpus on the *privacy* of the interior: its grief and gladness are *not fully shareable*.

**Colour 2 — glad vs crushed (with somatic register).** "A **glad heart** makes a **cheerful face**, but by sorrow of heart the **spirit is crushed**" (Pro 15:13); "a **joyful heart is good medicine**, but a **crushed spirit dries up the bones**" (Pro 17:22); and the floor — "a man's spirit will endure sickness, but a **crushed spirit who can bear?**" (Pro 18:14). The state *shows on the face* and *works on the bones*.

**Colour 3 — the cheerful heart's "continual feast."** "The **cheerful of heart has a continual feast**" (Pro 15:15). The inner disposition, not the outer event, *sets the taste of existence* — a self that feasts on ordinary days because its heart is cheerful.

**Colour 4 — anxiety as a WEIGHT.** "**Anxiety in a man's heart weighs him down**, but a good word makes him glad" (Pro 12:25); "when the **cares of my heart are many**, your consolations **cheer my soul**" (Ps 94:19). Worry as a *downward pull*, lifted from outside.

**Colour 5 — the TRANQUIL heart (peace as bodily life; envy as bodily decay).** "A **tranquil heart gives life to the flesh, but envy makes the bones rot**" (Pro 14:30). Inner peace is *somatic health*; corrosive feeling is *somatic rot*.

**Colour 6 — the HEAVY heart (that cannot be jollied).** "Whoever **sings songs to a heavy heart** is like vinegar on soda" (Pro 25:20) — ill-timed cheer *aggravates* the heavy heart; the felt state must be *matched*, not overridden.

**Colour 7 — the self UNDONE / dissolved (extremity).** "My **heart melts like wax**" (Ps 22:14); "I am weary with my **moaning**… my eye **wastes** away from grief" (Ps 6:6–7); "my **bones wasted away**" (Ps 32:3); "my heart is struck down like grass and has **withered**" (Ps 102:4); "my **heart and flesh may fail**" (Ps 73:26). The felt interior at its limit — the whole self coming apart.

**Colour 8 — the MASK (outward belies inward).** "**Even in laughter the heart may ache**, and the end of joy may be grief" (Pro 14:13). The face can *contradict* the state — laughter over an inward ache.

## Where it changes colour — the developing picture
Two discoveries stand out:
- **The felt interior is intensely SOMATIC.** Almost every state writes itself on the body — cheerful face, crushed/dried bones, rotting bones, withered heart, wasting eye, melting-wax heart, failing flesh. The inner and the outer are *not sealed off*: the heart's state is *legible on the body* and *affects* it (good medicine / dries the bones). This is a real anthropology, not decoration — the inner being is *embodied*, its feeling and its flesh interpenetrating.
- **Proverbs maps this more finely than the Psalms** — which is surprising, since Proverbs is terse, impersonal, third-person, and Psalms is first-person prayer. Yet Proverbs gives the *incommunicable heart* (14:10), the *continual feast* (15:15), the *mask* (14:13), the *mistimed comfort* (25:20) — sharper *general* observations of felt states than the Psalms' *particular* cries. **The Psalms feel it; Proverbs anatomises it.** The two modes may be complementary evidence-channels on one thing.

## Junctions (boundaries blurring)
- **the-heart** — Colour 9 there = Colours here; the felt heart is the heart in its experiential mode.
- **grief-lament** — Colour 7 (the undone self) is grief felt; the boundary with grief-as-*handled* is where feeling becomes operation.
- **joy-gladness** — the glad/cheerful heart is joy felt.
- **rest-stillness** — the tranquil heart (Colour 5).
- **self-mastery** — the tranquil vs raging heart (14:30); governing the felt states.
- **self-toward-others** — envy (Colour 5) as a corrosive relational feeling.

## Thin / uncertain evidence (noted, not yet weighed)
- **Comfort from OUTSIDE.** The heavy heart is lifted by "a **good word**" (12:25), by God's "**consolations**" (94:19), aggravated by mistimed song (25:20). The felt interior is *permeable* — reachable (or wounded) from outside. A distinct sub-theme (the economy of comfort) worth gathering.
- **The felt interior entered only by God.** If "no stranger shares its joy" (14:10), yet "you have **known the distress of my soul**" (Ps 31:7) — then God enters where no *stranger* can. The privacy of the heart is total *toward humans* and open *toward God*. A profound junction (being-known) on thin but pointed data.
- **The PANG / being pierced.** "My soul was **embittered**, I was **pricked** in heart" (Ps 73:21) — feeling as a *wound/sting*. Lightly attested; a distinct texture.

## Open questions (the debate, left open)
1. Is the felt interior a **distinct characteristic** or the **experiential face** of all of them (trust felt as security, being-heard felt as relief, etc.)?
2. Why does **Proverbs anatomise** the felt heart more finely than the **Psalms** *feel* it — and what does that say about how the inner being is best studied (cry vs observation)?
3. Is the **somatic register** (heart → bones/face/flesh) metaphor, or a real anthropology in which the inner *writes on* the outer?
4. Is the inner being finally **solitary** (14:10 — no stranger shares it), entered only by God (31:7)? Is privacy-toward-humans + openness-toward-God its basic condition?
5. What is the **economy of comfort** — the felt heart reached, lifted, or wounded from outside (a good word, God's consolations, mistimed song)?
