# Love (aheb) and its objects — a discovery document (developing)

> **Mode: DISCOVERY, not summary.** Gather as much as possible about the *love* operation across Psalms + Proverbs, trace how it **changes colour by its object**, note the junctions (esp. with desire) and the thin hints, and hold the boundaries open. Registry: `ib_characteristic.code = love-aheb`. (The aheb-fix, 2026-07-03, made this operation visible corpus-wide — it had been under-tagged as `standalone` ~8× in the Psalms.)

## Working sense (held open)
An affection / attachment of the inner being whose **object sets its valence** — the love itself is always the characteristic; what it is aimed at makes it holy or ruinous. This is its most striking feature and the source of most of its colours.

## The evidence, gathered by colour

**Colour 1 — love of God, his name, salvation, house, word.** "**I love the LORD**, because he has heard my voice" (Ps 116:1) — love born of being heard; "**Love the LORD**, all you his saints" (Ps 31:23) — love commanded; "those who **love your name**" exult (Ps 5:11; 69:36; 119:132); "**I love the habitation of your house**" (Ps 26:8; cf. 27:4); "**Oh how I love your law**!" (Ps 119:97; 113; 127; 163; 167); "those who **love your salvation**" (Ps 40:16; 70:4). The God-ward object across its range — person, name, dwelling, word, salvation.

**Colour 2 — love of wisdom, courted as a person.** "**Love her**, and she will guard you" (Pro 4:6); "call insight your **intimate friend**" (Pro 7:4); "**I love those who love me**, and those who seek me diligently find me" (Pro 8:17) — reciprocal love; "he who **loves wisdom** makes his father glad" (Pro 29:3). Here love has a *personified* object and becomes courtship.

**Colour 3 — misdirected love (the object corrupts the love).** "How long will you **love vanity**?" (Ps 4:2); "you **love evil** more than good… you **love all words that devour**" (Ps 52:3–4); the wicked "**loves violence**" (Ps 11:5); "how long will you **love being simple**?" (Pro 1:22); "whoever **loves transgression loves strife**" (Pro 17:19); "**love not sleep**, lest you come to poverty" (Pro 20:13); "whoever **loves pleasure** will be a poor man" (Pro 21:17); and the extreme — "**all who hate me love death**" (Pro 8:36). The same faculty, aimed low, becomes self-ruin.

**Colour 4 — love-as-covering (a relational ACT, not an attachment).** "Hatred stirs up strife, but **love covers all offenses**" (Pro 10:12); "**whoever covers an offense seeks love**" (Pro 17:9). Here love is not a feeling *toward* an object but an *act* — the taking-up and *hiding* of another's fault. This colour behaves differently from all the others (see junctions).

**Colour 5 — love of inner goods (discipline, knowledge, purity).** "Whoever **loves discipline loves knowledge**" (Pro 12:1); "he who **loves purity of heart**… will have the king as his friend" (Pro 22:11). Here the object is an inner-being good itself — the self in love with its own correction, its own cleanness.

**Colour 6 — the steadfast friend's love.** "A friend **loves at all times**, and a brother is born for adversity" (Pro 17:17). Love as *constancy* — proven in adversity, not object-selection.

## Where it changes colour — the developing picture
The decisive fact is **the object, not the affection, sets the valence.** The love that clings to God's name (Colour 1) and the love that clings to vanity/violence/death (Colour 3) are *the same operation* — aheb — pointed in opposite directions. This is why the aheb-fix mattered: "love vanity" (Ps 4:2), "love being simple" (Pro 1:22), and "love your name" (Ps 5:11) are one characteristic, and the reading must foreground the *love*, letting the object carry the moral weight.

But two colours **do not fit** the object-sets-valence rule, and that is the live edge of the discovery:
- **Love-as-covering (Colour 4)** is an *act* (hiding a fault), not an attachment to an object. It even *contrasts* with the masking heart (which hides its *own* evil): love covers *another's* offense; concealment hides *one's own*. Same motion (hiding), opposite morality — decided by *whose* fault is covered.
- **Love as constancy (Colour 6)** is about *duration* ("at all times"), not object.

So "love" may be at least **three things wearing one word**: an object-directed attachment (1,2,3,5), an act of covering (4), and a steadfastness (6).

## Junctions (boundaries blurring)
- **desire-appetite** — the deepest junction. Is love a distinct operation or the *affective face of desire*? Both have their valence set by direction/object; both range from God-ward to ruinous. Yet "love your law" (delighted attachment) feels different from "thirst for God" (aching want). Unresolved — possibly love = *settled* attachment, desire = *reaching* want.
- **fear-of-the-lord / delight** — fear *matures into* love/delight (Ps 112:1); love and reverent fear may be two ends of one Godward orientation.
- **teachability** — "love of discipline" (Colour 5) is love serving teachability; is loving-correction a form of love or of teachability?
- **self-toward-others** — love-as-covering (Colour 4) and the steadfast friend (Colour 6) are relational; they may belong as much to *self-toward-others* as to love.
- **formation-by-relation** — you become what you *love* as much as what you trust? (loving death → dying; loving wisdom → wised). Possible extension of the formation-law from trust to love.

## Thin / uncertain evidence (noted, not yet weighed)
- **The asymmetry of aheb.** The *self's* love for God is aheb; *God's* love for the self is almost always a *different* word (chesed / racham). Is love-upward (aheb) categorically distinct from love-downward (steadfast love)? A structural question raised by the vocabulary, thin on inner-being data so far.
- **Love born of being heard.** "I love the LORD *because* he has heard" (Ps 116:1) — love as a *response* to attentiveness, not a spontaneous act. One clear witness; watch for more (does love always have a cause?).
- **Reciprocal love with wisdom.** "I love those who love me" (Pro 8:17) — love answered. Thin, but suggests love is met, not one-way.

## Open questions (the debate, left open)
1. Is love **one operation** (object sets valence) or **three** (attachment / covering-act / constancy) sharing a word?
2. What is the true relation of **love and desire** — settled attachment vs reaching want, or the same thing?
3. **Can love be commanded** (Ps 31:23 "Love the LORD")? If aheb is an affection, what does a *command* to it mean — is it a command to *attach*, to *direct* an existing love?
4. How can the self **love its own ruin** — "all who hate me love death" (Pro 8:36)? The darkest datum: love aimed at self-destruction. Is this love malfunctioning, or love's terrifying freedom of object?
5. Does the **formation-law** run through love as through trust — do we *become what we love*?
