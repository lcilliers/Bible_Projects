## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, not fact).** An operation "bears on the inner being" when the text or span-data names or denotes an *interior* dimension of a human — a state, faculty, disposition, affect, or volition — **or** a movement whose stated source or target is such an interior dimension. Following the Dan 1:1 correction, this is applied *interrogatively*: a stated outward act does not close the question but opens it — Q1–Q2 are asked of the actor even when no interior is stated, and any interior reached that way is marked as inference (Q3). Widening the scope to count a non-human being's sovereign agency over a human's *circumstance* as bearing on the inner being would change several outcomes below; that fork is left open (see passage-level fork).

**Reading rule applied.** For `[AMBIGUOUS]` spans, the `STEP live` code-specific gloss is taken as authoritative over the base `meaning_tree`, which can diverge (confirmed at Dan 1:4 "competent", where the base tree still shows an unrelated "lizard" sense while the code-specific line reads *strength/power*).

**Passage-level open fork (raised at 1:2, never closed within this range).** Dan 1:2 states the Lord "gave Jehoiakim into his hand." Whether divine sourcing reaches only the *outcome/enablement* of the human acts in this passage, or also the *interior states* of the actors performing them, is the recurring question. It is held open and flagged for source-level adjudication, not resolved here.

## Dan 1:1

> In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it.

**Observation.** Temporal frame (third year of Jehoiakim). Nebuchadnezzar performs "came" (H0935G, code-specific *come/go in*) and "besieged" (H6696A, code-specific *bind, besiege, confine, shut in*) against Jerusalem, resumed by "it" (H9034, 3fs, agreeing with the city). All named actors are human; no interior state is stated for any party.

**Interpretation — questions considered.**
- Q1/Q2: Nebuchadnezzar is a focused inner being. Besieging is not reflex; it carries an implied driving-interior (resolve, will-to-subjugate, ambition, or hostility). *(Inference — named, not asserted.)*
- Q3: The interior is inferred, not stated. Raised as referential; its content is left unassigned — to name it would be to write an inner being the text does not give.
- Q4: Source of that interior is unstated here. The source-of-enablement question is answered only at 1:2 (the Lord). Source-of-state remains open. Keep the two distinct.
- Q5: Target — the city Jerusalem, and behind it (Q8) its inhabitants / Judah as a collective; but the verse states action on the *city*, not on any interior.
- Q6: Movement (external military act).
- Q7: Links forward to 1:2 (the same campaign, now with the Lord as stated source of the outcome).
- Q9: The document is sufficient to state the act; it is silent on the interior, which is itself the finding.

**Decision.** No interior state is stated. Under the conservative working scope: **set aside as a stated IB operation**, but the actor's implied driving-interior is **retained as a referential aspect** (correction from the first reading, which wrongly let the unstated interior collapse into "no bearing").

## Dan 1:2

> And the Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, with some of the vessels of the house of God. And he brought them to the land of Shinar, to the house of his god, and placed the vessels in the treasury of his god.

**Observation.** Non-human subject, the Lord (H0136), is subject of "gave" (H5414G, code-specific *give / deliver up / entrust*). Object: Jehoiakim, delivered "into his hand" — "hand" is H3027H, whose code-specific line leans anatomical (*hand/arm*) while the base tree carries the figurative *strength, power*. Nebuchadnezzar then "brought" and "placed" the vessels (both H0935P) to Shinar, to the house of his god, into the treasury of his god.

**Observation — idiom note.** "Give into the hand of" is idiomatically a transfer of *power/control* — the figurative sense the base tree carries — not the anatomical sense the code-specific line foregrounds. Flagged as a case where the code-specific gloss may under-capture the idiom. Not resolved here; referred to CC / source level.

**Interpretation — questions considered.**
- Q1/Q2 (Nebuchadnezzar): his enablement to prevail is now sourced. Whether his *disposition* is likewise sourced is the passage fork.
- Q4 (the decisive one): two distinct things could trace to the Lord and must not be merged — (i) *enablement/outcome*: the success of the subjugation is explicitly Lord-sourced ("the Lord gave"); (ii) *state of mind*: that Nebuchadnezzar's resolve itself was Lord-sourced is a *further* inference the verse does not make. Recorded as a live referential debate; the gap between outcome-sourcing and interior-sourcing is named, not closed.
- Q5: Target — Jehoiakim / Judah (human, and collective by extension), with the temple vessels as co-object.
- Q2d handling: the foreign "god" is named only as possessor of a house and treasury; it neither acts nor relates to any human interior → set aside per read-guidance 2d.
- Q6: Movement (a giving-over / delivering into power).

**Reflection.** This is the seam of the whole passage. If divine sourcing reaches only circumstance, the inner beings of Daniel 1 act on their own interior fuel while God governs outcomes; if it reaches disposition, the human interiors themselves become targets of divine operation. 1:2 leaves this open. Recommendation: hold it open rather than resolve by import; it is the richest question in the range.

**Decision.** Under the conservative scope: **set aside as a stated interior operation**, with the divine→human operation (giving-over) **retained as a referential aspect** and the outcome-vs-disposition sourcing debate recorded. Under the wider scope, this verse yields a full operation (subject = the Lord; movement = delivering-into-power; source = the Lord's agency; target = Jehoiakim-and-Judah). The choice between scopes is deferred to the researcher.

## Dan 1:3

> Then the king commanded Ashpenaz, his chief eunuch, to bring some of the people of Israel, both of the royal family and of the nobility,

**Observation.** The king (Nebuchadnezzar) is subject of "commanded" — H0559, whose base gloss range includes *to say in one's heart, to think, to intend*. So an interior dimension of commanding is carried in the lemma's own range, not only in inference.

**Interpretation — questions considered.**
- Q1/Q2 (Nebuchadnezzar): commanding implies a purposeful *intent* — a deliberate design to draw Judah's elite into his court. The verb's own semantic field (*think/intend*) brings this closer to the text than the siege-resolve of 1:1.
- Q3: The intent is lexically supported but not explicitly predicated; treated as a strong referential inference, still named as inference.
- Q4: Source of the intent stated as his own (he commands); whether it also traces to the Lord's 1:2 sourcing is the recurring fork.
- Q5: Target — Ashpenaz (immediate human recipient) and, beyond him, the collective to be selected.
- Q1 (Ashpenaz, second focused inner being): a command lands on him; the text states no interior response — no assent, reluctance, or zeal. Silence surfaced (not filled) as a referential aspect.
- Q8 (collective): "some of the people of Israel… royal family… nobility" — the target is the elite / lineage of Judah. Its *nature* (the flower of a nation's line, not anonymous captives) will bear on the formation in 1:4–1:5.

**Decision.** **Retain** an intent-operation (Nebuchadnezzar → selection of Judah's elite), lexically supported, with source-of-state open. Ashpenaz's interior stance recorded as unstated.

## Dan 1:4

> youths without blemish, of good appearance and skillful in all wisdom, endowed with knowledge, understanding learning, and competent to stand in the king's palace, and to teach them the literature and language of the Chaldeans.

**Observation.** The youths are specified by a cluster of interior terms: skillful/prudent (H7919A, *sakal* — be circumspect, have insight), wisdom (H2451, *chokmah*), knowledge (H3045 / H1847), understanding (H0995, *bin* — discern), learning (H4093, *madda* — knowledge, *mind*, thought), "competent" (H3581B, code-specific *strength/ability*), alongside outward qualities (without blemish, good appearance). Stated purposes: "to stand in the king's palace" (H5975G, *amad*) and "to teach them the literature and language of the Chaldeans" (H3925G, *lamad*).

**Interpretation — questions considered.** Here the inner being is *named directly, as attribute*, not merely inferred behind an act. Two distinct operations:

1. **State/status operation** — the youths *possess* interior faculties (prudence, wisdom, knowledge, discernment, mind).
   - Subject: the collective of youths (Q8). Operation: a possessed interior condition. Q6: state.
   - Q4 (source): the text predicates the qualities of them without naming an origin. Gap marked: are these God-given, native, or trained? The verse is silent — recorded, not filled.

2. **Movement operation** — these interior faculties become the **criterion/target** of another's selective action; the king (via Ashpenaz) selects *on* them.
   - Subject-in-focus shifts by vantage: the youths (whose interior is possessed) or the king (whose operation targets that interior). Q5: a human's inner being is the object of another human's assessment and appropriation.

3. **Second movement — "to teach them"** — aimed at the inner being: instilling Chaldean literature and language into the youths' minds.
   - Source: the Babylonian instructional apparatus. Target: the youths' interior (knowledge/formation). Q6: movement. This is the first operation in the passage whose target is unambiguously an inner being.

**Reflection (tension surfaced — Q7).** The youths are chosen *for* an interior excellence rooted in Judah, then subjected to a formation designed to *overwrite* the cultural furniture of that same interior. The verse does not comment; but the linkage between "selected for their wisdom" and "to be taught Chaldean learning" is real and is surfaced rather than passed over.

**Decision.** **Retain** three operations: possessed interior faculties (state); those faculties as selection-target (movement); formation-into-the-interior (movement). This is the density point of the passage.

## Dan 1:5

> The king assigned them a daily portion of the food that the king ate, and of the wine that he drank. They were to be educated for three years, and at the end of that time they were to stand before the king.

**Observation.** "Assigned" (H4487, *manah* — count/reckon/appoint) a daily portion of food and wine; "educated for three years" (H1431, here glossed *to magnify / bring up*); "to stand before the king" (H5975G).

**Interpretation — questions considered.**
- The provisioning itself (food, wine) is outward. The inner-being thread is the **formation**: a three-year process intended to qualify the youths' interior and capability to "stand before the king."
- Subject: the youths (collective). Operation: an intended transformation/qualification over time (Q6: movement, not static state). Source: the king's design. Target: the youths' formed capability (interior).
- Q7: links explicitly back to 1:4's "teach them" — 1:5 sets the *duration and telos* of the formation 1:4 named.
- Q1/Q3 (the youths' own interior stance): the youths are grammatically and narratively objects of assigning and educating; the text states no interior stance toward the provisioning — no acceptance, resistance, or ambivalence. Silence surfaced deliberately, with a **forward-pointer**: the chapter's later movement is known to turn on exactly such an interior stance, but that content lies outside this range and is *not* imported. What is recorded is the pointer, not the content.

**Decision.** **Retain** a formation-operation (king's design → youths' interior capability over three years), linked to 1:4. The youths' interior response recorded as unstated and referable forward.

## Dan 1:6

> Among these were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah of the tribe of Judah.

**Observation.** Four are named and located within a collective identity (tribe of Judah, Q8). The span-data identifies the *persons* but does not gloss the *meaning* of their names.

**Interpretation — questions considered.**
- Largely a *status* assertion (Q6: state): who these four are and where they belong. Identity/belonging is arguably an inner-being dimension (the self located in a people), functioning here as the **substrate** on which 1:7 operates (Q7).
- **Insufficiency (Q9).** The theophoric sense of the Hebrew names — what would make the belonging *covenantal* rather than merely tribal — is **not in the document**; it lives in name-etymology data the extract does not carry. So: a Judahite identity is asserted; the religious content of that identity cannot be established from this document alone. Named as insufficiency, not supplied from memory.

**Decision.** **Retain** an identity/belonging status as substrate for 1:7, with the covenantal content flagged insufficiently evidenced.

## Dan 1:7

> And the chief of the eunuchs gave them names: Daniel he called Belteshazzar, Hananiah he called Shadrach, Mishael he called Meshach, and Azariah he called Abednego.

**Observation.** "Gave them names" and "called" each youth by a new name — H7760M, code-specific *to set / appoint (a name)*. Four renamings: Daniel→Belteshazzar, Hananiah→Shadrach, Mishael→Meshach, Azariah→Abednego. The span-data links each person across both names but does not carry the *meaning* of either the Hebrew or the Babylonian names.

**Interpretation — questions considered.** The clearest operation in the passage whose target is the inner being as identity/self:
- Subject-in-focus: each youth's identity (a collective of four, individuated — Q8).
- Operation: a movement — an imposed relabeling of the self by an external authority (Q6).
- Source: the chief of the eunuchs, acting as agent of the Babylonian court/system (human source standing for an institutional one).
- Target: the youths' identity — the substrate 1:6 established (Q7, explicit linkage).
- Q1 (chief eunuch as focused inner being): his interior in doing this is unstated — administrative act, or deliberate assimilation? The category (an intent behind the renaming) is raised; its content left unassigned.

**Decisive insufficiency (Q9).** What would make renaming an operation on *religious/covenantal* identity — the erasure of Yahwistic theophoric names and the imposition of names invoking Babylonian deities — depends entirely on name-meaning data the document does not contain. From this extract: *that* an external authority relabeled the four, and *whom* each new name maps to, are establishable; the *interior significance* of the relabeling is not derivable from the document alone. This is the single most important place where visible operation outruns available data.

**Decision.** **Retain** an operation upon identity (institutional source → youths' identity), resting on 1:6, with its inner-being weight flagged insufficiently evidenced pending name-etymology data.

A single spine runs across the range: the Lord's sourcing in 1:2 stands behind a cascade of human operations — siege (1:1), delivering-into-hand (1:2), command and selection (1:3), formation and its telos (1:4–1:5), and relabeling of identity (1:7). Whether the divine sourcing reaches only the *outcomes* of these acts or also the *interior states* of the actors is the recurring open question, first raised at 1:2 and never closed by the text within this range. Carried as a passage-level referential aspect, referred for source-level adjudication.

## Insufficiencies register

1. **Name etymologies absent (1:6, 1:7).** Hebrew and Babylonian name meanings are not in the base extract; the covenantal/religious weight of the identity and its relabeling cannot be established from this document. Additional data required.
2. **Youths' interior stance unstated (1:5).** No interior response to the king's provision is given in-range; recorded as silence with a forward-pointer (content not imported).
3. **Source of the youths' faculties unstated (1:4).** Whether the possessed wisdom/knowledge is God-given, native, or trained is not stated; recorded as a gap.
4. **Idiom vs code-specific gloss (1:2).** "Into his hand" (H3027H): the idiom of power/control sits in the base tree, while the code-specific line foregrounds the anatomical sense — a possible under-capture. Referred to CC / source level.

## Emergent questions log (raised during this passage; carry forward)

- **EQ-1.** When a verb's own lexical range carries an interior sense (e.g. H0559 *amar* = also *to think / say in one's heart / intend*), does that count as the interior being *stated* (text-grounded) or *inferred*? Dan 1:3 sits between the two. A rule may be needed for lexically-carried interiors.
- **EQ-2.** How should "source of state" vs "source of enablement" be recorded as *parts* when only one of the two is stated and the other is an open fork? (Recurs at 1:1–1:3.)
- **EQ-3.** For operations that *target* an inner being to reshape it (formation, 1:4–1:5; relabeling, 1:7), is the appropriate subject-in-focus the acting party or the affected interior — or both, recorded from each vantage?
- **EQ-4.** When a collective's individuated members are named (1:6–1:7), is the operation recorded once (to the collection) or per-member, or both?
- **EQ-5.** A standing sufficiency question: how often does visible operation outrun the base extract's data (as at 1:7)? If frequent, name-etymology coverage may need to enter the extract itself.
