## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, not fact).** Dan 8:1-27 is taken as a single reading unit — Daniel's second apocalyptic vision, explicitly linked by its own opening verse to the first (Dan 7). The chapter returns to Hebrew (the language of chapters 1 and 8-12, framing the Aramaic central section 2:4b-7:28). Structurally it parallels Dan 7 closely: vision (8:1-14) → Daniel's own reaction and inquiry (8:15, 17-18, 27) → interpretation supplied by an angelic figure, here named for the first time in the corpus (8:15-26). "Bearing on the inner being" here includes: (a) Daniel's own stated interior — fear, sought understanding, and, more severely than any prior chapter, physical illness — the corpus's most extreme stated vision-aftermath; (b) the ram and goat, non-human in form but explicitly and directly resolved by name to specific historical kingdoms (8:20-21 — "Media and Persia," "Greece" — a more direct, individually-named resolution than Daniel 7's systemic "kings/kingdoms" framing, and closer in kind to Daniel 4's single named identification); (c) the little horn's own explicitly stated interior — "in his own mind he shall become great" (8:25) — the clearest Hebrew-register instance of the self-exaltation pattern tracked in Aramaic across Dan 4:20, 5:20, and 7:8/25; (d) the "holy ones" and Gabriel, non-human, in scope per note (b) as their dialogue and action directly inform and are directed at Daniel, the human in focus.

**Reading rule applied.** 100% meaning coverage reported for the available verses (348/348 non-particle spans). Hebrew throughout — the first Hebrew-language chapter debated in this series (Dan 1 was Hebrew but not yet covered in this passage-debate corpus; Dan 2:4b-7:28 was Aramaic).
