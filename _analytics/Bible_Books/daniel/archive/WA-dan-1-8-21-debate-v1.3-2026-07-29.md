## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, unchanged from prior debate).** An operation bears on
the inner being when the text or span-data names or denotes an *interior* dimension of a human —
state, faculty, disposition, affect, or volition — or a movement whose stated source or target is
such an interior dimension. Applied interrogatively. **Revised this pass:** per step-2 note (f),
every human named in the passage is a presumptive candidate, including unnamed/implied observers
recoverable from context (1:15) and recurring minor characters at the moment their earlier stakes
resolve (1:18's Ashpenaz). The scope fork (interior-named-only vs. also non-human agency on human
circumstance) remains **open and unsettled by the researcher**.

**Reading rule applied.** For `[AMBIGUOUS]` spans, the `STEP live` code-specific gloss is
authoritative over the base `meaning_tree`.

## Dan 1:8 — the pivot: resolve seated in the heart

> But Daniel resolved that he would not defile himself with the king's food, or with the wine that he drank. Therefore he asked the chief of the eunuchs to allow him not to defile himself.

**Observation.** "Resolved" is a three-span cluster — H7760A (*set / put / make*) + H5921A
(*upon*) + **H3820A (*leb*, heart)**: literally *Daniel set upon his heart*. The interior seat
(**heart**) is present as a lexical item in the text, not merely implied. "Defile" is H1351
(*gaal* — pollute, desecrate; moral/ritual). Then "asked" (H1245, *baqash* — seek, request) the
chief of the eunuchs.

**Operation 1 — interior resolve, heart-seated.**
- **Action-type:** resolved.
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** an interior determination of the will (resolve), seated in the heart —
  **stated**, not inferred: *leb* is lexically present. The strongest such case in Daniel 1 so
  far. (Advances EQ-1: a named interior-seat word = stated; contrast the *amar* case at 1:3,
  where the interior was only lexically latent = inferred.)
- **Source:** stated as Daniel's own — self-sourced volition. Whether God stands behind it is
  *not stated here* (contrast 1:9, 1:17 where divine sourcing of interiors *is* stated); raised
  as the recurring fork, left open.
- **Target:** Daniel's own conduct/integrity (his own inner being as object), with the king's
  food/wine as the medium.

**Operation 2 — movement outward: the request.**
- **Action-type:** asked.
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** the resolve produces a request to the chief of the eunuchs.
- **Source:** Operation 1 (the resolve).
- **Target:** the eunuch (human).

**Q9 (insufficiency).** The text states Daniel regards the food as defiling, but the extract does
**not** carry *why* (the dietary/covenantal grounds). The interior stance is stated; its
grounding is insufficiently evidenced in-document.

**Reflection.** This verse **closes the forward-pointer** left at 1:5 in the prior debate: the
youths' interior stance toward the king's provision, unstated there, is now stated here — as
Daniel's heart-seated refusal.

**Decision.** **Retain** two linked operations: interior resolve (self-sourced, heart-seated,
targeting own integrity) → producing an outward request (targeting the eunuch). Grounding of the
"defile" judgement flagged insufficient.

## Dan 1:9 — divine sourcing of an interior disposition

> And God gave Daniel favor and compassion in the sight of the chief of the eunuchs,

**Observation.** God (H0430G) "gave" (H5414G) Daniel "favor" (H2617A, *chesed*) and "compassion"
(H7356B, *rachamim*; code-specific line corrects the base tree's "womb" to *compassion*) "in the
sight of" (H6440L, *lifney*) the chief of the eunuchs.

**Operation 1 — divine→interior giving.**
- **Action-type:** gave.
- **Subject:** God.
- **Operation:** a giving — of favour/compassion, a relational disposition.
- **Source:** God, explicitly stated — unlike 1:2 (where only the *outcome* was Lord-sourced),
  1:9 sources an *interior* directly. The passage's clearest divine→interior operation on a
  disposition.
- **Target:** a genuine ambiguity, recorded as referential debate, not resolved. (a) the
  favour/compassion is a quality *given to Daniel* that lands in the eunuch's perception; target
  = Daniel's standing. (b) God moves *the eunuch's* interior to regard Daniel with
  favour/compassion; target = the eunuch's disposition. "In the sight of / before the face of"
  leaves both open. Both recorded; neither imported as settled.

**Q1 (the eunuch as focused inner being):** under reading (b), his interior is the
operated-upon field; under (a), his perception is the arena. Either way he is in focus.

**Reflection.** 1:9 is the hinge that makes Daniel's 1:8 request survivable: an interior resolve
(1:8) meets a divinely-sourced favour (1:9). The linkage between the two is explicit and
load-bearing (Q7).

**Decision.** **Retain** a divine→interior operation (God → favour/compassion, targeting the
Daniel/eunuch relational field). Target-vantage recorded as an open referential debate.

## Dan 1:10 — the official's stated fear, and his reasoning

> and the chief of the eunuchs said to Daniel, "I fear my lord the king, who assigned your food and your drink; for why should he see that you were in worse condition than the youths who are of your own age? So you would endanger my head with the king."

**Observation.** The eunuch "said" (H0559) and states "I fear" (H3373, *yare*). His reasoning:
the king "assigned" (H4487) the food; "why should he *see*" (H7200G, *ra'ah* — see, perceive,
observe, discern) the youths "in worse condition" (H2196, **not yet registered**); "you would
endanger" (H2325, **not yet registered**) "my head".

**Operation 1 — the eunuch's stated fear, issuing in refusal.**
- **Action-type:** feared.
- **Subject:** the chief eunuch.
- **Operation:** fear — **stated**, not inferred. This **closes another prior forward-pointer**:
  in the 1:1–7 debate the officials' interior was left unstated; here the same office-holder's
  interior is explicitly given.
- **Source:** stated — the king (a human) and the situation (risk to his own head). Human/
  situational, not divine.
- **Target:** shapes his refusal-response to Daniel; target = the negotiation, and by extension
  Daniel's request.

**Q9 (insufficiency):** "worse condition" and "endanger" are unregistered; the *intensity* and
exact force of his fear-reasoning lean on spans the extract does not gloss.

**Reflection.** Two interiors now stand in tension across 1:8–1:10: Daniel's heart-seated resolve
vs. the eunuch's self-preserving fear — the multi-IB case the read guidance anticipates. **This
fear is the stake that 1:18 will resolve, and — see below — the resolution point is where the
debate's own attention to the eunuch's interior originally stopped.**

**Decision.** **Retain** the eunuch's fear as a stated interior state (human/situational source)
issuing in a refusal-movement toward Daniel.

## Dan 1:11–1:13 — Daniel's counter-proposal

> [1:11] Then Daniel said to the steward whom the chief of the eunuchs had assigned over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, [1:12] "Test your servants for ten days; let us be given vegetables to eat and water to drink. [1:13] Then let our appearance and the appearance of the youths who eat the king's food be observed by you, and deal with your servants according to what you see."

**Observation.** Daniel "said" (H0559) to the steward (H4453, **not yet registered**). He
proposes: "Test" (H5254G, *nasah*) "ten days"; "be given" (H5414G) vegetables and water; then
"let our appearance… be observed" (H7200G, *ra'ah*) "and deal" (H6213A, *asah*) "according to
what you see" (H7200G — the same lexeme that will recur, unattributed, at 1:15).

**Operation 1 — Daniel's devised proposal.**
- **Action-type:** proposed (devised a test).
- **Subject:** Daniel.
- **Operation:** the continuation of the 1:8 resolve, translated into strategy — a devised,
  prudent plan to secure the resolve without endangering the official. The interior driving it
  is inferred, anchored in the stated 1:8 resolve.
- **Source:** Daniel (self). The plan proposes an *empirical criterion* (visible appearance) as
  the arbiter — an externalisation by which an interior conviction seeks vindication in
  observable outcome.
- **Target:** the steward (human), invited to become the assessor — the role that will
  (inferentially) be exercised, unnamed, at 1:15.

**Q9:** "steward" (H4453) unregistered; the office is under-specified in the extract.

**Reflection.** Note the epistemic move: Daniel stakes an interior conviction on a *visible*
test. The inner being here seeks external, observable warrant (EQ-7).

**Decision.** **Retain** a proposal-operation (Daniel's resolve → devised plan → steward as
invited assessor), continuous with 1:8.

## Dan 1:15 — the visible vindication, and the unnamed judgement behind it

> At the end of ten days it was seen that they were better in appearance and fatter in flesh than all the youths who ate the king's food.

**Observation.** "It was seen" (H7200G, Niphal — was seen/appeared); "better" (H2896A, *tov*) in
"appearance" (H4758); "fatter" (H1277, **not yet registered**) in "flesh" (H1320, *basar*). The
verb is passive; no subject is named.

**Operation 1 — the outward, physical outcome.**
- **Action-type:** (appeared better/fatter) — an outcome, not an act.
- **Subject:** the youths (as observed).
- **Operation:** an outward/physical result — appearance, flesh.
- **Source / Target:** n/a to the outcome itself.
- Under the conservative scope, no interior is stated here for the youths → not an IB operation
  in its own right.

**Operation 2 — the implied observer's judgement (new this revision — the miss the assessment
identified).**
- **Action-type:** it was seen (judged).
- **Subject:** `[INFERRED, not named]` — the passive construction names no observer, but 1:13's
  proposal explicitly assigns the steward the role of the one who will "observe" (same root,
  H7200 *ra'ah*) and "deal with" the youths "according to what you see." Continuity across
  1:13→1:14→1:15 makes the steward the best-supported candidate for who is doing the seeing at
  1:15, though this is itself an inference layered on an inference — flagged as such, held with
  more caution than 1:18–19's clearly-named observer (the king).
- **Operation:** a favourable, discerning judgement — an evaluative act, not a passive registering
  of fact. This is the *same type* of operation the debate retains at 1:18–19 ("none was found
  like Daniel… an act of evaluation, lexically latent"), but v1.0 of this debate dismissed 1:15
  outright while retaining 1:18–19 — an inconsistency between two structurally near-identical
  cases, with no stated ground for treating them differently. This revision corrects the
  asymmetry by giving 1:15 the same interrogative treatment, not by forcing the same conclusion.
- **Source:** the (inferred) steward's own perceptive faculty — continuous with his 1:14
  consent-disposition, if the identification is accepted.
- **Target:** the youths' outward appearance (object); behind it, implicitly, Daniel's 1:8
  resolve now facing empirical vindication.
- **Q9 (sufficiency):** insufficient to name the observer with certainty from this verse alone;
  the identification with the steward rests on inference from 1:13, not on anything stated at
  1:15 itself. Recorded as a lower-confidence referential operation than 1:18–19's, not as an
  equally firm one — the correction is to *ask the question*, not to manufacture false parity.

**Q7 (the load-bearing linkage, unchanged from v1.0):** the physical result and the interior
conviction of 1:8 are linked via 1:13's criterion; the linkage is the finding.

**Decision.** **Set aside** Operation 1 (the outward outcome) as a stated IB operation in its own
right. **Retain** Operation 2 as a referential, lower-confidence operation (the implied
observer's evaluative judgement), correcting the prior inconsistent treatment relative to
1:18–19. **Retain** the 1:8→1:13→1:15 vindication linkage as before.

## Dan 1:17 — divine gift of interior faculties

> As for these four youths, God gave them learning and skill in all literature and wisdom, and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams.

**Observation.** God (H0430G) "gave" (H5414G) the four "learning" (H4093, *madda*) and "skill"
(H7919A, *sakal*) in "literature" (H5612H) and "wisdom" (H2451, *chokmah*); Daniel "had
understanding" (H0995, *bin*) in "visions" (H2377, **not yet registered**) and "dreams" (H2472,
**not yet registered**).

**Operation 1 — divine→interior endowment.**
- **Action-type:** gave.
- **Subject:** God.
- **Operation:** a giving, producing a durable interior state — decisive: the interior faculties
  (learning, skill, wisdom, understanding) are **stated as God-given**. This **closes prior
  insufficiency #3** (the 1:4 source of the youths' faculties was unstated).
- **Source:** God, explicitly.
- **Target:** the four youths' interior (collective, Q8), with Daniel individuated for a further
  faculty (understanding in visions/dreams).

**Q7:** sharp contrast-linkage with 1:4 — there the youths were *selected for* an interior
excellence and *set to be taught* Chaldean learning; here the decisive interior endowment is
sourced not to the Chaldean formation but to **God**. The passage quietly re-sources the interior
excellence away from the Babylonian apparatus.

**Q9:** "visions" and "dreams" unregistered; the *content-domain* of Daniel's distinctive
understanding leans on unglossed spans.

**Reflection.** With 1:9 and 1:17 together, the text twice sources an interior to God within this
chapter. The asymmetry — God is named source of *good/faithful* interiors, but not of the
adversary's disposition (1:2) — is logged as EQ-6, not resolved.

**Decision.** **Retain** a divine→interior operation (God → interior faculties, targeting the
four collectively and Daniel distinctly). Closes prior insufficiency #3.

## Dan 1:18–1:19 — presentation and the king's assessment

> [1:18] At the end of the time, when the king had commanded that they should be brought in, the chief of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar. [1:19] And the king spoke with them, and among all of them none was found like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. Therefore they stood before the king.

**Observation.** King "commanded" (H0559); eunuch "brought them in" (H0935P) "before" (H6440G)
Nebuchadnezzar. King "spoke with" (H1696G, *dabar*); "none was found" (H4672, *matsa*) "like"
them; they "stood" (H5975G, *amad*) "before the king".

**Operation 1 — the king's assessment.**
- **Action-type:** assessed (spoke with, found none like).
- **Subject:** the king.
- **Operation:** "spoke with… none found like" implies an **assessment/judgement** — an interior
  act of evaluation, lexically latent (a finding-of-none-like presupposes discernment). Inferred,
  named.
- **Source:** the king's own evaluative faculty.
- **Target:** the youths' interior excellence, as the object of assessment.
- **Q7 (telos realised):** "they stood before the king" **closes the 1:5 telos** ("to stand
  before the king") set in the prior passage — the formation's stated goal is reached.

**Operation 2 — Ashpenaz's own interior at the moment of presentation (new this revision — the
second miss the assessment identified).**
- **Action-type:** brought them in.
- **Subject:** the chief eunuch (Ashpenaz), who personally "brought them in before
  Nebuchadnezzar."
- **Operation:** `[not stated]` — this is the exact moment his life-stake, declared explicitly at
  1:10 ("you would endanger my head with the king"), is resolved one way or the other. The act
  recorded is purely locational/administrative ("brought them in"); step-2 note (f) and step-3
  note (e) both require that this not be treated as therefore empty of interior — an interior
  (anxiety, hope, relief-in-waiting) is precisely the kind of thing that "may be hidden behind an
  act... with only the action being recorded." v1.0 of this debate applied Q1/Q2/Q3 carefully to
  Ashpenaz at 1:10 and did not bring that same attention back to him here, at the moment his
  stated fear is actually cashed out.
- **Source:** his own prior stake (1:10), self-sourced.
- **Target:** n/a — reflexive, his own state.
- **Q9 (sufficiency):** insufficient — the text gives no data on his state at this moment at all,
  moving straight from "brought them in" to the king's own reaction. This silence is arguably
  more notable than most in the passage, precisely because a prior stated fear (1:10) makes this
  the natural point for a reader to expect *some* resolution and the text supplies none.

**Decision.** **Retain** the king's-assessment operation as before, and the telos-closure of 1:5.
**Retain**, newly, Ashpenaz's interior at this moment as an explicit, flagged recorded silence —
notable precisely because it is the payoff-point of an earlier stated stake, not an unexamined
minor character.

## Passage-level linkages (Q7)

1. **The two-interior collision (1:8–1:16):** Daniel's heart-seated resolve (1:8) vs. the
   eunuch's fear (1:10), mediated by God-given favour (1:9), routed around via Daniel's devised
   test (1:12–13), enabled by the steward's consent (1:14), vindicated in visible outcome —
   assessed by the (inferred) steward at 1:15 — enacted (1:16).
2. **Re-sourcing of the interior excellence (1:4 → 1:17):** selected-for and set-to-be-taught in
   1:4; decisively **God-given** in 1:17.
3. **Telos closure (1:5 → 1:19):** "to stand before the king" (goal) → "they stood before the
   king" (realised).
4. **Divine→interior sourcing attested (1:9, 1:17)** — bearing on, but not closing, the 1:2 fork.
5. **Ashpenaz's risk, opened and never narratively closed (1:10 → 1:18) — new this revision.**
   His stated fear at 1:10 is never revisited at the moment it is either realised or averted
   (1:18). The text moves straight to the king's own reaction; Ashpenaz's own relief, continued
   anxiety, or anything else is simply absent. Recorded as a genuine textual silence, not a
   debate oversight, now that the question has actually been asked.
