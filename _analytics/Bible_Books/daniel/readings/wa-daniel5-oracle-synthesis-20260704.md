## DAN-weighed-wanting · Daniel 5:1–31 · [D, multi] — the heart that will not humble itself though it knew; the interior weighed and found wanting

**The reading.** Belshazzar feasts, drinking from the temple vessels and praising "**the gods of gold and silver**" (v.4). A hand writes on the wall; the king's "**limbs gave way, and his knees knocked together**" (v.6). Daniel's indictment fixes on the *interior* failure, sharpened by contrast with his father Nebuchadnezzar's humbling: "**you his son, Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this… the God in whose hand is your breath, and whose are all your ways, you have not honoured**" (vv.22–23). The verdict: "**Tekel, you have been weighed in the balances and found wanting**" (v.27). The finding is **a heart that *refuses to humble itself* despite *knowing* the lesson already given — and an interior *weighed* on God's scales and *found short*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the aggravated guilt of *knowing without heeding*: Belshazzar had before him the fully-documented case of his father's pride and humbling ("though you **knew** all this", v.22), yet "**did not humble his heart**" — so his sin is not ignorance but the *deliberate non-application* of a known truth; and the "weighing" (v.27) images the interior as something *measured for its true worth* against God's standard, and found *deficient*** *(inferred: the hinge is v.22 — "**you knew all this**": the finding is that guilt is *deepened by knowledge* — Belshazzar is not condemned for lacking the lesson but for *possessing it and not letting it humble him* (the exact aggravation Jesus names, "that servant who knew…", Luke 12:47); the failure is *of the heart's response to known truth*, not of information; and the specific un-humbled act is a *defiant self-exaltation* — "you have **lifted up yourself** against the Lord of heaven" (v.23), worshipping gods that "**do not see or hear or know**" while dishonouring "the God in whose hand is your breath" (the God on whom his very life depends, ignored); the finding is that the un-humbled heart *inverts dependence* — honouring the powerless (idols) and slighting the One who holds its breath; and the "**weighed in the balances and found wanting**" (v.27) is a piercing image of the interior — the self is not merely judged by deeds but *weighed for its substance*, its true mass assessed against God's standard, and found *light* — a life that looked full (the feast, the kingdom) shown to be, at the core, *deficient*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *knowing without humbling ("you have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this" — guilt deepened by the non-application of known truth)* (thread — a gem: the failure of the heart's response to what it already knows, w/ 5:22, Luke 12:47-48, Jas 4:17); *the un-humbled heart inverting dependence ("lifted up yourself against the Lord of heaven"; honouring idols that cannot see while slighting the God in whose hand is your breath)* (new — a gem: honouring the powerless and ignoring the One who holds one's life, w/ 5:23, Isa 42:8); *the interior weighed and found wanting ("weighed in the balances and found wanting" — the self assessed for its true substance and found light)* (new — a gem: a life that looked full shown deficient at the core, w/ 5:27, Job 31:6, Ps 62:9); *the terror of the exposed heart (knees knocking at the hand's writing)* (thread — w/ 5:6, Isa 21:3-4).
