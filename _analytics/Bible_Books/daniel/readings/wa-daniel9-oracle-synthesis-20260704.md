## DAN-confession-prayer · Daniel 9:1–27 · [D, multi] — intercessory confession; identifying with corporate sin; pleading mercy not merit

**The reading.** Reading in Jeremiah that the exile would last seventy years (v.2), Daniel does not merely wait — he *prays*: "**Then I turned my face to the Lord God, seeking him by prayer and pleas for mercy with fasting and sackcloth and ashes**" (v.3). His confession is striking for its *solidarity*: "**we have sinned and done wrong… we have rebelled**" (v.5) — the blameless man ("greatly loved", v.23) numbering himself with his guilty people. He grounds the plea not in worth but in mercy: "**we do not present our pleas before you because of our righteousness, but because of your great mercy**" (v.18), and presses: "**O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive… Delay not, for your own sake**" (v.19). The finding is **an *intercessory confession* — the righteous man *identifying himself with his people's sin* ("*we* have sinned") — and a plea grounded entirely in *God's mercy, not the petitioner's righteousness*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that, on learning God's promise, responds not with passivity but with *pleading prayer* — and whose confession is *corporate and self-implicating* (Daniel, personally faithful, says "**we** have sinned", refusing to stand apart from his people's guilt) — and whose whole appeal rests on *God's character* ("your great mercy", "for your own sake"), disclaiming any merit** *(inferred: three inner-being movements. First, *promise provokes prayer* — Daniel *reads* that the seventy years are nearly done (v.2) and, rather than sit back, "**turned my face to the Lord… by prayer and pleas for mercy**" (v.3): the finding is that God's revealed promise is an *incentive to pray*, not an excuse to wait passively — the certainty of the outcome moves the interior *toward* God, not away. Second, *self-implicating solidarity* — the man Scripture calls "greatly loved" and against whom "no fault was found" (6:4) prays "**WE have sinned… WE have rebelled**" (v.5): the finding is that the mature interior does not exempt itself from the sin of its community but *identifies with it*, confessing "we" where it might have said "they" — intercession that stands *inside* the guilt it pleads for (as Moses, Nehemiah, and supremely the Servant, Isa 53:12); third, *mercy not merit* — "**not because of our righteousness, but because of your great mercy**" (v.18): the finding is that the healthiest prayer disclaims all leverage, resting its whole weight on *who God is* — "for your own sake" (v.19) — so that even a saint's plea is a beggar's plea; the intercessor's interior is at once *urgent* ("delay not"), *humble* ("open shame belongs to us"), and *God-grounded* ("your great mercy"))*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *promise provoking prayer (reading God's certain word and responding with pleading, not passive waiting)* (new — a gem: the promise moves the interior toward God, not away, w/ 9:2-3, Ezek 36:37, Matt 6:10); *self-implicating confession ("WE have sinned" — the blameless man identifying with his people's guilt, standing inside the sin he pleads for)* (thread — a gem: intercession from within the guilt, refusing to stand apart, w/ 9:5, Neh 1:6, Isa 53:12, Exod 32:32); *pleading mercy not merit ("not because of our righteousness but because of your great mercy… for your own sake" — the whole weight on God's character)* (thread — a gem: even a saint's plea is a beggar's plea, w/ 9:18-19, Ps 51:1, Titus 3:5); *the urgency of intercession ("O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive… delay not")* (new — w/ 9:19).
