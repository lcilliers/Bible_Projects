## ISA-fear-man-forget-maker · Isa 51:1–23 · [D, multi] — the fear of dying man vs forgetting the eternal Maker; the cup of wrath removed

**The reading.** God calls his people to *remember their origin* ("**look to the rock from which you were hewn**", v.1) and comforts Zion (v.3). To the reproached he says: "**Fear not the reproach of man, nor be dismayed at their revilings**" (v.7). Then the searching diagnosis of misplaced fear: "**I, I am he who comforts you; who are you that you are afraid of man who dies, of the son of man who is made like grass, and have forgotten the LORD, your Maker… and you fear continually all the day because of the wrath of the oppressor?**" (vv.12–13). And the mercy: "**I have taken from your hand the cup of staggering… you shall drink no more**" (v.22). The finding is **the *fear of frail, dying man* exposed as absurd — because it grows precisely where the eternal *Maker is forgotten* — and the cup of wrath *lifted* from the afflicted** *(stated)*. The operation is **the inverse relation between two objects of the interior: when God (the eternal Maker) is *forgotten*, dying man (grass) is *feared* — the two move together, so that the cure for the fear of man is the *remembering of God*; the fear of the mortal swells to fill the vacuum left by the forgotten Immortal** *(inferred: vv.12–13 draw the connection with surgical precision — "**who are you that you are afraid of man who dies**… **and have forgotten the LORD your Maker**": the finding is that the fear of man and the forgetting of God are *causally linked* — the interior fears "man who dies… made like grass" (the frail, temporary) exactly to the degree that it has "forgotten" the eternal Maker; the two are a see-saw: as the memory of God sinks, the fear of man rises to "**continually all the day**" (v.13); so the cure is not to muster courage against the oppressor but to *remember* the Maker whose forgetting created the fear — the disproportion is restored by re-sizing the objects (dying grass-man vs the God "who stretched out the heavens"); and the tenderness is that God *identifies himself as the comforter* — "**I, I am he who comforts you**" (v.12): the finding is that the antidote to fear is the remembered presence of the comforting Maker, before whom the "wrath of the oppressor" (v.13) shrinks to a passing question ("and where is the wrath of the oppressor?"); and the cup of God's own wrath, once drunk to the dregs, is *removed* (v.22) — the deeper terror (God's judgment) lifted, so the lesser terror (man's) loses its ground)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the fear of man linked to forgetting the Maker ("afraid of man who dies… and have forgotten the LORD your Maker" — the two move together)* (thread — a gem: the fear of the mortal swells where the memory of the Immortal sinks, w/ 51:12-13, 8:12-13, Matt 10:28, Pro 29:25); *re-sizing the objects (dying grass-man vs the God who stretched out the heavens — proportion restored by remembering God)* (new — a gem: the cure for fear is remembering the Maker, not mustering courage, w/ 51:13, 40:6-8,22); *"I, I am he who comforts you" (the remembered presence of the comforting Maker as the antidote to fear)* (thread — w/ 51:12, 40:1, 66:13); *the cup of wrath removed (the deeper terror lifted, so the lesser loses its ground)* (new — w/ 51:22, 40:2).
