## ISA-earth-emptied · Isa 24:1–23 · [D, multi] — earthly mirth stripped; the majesty of the LORD as the only durable joy

**The reading.** The oracle widens from nations to the whole earth, "**emptied**" and "**defiled**" because "**they have broken the everlasting covenant**" (vv.1,5). What it dwells on is the *death of gladness*: "**the mirth of the tambourines is stilled, the noise of the jubilant has ceased, the mirth of the lyre is stilled… all joy has grown dark; the gladness of the earth is banished**" (vv.8,11). All ranks are levelled alike — "**as with the people, so with the priest; as with the slave, so with his master**" (v.2). Yet a counter-voice rises: "**They lift up their voices, they sing for joy; over the majesty of the LORD they shout from the west**" (v.14). The finding is **the *emptying of earthly joy* — every merriment (wine, song, tambourine) stilled and darkened — set against a *different* joy that survives the collapse: the song sung "over the majesty of the LORD"** *(stated)*. The operation is **two joys distinguished by the catastrophe: the *earth's* gladness (rooted in wine, mirth, prosperity) is shown to be *extinguishable* — "all joy has grown dark" — while a joy rooted in "the majesty of the LORD" *rises* precisely as the other falls; the levelling of all ranks (v.2) strips the props of earthly happiness so that what a heart truly rests its gladness on is exposed** *(inferred: the passage is an anatomy of *joy* — it lists the forms of earthly mirth (tambourine, jubilant noise, lyre, wine, singing, vv.8–9) and shows each one *stilled*, culminating in "**all joy has grown dark, the gladness of the earth is banished**" (v.11); the finding is that joy attached to earthly goods is *fragile* — it "grows dark" when the goods fail; and the great contrast is v.14 — "**they sing for joy; over the majesty of the LORD they shout**": a *different* joy, whose object is God's own majesty rather than wine or prosperity, actually *increases* in the collapse (the remnant sings from the west while the earth mourns); the levelling of every rank (v.2 — no one, priest or slave, is exempt) removes the social and material scaffolding of happiness, so the question the chapter forces on the interior is: *on what is my gladness founded?* — for only the joy founded on God's majesty survives the emptying; and even the prophet is not exempt from the sorrow — "**I waste away… woe is me**" (v.16) — the durable joy is not the denial of grief but the song that rises through it)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the two joys distinguished (earthly mirth extinguishable — "all joy has grown dark" — vs joy in God's majesty that rises as the other falls)* (thread — a gem: joy's durability depends on its object, w/ Hab 3:17-18, Ps 4:7, 43:4); *the levelling that strips the props of happiness (every rank alike — the scaffolding of earthly gladness removed)* (new — the exposure of what joy truly rests on); *the song that rises through grief (the prophet "wastes away" yet the remnant sings over God's majesty)* (thread — durable joy not the denial of sorrow, w/ 24:16, 26:8-9).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the two joys distinguished; the levelling that strips the props of happiness; the song that rises through grief. Chapter 24 opens the Apocalypse by emptying the earth of its mirth so that only joy founded on God's majesty is left standing — the question of *where gladness is rooted* posed on a cosmic scale.
- **Threads:** durable vs fragile joy (→ 25:9, "let us be glad and rejoice in his salvation"; 26:19, "awake and sing"; w/ Hab 3:17-18); the majesty of the LORD as the object of joy (→ 25:1, "I will exalt you").
