## ISA-taught-tongue-flint · Isa 50:1–11 · [D, multi] — the taught ear that sustains the weary; the set face; trust in the dark (Servant Song 3)

**The reading.** The servant describes his *inner discipline of learning*: "**The Lord GOD has given me the tongue of those who are taught, that I may know how to sustain with a word him who is weary. Morning by morning he awakens; he awakens my ear to hear as those who are taught**" (v.4). This teachability is joined to obedience under suffering: "**The Lord GOD has opened my ear, and I was not rebellious; I turned not backward. I gave my back to those who strike, and my cheeks to those who pull out the beard; I hid not my face from disgrace and spitting**" (vv.5–6); "**I have set my face like a flint, and I know that I shall not be put to shame**" (v.7). It closes with a word to any in darkness: "**Let him who walks in darkness and has no light trust in the name of the LORD and rely on his God**" (v.10). The finding is **the servant's *taught ear* (learning "morning by morning" a word to *sustain the weary*), his *unrebellious obedience* under abuse, and his *flint-set face* of resolve — with the summons to the one in darkness to *trust*** *(stated)*. The operation is **an interior formed by *daily listening* ("morning by morning he awakens my ear") whose learned purpose is to *help the weary* — and whose obedience holds under violence not by insensitivity but by a *chosen resolve* ("I set my face like a flint") grounded in confidence of vindication ("he who vindicates me is near")** *(inferred: v.4 is a jewel of the interior life — the servant's eloquence is *received*, not native: "the tongue of those who are **taught**… morning by morning he **awakens my ear**": the finding is that the capacity "to sustain with a word him who is weary" comes from a *habit of daily listening* — the helping word is first a *heard* word, learned in the morning stillness before it is spoken to others; and the teachable ear is joined to a *non-rebellious will* ("I was not rebellious; I turned not backward", v.5) and an *endurance* that does not flinch from abuse ("I gave my back… hid not my face from spitting", v.6); the resolve is imaged as "**set my face like a flint**" (v.7) — not numbness but a *deliberate hardening of purpose* against the pressure to retreat, made possible by trust that "he who vindicates me is near" (v.8); the finding is that the servant's steadfastness under suffering is not stoic self-reliance but *God-grounded resolve* — the flint face rests on the nearness of the Vindicator; and the chapter turns this into a general summons (v.10): the one "who walks in darkness and has no light" is told to do exactly what the servant did — "**trust in the name of the LORD and rely on his God**": the finding is that faith is precisely trust *without present light*, leaning on God when one cannot see — the servant's own posture offered to every dark-walking interior)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the taught ear formed by daily listening ("morning by morning he awakens my ear" — the helping word first a heard word)* (new — a gem: the capacity to sustain the weary learned in daily listening, w/ 50:4, Ps 5:3, John 8:28); *the word that sustains the weary (learning aimed at helping the exhausted)* (thread — a gem: instruction whose purpose is compassion, w/ 50:4, 40:29, Gal 6:2); *the flint-set face (deliberate resolve under abuse, grounded in the nearness of the Vindicator, not stoic self-reliance)* (new — a gem: God-grounded steadfastness, w/ 50:7-8, Luke 9:51, Ezek 3:8-9); *trust in the dark ("let him who walks in darkness… trust in the name of the LORD" — faith as leaning on God without present light)* (thread — a gem: faith precisely when one cannot see, w/ 50:10, 8:17, 2 Cor 5:7).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the taught ear formed by daily listening; the word that sustains the weary; the flint-set face; trust in the dark. Chapter 50 (Servant Song 3) portrays the interior of the true servant — daily taught, unrebellious, resolute under suffering — and offers his posture (trust without light) to every dark-walking heart.
- **Threads:** the servant (← 42, 49; → 53); the taught ear / listening (← 30:21; → 55:3); trust in darkness (← 8:17; w/ 2 Cor 5:7); the set face / endurance (→ 53:7).
