## ISA-you-are-mine · Isa 43:1–28 · [D, multi] — you are mine, redeemed and precious; presence through the waters; sins blotted out

**The reading.** One of the tenderest passages in Scripture: "**Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine. When you pass through the waters, I will be with you; and through the rivers, they shall not overwhelm you; when you walk through fire you shall not be burned**" (vv.1–2). The ground is love: "**Because you are precious in my eyes, and honoured, and I love you**" (v.4). God promises novelty — "**Behold, I am doing a new thing… I will make a way in the wilderness**" (v.19) — and, despite the people's weariness of him (v.22), free pardon: "**I, I am he who blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and I will not remember your sins**" (v.25). The finding is **the interior *claimed* ("you are mine"), *named*, *loved* ("precious in my eyes… I love you"), *accompanied* through waters and fire, and *freely pardoned* ("I blot out… I will not remember")** *(stated)*. The operation is **belonging, worth, and presence conferred on the self entirely by God's word: the "fear not" rests on *possession* ("you are **mine**") and *value* ("**precious** in my eyes… I **love** you"), and the promise is not exemption from the waters and fire but God's *presence within* them ("I will be **with** you")** *(inferred: the passage answers the deepest questions of the interior — *whose am I? am I of worth? am I alone in suffering?* — with three declarations: (1) *belonging* — "**I have called you by name, you are mine**" (v.1): the finding is that identity and security rest on being *possessed and named* by God, not on self-definition; (2) *worth* — "**you are precious in my eyes, and honoured, and I love you**" (v.4): the self's value is *conferred*, spoken over it by God ("in my eyes"), not earned or self-generated — a stunning word given that the same people are "weary of me" (v.22) and sin-laden; (3) *accompanied suffering* — "**when you pass through the waters, I will be with you**" (v.2): the finding is that the promise is not that the waters and fire are removed but that God is *present in* them ("with you", "they shall not overwhelm"), so the interior's comfort in trial is *companionship*, not exemption; and over all of it, *free pardon* "**for my own sake**" (v.25) — the blotting-out grounded not in the people's merit (they have "burdened me with your sins", v.24) but in God's own character; the finding is that the whole security of the interior — belonging, worth, presence, forgiveness — is *given*, spoken over a people who deserve none of it)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *belonging conferred ("I have called you by name, you are mine" — identity and security in being possessed and named by God)* (thread — a gem: whose-am-I answered by God's claim, not self-definition, w/ 43:1, 44:5, 45:4); *worth conferred ("you are precious in my eyes, and honoured, and I love you" — value spoken over the self, not earned)* (new — a gem: the self's worth given by God's word, stunning given the people's sin, w/ 43:4, 49:15-16); *presence within suffering, not exemption from it ("when you pass through the waters, I will be with you")* (thread — a gem: comfort in trial as companionship, not the removal of the trial, w/ 43:2, 41:10, Ps 23:4); *free pardon for God's own sake ("I blot out your transgressions for my own sake… I will not remember")* (thread — forgiveness grounded in God's character, not the sinner's merit, w/ 43:25, 44:22, 48:9,11).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** belonging conferred; worth conferred; presence within suffering; free pardon for God's own sake. Chapter 43 answers the interior's deepest questions — *whose am I, am I of worth, am I alone, am I forgiven* — with God's fourfold word: you are mine, you are precious, I am with you, I blot out your sins.
- **Threads:** "fear not… you are mine" (← 41:10; → 44:2); worth/love conferred (→ 49:15-16, 54:10); presence in the waters/fire (← 41:10; w/ Ps 23:4); free pardon (→ 44:22, 55:7).
