## ISA-moab-pride-tears · Isa 15:1–16:14 · [D, multi] — Moab's pride judged; the prophet's grief for the doomed

**The reading.** The Moab oracle names the sin plainly — "**We have heard of the pride of Moab — how proud he is! — of his arrogance, his pride, and his insolence; in his idle boasting he is not right**" (16:6) — and pronounces the fall. But what is striking is the *voice* of the oracle: it weeps. "**My heart cries out for Moab**" (15:5); "**I weep with the weeping of Jazer… I drench you with my tears, O Heshbon**" (16:9); "**Therefore my inner parts moan like a lyre for Moab, and my inmost self for Kir-hareseth**" (16:11). And in the middle, a vision of the only durable throne: "**a throne will be established in steadfast love, and on it will sit in faithfulness… one who judges and seeks justice**" (16:5). The finding is **pride judged, yet held together with a *genuine grief* for the one under judgment — the prophet's "inner parts moaning like a lyre" for a doomed enemy — and a glimpse of the throne founded not on pride but on steadfast love** *(stated)*. The operation is **the compassionate interior of the messenger: the same oracle that condemns Moab's arrogance *grieves* over its ruin — judgment and pity coexisting in one heart — modelling that to announce another's fall need not harden the announcer, and setting the proud throne of Moab against the throne "in steadfast love"** *(inferred: the inner-being value of this oracle is less in Moab's pride (a familiar theme) than in the *prophet's response to it* — "**my inner parts moan like a lyre**" (16:11, me'ay — the visceral inward organs, resonating like strings): the finding is that a heart aligned with God feels *grief*, not satisfaction, over the judgment of the proud — the messenger weeps for the very nation whose arrogance he condemns; this guards against the corruption of the one who pronounces judgment (the temptation to relish another's fall); and the contrast of thrones (16:5, "**established in steadfast love… faithfulness**") is quietly decisive — Moab's throne rests on "pride… arrogance… insolence" (16:6) and collapses, while the enduring throne is founded on chesed and emeth, the opposite interior basis: love and faithfulness, not self-exaltation, are what make a throne *stand*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *grief for the judged (the prophet's "inner parts moan like a lyre" for a doomed enemy — pity, not satisfaction, at another's fall)* (new — a gem: the compassionate interior of the messenger, w/ Jer 9:1, 48:31-36, Luke 19:41); *judgment and pity held in one heart (condemning the pride yet weeping over the ruin)* (thread — the un-hardened announcer, w/ Mic 1:8, Ezek 33:11); *idle boasting "not right" (pride as a self-inflating unreality)* (thread — w/ 16:6, Hab 2:4); *the throne "established in steadfast love" vs the throne of pride (love/faithfulness, not self-exaltation, is what makes a throne stand)* (new — a gem: the enduring vs the collapsing interior basis of authority, w/ Ps 89:14, Pro 20:28).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** grief for the judged; judgment and pity in one heart; idle boasting as unreality; the throne of steadfast love. The Moab oracle's gift to the study is the *prophet's compassion* — that the announcement of judgment, rightly held, weeps rather than gloats.
- **Threads:** grief for the doomed (→ 21:3-4, 22:4, the seer's anguish); pride (← 14:13; 16:6); the throne of steadfast love (→ 9:7, 32:1, 55:3).
