## ISA-vineyard-kept · Isa 27:1–13 · [D, multi] — the tended vineyard kept without wrath; the invitation to make peace

**The reading.** Leviathan the sea-dragon is slain (v.1), and then the Apocalypse returns to the *vineyard* — but everything has changed since chapter 5. Where that vineyard yielded wild grapes and was abandoned to briers, this one is cherished: "**A pleasant vineyard, sing of it! I, the LORD, am its keeper; every moment I water it. Lest anyone punish it, I keep it night and day. I have no wrath**" (vv.2–4). And the open door: "**Or let them lay hold of my protection, let them make peace with me, let them make peace with me**" (v.5). The end is fruitfulness: "**Jacob shall take root, Israel shall blossom… and fill the whole world with fruit**" (v.6), with guilt atoned (v.9). The finding is **the vineyard *cherished, watered every moment, kept night and day, with "no wrath"* — the anxious, wrathful relation of chapter 5 resolved into tender keeping — and the *invitation to make peace* held out, twice** *(stated)*. The operation is **the same interior object (the vineyard = God's people) now met by a wholly different divine disposition — vigilant tenderness rather than disappointed anger — and the corresponding human move made explicit: *lay hold of God's protection and make peace with him*; the guarded interior invited out of hostility into reconciliation** *(inferred: the reversal of chapter 5 is the key — there the beloved "**looked for** grapes but got wild grapes" and withdrew his hedge (5:2,5); here the keeper "**every moment waters it**… **keep[s] it night and day**" and declares "**I have no wrath**" (vv.3–4): the finding is that God's settled disposition toward his own is *tender vigilance*, not the smouldering anger the guilty heart projects onto him; and the tender keeping is joined to an *appeal* — "**let them make peace with me, let them make peace with me**" (v.5, the doubling an urgent, almost pleading repetition): the finding is that the reconciliation is God-initiated and God-desired — he all but begs the vineyard to "lay hold of my protection" and end the hostility; the interior lesson is the exposure of a false picture of God (the wrathful vinekeeper waiting to punish) and its replacement with the true one (the sleepless, waterful keeper who has "no wrath" and pleads for peace) — the guilt-ridden heart, which expects anger, is met with an invitation to be reconciled)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the vineyard cherished without wrath (God's settled disposition as tender vigilance, not disappointed anger — the reversal of ch.5)* (thread — a gem: the guilty heart's false picture of a wrathful God corrected, w/ 5:1-7 inverted, 54:7-8, Ps 121:4); *God's kept-every-moment care ("I keep it night and day; every moment I water it")* (new — sleepless, continuous tending, w/ 27:3, Ps 121:3-4); *the God-initiated plea to make peace ("let them make peace with me, let them make peace with me" — the doubling as urgent appeal)* (new — a gem: reconciliation God-desired and all-but-begged, w/ 2 Cor 5:20, Isa 55:6-7); *fruitfulness as the fruit of being kept (Jacob takes root, fills the world with fruit)* (thread — the cherished vineyard now yielding, w/ 37:31, John 15:4-5).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the vineyard cherished without wrath; kept-every-moment care; the God-initiated plea to make peace; fruitfulness as the fruit of being kept. Chapter 27 closes the Apocalypse by *reversing chapter 5* — the wild, abandoned vineyard now the cherished, sleeplessly-watered one, its keeper declaring "I have no wrath" and pleading twice for peace.
- **Book-level note (batch 3, chs 24–27):** the Apocalypse's inner-being thread is the *durable interior* against the emptied cosmos — earthly joy stripped so that only joy in God's majesty survives (24); waiting consummated as death is swallowed and every tear wiped (25); *perfect peace* located in a mind *stayed* on God, trust textured as *yearning*, and self-effort exposed as "giving birth to wind" (26); the vineyard cherished "without wrath" with the plea to *make peace* (27). Together they portray the tested interior's rest: **peace, joy, and hope are the fruit of trust that waits and stays its mind on God — not of circumstance, and never of self-strain.** The great gems — *"you keep him in perfect peace whose mind is stayed on you"* (26:3), *"we have waited for him… let us be glad"* (25:9), *"he will swallow up death forever"* (25:8) — are the reward of the waiting posture the whole book commends.
- **Threads carried forward:** the stayed mind / perfect peace (→ 30:15, 32:17); waiting rewarded (→ 40:31); tender keeping vs projected wrath (→ 40:11, 54:7-10); make-peace / reconciliation (→ 55:6-7).
