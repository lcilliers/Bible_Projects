## ISA-babylon-i-am · Isa 47:1–15 · [D, multi] — self-deifying complacency ("I am, and there is no one besides me")

**The reading.** Babylon, "**mistress of kingdoms**", is brought down from her throne to sit in the dust (vv.1–5). Her sin is a *self-deifying* complacency spoken twice from the heart: "**you who sit securely, who say in your heart, 'I am, and there is no one besides me; I shall not sit as a widow'**" (v.8); "**You felt secure in your wickedness; you said, 'No one sees me'; your wisdom and your knowledge led you astray, and you said in your heart, 'I am, and there is no one besides me'**" (v.10). Her sorceries and counsels cannot save her when ruin comes "**suddenly**" (v.11). The finding is **the interior of *self-deifying security* — "**I am, and there is no one besides me**" (God's own self-designation usurped) — joined to the illusions "**no one sees me**" and "**I shall not know loss**"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the creature *arrogating God's own self-description* ("I am, and there is none besides me" — the exact phrase God uses of himself, 45:5) — the ultimate pride of the self that has made itself the centre and denies both accountability ("no one sees me") and vulnerability ("I shall not sit as a widow"); and the exposure that this self-security is *blind* ("your wisdom led you astray") and *doomed* to sudden collapse** *(inferred: the chilling thing about Babylon's boast is that it is *God's own language* stolen — "**I am, and there is no one besides me**" (ani we'afsi od, v.8,10) is nearly verbatim what the LORD says of himself throughout these chapters ("I am, and there is no other", 45:5,6,18): the finding is that the essence of the self-deifying interior is to occupy God's unique place — to say of *itself* what only God may say, "there is none besides me"; and this usurpation carries two daughter-illusions: "**no one sees me**" (v.10, the denial of accountability — imagining oneself unobserved, the practical atheism of 29:15) and "**I shall not sit as a widow or know the loss of children**" (v.8, the denial of vulnerability — the fantasy of invulnerable permanence); the finding is that self-deification always breeds these two lies — *I am unaccountable* and *I am invulnerable* — and both are shattered "in a moment, in one day" (v.9); the deepest irony is that "**your wisdom and your knowledge led you astray**" (v.10): the very faculties Babylon trusted (her fabled learning, her sorceries) *deceived* her — the self-secured interior is not only proud but *self-deceived*, its cleverness the instrument of its blindness)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *self-deification ("I am, and there is no one besides me" — the creature usurping God's own self-designation)* (thread — a gem: the self occupying God's unique place, saying of itself what only God may say, w/ 47:8,10, 45:5, 14:14, Ezek 28:2); *the illusion of unaccountability ("no one sees me" — imagining oneself unobserved)* (thread — practical atheism, w/ 47:10, 29:15, Ps 10:11); *the illusion of invulnerability ("I shall not sit as a widow… know the loss of children" — the fantasy of permanent security)* (new — a gem: self-deification breeds the denial of vulnerability, w/ 47:8, Rev 18:7); *cleverness as the instrument of blindness ("your wisdom and knowledge led you astray")* (thread — the self-secured interior self-deceived by its own faculties, w/ 47:10, 5:21, 44:20).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** self-deification; the illusion of unaccountability; the illusion of invulnerability; cleverness as the instrument of blindness. Chapter 47 anatomises the god-usurping self — Babylon saying "I am, and there is none besides me", denying that she is seen or that she can fall — and shows the two lies (unaccountable, invulnerable) shattered in a day.
- **Threads:** self-deification / usurping God's place (← 14:13-14; 47:8,10; the exact inverse of God's "I am he", 43:10-13, 45:5); practical atheism, "no one sees" (← 29:15); self-deceiving wisdom (← 5:21, 44:20).
