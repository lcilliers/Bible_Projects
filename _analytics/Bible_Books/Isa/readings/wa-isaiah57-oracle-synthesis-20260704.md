## ISA-contrite-revived · Isa 57:1–21 · [D, multi] — the high God who dwells with the contrite; healing the backslider; peace to far and near

**The reading.** The chapter exposes a restless, adulterous idolatry — "**you were wearied with the length of your way, but you did not say, 'It is hopeless'**" (v.10) — and asks, "**Whom did you dread and fear, so that you lied, and did not remember me, did not lay it to heart?**" (v.11). Then comes one of the two summits of the book: "**Thus says the One who is high and lifted up, who inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy: 'I dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit, to revive the spirit of the lowly, and to revive the heart of the contrite'**" (v.15). And the mercy toward the very backslider: "**I have seen his ways, but I will heal him; I will lead him and restore comfort to him… Peace, peace, to the far and to the near… and I will heal him**" (vv.18–19); "**There is no peace, says my God, for the wicked**" (v.21). The finding is **the *high and holy* God who *also dwells with the contrite and lowly* — to *revive* them — and who heals even the backslider he had struck, speaking "peace, peace, to the far and near"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the astonishing conjunction of God's *transcendence* ("high and lifted up… inhabits eternity") with his *nearness to the lowly interior* — the same God whose height nothing can reach *chooses to dwell* with "a contrite and lowly spirit"; the disposition that draws God near is not achievement but *contrition* — a spirit broken and low — and his action toward it is *revival*** *(inferred: v.15 is the gem — the God "high and lifted up, inhabiting eternity" (the very language of unapproachable majesty, echoing the throne-vision of 6:1) says he dwells in *two* places: "**the high and holy place, AND ALSO with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit**"; the finding is that the same infinite God who is utterly above all is *most intimately present* to the *lowest* interior — not the proud, the accomplished, or the self-secure, but the "**contrite**" (dakka — crushed, broken) and "**lowly**" (shephal — brought low); the disposition God draws near to is precisely the *broken* one; and his purpose there is "**to revive**" (twice, v.15) — to breathe life back into the crushed spirit and heart; the finding is that contrition is not a barrier to God but the *doorway* to his nearness — the interior that is broken and low is the one the high God chooses to inhabit and revive; and even the backslider ("he went on backsliding in the way of his own heart", v.17) is met with "**I will heal him**" (v.18) and "**peace, peace**" (v.19) — the healing reaching both "far and near"; the only interior excluded is the "wicked" who, like a "tossing sea" (v.20), *cannot be quiet* — the un-contrite restlessness that will not be broken and low, and so cannot receive the peace)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the high God dwelling with the contrite ("high and lifted up… and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit")* (thread — a gem, one of the book's two summits: transcendence joined to nearness to the broken, w/ 57:15, 66:2, Ps 34:18, 51:17); *contrition as the doorway, not the barrier (the crushed/lowly spirit is the one God draws near to revive)* (new — a gem: brokenness the condition of God's nearness, not an obstacle, w/ 57:15, Matt 5:3-4); *healing the very backslider ("I have seen his ways, but I will heal him… peace, peace to far and near")* (thread — mercy toward the one God struck, w/ 57:18-19, 19:22, Hos 14:4); *the restless wicked who cannot be quiet ("like the tossing sea… no peace" — the un-contrite restlessness that cannot receive peace)* (thread — w/ 57:20-21, 48:22).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the high God dwelling with the contrite; contrition as the doorway; healing the backslider; the restless wicked. Chapter 57 holds the first of the book's two great "contrite" texts — the infinite God most intimately present to the *broken and low* interior, to revive it, while the un-contrite restlessness alone stays without peace.
- **Threads:** the contrite/lowly spirit God dwells with (→ 66:2, the book's other summit; ← 6:5, the undone self); healing/peace (← 53:5, 48:18; → 61:1-3); the restless wicked without peace (← 48:22, 57:21).
