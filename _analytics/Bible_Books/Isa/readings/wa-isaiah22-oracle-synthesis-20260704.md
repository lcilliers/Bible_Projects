## ISA-valley-vision · Isa 22:1–14 · [D, multi] — trusting the defences not the Maker; the refusal to mourn

**The reading.** Jerusalem, under threat, throws itself into feverish self-defence: "**you looked to the weapons… you saw that the breaches of the city of David were many… you collected the waters… you counted the houses… you broke down the houses to fortify the wall**" (vv.8–10) — a flurry of competent activity. And the verdict: "**But you did not look to him who did it, or see him who planned it long ago**" (v.11). When God "**called for weeping and mourning**" (v.12), the response was the opposite: "**and behold, joy and gladness… 'Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die'**" (v.13). The finding is **frantic *self*-reliance ("you looked to the weapons… but you did not look to him who did it") joined to a *refusal to mourn* — the summons to repentant grief answered by fatalistic revelry** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that, faced with crisis, *looks everywhere but to God* — busying itself with defences and provisions while never lifting its eyes to "him who planned it long ago" — and then, when called to the grief that would turn it, chooses the anaesthesia of "eat and drink, for tomorrow we die"** *(inferred: the contrast in vv.8–11 is exact and devastating — an entire list of *looking* verbs ("you **looked** to the weapons… you **saw** the breaches… you **collected**… **counted**… **broke down**") culminating in the one look never taken: "**you did not look to him who did it**" (v.11); the finding is that competent self-reliance can be a way of *not* looking to God — the busyness of securing oneself substitutes for the single upward glance; and vv.12–13 name the deeper sin — the *refusal to mourn*: God "called for weeping" (the grief that precedes repentance), and the city answered with "**joy and gladness… let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die**" — the choice of *numbing pleasure over honest sorrow*, a fatalism that would rather feast than face itself; the finding is that the un-mourning heart, offered the grief that could save it, prefers the anaesthetic of revelry — and this is the one iniquity that "**will not be atoned for you until you die**" (v.14), the hardness that will not grieve)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *looking everywhere but to God (frantic self-defence that never lifts its eyes to "him who did it")* (thread — a gem: competent self-reliance as a way of not looking to God, w/ 17:7, 31:1, 22:11); *the refusal to mourn (the summons to repentant grief answered by fatalistic revelry)* (new — a gem: numbing pleasure preferred to honest sorrow, w/ 22:12-13, Amos 6:3-6, Luke 17:27-28); *"eat and drink, for tomorrow we die" (fatalism as anaesthesia against self-examination)* (new — despair masked as festivity, w/ Ecc 8:15 contrast, 1 Cor 15:32); *the un-grieving hardness that will not be atoned (the heart that will not weep)* (thread — w/ 22:14, 6:9-10).

## ISA-shebna · Isa 22:15–25 · [D, multi] — self-monumentalising pride; the peg that gives way

**The reading.** A named individual: Shebna the steward, indicted not for oppression but for *self-monumentalising* — "**you have cut out here a tomb for yourself… you who cut out a tomb on the height and carve a dwelling for yourself in the rock**" (v.16). He will be "**hurled away violently… whirled around and around, and thrown like a ball into a wide land**" (vv.17–18). Eliakim replaces him — "**a peg in a secure place… a throne of honour to his father's house**" (v.23) — yet even that secure peg is not final: "**the peg… will give way, and it will be cut down and fall**" (v.25). The finding is **pride expressed as *building one's own monument* (a lofty tomb, a rock-cut dwelling for the self) — displaced and hurled away — and even the honoured replacement shown to be a "peg" that will finally "give way": no human security is ultimate** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self that spends itself *securing its own name* (carving a high tomb, a permanent dwelling) exposed as building on nothing — hurled "like a ball" — and the sober coda that even the best human "peg" gives way, so that *no* creaturely support, however honoured, can bear ultimate weight** *(inferred: Shebna's particular vanity is *self-perpetuation* — "a tomb **for yourself**… a dwelling **for yourself**" carved "on the height" (v.16): the finding is that pride often takes the form of *monument-building*, the drive to secure one's own name and permanence (the same instinct as Babel, Gen 11:4) — and it is precisely this self-securing that God "hurls away like a ball", the carefully carved permanence proving weightless; the deeper note is the coda (v.25) — even Eliakim, the *good* replacement, the "**peg in a secure place**" on whom "the whole honour of his father's house" hangs, will "**give way… and fall**": the finding is that *every* human support, even the honourable and God-appointed, is finally a peg that gives way — the reading gently refuses to let even the good official become the ultimate security; the weight the interior would hang on any creaturely peg is more than a peg can bear, and only God is the "secure place" that does not give way)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *self-monumentalising pride (carving one's own high tomb — the drive to secure one's name and permanence)* (thread — a gem: pride as monument-building, w/ Gen 11:4, 2 Sam 18:18, Ps 49:11); *self-securing exposed as weightless (the carved permanence hurled "like a ball")* (new — the self-secured name proving to have no substance); *even the honoured peg gives way (no creaturely support, however good, is the ultimate security)* (new — a gem: every human peg finally fails; only God is the secure place, w/ 22:25, Ps 146:3-5).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** looking everywhere but to God; the refusal to mourn; fatalistic anaesthesia; self-monumentalising pride; the peg that gives way. Chapter 22 pairs two forms of *misplaced security* — the city trusting its defences (and refusing to grieve) and the official building his own monument — both answered by God: neither the fortified wall nor the carved tomb nor even the honoured peg can bear the weight only God can hold.
- **Threads:** looking to self/defences not God (← 17:7; → 31:1); refusal to mourn (→ 57:1, "no one lays it to heart"); self-monumentalising pride (← 14:13; 23:9); no creaturely security final (→ 40:6-8, "all flesh is grass").
