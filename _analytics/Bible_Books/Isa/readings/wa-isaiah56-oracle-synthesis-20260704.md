## ISA-outcast-included · Isa 56:1–12 · [D, multi] — the outsider gathered in; the self-serving blind watchmen

**The reading.** Two interiors haunted by exclusion are reassured. The foreigner fears "**The LORD will surely separate me from his people**"; the eunuch says "**Behold, I am a dry tree**" (v.3). To them God promises "**a monument and a name better than sons and daughters… an everlasting name that shall not be cut off**" (v.5), and declares, "**my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples**" (v.7). Against this welcome stand the leaders: "**His watchmen are blind… they have all turned to their own way, each to his own gain**" (vv.10–11). The finding is **the *self-excluding* interior (the outsider who assumes he is barred, the "dry tree" who feels fruitless) *welcomed and named* by God — set against *self-serving* shepherds who gather only "gain" for themselves** *(stated)*. The operation is **the anxieties of the marginal ("I will be separated"; "I am a dry tree") answered by *positive inclusion* — a name, a place, acceptance — while the insiders who should have gathered them are exposed as *turned to their own gain*; belonging is conferred by God on the very ones who feared exclusion** *(inferred: the inner-being interest is the *self-exclusion* of vv.3–5 — the foreigner and eunuch have already written themselves off ("the LORD will surely separate me… I am a dry tree", a barren, name-less future); the finding is that God *contradicts the outsider's own verdict on himself* — precisely to the eunuch (who can have no children, no "name" through offspring) he promises "**a name better than sons and daughters**" (v.5), and to the foreigner (who assumes the boundary excludes him) he opens "**a house of prayer for all peoples**" (v.7): the finding is that the interior which has resigned itself to exclusion is *sought out* and given a belonging it did not expect — God's welcome reaches past the self-disqualifying fear; and the dark foil (vv.10–11) is the *insider* failure — the watchmen "turned to their own way, each to his own gain": the finding is that those already inside can be *self-serving* while the anxious outsider is the one truly gathered — proximity is no guarantee of a right heart, and the excluded-feeling may be nearer than the complacent shepherd)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the self-excluding interior welcomed ("the LORD will surely separate me… I am a dry tree" contradicted by God's "a name better than sons and daughters")* (thread — a gem: God contradicting the outsider's verdict on himself, w/ 56:3-7, 66:2, Acts 8:27-39); *belonging conferred past the self-disqualifying fear (the marginal sought out and named)* (new — the interior resigned to exclusion given an unexpected place, w/ 56:5); *the self-serving insider vs the gathered outsider ("each to his own gain" — proximity no guarantee of a right heart)* (thread — w/ 56:10-11, 57:1).
