## ISA-turn-be-saved · Isa 45:1–25 · [D, multi] — the clay disputing its Maker; the God who hides himself; turn to me and be saved

**The reading.** God names a pagan king, Cyrus, as his instrument "**though you do not know me**" (v.4), and rebukes any who would question the arrangement: "**Woe to him who strives with him who formed him, a pot among earthen pots! Does the clay say to him who forms it, 'What are you making?'**" (v.9). A worshipper confesses the mystery: "**Truly, you are a God who hides himself, O God of Israel, the Saviour**" (v.15). And the chapter opens into the widest invitation of the book: "**Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth! For I am God, and there is no other**" (v.22); "**To me every knee shall bow**" (v.23). The finding is **the *creature's presumption* in disputing its Maker's ways (the clay questioning the potter) rebuked — with the acknowledgement that God's ways are often *hidden* — and the universal summons, "**turn to me and be saved**"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior tempted to *arraign God* over his methods (using a foreigner, hiding his purposes) recalled to its creaturely place — "shall the clay say to the potter, what are you making?" — while being assured the hiddenness is not absence ("a God who hides himself, the *Saviour*"); and the corrective to all disputing is the simple *turn*: "turn to me and be saved"** *(inferred: the clay/potter woe (v.9) targets the interior disposition that *argues with God's providence* — objecting to how God runs things (here, saving through a pagan king); the finding is that the creature's proper posture is not to *interrogate* its Maker's design ("what are you making? your work has no handles?") but to trust the Potter's competence — the same lesson as 29:16, now applied to God's *ways* not just his *making*; and v.15 gives the mature interior's confession — "**you are a God who hides himself**" (el mistater): the finding is that faith must reckon with a God whose purposes are often *concealed* (working through Cyrus, through delay, through the un-obvious), and that this hiddenness is held together with salvation ("a God who hides himself, the Saviour") — hiddenness is not abandonment; and the resolution of all the disputing and hiding is not further explanation but *invitation* — "**turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth**" (v.22): the finding is that the creature's questions are ultimately answered by a *summons to turn* — the widest, simplest call, in which the presuming clay and the whole watching earth are alike invited to stop arguing and *turn*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the clay disputing its Maker's ways ("shall the clay say to the potter, what are you making?" — arraigning God over his providence)* (thread — a gem: the creature interrogating God's design vs trusting the Potter, w/ 45:9, 29:16, 64:8, Rom 9:20-21); *the God who hides himself ("truly you are a God who hides himself… the Saviour" — hiddenness held together with salvation, not absence)* (new — a gem: faith reckoning with concealed purposes, w/ 45:15, 8:17, Deut 29:29); *turn and be saved ("turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth" — the creature's questions answered by a summons to turn)* (thread — a gem: the widest, simplest call, w/ 45:22, 55:1-7); *every knee shall bow (the universal claim of the one God)* (thread — w/ 45:23, Phil 2:10).
