## ISA-refined-affliction · Isa 48:1–22 · [D, multi] — the obstinate heart; refining in the furnace; the peace that could have been a river

**The reading.** God addresses a people religious "**but not in truth or right**" (v.1), diagnosing an *obstinacy* he foreknew: "**Because I know that you are obstinate, and your neck is an iron sinew and your forehead brass**" (v.4). He has dealt with them by refining, not destroying: "**Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have tried you in the furnace of affliction. For my own sake, for my own sake, I do it**" (vv.10–11). Then the tender teaching and the great "if only": "**I am the LORD your God, who teaches you to profit, who leads you in the way you should go. Oh that you had paid attention to my commandments! Then your peace would have been like a river, and your righteousness like the waves of the sea**" (vv.17–18). It closes: "**There is no peace, says the LORD, for the wicked**" (v.22). The finding is **an *obstinate* interior ("iron sinew" neck) *refined in the furnace of affliction* — and God's grieved "**if only you had listened, your peace would have been like a river**"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the stubborn self met by God in two ways: *refined* (affliction as the furnace that purifies rather than the fire that destroys) and *taught* ("who teaches you to profit, leads you in the way") — with the aching disclosure that the *peace* the interior lacks was fully *available* and forfeited only by inattention ("oh that you had paid attention… then your peace would have been like a river")** *(inferred: two inner-being notes stand out — (1) *affliction as refining* — "**I have refined you… in the furnace of affliction**" (v.10): the finding is that God's severity toward the obstinate is *purgative*, aimed at removing dross, not annihilating (as 1:25, 48:10) — suffering reframed (as in Hezekiah's "for my welfare", 38:17) as the furnace in which the stubborn self is purified; (2) the *forfeited peace* — "**oh that you had paid attention to my commandments! then your PEACE would have been like a RIVER**" (v.18): the finding is that the deep peace the interior craves was *not withheld* by God but *forfeited* by the self's inattention — the tragedy is not that peace was unavailable but that it was *there for the listening* and missed; "like a river… like the waves of the sea" images a peace that is *abundant and continuous* (not a trickle but a river, not occasional but tidal), and it was lost only because the "iron sinew" neck would not turn to heed; and the closing "**no peace for the wicked**" (v.22) seals the causal link — peace is not an arbitrary reward but the *organic fruit* of heeding God, so the wicked (the un-heeding) simply cannot have it; the finding is that the obstinate interior's peacelessness is self-inflicted, and the door (attention to God's leading) stands open)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the obstinate interior ("your neck is an iron sinew and your forehead brass" — foreknown stubbornness)* (thread — the hard, un-turning heart, w/ 48:4, 46:12, Exod 32:9); *affliction as refining, not destroying ("I have tried you in the furnace of affliction" — suffering purgative, aimed at the dross)* (thread — a gem: severity reframed as the furnace that purifies, w/ 48:10, 1:25, 38:17, Mal 3:3, 1 Pet 1:7); *the forfeited peace ("oh that you had paid attention… then your peace would have been like a river" — the peace craved was available and lost by inattention)* (new — a gem: peace not withheld by God but forfeited by the self, abundant and continuous, w/ 48:18, 26:3, 32:17); *peace as the organic fruit of heeding ("no peace for the wicked" — the un-heeding simply cannot have it)* (thread — w/ 48:22, 57:21, 32:17).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the obstinate interior; affliction as refining; the forfeited peace; peace as the organic fruit of heeding. Chapter 48 closes batch 6 with a poignant "if only" — the stubborn self, refined in affliction, told that the river-like peace it lacks was fully available and lost only by not listening.
- **Book-level note (batch 6, chs 40–48):** Part II turns the whole book toward *comfort and the exposure of idols*. Its inner-being centre is the consoling of the frail, fearful, discouraged interior: *comfort spoken to the heart* and the God who *carries* the weak (40:1-11); *waiting* that renews the faint (40:31); the sevenfold "**fear not, I am with you… I hold your right hand**" spoken to the "worm" (41); the gentle Servant who *will not break the bruised reed* (42:1-9) beside the *blind* servant who sees but does not perceive (42:18-25); the tender *"you are mine… precious… I am with you through the waters… I blot out your sins"* (43); the Spirit *poured on the thirsty* and the *self-deceived idolater feeding on ashes* (44); the clay recalled from disputing its Maker and summoned to *"turn and be saved"* (45); the *God who carries* vs the god that must be carried (46); Babylon's self-deifying *"I am, and there is none besides me"* (47); and the obstinate self *refined in affliction*, grieved over with *"your peace could have been a river"* (48). The through-line: **the frail, fearful, sin-laden interior is comforted, carried, and claimed by the living God — while every self-made or self-deifying substitute (idol, boast, obstinacy) is exposed as a burden that cannot save.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the Servant (→ 49:1-6, 50:4-9, 53); "fear not" / comfort (→ 49:13-15, 51:12, 54:10); waiting/being carried (→ 49:23, 63:9); turn/return and be saved (→ 55:1-7); peace as the fruit of heeding (→ 54:10,13, 57:19-21).
