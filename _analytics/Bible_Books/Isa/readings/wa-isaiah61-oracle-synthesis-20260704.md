## ISA-good-news-poor · Isa 61:1–11 · [D, multi] — the Spirit-anointed herald; ashes to beauty; the garments of salvation

**The reading.** The Spirit-anointed servant announces his commission to the wounded interior: "**The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me to bring good news to the poor; he has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives… to comfort all who mourn**" (vv.1–2). The heart of it is a series of *exchanges*: "**to give them a beautiful headdress instead of ashes, the oil of gladness instead of mourning, the garment of praise instead of a faint spirit**" (v.3). And the response: "**I will greatly rejoice in the LORD; my soul shall exult in my God, for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation… the robe of righteousness**" (v.10). The finding is **the *poor, brokenhearted, mourning, faint-spirited* interior met by a Spirit-anointed herald who *exchanges* its griefs for gladness — "ashes" for "a beautiful headdress", "mourning" for "the oil of gladness", "a faint spirit" for "the garment of praise"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the wounded interior addressed at each of its specific ailments (poverty, broken-heartedness, captivity, mourning, faintness) and given not mere relief but *substitution* — each grief *replaced* by its opposite good, so that the transformation is a re-clothing of the self from within (ashes→beauty, mourning→gladness, faintness→praise)** *(inferred: the commission (vv.1–3) is remarkable for how *specifically* it targets the interior's wounds — "**good news to the POOR… bind up the BROKENHEARTED… liberty to the CAPTIVES… comfort all who MOURN**": the finding is that God's anointed does not address people in the abstract but reaches each precise condition of the suffering interior; and the mechanism is *exchange*, imaged as re-clothing — "instead of ashes… instead of mourning… instead of a faint spirit" (v.3): the finding is that the gospel to the wounded self is not the numbing of grief but its *replacement* — the ashes (the sign of mourning worn on the head) taken off and a "beautiful headdress" put on, the "faint spirit" (the drooping, dimmed interior) exchanged for "the garment of praise"; the self is *re-dressed* in the opposite of what afflicted it; and the recipient's response completes the circuit — "**my soul shall exult… he has clothed me with the garments of salvation**" (v.10): the finding is that the interior re-clothed by God *exults*, its new clothing (salvation, righteousness) as gladly worn as a bridegroom's garland or a bride's jewels — the mourning self becomes the rejoicing one, dressed by God in what it could never make for itself)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the wounded interior addressed at each ailment ("good news to the poor… bind up the brokenhearted… comfort all who mourn" — grief met specifically, not abstractly)* (thread — a gem: God's anointed reaching each precise condition, w/ 61:1-2, 42:3, Luke 4:18); *griefs exchanged, not merely relieved ("beauty instead of ashes, oil of gladness instead of mourning, garment of praise instead of a faint spirit")* (new — a gem: the gospel as substitution/re-clothing, each grief replaced by its opposite, w/ 61:3); *the self re-clothed by God ("he has clothed me with the garments of salvation… the robe of righteousness")* (thread — a gem: the interior dressed in what it could not make for itself, w/ 61:10, 3:24 inverted, 64:6, Luke 15:22); *the exulting response ("my soul shall exult in my God" — the re-clothed interior rejoices)* (thread — w/ 61:10, 60:5).
