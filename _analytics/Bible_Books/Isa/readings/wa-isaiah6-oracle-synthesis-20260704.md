## ISA-call-vision · Isa 6:1–13 · [D, multi] — the self undone by holiness, cleansed, and freely offered

**The reading.** The prophet sees "**the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up**" (v.1), the seraphim crying "**Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory**" (v.3). The immediate interior effect is *disintegration*: "**Woe is me! For I am lost; for I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips; for my eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts!**" (v.5). A seraph brings a burning coal from the altar and touches his mouth: "**your guilt is taken away, and your sin atoned for**" (v.7). Then the overheard question and the free answer: "**Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then I said, Here I am! Send me**" (v.8). The commission that follows is dark — "**make the heart of this people dull, and their ears heavy, and blind their eyes, lest they… turn and be healed**" (v.10) — and its term is "**until cities lie waste**… the holy seed is its stump" (vv.11–13). The finding is **the encounter with *holiness* that first *undoes* the self (the sight of the Holy makes the prophet aware he is "lost", unclean), then *cleanses* it (the coal), then *sends* it (the volunteered "here am I")** *(stated)*. The operation is **holiness experienced as *exposure* — proximity to the utterly Holy makes the self's uncleanness unbearable to itself ("woe is me, I am undone"), and only after atonement (unasked, given) is the self free to *offer itself*; the sequence is fixed: undoing → cleansing → sending** *(inferred: the inner-being architecture of ch.6 is the paradigm of encounter with the Holy — (a) the vision of holiness produces not elation but *self-dissolution*: "**woe is me! for I am lost**" (nidmeti — I am undone/silenced/destroyed), and the self-diagnosis is specific — "**unclean lips**", the very organ the prophet will need (the mouth of the messenger is what he knows to be defiled); the finding is that true sight of God's holiness first *ruins* one's self-estimate — holiness is felt as the measure against which the self is found wanting; (b) the cleansing is *initiated by heaven, not achieved by the prophet* — the seraph flies, the coal is brought, "your guilt **is taken away**" (passive, done *to* him) — atonement precedes commission; (c) only the cleansed self can *volunteer* — "**here am I! send me**" (v.8) is the freest sentence in the book, and it is possible only because the unclean lips have been touched; the interior progression undone→cleansed→sent is the pattern of every true call; and note the sober coda — the commission is to a *hardening* ministry (v.10, faithfulness that will not "succeed" by turning hearts), so the cleansed, willing self is sent not to triumph but to speak into deafness, its only hope the "**holy seed**" in the stump (v.13) — the willing interior must hold obedience apart from visible fruit)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *holiness as self-undoing (the sight of the Holy ruins the self's estimate — "woe is me, I am lost")* (new — a gem: proximity to holiness felt as exposure, not elation, w/ Job 42:5-6, Luke 5:8); *the diagnosis of "unclean lips" (the self indicts the very faculty it will need)* (new — self-knowledge naming the specific defilement); *atonement given, not achieved (the coal brought by heaven; guilt "taken away" done to him)* (thread — a gem: cleansing precedes and enables commission, w/ 1:18, 44:22); *the free "here am I, send me" (only the cleansed self can volunteer)* (new — a gem: willing self-offering as the fruit of atonement); *obedience sent into deafness (faithfulness apart from visible fruit; the holy seed in the stump)* (new — the willing interior holding obedience without success, w/ 53:1, hope in the remnant).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** holiness as self-undoing; the diagnosis of unclean lips; atonement given not achieved; the free "here am I, send me"; obedience sent into deafness. Chapter 6 is the inner-being summit of the batch — the paradigm of encounter with the Holy: the self is *ruined, cleansed, and sent*, in that order, and the willing "here am I" is shown to be the *fruit* of being touched, not a native resource of the self.
- **Threads:** cleansing/atonement (← 1:18; → 44:22, 53:5); the undone self before God (w/ Job 42; → Isa 66:2, the contrite); willing self-offering (→ 50:4-9, the servant's taught ear and set face); obedience without visible fruit (→ 49:4, "I have laboured in vain… yet my reward is with the LORD").
