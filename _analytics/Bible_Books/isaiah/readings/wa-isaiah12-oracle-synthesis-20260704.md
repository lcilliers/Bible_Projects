## ISA-salvation-song · Isa 12:1–6 · [D, multi] — trust that dissolves fear; joy drawing from the wells of salvation

**The reading.** The whole movement of chs 1–11 resolves into a song. First, anger turned to comfort: "**I will give thanks to you, O LORD, for though you were angry with me, your anger turned away, that you might comfort me**" (v.1). Then the confession that undoes the shaking heart of chapter 7: "**Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and will not be afraid; for the LORD God is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation**" (v.2). Then joy at the source: "**With joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation**" (v.3), and praise that overflows outward — "**make known his deeds among the peoples… shout, and sing for joy, O inhabitant of Zion**" (vv.4–6). The finding is **the redeemed interior at rest — *trust that has displaced fear* ("I will trust and not be afraid"), God himself become "**strength and song**", and a *joy that draws* from salvation as from a well and spills over into praise** *(stated)*. The operation is **the terminus of the fear/faith axis of chs 7–11: the heart that once "shook like trees before the wind" (7:2) now says "**I will trust, and will not be afraid**" (v.2) — fear resolved *by trust*, not by the removal of danger; and the interior's relation to salvation is figured as *drawing water* (an ongoing, joyful *appropriation*), issuing in outward praise** *(inferred: the song is deliberately constructed from the book's own earlier threads — "**your anger turned away**" (v.1) answers the tolling refrain of 9:12–10:4 ("his anger has not turned away") — what would not turn while the heart was hard *turns* once the heart gives thanks; "**I will trust, and will not be afraid**" (v.2) is the *achieved* state of what 7:9 demanded ("if you are not firm in faith…") and 8:12–13 taught ("let him be your fear") — the shaking heart has become the trusting heart; and "**the LORD is my strength**" retires the theme of borrowed-vs-owned strength (10:13,15) into worship — strength confessed as *his*, and therefore *mine* as gift; the deepest inner-being image is v.3 — "**with joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation**": salvation is not a single past rescue but a *well* the interior keeps returning to, drawing gladness repeatedly (the ongoing appropriation of grace); and the movement ends *outward* (vv.4–6, "make known… among the peoples") — the healed interior does not hoard its joy but publishes it: the private trust becomes public song)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *trust displacing fear ("I will trust, and will not be afraid" — the shaking heart become the trusting heart)* (thread — a gem: the terminus of the fear/faith axis; fear resolved by trust, not by removed danger, w/ 7:2,9, 8:12-13, 26:3); *anger turned away once the heart gives thanks (the un-turning refrain of 9-10 reversed)* (new — a gem: what would not turn while the heart was hard turns at thanksgiving); *God himself as strength and song (strength confessed as his, therefore mine as gift)* (thread — w/ 10:13-15 retired into worship, Ps 118:14, Exod 15:2); *joy drawn as from a well (salvation as an ongoing appropriation, not a single past rescue)* (new — a gem: grace repeatedly drawn with gladness, w/ 55:1, John 4:14); *private trust becoming public song (the healed interior publishes its joy)* (thread — praise overflowing outward, w/ 12:4-6).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** trust displacing fear; anger turned away at thanksgiving; God as strength and song; joy drawn as from a well; private trust becoming public song. Chapter 12 is the *doxological terminus* of batch 1 — the fear/faith axis of chs 7–11 resolved into a heart that trusts and is unafraid, and the whole indictment-and-hope movement of chs 1–11 gathered into a song drawn joyfully from the wells of salvation.
- **Book-level note (chs 1–12):** Part I opens by staging the whole drama of the interior — the *diseased, unknowing heart* offered cleansing (1), the *vertical axis* of teachable ascent vs. self-exalting pride (2), sin *worn on the face* and vanity *stripped and cleansed* (3–4), the *anatomy of autonomy* in the six woes (5), the paradigm *encounter with holiness* that undoes-cleanses-sends (6), the *fear/faith* crisis where "believe or you have no footing" (7) and fear is *relocated* onto God (8), *given light and joy* against the *heart that will not turn* (9–10:4), the *axe that boasts over the hand* answered by the remnant that *leans in truth* (10), the *Spirit-rested ideal self* who *delights in the fear of the LORD* (11), and finally the *trusting, unafraid, joy-drawing* heart (12). The controlling inner-being movement is **from a shaking, self-exalting, unturning interior to a trusting, God-fearing, joy-drawing one** — and the hinge in every case is *reception* (light seen, coal brought, child given, wells drawn) rather than self-achievement.
- **Threads carried forward:** trust vs fear (→ 26:3, 30:15, 40:31, 43:1); the fear of the LORD as delight (→ 33:6); joy/water drawn from salvation (→ 35:10, 55:1); the contrite/undone self before holiness (→ 57:15, 66:2); leaning/reliance relocated (→ 31:1, 50:10).
