## ISA-arise-shine · Isa 60:1–22 · [D, multi] — the risen light; the heart that thrills; mourning ended

**The reading.** The command that answers the darkness: "**Arise, shine, for your light has come, and the glory of the LORD has risen upon you. For behold, darkness shall cover the earth… but the LORD will arise upon you, and his glory will be seen upon you**" (vv.1–2). The effect on the interior is exultation: "**Then you shall see and be radiant; your heart shall thrill and exult**" (v.5). And the consummation: "**The sun shall be no more your light by day… but the LORD will be your everlasting light, and your God will be your glory. Your sun shall no more go down… for the LORD will be your everlasting light, and your days of mourning shall be ended**" (vv.19–20). The finding is **the darkened self commanded to "**arise, shine**" because a *light has come to it* — its "**heart thrills and exults**" — and its "**days of mourning**" are *ended* by God himself becoming its "everlasting light"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior told to *reflect* a light it did not generate ("arise, shine, for your light HAS COME") — the shining is response, not self-illumination — and the deepest note is that God *himself* becomes the light source ("the LORD will be your everlasting light"), so that the mourning ends not by circumstance improving but by the interior being permanently lit from a source that "shall no more go down"** *(inferred: "**arise, shine**" (v.1) is imperative, but the ground is *indicative* — "**for your light HAS COME**": the finding is that the summons to radiance is possible only because a light has *already arrived* — the self does not manufacture its glow but *reflects* the "glory of the LORD [that] has risen upon you"; the shining is derivative, a moon to God's sun; and the interior effect is visceral joy — "**your heart shall thrill and exult**" (v.5, the heart *trembling* with gladness, pachad here of joyful awe not fear); and the climax relocates the light *permanently* into God himself — "**the LORD will be your everlasting light… your sun shall no more go down**" (vv.19–20): the finding is that the ending of "days of mourning" is not the temporary lift of a mood but the *permanent illumination* of the interior by an unsetting source — grief ends because the light that has come never goes down; the darkened, mourning self of the whole book (8:22, 9:2, 59:9-10) is here flooded and kept flooded)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *shining as reflection, not self-illumination ("arise, shine, for your light HAS COME" — the summons to radiance grounded in a light already arrived)* (thread — a gem: the self reflects a light it did not generate, w/ 60:1-2, 2 Cor 3:18, Matt 5:14-16); *the heart that thrills ("you shall see and be radiant; your heart shall thrill and exult" — visceral joy at the risen light)* (new — the interior trembling with gladness, w/ 60:5); *God himself as the everlasting light ("the LORD will be your everlasting light… your sun shall no more go down")* (thread — a gem: the light source relocated permanently into God, w/ 60:19-20, Rev 21:23); *mourning ended by an unsetting light ("your days of mourning shall be ended" — grief ended not by circumstance but by permanent illumination)* (thread — w/ 60:20, 25:8, 35:10, 65:19).
