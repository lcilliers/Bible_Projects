## ISA-come-thirst-seek · Isa 55:1–13 · [D, multi] — the free feast for the thirsty; the labour that does not satisfy; seek, return, and the higher thoughts

**The reading.** The great invitation: "**Come, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters; and he who has no money, come, buy and eat! Come, buy wine and milk without money and without price**" (v.1). Then the diagnosis of misspent hunger: "**Why do you spend your money for that which is not bread, and your labour for that which does not satisfy? Listen diligently to me, and eat what is good… Incline your ear, and come to me; hear, that your soul may live**" (vv.2–3). The summons to turn: "**Seek the LORD while he may be found; call upon him while he is near; let the wicked forsake his way… let him return to the LORD… for he will abundantly pardon**" (vv.6–7). And the gulf between minds: "**For my thoughts are not your thoughts… as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways**" (vv.8–9). The word "**shall not return to me empty**" (v.11), and the end is joy: "**you shall go out in joy and be led forth in peace**" (v.12). The finding is **the *thirsty, unsatisfied* interior — spending itself on "that which is not bread" — invited to a *free feast* that makes "the soul live", and summoned to *seek and return* to a God whose thoughts are *higher* and whose pardon is *abundant*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the exposure of the interior's *misdirected appetite* (labouring and spending on "that which does not satisfy") and its redirection to the only nourishment that makes "the soul live" — freely given ("without money"), received by "inclining the ear" and "coming" — with the assurance that God's higher thoughts include an *abundant pardon* that the small human calculus would not expect** *(inferred: v.2 names a universal inner-being condition — "**you spend your money for that which is not bread, and your labour for that which does not satisfy**": the finding is that the interior chronically pours its resources (money, labour, desire) into things that *cannot* satisfy it — a thirst mis-aimed at what is "not bread"; and the remedy is not to *strive harder* but to *come and receive* what is free — "**come, buy… without money and without price**" (v.1): the finding is that the deepest nourishment of the soul ("that your **soul may live**", v.3) is *given, not earned* — the exhausting economy of self-satisfaction replaced by the gift; and the appropriating act is *listening and coming* — "**incline your ear, and come to me; hear, that your soul may live**" (v.3), the soul fed by attention to God; then the summons to *seek* and *return* carries a stunning promise — "**he will abundantly pardon**" (v.7, yarbeh lislo'ach — he will *multiply* forgiveness), immediately grounded in "**my thoughts are not your thoughts… higher than the earth**" (vv.8–9): the finding is that God's "higher thoughts" here specifically mean a mercy *larger than the human heart expects* — the wicked, calculating by their own small measure, cannot imagine the *abundance* of the pardon on offer, and the "higher ways" are chiefly higher in *grace*; the whole chapter redirects the interior from self-exhausting, dissatisfying labour to a freely-given feast and an abundantly-pardoning God, and its end is "joy… and peace" (v.12) — the satisfied soul at last)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *misdirected appetite ("you spend your labour for that which does not satisfy" — the interior pouring itself into what cannot fill it)* (thread — a gem: the chronic mis-aiming of the soul's thirst, w/ 55:2, Ecc 1-2, Jer 2:13, John 6:27); *the free feast that makes the soul live (nourishment given not earned — "buy without money… that your soul may live")* (thread — a gem: the deepest satisfaction received, not achieved, w/ 55:1-3, 41:17-18, John 7:37); *the soul fed by listening ("incline your ear, and come to me; hear, that your soul may live")* (new — the interior nourished by attention to God, w/ 55:3, Deut 8:3); *the abundant pardon larger than expected ("he will abundantly pardon… my thoughts are higher" — grace beyond the human calculus)* (new — a gem: God's "higher thoughts" chiefly higher in mercy, w/ 55:7-9, Ps 103:11-12, Eph 3:20); *seeking and returning while near ("seek the LORD while he may be found… return to the LORD")* (thread — the urgency of the turn, w/ 55:6-7, 45:22).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** misdirected appetite; the free feast that makes the soul live; the soul fed by listening; the abundant pardon larger than expected; seeking and returning while near. Chapter 55 closes batch 7 (and Part II) by redirecting the thirsty, self-exhausting interior to a free feast and an abundantly-pardoning God — the soul's misspent labour answered by grace, and its end "joy and peace."
- **Book-level note (batch 7, chs 49–55):** the inner-being heart of Isaiah. The servant's interior is disclosed across the Songs — his *felt-futility held with trust* ("I laboured in vain, yet my right is with the LORD", 49:4), his *taught ear and flint-set face* under abuse (50:4-7), and supremely his *substitutionary, silent, fruitful anguish* — bearing "the iniquity of us all", opening "not his mouth", and "out of the anguish of his soul" seeing and being "satisfied" (53). Around this, God's answers to the wounded interior: *"I will not forget you… engraved on the palms of my hands"* (49:15-16); the *fear of man* dissolved by *remembering the Maker* (51:12-13); the summons to *rouse from the dust* on the news "your God reigns" (52:7); the *deserted wife* reclaimed in *everlasting love* and a *covenant of peace more fixed than the mountains* (54); and the free *feast for the thirsty* with its *abundant pardon* (55). The through-line: **the deepest needs of the interior — to be remembered, unafraid, forgiven, satisfied — are met by a self-giving God whose servant bears the very sin that estranged us, and whose love outlasts the mountains.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the servant's self-giving (→ 61:1-3, the Spirit-anointed herald); free grace / thirst satisfied (→ 58, true worship; 65:13); the abundant pardon (→ 57:15-19, the contrite revived); everlasting love/covenant (→ 61:8).
