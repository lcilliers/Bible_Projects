## ISA-consuming-fire-dweller · Isa 33:1–24 · [D, multi] — who may dwell with the consuming fire; the fear of the LORD as treasure; the king in his beauty

**The reading.** A prayer of dependence: "**O LORD, be gracious to us; we wait for you. Be our arm every morning, our salvation in the time of trouble**" (v.2). God is named the true wealth: "**he will be the stability of your times, abundance of salvation, wisdom and knowledge; the fear of the LORD is Zion's treasure**" (v.6). Then the searching question, and its answer: "**The sinners in Zion are afraid… 'Who among us can dwell with the consuming fire? Who among us can dwell with everlasting burnings?'**" (v.14) — "**He who walks righteously and speaks uprightly, who despises the gain of oppressions, who shakes his hands lest they hold a bribe… he will dwell on the heights**" (vv.15–16). And the reward of the eyes: "**Your eyes will behold the king in his beauty**" (v.17). The finding is **the question of *who can dwell with God* ("the consuming fire") answered by *integrity of walk, speech, and hands* — with "the fear of the LORD" named as the true *treasure* and the vision of "the king in his beauty" as the reward** *(stated)*. The operation is **God's holiness figured as "everlasting burnings" that the sinful interior cannot survive — and the one who *can* "dwell" there defined not by ritual but by a *cleanness of conduct and desire* (righteous walk, upright speech, hands that refuse the bribe); the fear of the LORD is the "treasure" that orders such an interior, and its consummation is *seeing* the beautiful King** *(inferred: v.14's question is one of Scripture's most piercing — "**who can dwell with the consuming fire?**": the finding is that God's presence is *unbearable* to the unclean interior (the same terror as 6:5, "woe is me, I am undone"), so the question of dwelling-with-God is the question of the whole book; and the answer (vv.15–16) is *ethical-interior*, not ceremonial — "**walks righteously… speaks uprightly… despises unjust gain… shakes his hands lest they hold a bribe… stops his ears from hearing of bloodshed, shuts his eyes from looking on evil**": a portrait of an interior kept clean at every gate (feet, mouth, hands, ears, eyes), the whole self oriented away from evil; the finding is that the one who may "dwell on the heights" with the consuming fire is the person of *integrated cleanness* — not sinless perfection but a heart set against corruption at every sense; and v.6 names what orders such a life — "**the fear of the LORD is Zion's treasure**": reverence is not a burden but the true *wealth*, the ordering principle that makes a self able to stand in the fire; and the reward is *vision* — "**your eyes will behold the king in his beauty**" (v.17): the clean interior's consummation is to *see God* as beautiful rather than as terror — the same fire that consumes the sinner is beheld as beauty by the pure)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *who can dwell with the consuming fire (God's holiness unbearable to the unclean; dwelling-with-God as the whole question)* (thread — a gem: the terror of the Holy to the impure interior, w/ 6:5, Heb 12:29, Ps 15, 24:3-4); *cleanness at every gate (righteous walk, upright speech, hands refusing the bribe, ears/eyes shut to evil — the whole self oriented from corruption)* (new — a gem: integrated interior cleanness, not ceremony, as the condition of dwelling with God, w/ 33:15-16, Ps 15:2-5); *the fear of the LORD as treasure (reverence as the true wealth that orders the interior)* (thread — a gem: the fear of God as wealth, not burden, w/ 33:6, 11:2-3, Pro 15:16); *the king in his beauty (the clean interior's reward is to see God as beauty, not terror)* (new — a gem: the same fire beheld as beauty by the pure, w/ 33:17, Ps 27:4, Matt 5:8).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** who can dwell with the consuming fire; cleanness at every gate; the fear of the LORD as treasure; the king in his beauty. Chapter 33 poses the book's ultimate inner-being question — *who can live in God's presence?* — and answers it with an integrated cleanness ordered by the fear of the LORD, whose reward is to behold the King as beauty.
- **Threads:** dwelling with the Holy / the terror of holiness (← 6:5; w/ Ps 15, 24; Heb 12:29); the fear of the LORD as treasure/delight (← 11:2-3; → 50:10); seeing God / the king's beauty (→ 40:5, 52:8, 60:1-2; w/ Ps 27:4); waiting for God (← 25:9, 30:18; "we wait for you", 33:2).
