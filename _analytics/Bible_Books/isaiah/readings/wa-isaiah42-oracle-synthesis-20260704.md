## ISA-servant-bruised-reed · Isa 42:1–9 · [D, multi] — the gentle, un-showy, persevering servant (Servant Song 1)

**The reading.** The first Servant Song portrays an interior of quiet, Spirit-borne strength: "**Behold my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him; he will bring forth justice to the nations**" (v.1). His manner is the opposite of self-display: "**He will not cry aloud or lift up his voice, or make it heard in the street; a bruised reed he will not break, and a faintly burning wick he will not quench; he will faithfully bring forth justice**" (vv.2–3); "**He will not grow faint or be discouraged till he has established justice**" (v.4). The finding is **a servant whose strength is *gentle and un-showy* — not crying out in the street, not breaking the "bruised reed" or quenching the "faintly burning wick" — and *persevering* ("will not grow faint or be discouraged")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the portrait of an ideal interior defined by *restraint and tenderness*: power that does not need to *display* itself (no shouting in the street), that *protects rather than crushes* the weak (the bruised reed, the dim wick), yet is *unbreakably persistent* — gentleness and steadfastness held together, both Spirit-given ("I have put my Spirit upon him")** *(inferred: the inner-being signature of the servant is the pairing of *gentleness* and *strength* — three "he will not"s of restraint ("**not cry aloud… not break the bruised reed… not quench the faintly burning wick**", vv.2–3) with one "he will not" of endurance ("**not grow faint or be discouraged**", v.4); the finding is that true strength, in this portrait, is *self-restraining and protective* — it declines to dominate or to crush what is already weak; the "**bruised reed**" and "**faintly burning wick**" are images of the barely-surviving (a person nearly broken, a faith nearly extinguished), and the servant's greatness is that he *nurses* rather than finishes them; yet this tenderness is not weakness — he "will not grow faint or be discouraged" until justice is established (the same word "faint"/kahah that describes the *wick* is what the servant will *not* do — he will not himself become the dim wick); the finding is the model of an interior that is at once *gentle with the weak* and *tireless in purpose* — power under the restraint of compassion, and compassion undergirded by unbreakable resolve; and it is "in whom my soul delights" (v.1) — this interior is what God finds *delightful*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *strength that does not display itself ("he will not cry aloud… in the street" — power without self-advertisement)* (new — a gem: greatness that declines to dominate, w/ 42:2, Zech 4:6, Matt 12:19); *the bruised reed not broken / the dim wick not quenched (power that protects rather than crushes the barely-surviving)* (new — a gem: tenderness to the nearly-broken, w/ 42:3, Matt 12:20, Isa 61:1-3); *gentleness and tireless resolve held together ("he will not grow faint or be discouraged" — the dim-wick word he will not himself become)* (new — a gem: compassion undergirded by unbreakable purpose, w/ 42:4); *the Spirit-borne, delighted-in servant ("in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him")* (thread — the ideal interior God finds delightful, w/ 11:2, 61:1).

## ISA-blind-servant · Isa 42:10–25 · [D, multi] — seeing but not observing; suffering not taken to heart

**The reading.** After a new song of praise (vv.10–13) and God's own labouring zeal ("**now I will cry out like a woman in labour**", v.14), the chapter turns to a painful paradox: the servant-people are *blind*. "**Who is blind but my servant, or deaf as my messenger whom I send?… He sees many things, but does not observe them; his ears are open, but he does not hear**" (vv.19–20). Plundered and imprisoned (v.22), the people still miss the meaning: "**it burned him up, but he did not take it to heart**" (v.25). The finding is **a *seeing that does not perceive* and a *hearing that does not hear* — faculties open but inoperative — and suffering that fails to be *"taken to heart"*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior whose organs of perception are *functional yet unengaged*: "he sees many things, but does not observe… his ears are open, but he does not hear" — the failure is not sensory but *of the heart*, an incapacity (or refusal) to let what is seen and suffered *register* and *instruct*; even fire that "burned him up" is "not taken to heart"** *(inferred: the paradox of vv.19–20 is a precise inner-being diagnosis — the servant "**sees many things but does not observe**", "**his ears are open but he does not hear**": the finding is that *perception* is not the same as *reception* — the eyes and ears may work perfectly while the *heart* remains closed, so that experience passes through without instructing; and the sharpest form is v.25 — even devastating suffering ("it burned him up") is "**not taken to heart**" (lo yasim al-lev — did not lay it upon the heart): the finding is that the deepest blindness is the heart's failure to *let experience teach it* — a person can undergo judgment, loss, even fire, and extract no meaning, because the interior organ that would receive and interpret is dormant; this is the negative of the whole book's summons (to "know", "understand", "consider") — a people with open eyes and ears and yet no *heart-perception*; the tragedy is that the faculties are all present and the lesson all available, and still nothing lands)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *seeing without perceiving / hearing without hearing (faculties open but unengaged — perception is not reception)* (thread — a gem: the eyes/ears working while the heart stays closed, w/ 42:19-20, 6:9-10, Deut 29:4, Mark 8:18); *suffering not taken to heart ("it burned him up, but he did not take it to heart" — experience passing through without instructing)* (new — a gem: the heart's failure to let even devastation teach it, w/ 42:25, 57:1, Hag 1:5); *the dormant heart-organ (the deepest blindness is not sensory but of the heart)* (thread — w/ 6:10, 44:18).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** strength that does not display itself; the bruised reed not broken; gentleness and tireless resolve; the Spirit-borne servant; seeing without perceiving; suffering not taken to heart. Chapter 42 sets the *ideal* servant (gentle, protective, tireless, Spirit-borne) beside the *actual* servant-people (blind, deaf, un-teachable) — the interior as it should be and as it tragically is.
- **Threads:** the Servant (→ 49:1-6, 50:4-9, 52:13-53:12, the later Songs); the bruised reed / tenderness to the weak (→ 61:1-3); seeing without perceiving / heart-blindness (← 6:9-10, 29:9-13; → 44:18); suffering not taken to heart (→ 57:1).
