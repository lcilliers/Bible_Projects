# Isaiah 3 — inner-being oracle readings (Phase-2 meaning synthesis)

- **Source:** Phase-1 lexicals per oracle unit (provenance `isaiah-oracle-segmentation-batch1-v1-20260704`). Cited; grounded; *stated* | *inferred*.
- Note: the second unit (**ISA-daughters-branch**) is **cross-chapter** (3:16–26 + 4:1–6), filed here at its anchor.

## ISA-leaders-judged · Isa 3:1–15 · [D, multi] — sin worn on the face; power that devours the weak

**The reading.** God removes the props of society — bread, water, leader, judge, prophet — and a leaderless chaos follows in which "**the people will oppress one another**… the youth will be insolent to the elder" (v.5). At the centre is a diagnosis of the *face*: "**The look on their faces bears witness against them; they proclaim their sin like Sodom, they do not hide it**" (v.9). Then the LORD rises to contend: "**It is you who have devoured the vineyard, the spoil of the poor is in your houses. What do you mean by crushing my people, by grinding the face of the poor?**" (vv.14–15). The finding is **sin so unabashed it is *written on the face* and openly proclaimed, joined to a leadership whose inner disposition *devours* and *grinds* the powerless** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that has lost shame — sin no longer hidden but *displayed* — set against the God who reads faces and who takes the crushing of the poor as a *personal* affront ("my people… the face of the poor")** *(inferred: v.9 is the inner-being hinge — "the **look on their faces** bears witness against them": sin has reached the stage where the interior no longer even *conceals* itself (contrast the fig-leaf instinct of Gen 3) — they "**do not hide it**", proclaiming it "like Sodom"; the loss of shame is itself a deep corruption (the conscience that no longer flinches); and the leaders' sin is specifically *appetitive and violent toward the weak* — "devoured the vineyard", "grinding the **face** of the poor" (v.15): the same word *face* that betrays the sinner (v.9) is the face of the poor being ground — the finding is that God notices *both* faces, the brazen face of the sinner and the ground-down face of the victim, and identifies with the latter ("**my people**"); the interior indicted here is the one that can crush a fellow human and feel nothing)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the loss of shame (sin worn openly on the face, "they do not hide it")* (new — a gem: the conscience that no longer conceals, w/ Jer 6:15, 8:12); *the face as witness (the interior legible on the countenance)* (new — the face betraying the heart); *the devouring disposition toward the weak (grinding the face of the poor)* (thread — power that consumes, w/ Mic 3:2-3, Amos); *God's personal identification with the crushed ("my people… the poor")* (thread — w/ Ps 12:5).

## ISA-daughters-branch · Isa 3:16–4:6 · [D, multi] — haughty vanity stripped; the cleansed remnant made holy

**The reading.** Vanity anatomised: "**the daughters of Zion are haughty and walk with outstretched necks, glancing wantonly with their eyes, mincing along… tinkling with their feet**" (v.16); so their whole apparatus of finery is inventoried and then stripped — "**instead of perfume there will be rottenness; instead of a belt, a rope… instead of beauty, branding**" (v.24). Then the movement reverses (4:2–6): "**the branch of the LORD shall be beautiful and glorious**"; the survivor "will be called **holy**" (4:3), "**when the Lord shall have washed away the filth of the daughters of Zion… by a spirit of judgment and by a spirit of burning**" (4:4), and over the cleansed city a canopy of glory. The finding is **a *haughty self-display* (the interior betrayed by the eyes, the neck, the gait) exposed and stripped — then a remnant *washed* by a spirit of burning and *called holy*, adorned no longer with finery but with glory** *(stated)*. The operation is **pride manifest in the *body's bearing* (outstretched neck, wanton glance, mincing step) reduced to shame — and the true adornment relocated: from the self's finery to God's cleansing, so that "holy" replaces "haughty" as the interior's name** *(inferred: the inner-being reading turns on the *bearing of the body as the leak of the interior* — the "outstretched necks", "wanton eyes", "mincing… tinkling" (v.16) are the *posture* of a haughty heart; Isaiah reads pride off the gait; the stripping (vv.18–24, the long list of ornaments removed) enacts the exposure — everything the vain self used to display itself is taken; but the passage does not end in mere humiliation — 4:4's "**washed away the filth… by a spirit of judgment and burning**" makes the same *burning* that stripped them the agent of *cleansing* (judgment as purgation again, as in 1:25); the finding is the exchange: the daughters who adorned themselves become a remnant "**called holy**" (4:3) whose covering is now God's glory-canopy (4:5–6), not their own finery — the self's adornment moved from what it hangs on itself to what God's cleansing confers)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *pride read off the body's bearing (neck, eyes, gait — the interior leaking through posture)* (new — a gem: haughtiness legible in how one walks, w/ Pro 6:17); *the stripping of self-display (every ornament of vanity removed)* (thread — vanity exposed, w/ 2:11-17); *the spirit of judgment-and-burning as cleansing (the fire that strips also purifies)* (thread — purgative judgment, w/ 1:25, 6:6-7); *"called holy" replacing "haughty" (the interior renamed by God's cleansing; adornment relocated from finery to glory)* (new — the exchange of self-adornment for God-conferred holiness).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the loss of shame; the face as witness; the devouring of the weak; pride read off the body; vanity stripped; the spirit of burning as cleansing; "holy" replacing "haughty." Chapter 3 (with 4) exposes the *unconcealed* interior — sin on the face, pride in the gait — and answers it with a *burning that cleanses*, leaving a remnant renamed holy.
- **Threads:** the face/countenance as the interior's witness (→ 3:15 the poor's face); purgative burning (→ 6:6-7); the remnant made holy (→ 6:13, 10:20-22); adornment relocated from self to God (→ 61:10, "garments of salvation").
