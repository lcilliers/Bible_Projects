## ISA-hez-rabshakeh · Isa 36:1–22 · [D, multi] — the assault on trust; warfare against the interior's confidence

**The reading.** The Assyrian field-commander's speech is a sustained attack not on the walls but on the *heart*. Its refrain is the word *trust*: "**On what do you rest this trust of yours?… In whom do you now trust?**" (vv.4–5); "**you are trusting in Egypt, that broken reed**" (v.6); "**Do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD**" (v.15); and the climactic blasphemy, "**Who among all the gods of these lands have delivered their lands out of my hand, that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem?**" (v.20). To all of it: "**they were silent and answered him not a word, for the king's command was, 'Do not answer him'**" (v.21). The finding is **a calculated *assault on trust* — the enemy working directly on the interior to corrode its confidence in God ("do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD") — met by a *disciplined silence*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the recognition that the deepest warfare is aimed at the *faculty of trust itself*: the Rabshakeh does not merely threaten force, he argues the *futility of faith* — cataloguing gods that failed, reframing reliance on God as self-deception — so that the battle is for the interior's confidence; and the answer is not debate but *guarded silence*, refusing to let the corrosive words find purchase** *(inferred: the Rabshakeh's genius is that he attacks *trust* as such — the word recurs seven times: he first discredits the *wrong* trust ("Egypt, that broken reed", v.6 — which Isaiah himself had condemned, 31:1) and then, more dangerously, the *right* one ("do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD", v.15); the finding is that the interior's confidence in God is the real target of assault — the enemy seeks to *argue faith into despair*, marshalling evidence ("has any god delivered?", vv.18–20) to make trust look naïve; and the discipline of v.21 is instructive — "**they answered him not a word**": some assaults on faith are not to be *debated* on their own terms (which would grant the corrosive framing) but met with a silence that denies them entry, carrying the matter instead to God (as ch.37 will); the finding is that when the interior's trust is under deliberate attack, the wise response is not to argue with the tempter's catalogue of reasons for despair but to withhold engagement and take the threat to God)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the assault on trust ("do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD" — the enemy attacking the faculty of faith itself)* (thread — a gem: the deepest warfare aimed at the interior's confidence in God, w/ 36:4-20, Gen 3:1, Matt 4:3-6); *faith argued toward despair (the catalogue of failed gods marshalled to make trust look naïve)* (new — evidence deployed to corrode confidence, w/ 36:18-20); *disciplined silence before the corrosive word ("they answered him not a word" — refusing the tempter's framing)* (new — a gem: some assaults on faith met not by debate but by withholding entry, w/ 36:21, Ps 39:1-2).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the assault on trust; faith argued toward despair; disciplined silence. Chapter 36 stages the enemy's true target — not the city but the *interior's confidence in God* — and the first, restrained response: silence, and the carrying of the threat elsewhere.
- **Threads:** trust as the battleground (→ 37:14-20, trust brought to God; ← 30:15, 31:1); silence/waiting under assault (→ 37:1, the turn to the house of the LORD).
