## ISA-fear-not-worm · Isa 41:1–29 · [D, multi] — fear not, I am with you; the worm upheld; idols are empty wind

**The reading.** Against the trembling coastlands who make idols to steady themselves ("**everyone helps his neighbour… 'Be strong!'**… they strengthen it with nails so that it cannot be moved", vv.6–7), God speaks to his servant the great reassurance: "**Fear not, for I am with you; be not dismayed, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, I will help you, I will uphold you with my righteous right hand**" (v.10); "**I, the LORD your God, hold your right hand**" (v.13); "**Fear not, you worm Jacob… I am the one who helps you, declares the LORD; your Redeemer is the Holy One of Israel**" (v.14). The poor seeking water are answered (vv.17–18); the idols are exposed as "**a delusion… empty wind**" (v.29). The finding is **the fearful, *contemptible* self ("you worm Jacob") met with "**fear not… I am with you… I will uphold you**" — reassurance grounded in God's presence and grip, over against idols that are "empty wind"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the antidote to fear given as *presence and holding* — not "you are strong" but "**I am with you… I hold your right hand**" — so that the "worm" (the self at its most powerless and despised) is steadied not by its own resources (the idol-makers' frantic nailing-down) but by being *gripped* by God** *(inferred: the contrast is pointed — the frightened nations *manufacture* stability, hammering idols and fastening them "with nails so that it cannot be moved" (v.7, self-made security against fear), while God's servant is told the opposite: your steadiness is not something you make but Someone who holds you — "**I will uphold you… I hold your right hand**" (vv.10,13); the finding is that the cure for fear is *relational*, not resource-based — "fear not, **for I am with you**" (the reason is presence, not the removal of danger); and the recipient is deliberately named "**worm Jacob**" (v.14, tola'at — the crushed, the negligible): the finding is that God's "fear not" is addressed precisely to the self at its most contemptible and weak — the worm, not the mighty — and its dignity is conferred entirely from outside ("your Redeemer is the Holy One"); the fearful interior is not told to summon courage but to receive a *grip*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *"fear not, for I am with you" (the cure for fear is presence and holding, not the removal of danger)* (thread — a gem, the refrain of Part II: fear answered relationally, w/ 41:10,13, 43:1,5, 35:4); *steadied by being gripped, not by self-made security ("I hold your right hand" vs the idol nailed down so it cannot move)* (new — a gem: stability as Someone who holds you, not something you make, w/ 41:7,10,13); *the "worm" cherished ("fear not, you worm Jacob" — the fear-not addressed to the self at its most contemptible)* (new — a gem: dignity conferred on the negligible from outside, w/ 41:14, Ps 22:6); *idols as empty wind (the manufactured securities exposed as "a delusion")* (thread — w/ 41:29, 44:9-20).
