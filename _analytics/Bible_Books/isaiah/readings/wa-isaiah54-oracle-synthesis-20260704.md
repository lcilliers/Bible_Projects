## ISA-covenant-peace-husband · Isa 54:1–17 · [D, multi] — the deserted wife restored; everlasting love; the covenant of peace unremovable

**The reading.** The barren, deserted woman is told to sing (v.1), for God claims her: "**Fear not, for you will not be ashamed… For your Maker is your husband, the LORD of hosts is his name… For the LORD has called you like a wife deserted and grieved in spirit**" (vv.4–6). The heart of it distinguishes a *moment* from *forever*: "**For a brief moment I deserted you, but with great compassion I will gather you. In overflowing anger for a moment I hid my face from you, but with everlasting love I will have compassion on you**" (vv.7–8); "**For the mountains may depart and the hills be removed, but my steadfast love shall not depart from you, and my covenant of peace shall not be removed**" (v.10). And the children: "**All your children shall be taught by the LORD, and great shall be the peace of your children**" (v.13). The finding is **the *grieved-in-spirit, deserted* self reclaimed in *everlasting love*, its felt-abandonment reframed as a "brief moment" against a love and "covenant of peace" *more permanent than the mountains*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that felt *cast off* ("a wife deserted and grieved in spirit") given a new *proportion* of its experience — the desertion a "brief moment", the compassion "everlasting" — so that the pain is neither denied nor allowed to define the whole; and the security relocated to a love "steadfast" beyond the mountains and a "covenant of peace" that "shall not be removed"** *(inferred: the pastoral genius is in the *ratios of time* — "**for a BRIEF MOMENT I deserted you, but with great compassion I will gather**… for a moment I hid my face, but with **EVERLASTING** love**" (vv.7–8): the finding is that the grieved interior is not told its pain was unreal but is given its *true scale* — the felt-abandonment was real but *momentary*, set against a compassion that is *everlasting*; the reframing does not erase the "brief moment" of hiding but *dwarfs* it by the permanence of the love; (2) the *most permanent thing* is the love — "**the mountains may depart… but my steadfast love shall not depart, my covenant of peace shall not be removed**" (v.10): the finding is that the interior's security is anchored to the one thing *more stable than creation itself* — God's chesed and his "covenant of peace"; the mountains (the very image of permanence) are *less* fixed than God's love; and (3) the fruit is a taught, peaceful next generation — "**all your children shall be TAUGHT BY THE LORD, and great shall be the PEACE of your children**" (v.13): the finding is that the restored interior's legacy is peace — the deserted, grieved mother becomes the mother of God-taught, peace-filled children; the whole movement is the reclamation of a broken-hearted self by a love that outlasts the mountains)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the deserted, grieved self reclaimed ("a wife deserted and grieved in spirit… your Maker is your husband")* (thread — a gem: the broken-hearted interior claimed in love, w/ 54:5-6, 62:4-5, Hos 2:14-20); *the true proportion of pain ("for a brief moment… but with everlasting love" — the felt-abandonment real but momentary against everlasting compassion)* (new — a gem: pain given its true scale, neither denied nor allowed to define the whole, w/ 54:7-8, Ps 30:5, 2 Cor 4:17); *love more permanent than the mountains ("the mountains may depart… but my steadfast love shall not depart… my covenant of peace shall not be removed")* (thread — a gem: security anchored to the one thing more stable than creation, w/ 54:10, 51:6, Rom 8:38-39); *the taught, peaceful legacy ("all your children shall be taught by the LORD, and great shall be their peace")* (new — the restored self's fruit is peace, w/ 54:13, 48:18).
