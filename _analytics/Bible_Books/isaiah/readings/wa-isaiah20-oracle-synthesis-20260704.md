## ISA-naked-sign · Isa 20:1–6 · [D, multi] — misplaced hope shamed; the boast that cannot deliver

**The reading.** Isaiah walks "**naked and barefoot for three years as a sign**" (v.3) that Egypt and Cush will be led away captive. The point lands on the *interior* of those who had trusted them: "**they shall be dismayed and ashamed because of Cush their hope and of Egypt their boast**" (v.5); and the coastland's rueful confession: "**Behold, this is what has happened to those in whom we hoped and to whom we fled for help… and we, how shall we escape?**" (v.6). The finding is **misplaced *hope* and *boast* exposed and *shamed* — the refuge trusted in (Egypt, Cush) proving unable to save, and the trusting heart left with nothing but "how shall we escape?"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior act of *hoping in* and *boasting of* a false security tracked to its end — the shame that follows when the object of one's confidence collapses; the sign dramatises that trust placed in the strong-but-creaturely is trust that will be "dismayed and ashamed"** *(inferred: the two words carry the reading — "**their hope**" (mabbat — that to which they *looked*) and "**their boast**" (tif'eret — their glorying): the finding is that hope and boast are *directional* acts of the interior, and their rightness depends wholly on their object; here both are aimed at Egypt/Cush — a human power — and so both end in shame (v.5); the exposed heart's cry, "**those in whom we hoped… how shall we escape?**" (v.6), is the sound of a trust that has outlived its object — the security is gone and the self is left defenceless; the sign thus makes visible an inner-being law running through the whole book (2:22, 17:7, 31:1): to hope in and boast of the creature is to build one's confidence on what will fail, and the failure returns as *shame* — the specific grief of the interior whose refuge has proven false)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *misplaced hope shamed ("Cush their hope, Egypt their boast" — confidence in a creaturely refuge ending in shame)* (thread — a gem: hope's rightness depends wholly on its object, w/ 30:1-5, 31:1-3, Jer 17:5-6); *hope and boast as directional acts of the interior (the self glorying in what it looks to)* (new — the orientation of confidence); *the trust that outlives its object ("those in whom we hoped… how shall we escape?")* (new — the defenceless heart when a false refuge collapses, w/ 20:6, Ps 146:3).
