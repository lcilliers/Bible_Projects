## ISA-sin-separation · Isa 59:1–21 · [D, multi] — iniquity as separation from God; the confession; God's self-provided rescue

**The reading.** The chapter locates the problem precisely: not in God's power but in sin's *barrier*. "**Behold, the LORD's hand is not shortened, that it cannot save… but your iniquities have made a separation between you and your God, and your sins have hidden his face from you so that he does not hear**" (vv.1–2). A long, honest confession follows: "**We grope for the wall like the blind… we hope for justice, but there is none… our sins testify against us… we know our iniquities**" (vv.10–12). Then the astonishing turn: "**He saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no one to intercede; then his own arm brought him salvation**" (v.16), and "**a Redeemer will come to Zion, to those in Jacob who turn from transgression**" (v.20). The finding is **sin diagnosed as *separation* — a self-made barrier that "hides God's face" — honestly *confessed*, and then met by a salvation God *provides from himself* when no human can** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's estrangement traced to its true cause — not God's distance but *sin's barrier* ("your iniquities have made a separation") — owned in unflinching confession ("we know our iniquities"), and then relieved not by human remedy (there was "no one to intercede") but by God's *own arm*, which "brought him salvation"** *(inferred: v.2 corrects a common misreading of the interior's experience — when God seems absent ("his face hidden", "he does not hear"), the cause is not divine unwillingness ("the LORD's hand is not shortened") but *the barrier sin erects*: the finding is that sin *separates* — it is a wall between the self and God, and the felt-silence of heaven is often the shadow of that wall, not God's indifference; and the chapter models the right response — *honest confession* (vv.9–15), the interior owning its blindness and guilt without excuse ("**we know our iniquities**", v.12); but the decisive finding is v.16 — "**he saw that there was no man… no one to intercede; then his OWN ARM brought him salvation**": the finding is that when the human side is bankrupt (no intercessor, no remedy from within), God *supplies from himself* what the situation lacks — the salvation the separated interior cannot manufacture is *provided by God's own arm*; the barrier sin built, humanity cannot breach, so God breaches it himself, coming as "a Redeemer… to those who turn" (v.20): the estrangement is real and self-caused, but the reconciliation is God-initiated and God-achieved)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *sin as separation ("your iniquities have made a separation between you and your God" — the felt-silence of heaven the shadow of sin's barrier, not God's indifference)* (thread — a gem: estrangement traced to sin's wall, not divine unwillingness, w/ 59:1-2, Gen 3:8, Ps 66:18); *honest confession without excuse ("we grope like the blind… we know our iniquities")* (thread — the interior owning its guilt, w/ 59:9-15, Dan 9, Ps 51); *God's self-provided salvation ("no one to intercede; then his own arm brought him salvation" — God supplying from himself what humanity lacks)* (new — a gem: the reconciliation God-initiated when the human side is bankrupt, w/ 59:16, 63:5, Rom 5:6-8); *the Redeemer to those who turn (rescue reaching the interior that turns from transgression)* (thread — w/ 59:20, 44:22, 55:7).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** sin as separation; honest confession without excuse; God's self-provided salvation; the Redeemer to those who turn. Chapter 59 names the true cause of the interior's estrangement (sin's barrier, not God's distance), models honest confession, and shows salvation supplied by God's own arm when no human could.
- **Threads:** sin's barrier / hidden face (← 8:17, 57:17; w/ Gen 3); confession (w/ Dan 9, Ps 51); God's own arm (→ 63:5); the Redeemer to those who turn (← 55:7; 59:20).
