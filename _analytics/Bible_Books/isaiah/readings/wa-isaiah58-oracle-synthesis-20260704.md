## ISA-true-fast · Isa 58:1–14 · [D, multi] — hollow worship vs the fast God chooses; the light that breaks forth

**The reading.** The people are diligently religious and *puzzled* by God's silence: "**Why have we fasted, and you see it not? Why have we humbled ourselves, and you take no knowledge of it?**" (v.3). God answers that their fasting coexists with self-interest and cruelty: "**in the day of your fast you seek your own pleasure, and oppress all your workers… you fast only to quarrel and to fight**" (vv.3–4). Then the redefinition: "**Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of wickedness… to let the oppressed go free… Is it not to share your bread with the hungry and bring the homeless poor into your house?**" (vv.6–7). And the promise: "**Then shall your light break forth like the dawn… the glory of the LORD shall be your rear guard… if you pour yourself out for the hungry… then shall your light rise in the darkness**" (vv.8–10). The finding is **religious devotion (fasting, self-humbling) *voided* because it coexists with self-seeking and oppression — and *true* worship redefined as *mercy* ("loose the bonds… share your bread"), which alone makes the interior's "light break forth"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the exposure of a worship that is *ritually intense but morally empty* — the bewilderment "why have we fasted and you see it not?" answered by naming what the fast conceals (self-pleasure, oppression) — and the relocation of acceptable devotion from *ceremony directed at God* to *mercy directed at the neighbour*, with the interior's own "light" and "healing" as the fruit** *(inferred: the inner-being crux is the people's *sincere bewilderment* (v.3) — they genuinely cannot see why their religion "works" so poorly; the finding is that a worship can be *earnest and diligent* ("they seek me daily and delight to know my ways", v.2) and yet *hollow*, because the heart behind it is still turned to "your own pleasure" and against the vulnerable ("oppress all your workers", v.3); God's redefinition (vv.6–7) relocates the whole meaning of worship — from what one *withholds from oneself* (fasting) to what one *gives to others* (bread, freedom, shelter): the finding is that the devotion God "chooses" is *mercy*, that true fasting is self-*pouring-out* for the hungry and afflicted (v.10) rather than self-affliction for God's notice; and the reward is *interior* as much as outward — "**your light shall break forth… your healing shall spring up… your light shall rise in the darkness**" (vv.8,10): the finding is that mercy to others is the very thing that *illuminates and heals the merciful* — the self that "pours itself out for the hungry" finds its own gloom turned to "noonday"; worship that reaches the neighbour is the worship that reaches back into the worshipper's own darkness)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *earnest worship voided by an unmerciful heart ("why have we fasted, and you see it not?" — devotion sincere yet hollow because self-seeking coexists with it)* (thread — a gem: ritual intensity with moral emptiness, w/ 58:2-4, 1:11-15, 29:13, Amos 5:21-24); *worship redefined as mercy ("is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds… share your bread" — devotion relocated from self-affliction to self-giving)* (new — a gem: acceptable worship is mercy to the neighbour, not ceremony toward God, w/ 58:6-7, Mic 6:8, Jas 1:27); *mercy that illuminates the merciful ("pour yourself out for the hungry… then shall your light rise in the darkness")* (new — a gem: giving to others heals the giver's own gloom, w/ 58:8-11, Pro 11:25, Luke 6:38); *the Sabbath as delight ("call the Sabbath a delight… then you shall take delight in the LORD")* (thread — worship as gladness, not burden, w/ 58:13-14).
