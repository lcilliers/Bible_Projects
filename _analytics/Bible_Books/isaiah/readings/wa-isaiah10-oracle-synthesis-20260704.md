## ISA-assyria-rod · Isa 10:5–34 · [D, multi] — the boasting instrument; the axe vaunting over the hand; the remnant that leans on the LORD

**The reading.** Assyria is God's tool — "**Woe to Assyria, the rod of my anger; the staff in their hands is my fury!**" (v.5) — but the tool does not know it: "**he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy**" (v.7). Its self-account is pure self-derivation: "**By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom, for I have understanding**" (v.13). To this God poses the decisive question: "**Shall the axe boast over him who hews with it, or the saw magnify itself against him who wields it? As if a rod should wield him who lifts it!**" (v.15). And the counter-movement, the interior of the remnant: "**In that day the remnant of Israel… will no more lean on him who struck them, but will lean on the LORD, the Holy One of Israel, in truth**" (v.20). The finding is **a *creature* mistaking its borrowed power for its own ("by the strength of my hand"), and so *boasting over the very Hand that wields it* — set against a remnant that *leans*, transferring its reliance from the striker to the LORD "in truth"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's basic error of *attribution* — the instrument crediting itself with what flows through it — exposed by the axe/hand image, and its cure named as *leaning* rightly: dependence relocated from the arm that happens to strike to God himself** *(inferred: the axe-and-hewer figure (v.15) is one of Scripture's sharpest exposures of pride — the tool has *no* power of its own; every stroke is the wielder's; so Assyria's "by my hand… by my wisdom" (v.13) is not merely arrogant but *metaphysically absurd*, the axe boasting over the arm; the finding is that the proud interior's foundational sin is a *false attribution* — taking the strength that passes *through* it as native *to* it, forgetting it is wielded; and the antidote is precisely a re-attribution — the remnant "**will no more lean on him who struck them, but will lean on the LORD… in truth**" (v.20): the inner-being verb is *sha'an*, to lean/support one's weight — the question is never *whether* the self leans (it always rests its weight on something) but *on what*, and the mark of the healed interior is that it leans on God "in truth" (be'emet — really, not nominally), having learned that its strength was never its own; the frightened self of chs 7–8 and the proud self of Assyria meet here in one lesson — *your standing was always borrowed; lean on the Lender*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the creature crediting itself with borrowed power ("by the strength of my hand… by my wisdom")* (thread — a gem: false attribution as the root of pride, w/ 5:21, Deut 8:17, Dan 4:30); *the axe boasting over the hand (the instrument's power is wholly the wielder's — pride as metaphysical absurdity)* (new — a gem: the exposure of self-derived strength, w/ 10:15); *leaning rightly ("lean on the LORD… in truth" — the self always rests its weight on something; the question is on what)* (thread — a gem: dependence relocated from the striker to God, w/ 2 Chr 16:7-9, Pro 3:5); *reliance "in truth" not in name (be'emet — real leaning, not nominal)* (new — the sincerity of dependence).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the creature crediting itself with borrowed power; the axe boasting over the hand; leaning rightly; reliance in truth. Chapter 10 gives the book's clearest anatomy of *false attribution* (Assyria as the axe that thinks it hews of itself) and its cure (the remnant that *leans* on the LORD in truth) — pride and faith framed as rival *attributions* of one's strength.
- **Threads:** self-derived strength / false attribution (← 5:21; → 31:3, "the Egyptians are man, not God"); leaning/trust relocated (→ 26:3-4, 30:15, 50:10); the remnant (← 6:13, 10:20-22; → 11:11, 37:31-32).
