## ISA-feast-death-swallowed · Isa 25:1–12 · [D, multi] — the rewarded waiting; death swallowed, tears wiped; God the stronghold of the poor

**The reading.** The chapter opens in personal praise: "**O LORD, you are my God; I will exalt you; I will praise your name, for you have done wonderful things**" (v.1). It grounds the praise in God's character toward the lowly: "**you have been a stronghold to the poor, a stronghold to the needy in his distress, a shelter from the storm and a shade from the heat**" (v.4). Then the vision that crowns the Old Testament's hope: on the mountain a feast for all peoples (v.6), and "**he will swallow up death forever; and the LORD God will wipe away tears from all faces, and the reproach of his people he will take away**" (vv.7–8). And the response of the waiting heart: "**Behold, this is our God; we have waited for him, that he might save us… let us be glad and rejoice in his salvation**" (v.9). The finding is **the *reward of waiting* — the God long-awaited now revealed as Saviour — issuing in exultant joy, grounded in a God who is *stronghold to the poor* and who *swallows death* and *wipes every tear*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior posture of *waiting* brought to its consummation: the same soul that "waited" (chs 8, 26) now cries "this is our God; we have waited for him" — and the waiting is vindicated by the two deepest interior needs met at once (the end of death, the end of tears); joy here is the *fruit of vindicated patience*** *(inferred: the inner-being heart of the chapter is the movement from *waiting* to *rejoicing* — "**we have waited for him**… let us be **glad and rejoice** in his salvation" (v.9): the finding is that the long inner discipline of waiting on God (qavah — the strung-taut hope of 8:17, 26:8) is not endless but *consummated* — the waited-for God arrives and saves, and the appropriate interior response is exultation; and what he does answers the interior's two most inconsolable griefs — "**he will swallow up death forever**" (the fear behind all fears) and "**wipe away tears from all faces**" (the sorrow behind all sorrows): the finding is that the God worth waiting for is the one who reaches to the *root* of the human interior's dread and grief — undoing death and drying tears — so that the waiting was never naïve; and note the ground of it all (v.4) — this cosmic Saviour is known first as "**a stronghold to the poor… the needy in his distress**": the God who swallows death is the God who shelters the lowly, so that the *poor* who wait are exactly those whose waiting is answered)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the reward of waiting ("we have waited for him… let us be glad and rejoice" — the long discipline of waiting consummated in joy)* (thread — a gem: waiting on God vindicated, not endless, w/ 8:17, 26:8, 40:31, 49:23); *death swallowed / tears wiped (the God who reaches the root of the interior's deepest dread and grief)* (new — a gem: the two most inconsolable griefs undone at once, w/ 25:8, Rev 21:4, 1 Cor 15:54); *God the stronghold of the poor (the cosmic Saviour known first as shelter to the needy)* (thread — the God who swallows death is the God who shelters the lowly, w/ 25:4, 14:32, Ps 9:9); *joy as the fruit of vindicated patience (gladness born of a hope proven true)* (new — w/ 25:9).
