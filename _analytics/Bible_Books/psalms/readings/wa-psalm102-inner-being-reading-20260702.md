## The reading

**I. Affliction saturating the whole life (vv.3–11).**
"My **days** pass away like **smoke**… My **heart** (H3820, the seat — M47) is **struck down** like grass and has **withered**; I forget to eat my bread… I eat **ashes** like bread and mingle **tears** (H1065, M03) with my drink… My days are like an evening **shadow**; I **wither** like grass" (vv.3–11). The finding: the affliction **pervades every dimension** of the inner being's life — the *heart* withers, the *food* becomes ashes, the *drink* becomes tears, the *days* become smoke and shadow *(stated)*. The operation: the affliction is not localised but **total** — it saturates the heart, the appetite, and the very texture of the days *(inferred: heart-withered, ashes-for-bread, tears-for-drink, days-as-smoke pile up to make the affliction pervade the whole life, not one faculty)*.

**II. The isolation — a lonely bird in waste places (vv.6–7).**
"I am like a **desert owl** of the wilderness, like an owl of the **waste places**; I lie **awake**; I am like a **lonely sparrow** on the housetop" (vv.6–7). The gem-finding: the afflicted inner being's **loneliness** is figured as a **solitary bird** — the owl in the desolate wilderness, the sleepless *lonely sparrow* alone on the roof *(stated)*. The operation: the self's **isolation and sleeplessness** — a creature alone in waste places, awake through the night *(inferred: the owl/sparrow images name a self cut off and unable to rest, the solitude of affliction; cf. Ps 88's darkness-companion)*.

**III. The fleeting self anchored on God's unchanging eternity (vv.11–12, 27).**
"My days are like an evening shadow; I wither like grass. **But you**, O LORD, are enthroned **forever**… you are the **same**, and your years have **no end**" (vv.11–12, 27). The gem-finding: the **withering, fleeting** inner being is set against God's **unchanging eternity** — "you are the *same*" *(stated)*. The operation: the transient self **anchors on the permanent God** — where the self is grass/shadow/smoke (soon gone), God *endures unchanged*, and *that* is the ground the fleeting self stands on *(inferred: the "but you… are the same… your years have no end" turns from the self's brevity to God's permanence as the counter and the hope — the withering self leans on the One who does not wither; cf. Ps 90:1–4)*. The self's very transience drives it to the eternal God *(inferred)*.

## What Psalm 102 says about the inner being
- **Affliction can *saturate the whole life* (vv.3–11).** The heart withers, food becomes ashes, drink becomes tears, days become smoke — the affliction pervades every dimension, not one faculty.
- **Isolation is figured as a *lonely bird* (vv.6–7).** The afflicted self is a solitary owl in waste places, a sleepless sparrow alone on the roof — cut off, unable to rest.
- **The fleeting self *anchors on God's unchanging eternity* (vv.11–12, 27).** Where the inner being is grass, shadow, smoke — soon gone — God *is the same*, his years without end; the transient self leans on the permanent God, and its very brevity drives it there.
