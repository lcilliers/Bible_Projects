## The reading
Psalm 7 puts the inner being *on the stand* — offering itself for God's examination, and watching evil recoil on its author.

**I. Refuge and the oath of innocence (vv.1–5).**
It opens where these psalms keep landing: "O LORD my God, in you I **take refuge** (H2620, M19); save me" (v.1), from a pursuer who would tear the **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) like a lion (v.2). Then a self-imprecating oath: "if there is **wrong** (H5766, M10) in my hands, if I have repaid my friend with evil… let the enemy trample my **soul**… and lay my **glory** (H3519, M22) in the dust" (vv.3–5). The inner being stakes everything on its own **integrity** — inviting ruin if it is guilty *(stated: the conditional curse)*.

**II. Appeal to the righteous Judge who tests hearts (vv.6–11).**
"**Arise**, O LORD… **judge** (H8199, M26) me according to my **righteousness** (H6664, M26) and the **integrity** (H8537, M13) that is in me" (vv.6–8). The ground of appeal is God's own office: "the LORD **judges** the peoples… you who **test** the **hearts** (H3826, the seat — M47), O **righteous** (H6662, M26) God" (vv.8–9). This is the psalm's centre: **God assays the inner being** — heart and mind laid open and tried *(stated: you test hearts)* — and "God **saves the upright in heart** (H3477, M13; H3820, M47)" (v.10). He is "a righteous judge, a God who **feels indignation** (H2194, M02) every day" (v.11) — the divine inner life is *engaged*, not indifferent.

**III. Evil gestates within and recoils on its author (vv.12–16).**
The wicked's inner life is drawn as a **pregnancy**: he "**conceives** evil (H0205, M10), is **pregnant** with **mischief** (H5999, M03), and gives **birth** to **lies** (H8267, M14)" (v.14) — evil is an *inward gestation* that must come out *(inferred: the conception image locates evil's origin in the inner being)*. And it is **self-defeating**: "he makes a **pit**… and falls into the hole he has made; his mischief **returns** upon his own **head**; his violence **descends** on his own skull" (vv.15–16). The structure of wickedness is a **boomerang** — what the inner being breeds falls back on it *(stated: returns on his own head)*.

**IV. Thanksgiving (v.17).**
The close: "I will give **thanks** (H3034, M22) to the LORD for his righteousness, and **sing** (H2167, M42) praise to the name of the LORD Most High" — vindication answered by praise.

## What Psalm 7 says about the inner being
- The inner being is **transparent to God and assayed by him**: he *tests the heart* (v.9) and *saves the upright in heart* (v.10) — the decisive fact about a person is the inward state God examines, not the accusation others bring.
- It can therefore **offer its integrity for examination** (vv.3–8): the confidence to invite God's scrutiny — even to curse itself if guilty — is a distinct posture of the clear conscience.
- **Evil originates inwardly and is self-destructive** (vv.14–16): mischief is *conceived, carried, and born* within, and then **recoils on its author's own head** — the inner being that breeds evil digs its own pit.
- The divine inner life is **morally engaged** — a righteous judge who "feels indignation every day" (v.11) — not neutral toward cruelty.
