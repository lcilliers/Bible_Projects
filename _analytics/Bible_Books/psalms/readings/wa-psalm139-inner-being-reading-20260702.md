# Psalm 139 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **The self fully *known*, inescapably *accompanied*, intricately *made* — and its final self-offering to be *searched*. A major cornerstone.** Psalm 139 is the Psalter's deepest meditation on the inner being *before God's knowledge*. Its findings: the self **exhaustively known from within** (vv.1–6); the self that **cannot flee God's presence** (vv.7–12); the self **formed and foreknown before birth** (vv.13–16); and the climactic move — the fully-known self **inviting God to search it further** (vv.23–24).

## The reading

**I. The self exhaustively known from within (vv.1–6).**
"O LORD, you have **searched me and known me**! You know when I sit down and when I rise up; you **discern my thoughts from afar**… you are **acquainted with all my ways**" (vv.1–3). The gem-finding: the inner being is **known utterly** — its sitting and rising, its *thoughts discerned from afar*, its every way traced *(stated)*. The operation: the self reckons that its ***interior is completely open* to God** — not behaviour only but *thoughts*, and those known "from afar" (before they surface); there is no hidden chamber, no unread thought; the inner being lives *wholly exposed* to a knowing that precedes even its own self-awareness *(inferred: "you discern my thoughts from afar… acquainted with all my ways" makes the self *transparent* to God — the interior fully read, the thought known before it is thought; "such knowledge is too wonderful for me" (v.6) is the self's awe at being *this* known; cf. Ps 44:21 "he knows the secrets of the heart", Heb 4:13)*.

**II. The self that cannot flee God's presence (vv.7–12).**
"Where shall I go from your Spirit? Or where shall I **flee from your presence**? If I ascend to heaven, **you are there**! If I make my bed in **Sheol**, you are there!… even the **darkness is not dark to you**" (vv.7–12). The gem-finding: there is **nowhere the self could go to escape God** — height, depth, darkness, the far sea — God is *there* *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being confronts the **impossibility of *hiding* from God** — and, in this psalm's key, receives it not as threat but as *inescapable accompaniment*; wherever the self flees, even to Sheol or darkness, it finds God *already there*, holding it ("your right hand shall hold me," v.10) *(inferred: "if I make my bed in Sheol, you are there… the darkness is not dark to you" turns the impossibility-of-flight into *presence-in-every-place* — the self can never be *alone* or *lost*; the same omnipresence that forbids hiding also guarantees company; cf. Ps 23:4, Rom 8:38–39)*.

**III. The self formed and foreknown before birth (vv.13–16).**
"For you **formed my inward parts**; you **knitted me together** in my mother's womb. I praise you, for I am **fearfully and wonderfully made**… your eyes saw my **unformed substance**; in your book were written… the **days that were formed for me**, when as yet there was none of them" (vv.13–16). The gem-finding: the self is God's **intimate handiwork** — knitted in the womb, *fearfully and wonderfully made* — and **foreknown before it existed**, its days written before one was lived *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being marvels at its own ***createdness and foreknownness*** — it did not make itself; it is *woven* by God, *wonderful* in its making, and *known from before its origin*; the self's very existence is God's craft and God's foreknowledge, and the fitting response is praise *(inferred: "you formed my inward parts… fearfully and wonderfully made… days written when as yet there was none" grounds the self's being in God's *forming and foreknowing* — the inner being is neither accident nor self-origin but intimate, praised handiwork; "my soul knows it very well" (v.14) is the self's inward assent to its own wonder; cf. Ps 119:73, Jer 1:5)*.

**IV. The fully-known self *invites the searching* (vv.23–24).**
"**Search me, O God, and know my heart** (H3824, M47)! **Try me and know my thoughts**! And **see if there be any grievous way in me**, and **lead me in the way everlasting**!" (vv.23–24). The gem-finding — the psalm's climactic turn: the self that *began* by marvelling it is searched and known (v.1) now **asks to be searched** — "search me… try me… see if there be any grievous way" *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's response to being fully known is **not to hide but to *open itself further*** — it welcomes, even *requests*, the divine examination, wanting its *own hidden faults exposed* for the sake of being *led rightly*; a self so secure in God's knowing-and-loving that it makes itself *voluntarily transparent*, inviting God to find and correct what the self cannot see in itself *(inferred: the movement from "you have searched me" (v.1, fact) to "search me" (v.23, invitation) is the whole psalm's arc — the self does not resent being known but *embraces* it, asking God to try it and expose any "grievous way"; transparency becomes *desirable* because the searcher is trusted and the aim is "the way everlasting"; the summit of trust — to want to be fully seen; cf. Ps 26:2 "prove me… try my heart", John 3:21)*.

## What Psalm 139 says about the inner being
- **The self is *exhaustively known from within* (vv.1–6).** Not behaviour only but *thoughts discerned from afar* — the interior wholly transparent to a knowing that precedes the self's own awareness; no unread thought, no hidden chamber.
- **The self *cannot flee God — and is never alone* (vv.7–12).** Nowhere (height, depth, darkness, far sea) is outside God's presence; the impossibility of hiding is also the guarantee of accompaniment — the self can never be lost, for God is *already there*, holding it.
- **The self is *formed and foreknown before birth* (vv.13–16).** God's intimate handiwork — knitted in the womb, *fearfully and wonderfully made*, its days written before one was lived; the inner being's existence is God's craft and foreknowledge, and praise is the fitting response.
- **The fully-known self *invites the searching* (vv.23–24).** The psalm's summit: from "you have searched me" to "*search* me" — the self opens itself further, *asking* God to try it and expose its hidden faults for the sake of being led rightly; the deepest trust is to *want* to be fully seen.

The v.1→v.23 arc ("you have searched me" → "search me") is the key inner-being movement: being known, embraced rather than resented, turned into an *invitation*. The finding is that self-submission, not the imprecation's content.
