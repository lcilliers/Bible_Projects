## The reading

**I. "Unite my heart" — the divided heart gathered into one (v.11).**
"Teach me your way, O LORD, that I may walk in your **truth** (H0571, M13); **unite** (*yachad* — make one, join together) my **heart** (H3824, the seat — M47) to **fear** (H3372, M01) your name" (v.11). The gem-finding: the inner being asks to have its heart **made *one*** — which implies the heart is **divided, scattered** and needs **gathering** *(stated: "unite my heart" presupposes a heart not yet single)*. The operation: the **unification of a fragmented heart** — the many pulls of the inner being drawn together into **a single focus, the fear of God's name** *(inferred: yachad "unite/make one" gathers the divided heart from its scatter into one object; the inverse of the "double heart" of Ps 12)*. And crucially, the self **cannot do this to itself** — it *asks God* to unite it: the gathering of the divided heart is **God's work requested**, not the self's achievement *(inferred: "*unite* my heart" is a petition, the self handing its fragmentation to God to be made whole)*.

**II. Thanks with the *whole* heart — the fruit (v.12).**
"I give **thanks** (H3034, M22) to you, O Lord my God, **with my whole heart** (H3824, M47), and I will glorify your name forever" (v.12). The finding: the response is from the **whole, undivided heart** — the *fruit* of the unification asked in v.11 *(stated)*. The operation: the **united heart issues in whole-hearted thanks** — where v.11 asked for the heart to be made one, v.12 gives thanks from that oneness; the gathered heart is a heart that can worship *entire*, not in fragments *(inferred: "unite my heart" (v.11) → "with my whole heart" (v.12) — the petition and its fruit)*.

**III. The soul lifted up to God (v.4).**
"Gladden the **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) of your servant, for **to you, O Lord, do I lift up my soul** (H5375, M19, *nasa nephesh*)" (v.4). The finding: the inner being **lifts up its soul to God** — the aspiration/longing directed upward *(stated; the *nasa-nephesh* idiom of Ps 25:1, 143:8)*. The operation: the self **raises its whole inner being toward God** as the ground of its gladdening — "gladden my soul, *for* to you I lift it up" *(inferred: the lifted soul is the posture within which it asks to be gladdened)*.

## What Psalm 86 says about the inner being
- **The heart can be *divided*, and needs to be *united* (v.11).** "Unite my heart to fear your name" presupposes a scattered heart pulled many ways; the operation is its *gathering* into a single focus on God. And the self *cannot unify itself* — it asks God to make it one; the wholeness is God's work requested.
- **The united heart issues in *whole-hearted* thanks (v.12).** Where v.11 asks for oneness, v.12 gives thanks "with my whole heart" — the gathered heart can worship *entire*, not in fragments. Petition and fruit.
- **The inner being *lifts up its soul* to God (v.4).** The whole self raised toward God — the posture within which it asks to be gladdened.
