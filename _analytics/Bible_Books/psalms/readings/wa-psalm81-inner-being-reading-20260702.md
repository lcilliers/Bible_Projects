## The reading

**I. Refusal to listen → given over to the stubborn heart (vv.11–12).**
"But my people **did not listen** (H8085) to my voice; Israel **would not submit** (H0014, *abah*) to me. So **I gave them over to their stubborn hearts** (H8307, M30, *sheriruth* + H3820, the seat — M47), to follow their **own counsels** (H4156, M17, *moetsah*)" (vv.11–12). The gem-finding: the judgment on the non-listening inner being is to be **handed back to its own hardened heart** — "given over to their stubborn hearts, to follow their own counsels" *(stated)*. The operation: **refusal to listen → abandonment to the self** — God's deepest response to the closed ear is not to force it but to **let it follow its own stubborn heart**, which *is* the punishment *(inferred: "would not submit… so I gave them over to their stubborn hearts" makes the being-left-to-oneself the consequence of the not-listening — a self-perpetuating captivity to one's own hardness)*. To not listen is to be **shut up in one's own counsels** *(inferred)*.

**II. Receptivity met by filling — "open your mouth wide" (v.10).**
"I am the LORD your God, who brought you up out of Egypt. **Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it**" (v.10). The gem-finding: the inner being that **opens itself wide** to God is **filled** by him *(stated)*. The operation: **receptivity is the condition of being filled** — the *open* mouth is met by God's abundance, the exact inverse of the *closed*, stubborn heart of v.12 *(inferred: the "open wide → I will fill" of v.10 contrasts the un-submitting, self-following heart of vv.11–12 — the open self is filled, the closed self is left empty to itself)*. The self's part is simply to **open**; the filling is God's *(inferred; cf. Ps 40:6's dug ear — receptivity as the thing God seeks)*.

**III. The forfeited good — God's thwarted longing (vv.13, 16).**
"**Oh, that my people would listen to me**, that Israel would walk in my ways!… But he would **feed** you with the **finest of the wheat**, and with honey from the rock I would **satisfy** you" (vv.13, 16). The finding: God **yearns** for the self to listen — "*oh, that* my people would listen" — because a **satisfaction is waiting** that the not-listening forfeits *(stated)*. The operation: the refusal to listen **forfeits the good God longed to give** — the finest wheat, honey from the rock, a satisfaction held out but not received *(inferred: the "oh that… I would feed/satisfy you" names the thwarted good, lost by the closed ear)*. The inner being's non-listening does not merely disobey; it **loses the feast** *(inferred)*.

## What Psalm 81 says about the inner being
- **The judgment on the non-listening self is to be *given over to its own stubborn heart* (vv.11–12).** God's response to the closed ear is not to force it but to let it follow its own hardened counsels — abandonment to the self, a self-perpetuating captivity to one's own stubbornness. To not listen is to be shut up in one's own heart.
- **Receptivity — the open self — is met by *filling* (v.10).** "Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it." The inner being's part is simply to open; the filling is God's. The open self is filled; the closed, stubborn self is left empty to itself.
- **Not-listening *forfeits the waiting good* (vv.13, 16).** God yearns for the self to listen because a satisfaction — the finest wheat, honey from the rock — is held out; the refusal loses the feast, not merely disobeys.
