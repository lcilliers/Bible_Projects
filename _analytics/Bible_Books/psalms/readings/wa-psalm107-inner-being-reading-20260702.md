- **The soul at the end of itself cries out and is heard — four times over. Book V opens.** Psalm 107 is built on a fourfold refrain: four portraits of the self *in extremity*, each turning on "**Then they cried to the LORD in their trouble, and he delivered them**" (vv.6, 13, 19, 28). Its findings are operations of the **inner being pressed to its limit** — the *fainting/longing* soul (vv.5, 9), the *bowed-down* heart (v.12), the soul that *loathes food* at death's gate (v.18), the *melting, wits'-end* self in the storm (vv.26–27) — and the single move that answers each: **the cry that is heard**.

## The reading

**I. The cry from extremity that is heard — the fourfold refrain (vv.6, 13, 19, 28).**
Four times: "**Then they cried** (H2199, *zaaq*) **to the LORD in their trouble, and he delivered them** from their distress" (vv.6, 13, 19, 28). The gem-finding: the one operation that recurs at every depth is the **cry of the self at the end of itself** — and it is *always heard* *(stated)*. The operation: **extremity drives the inner being to cry out, and the cry is the hinge of deliverance** — wanderer, prisoner, fool, sailor all reach the point where nothing is left *but* to cry, and *that* cry turns the whole vignette *(inferred: the refrain makes the cry-from-the-bottom the universal inner-being move — not strength or merit but the honest cry of the undone self; cf. Ps 34:6 "this poor man cried")*.

**II. The fainting, longing soul satisfied (vv.5, 9).**
"hungry and thirsty, their **soul** (H5315, M47) **fainted** within them" (v.5)… "For he **satisfies** the **longing** soul, and the hungry soul he **fills** with good things" (v.9). The gem-finding: the *longing* soul — fainting with hunger and thirst — is **satisfied and filled** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being has a **capacity of *longing* that only God fills** — the fainting, craving soul is not rebuked but *satisfied*, its emptiness met with good *(inferred: "he satisfies the longing soul… fills the hungry soul with good" makes the soul's longing the very thing God meets; the appetite is answered, not shamed — contrast the disordered craving of Ps 106:15; here the honest longing is filled)*.

**III. The bowed-down heart; the soul that loathes food at death's gate (vv.12, 18).**
"So he **bowed** their **hearts** (H3820, M47) **down** (H3665) with hard labor; they fell down, with none to help" (v.12); and of the fools in their affliction, "their **soul loathed** (H8581, M06) any kind of food, and they drew near to the gates of death" (v.18). The finding: affliction **bows the heart down** and drives the soul to **loathe food** — the body's sustenance made repellent by inner distress *(stated)*. The operation: extremity **reaches into the inner being's very *appetites*** — the heart is pressed low, and the soul's aversion to food marks a self so afflicted it turns from life itself, at the gates of death *(inferred: "their soul loathed any food… near the gates of death" shows distress reaching down into appetite — the afflicted self loses even the will to eat; cf. Ps 102:4 "I forget to eat my bread")*.

**IV. The melting, wits'-end self in the storm (vv.26–27, 30).**
"They mounted up to heaven; they went down to the depths; their **courage** (H5315, *nephesh* — the self/soul — M47) **melted away** in their evil plight; they **reeled and staggered** like drunken men and were **at their wits' end**" (vv.26–27); then, delivered, "they were **glad** (H8055, M04) that the waters were quiet, and he brought them to their **desired** (H2656) haven" (v.30). The gem-finding: the storm-tossed self's **very courage *melts*** — the inner being unstrung, reeling, *at its wits' end* — and after the cry, made **glad** at the hushed sea, brought to its *desired* haven *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being can be **dissolved past its own competence** — "at their wits' end," every inner resource exhausted — and the cry from *that* place is answered with stillness and gladness *(inferred: "their courage melted… at their wits' end" names the total exhaustion of the self's coping, the precise point where the cry becomes the only move; the "glad… desired haven" is the inner rest on the far side; cf. Ps 46:10 the stilled)*.

## What Psalm 107 says about the inner being
- **The *cry from the bottom* is the universal move — and it is heard (vv.6, 13, 19, 28).** At every depth the one operation left to the undone self is the honest cry, and that cry is the hinge of deliverance; not strength or merit but the cry of the end-of-itself self.
- **The soul's *longing* is met, not shamed (vv.5, 9).** The fainting, hungry soul is satisfied and filled with good — the inner being's honest appetite answered by God.
- **Affliction reaches into the *appetites* (vv.12, 18).** The heart is bowed down; the soul loathes food at death's gate — distress penetrates to where the self turns from life itself.
- **The self can be *dissolved past its own competence* (vv.26–27).** Courage melts, the self reels "at its wits' end" — and from that exhaustion the cry is answered with stillness and gladness, brought to its desired haven.

## Honest notes
- **A coherent inner-being architecture:** four states of the self at its limit (fainting / bowed / loathing / melting), each resolved by the *same* cry-and-deliverance. The psalm is practically a *taxonomy of the inner being in extremity* — a strong Book-V-opening datum, and it closes urging the wise to "consider the steadfast love of the LORD" (v.43).
