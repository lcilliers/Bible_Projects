## The reading

**I. Refuge as a sheltering *until* the storm passes (v.1).**
"For in you my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) **takes refuge** (H2620, M19, *chasah*); in the shadow of your **wings** I will take refuge, **till the storms of destruction pass by**" (v.1). The operation: the inner being **takes cover under the wings for the *duration* of the danger** — refuge here is explicitly **temporal**, a sheltering *until* the storm passes *(stated)*. The finding: refuge is not a permanent hiding but an **active waiting-out** — the soul lodges under the wings *through* the danger, expecting it to pass *(inferred: the "till the storms pass by" makes the refuge a bounded, waited-out shelter)*.

**II. From the bowed soul to the fixed heart (vv.6–7).**
"They set a net for my steps; my **soul** (H5315, M47) was **bowed down** (H3721, *kaphaph* — pressed low). They dug a pit… But now: **My heart** (H3820, the seat — M47) is **steadfast** (H3559, *kun*/*nakon* — fixed, established), O God, my heart is steadfast!" (vv.6–7). The gem-finding: the inner being moves from a **soul *pressed low*** (v.6) to a **heart *fixed and established*** (v.7) — and the affirmation is **doubled** for emphasis *(stated: "my heart is steadfast… my heart is steadfast")*. The operation: the same inner being **shifts from bowed to settled** — *nakon* names a heart that no longer wavers, a **fixed core** *(inferred: the bowed soul of v.6 and the fixed heart of v.7 are the one self, moved from pressed to established)*. And the fixity is the **ground of praise**: "my heart is steadfast… **I will sing and make melody**" — the settled heart *issues in* song *(stated)*.

**III. "Awake, my glory!" — the inner being rousing itself (v.8).**
"**Awake** (H5782, M25, *ur*), my **glory** (H3519, M22, *kabod* — here the inner self/soul, as Ps 30:12)! Awake, O harp and lyre! **I will awake the dawn**!" (v.8). The gem-operation: the inner being **commands *itself* to wake** — "awake, my glory" is self-address in the **positive mode**, the self *rousing* its own inner being to praise *(stated)*. The finding: where Ps 42's self-address *interrogated* a downcast soul, here the inner being **stirs itself to action** — waking its "glory" (the God-facing inner self) and even, in eagerness, **waking the dawn itself** rather than being woken by it *(inferred: "I will awake the dawn" reverses the usual order — the roused self outruns the morning)*. The steadfast heart (v.7) does not wait to feel like praising; it **rouses itself** *(inferred)*.

## What Psalm 57 says about the inner being
- **Refuge is a sheltering *until* the storm passes (v.1).** The inner being takes cover under the wings for the duration of the danger — an active waiting-out, not a permanent hiding; it expects the storm to pass.
- **The inner being can move from *bowed* to *fixed* (vv.6–7).** From a soul pressed low to a heart *steadfast* (*nakon*) — doubled — the same self shifting from pressed to settled; and the fixed heart is the ground from which song issues.
- **The inner being can rouse *itself* to praise (v.8).** "Awake, my glory!" — self-address in the positive mode; the steadfast heart stirs its own inner self to action rather than waiting to feel like it, outrunning even the dawn.
