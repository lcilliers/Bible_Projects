## The reading

**I. The unsteady, unfaithful heart (vv.8, 37).**
The rebellious generation is diagnosed inwardly: "a generation whose **heart** (H3820, the seat — M47) was **not steadfast** (H3559, *nakon* — fixed, established), whose **spirit** (H7307, the seat) was **not faithful** (H0539, M13, *aman*) to God" (v.8); and again, "their **heart** was **not steadfast** toward him; they were **not faithful** to his covenant" (v.37). The gem-finding: the mark of the rebellious inner being is a heart that **will not stay *fixed*** (*nakon*) and a spirit that **will not stay *faithful*** *(stated)*. The operation: **instability** — the heart keeps *shifting*, never established toward God *(inferred: "not steadfast/nakon" names an unfixed heart, the opposite of Ps 57:7's doubled "my heart is *nakon*" and 112:7's fixed heart)*. Rebellion is, at root, a heart that **won't hold still** toward God *(inferred)*.

**II. The mouth–heart split (vv.36–37).**
"But they **flattered** (H6601, *patah*) him with their **mouths**; they **lied** to him with their tongues. Their **heart** was not steadfast toward him" (vv.36–37). The finding: the inner being can **flatter God with the mouth while the heart is unfixed** — outward words masking an unsteady heart *(stated)*. The operation: the **speech–heart disjunction** (cf. Ps 12, 28:3, 62:4) — the tongue's flattery contradicting the heart's instability *(inferred: the "flattered with mouths… heart not steadfast" pairs false speech with an unfixed heart)*.

**III. The craving heart that tests God (v.18).**
"They **tested** (H5254, *nasah*) God **in their heart** (H3824, M47) by demanding the food they **craved** (H8378, M28, *ta'avah*)" (v.18). The finding: the inner being's **craving** (*ta'avah* — the appetite/desire) **drove it to *test* God** — putting God on trial out of appetite *(stated)*. The operation: disordered **craving becomes a testing** — the demanding desire turns the inner being against God, making it *try* him rather than trust him *(inferred: "tested God in their heart… the food they craved" makes the appetite the engine of the testing)*.

**IV. "He remembered that they were but flesh" (v.39).**
"He **remembered** (H2142, M41) that they were but **flesh** (H1320, the seat — M47, *basar*), a **wind** that passes and comes not again" (v.39). The gem-finding: God's compassion is grounded in his **accounting of the self's frailty** — he *remembered* they were "but flesh," a passing wind *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's **weakness** (flesh, wind) is **factored in** by God — his restraint flows from *remembering* how frail the creature is *(inferred: "he remembered they were but flesh" makes the divine compassion turn on a reckoning of human frailty; a tender datum — the self's weakness is *seen and allowed for*, cf. Ps 103:14 "he knows our frame")*.

**V. Transmission — that the next generation set its *hope* and not *forget* (vv.7–8), and the upright-hearted shepherd (v.72).**
"So that they should **set their hope** in God and **not forget** the works of God… and that they should **not be like their fathers**" (vv.7–8). The finding: the point of handing on the story is that the next inner being **set its hope** and **not forget** — hope and memory are the inner things transmitted *(stated; cf. Ps 48:13, 71:18)*. And the psalm closes on the counter-image of the unsteady heart: "With **upright heart** (*tom levav*) he shepherded them" (v.72) — the shepherd's **integrity of heart** against the people's instability *(stated)*.

## What Psalm 78 says about the inner being
- **Rebellion is, at root, an *unsteady heart* (vv.8, 37).** A heart not *fixed* (*nakon*) and a spirit not *faithful* — the inner being that will not hold still toward God. Instability is the mark, against the steadfast/fixed heart (57:7, 112:7).
- **The mouth can flatter while the heart is unfixed (vv.36–37).** Outward words mask an unsteady heart — the speech–heart disjunction.
- **Craving turns to *testing* God (v.18).** Disordered appetite drives the inner being to *try* God rather than trust him — the demanding desire the engine of the testing.
- **God accounts for the self's *frailty* (v.39).** His compassion is grounded in *remembering* the inner being is "but flesh," a passing wind — the self's weakness is seen and allowed for.
- **Hope and memory are what is transmitted (vv.7–8).** The story is handed on so the next inner being sets its *hope* and does not *forget* — against the counter-image of the shepherd's *upright heart* (v.72).
