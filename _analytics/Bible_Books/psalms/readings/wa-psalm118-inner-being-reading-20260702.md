- **From distress to fearlessness; the deliberate choice of refuge; God as strength *and song*.** Psalm 118 is the climactic Hallel thanksgiving. Its inner-being findings: the self **freed from the fear of man** by God's answer (vv.5–6); the **deliberate valuation that refuge in the LORD is *better* than trust in man** (vv.8–9); and God become the self's **strength *and song*** (v.14).

## The reading

**I. From distress to fearlessness (vv.5–6).**
"Out of my **distress** (H4712, M24) I **called** on the LORD; the LORD answered me and **set me free**. The LORD is on my side; **I will not fear** (H3372, M01). What can man do to me?" (vv.5–6). The gem-finding: the answered cry **converts distress into *fearlessness*** — "I will not fear; what can man do to me?" *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being, once God's presence is assured ("the LORD is on my side"), is **released from the fear of *man*** — the vertical security *cancels* the horizontal threat; the self reasons from God's nearness to its own fearlessness *(inferred: "the LORD is on my side; I will not fear. What can man do to me?" makes the fearlessness a *derived* conclusion — the assured presence of God *dissolves* the fear of what man can do; cf. Ps 27:1, 56:11 "in God I trust; I shall not be afraid. What can man do to me?", 112:7)*.

**II. The deliberate valuation — refuge in the LORD is *better* (vv.8–9).**
"It is **better** (H2896, M04) to **take refuge** (H2620, M19) in the LORD than to **trust** (H0982, M19) in **man**. It is better to take refuge in the LORD than to trust in **princes**" (vv.8–9). The gem-finding: the inner being makes a **comparative judgment** — refuge in the LORD is *better* than trust in man, even in *princes* *(stated)*. The operation: the self **deliberately weighs and *chooses* where to place its trust** — "better… than" is a *valuation*, ranking God above the strongest human recourse; the inner being sets its refuge by a reasoned preference, not drift *(inferred: the doubled "better to take refuge in the LORD than to trust in man/princes" is an act of *evaluation* — the self appraises its options and ranks God first; central verses of the Psalter (v.8 is its midpoint), and a clean datum on trust as a *chosen* orientation; cf. Ps 146:3 "put not your trust in princes", 40:4)*.

**III. God as strength *and song* (v.14).**
"The LORD is my **strength** (H5797, M23) **and my song** (H2176, M42); he has become my **salvation**" (v.14). The gem-finding: God is the self's **strength *and* its song** — both its power and its music *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being finds in God **both what *upholds* it and what it *sings*** — the source of its strength *is* the theme of its joy; power and praise converge in one object *(inferred: "my strength and my song" fuses the sustaining and the celebrating — God is not only the self's support but the *content* of its gladness; the strength that holds becomes the song that rejoices; cf. Ex 15:2, Isa 12:2)*.

## What Psalm 118 says about the inner being
- **God's assured presence *cancels the fear of man* (vv.5–6).** From distress the self calls, is set free, and reasons to fearlessness — "the LORD is on my side; what can man do to me?"; the vertical security dissolves the horizontal threat.
- **Trust is a *deliberately chosen* orientation (vv.8–9).** "Better to take refuge in the LORD than to trust in man/princes" — the inner being *weighs and ranks* where to place its refuge, choosing God above the strongest human recourse.
- **God is *strength and song* together (v.14).** The self finds in God both what upholds it and what it sings — the source of its power *is* the theme of its joy; strength and praise converge.

## Honest notes

- **Scope honesty:** much of the psalm is corporate liturgy and the rejected-stone/gate-of-righteousness sequence (vv.19–29, the arena of thanksgiving procession).
