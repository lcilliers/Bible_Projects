# Psalm 31 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **The psalm of entrustment.** A long lament-to-trust that oscillates between the depths (social death, whole-person wasting, "terror on every side") and renewed trust — held together by its signature act: **"Into your hand I commit my spirit" (v.5), "My times are in your hand" (v.15).** The inner being **deposits itself and its whole life-span into God's hand** rather than the enemy's. Its two peaks are that **entrustment** and the **panic-verdict overturned** — "I had said in my alarm, 'I am cut off from your sight.' But you heard" (v.22). Across it, the inner being is named by all three of its seats — **spirit** (v.5), **soul** (vv.7, 9), **heart** (v.24).

## The reading

**I. Entrustment — the spirit committed into God's hand (vv.1–5).**
"In you, O LORD, do I **take refuge** (H2620, M19)… Be a **rock** (H4581, M19) of refuge for me… **Into your hand I commit** (*paqad* — deposit for safekeeping) my **spirit** (H7307, the seat — *ruach*); you have **redeemed** (H6299, M38) me, O LORD, **faithful** (H0571, M13) God" (vv.1–5). The peak inner-being act: the innermost self (*ruach*) is **entrusted into God's hand as one deposits a treasure for safekeeping** *(stated; paqad is the entrusting word)*. And it rests not on the self's strength but on God's proven character — "you *have redeemed* me, faithful God" — the entrustment leans on **God's faithfulness already shown** *(stated)*.

**II. Trust against idols; the joy of being *seen and known* (vv.6–8).**
"I **hate** (H8130, M06) those who pay regard to worthless idols, but I **trust** (H0982, M19) in the LORD. I will **rejoice** (H1523, M04) and be **glad** (H8055, M04) in your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05), **because you have seen my affliction; you have known** (*yada*) the **distress** (H6869, M03) of my **soul** (H5315, M47)" (vv.6–7). The gem: the inner being's joy is grounded in **being seen and known** — "you have *known the distress of my soul*" *(stated)*. God's **intimate knowledge of the inner being's straits** is itself the reason for gladness (the God-sees thread, Ps 10:14) — *because* God has seen and known, the soul can rejoice *(inferred: the "because" makes being-known the ground of the joy)*.

**III. The whole-person wasting and the social death (vv.9–13).**
Then the plunge. "my **eye** is wasted from **grief** (H3708, M02, *kaas*); my **soul** (H5315, M47) and my **body**… my **life** (H2416, M25) is spent with **sorrow** (H3015, M03)… my **strength** (H3581, M23) **fails because of my iniquity** (H5771, M10); my bones waste" (vv.9–10). The inner being dissolves **whole** — eye, soul, body, strength, bones — and, tellingly, it **implicates itself**: the strength fails "because of my *iniquity*," not only from outward affliction *(stated: the wasting is tied to the self's own guilt)*. Then social annihilation: "I have become a **reproach** (H2781, M06)… an object of **dread** (H6343, M01) to my acquaintances; those who see me flee. I have been **forgotten** like one who is dead; like a **broken vessel** (v.12). For I hear the whispering of many — **terror** (H4032, M01) on every side!" (vv.11–13). The gem: the inner being's experience of being **reduced to a discarded thing** — dreaded, shunned, *forgotten as if dead*, a **broken/useless vessel** *(stated)*. This is the pain of the self erased from others' regard.

**IV. The pivot — "But I trust… You are my God"; my times in your hand (vv.14–16).**
Against all of it: "**But I trust** (H0982, M19) in you, O LORD; I **say**, 'You are my God.' My **times** are in your **hand**" (vv.14–15). The emphatic turn — trust declared **at the lowest point**, and made personal ("You are my God") *(stated)*. And the entrustment of v.5 widens: not only the *spirit* but the **times** — the whole course and moments of the life — are placed **in God's hand** *(stated)*. The **two hands** frame the psalm: the inner being commits itself and its times to *God's* hand (vv.5, 15) and pleads rescue from the *enemy's* hand (vv.8, 15) — **security is a matter of *whose hand* holds the self** *(inferred: the same "hand" motif opposes God's keeping to the enemy's grip)*. Then the counter to every hidden face: "Make your **face shine** on your servant; save me in your steadfast love" (v.16) — the plea for God's **lit** face (the inner-light thread, Ps 13:3, 18:28) *(stated)*.

**V. Goodness stored up; the inner being hidden in God's presence (vv.19–20).**
"How **abundant** is your **goodness**, which you have **stored up** (*tsaphan* — treasured/hidden) for those who **fear** (H3373, M01) you… In the cover of your **presence** you **hide** them from the plots of men; you store them in your **shelter** from the **strife** (H7379, M02) of tongues" (vv.19–20). Two gems. God's goodness is **treasured up in reserve** for the God-fearers — a laid-up store, not just present help *(stated)*. And the inner being is **hidden *in* God's presence** — sheltered "from the strife of tongues" not by the enemies' absence but by being **concealed in God's own presence** *(stated; the presence is the hiding-place, cf. Ps 27:5)*.

**VI. The panic-verdict overturned — "I am cut off" / "but you heard" (vv.21–22).**
"Blessed be the LORD, for he has **wondrously** (H6381, M04) shown his **steadfast love** (H2617, M05)… I had **said in my alarm** (*chaphaz* — panic), 'I am **cut off** from your sight.' **But you heard** (H8085, M41) the voice of my **pleas** (H8469, M21)" (vv.21–22). The gem: the inner being's **panic-driven verdict** — "I am cut off from your eyes" — is **a false read**, corrected by the reality "**but you heard**" *(stated)*. In alarm the self **hastily concludes God has abandoned it**, and that conclusion is overturned *(inferred: the "but" reverses the panic-verdict)*. Like Ps 30:6's presumption, this is a **fear-distorted misjudgement of God** — here the misjudgement is despair rather than complacency, and it too is answered by God's hearing (the "he has heard" turn of Ps 22:24, 28:6).

**VII. The closing exhortation — be strong, take courage, wait (vv.23–24).**
"**Love** (H0157, *aheb*) the LORD, all you his **saints** (H2623, M05)!… Be **strong** (H2388, M23), and let your **heart** (H3824, the seat — M47) **take courage** (H0553, M23), all you who **wait** (H3176, M18, *yachal*) for the LORD!" (vv.23–24). The inner being turns outward, **calling the community to the same postures it has learned** — to love, to be strong, to let the heart take courage, and to **wait** *(stated)*. As in Ps 27:14, the psalm ends with the self exhorting others' hearts to courage and waiting *(inferred: the self-address of 27:14 here becomes address to all the saints)*.

## What Psalm 31 says about the inner being
- **The inner being can *entrust itself* into God's hand (vv.5, 15).** "Into your hand I commit my spirit… my times are in your hand" — the innermost self and the whole life-span deposited with God for safekeeping, resting on his proven faithfulness. Security is a matter of *whose hand* holds the self — God's, not the enemy's.
- **Being *seen and known* is the ground of joy (v.7).** "You have known the distress of my soul" — the inner being rejoices *because* God intimately knows its straits.
- **Distress dissolves the whole person, and the self implicates itself (vv.9–10).** Eye, soul, body, strength, bones waste — and "my strength fails *because of my iniquity*": the wasting is not only from without.
- **The self can be reduced to a discarded thing (vv.11–12).** Reproach, an object of dread, forgotten as if dead, a broken vessel — the pain of erasure from others' regard.
- **Trust is declared at the lowest point (v.14).** "But I trust in you… You are my God" — the pivot against all the evidence of the depths.
- **The inner being is hidden *in* God's presence (v.20).** Sheltered from the strife of tongues not by the enemies' removal but by concealment in God's own presence; his goodness is treasured up in reserve.
- **Panic produces false verdicts about God — corrected by his hearing (v.22).** "I am cut off from your sight" is the alarm's misjudgement; "but you heard" overturns it (as complacency was overturned in Ps 30).
- **The inner being names itself by all three seats — spirit, soul, heart (vv.5, 7, 24)** — and, having learned to wait, calls others' hearts to strength and courage.
