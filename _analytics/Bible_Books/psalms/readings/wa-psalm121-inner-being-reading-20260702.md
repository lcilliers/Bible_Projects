## The reading

**I. The self lifts its eyes and *locates its help* (vv.1–2).**
"I **lift up my eyes** to the hills. **From where does my help come?** My **help comes from the LORD**, who made heaven and earth" (vv.1–2). The gem-finding: the self **asks itself where its help is, and answers** — the lifted eyes moving past the hills to God *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being performs a **deliberate redirection of its gaze** — from the hills (whether threat or false refuge) to the LORD; a self-interrogation ("from where…?") resolved by a self-answer ("from the LORD") *(inferred: "I lift up my eyes… from where does my help come? My help comes from the LORD" is an inner dialogue of faith — the self questions its own dependence and *reorients* it to God, past every lesser source; the lifted eyes enact the turn, cf. Ps 123:1, 25:15 "my eyes are ever toward the LORD")*.

**II. The settled security of the kept (vv.3–8).**
"He who **keeps** (H8104, M30) you will not **slumber**… The LORD is your **keeper**… The LORD will keep your **life** (H5315, M47)… your going out and your coming in" (vv.3–8). The finding: the self is assured it is **kept** — comprehensively, by one who never sleeps *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **rests in being *kept*** — the sixfold "keep" settles the pilgrim's anxiety into security; the self is not self-guarding but *guarded*, and can therefore be at rest *(inferred: the piling of "keeps… will not slumber… your keeper… keep your life… going out and coming in" functions to *quiet the self* — the effect on the inner being is settled security, the anxious pilgrim stilled by the unsleeping keeper; the keeping is God's arena-action, its inner-being fruit is rest; cf. Ps 4:8, 127:2)*.

## What Psalm 121 says about the inner being
- **The self *reorients its gaze* to locate its help (vv.1–2).** An inner dialogue of faith — "from where does my help come? From the LORD" — the lifted eyes moving past every lesser source to God; dependence questioned and *redirected*.
- **The inner being *rests in being kept* (vv.3–8).** Not self-guarding but guarded by an unsleeping keeper — the sixfold "keep" quiets the anxious pilgrim into settled security.
