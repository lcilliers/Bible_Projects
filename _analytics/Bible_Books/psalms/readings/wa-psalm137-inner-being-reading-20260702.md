## The reading

**I. Grief that suspends song and refuses to fake joy (vv.1–4).**
"By the waters of Babylon, there we **sat down and wept**, when we **remembered Zion**. On the willows there we **hung up our lyres**. For there our captors required of us songs… 'Sing us one of the songs of Zion!' **How shall we sing the LORD's song in a foreign land?**" (vv.1–4). The gem-finding: memory of Zion produces **tears**, the harps are **hung up** — music suspended — and the self **cannot and will not perform joy on demand** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being in real grief **loses (and guards) its capacity for song** — it will not counterfeit mirth for its captors; the honest self *cannot* sing joy while the heart mourns, and refuses to *fake* it ("how shall we sing… in a foreign land?") *(inferred: "we hung up our lyres… how shall we sing the LORD's song in a foreign land?" makes grief *incompatible with performed joy* — the self keeps faith with its sorrow rather than manufacture gladness; the integrity of grief that will not lie; cf. Ps 42:3 "my tears have been my food", the refusal of hollow praise)*.

**II. The self-binding vow of memory — Jerusalem above the highest joy (vv.5–6).**
"**If I forget you, O Jerusalem**, let my **right hand forget its skill**! Let my **tongue stick to the roof of my mouth**, if I do not **remember you**, if I **do not set Jerusalem above my highest joy**!" (vv.5–6). The gem-finding: the self binds itself by **self-curse never to forget** — invoking loss of hand and tongue if it lets the memory lapse — and *ranks* Jerusalem **above its highest joy** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **governs its own memory by oath and *orders its affections*** — it commits, on pain of self-curse, to keep the beloved city in memory, and *sets* it at the very top of its hierarchy of joys ("above my highest joy"); memory here is a *willed fidelity*, and love a deliberate *ranking* *(inferred: "if I forget you… let my hand forget… if I do not set Jerusalem above my highest joy" makes remembrance a *vowed discipline* and love an *ordering of joys* — the self decides what its chief joy shall be and binds itself to it; the counterpart to the forgetting-breeds-rebellion of Ps 106, here memory guarded by oath; cf. Ps 42:6 "therefore I remember you", 84:2)*.

## What Psalm 137 says about the inner being
- **Real grief *suspends song and refuses to fake joy* (vv.1–4).** The mourning self hangs up its harp and will not perform mirth on command — it keeps faith with its sorrow rather than counterfeit gladness; the integrity of grief that will not lie.
- **The self *binds its own memory by vow and orders its affections* (vv.5–6).** On pain of self-curse it commits never to forget Jerusalem, and *sets* it "above my highest joy" — memory as willed fidelity, love as a deliberate ranking of joys; the self decides what its chief joy shall be.

## Honest notes
- **A cornerstone on grief and memory:** the *integrity of sorrow* (won't fake joy, vv.1–4) and the *vowed, ordered love* that ranks the beloved above the self's highest joy (vv.5–6) are prime inner-being data — memory as willed discipline, love as chosen hierarchy (with Ps 42, 84, and the anti-type of 106's forgetting). Foregrounded.
- **Scope honesty — the imprecation:** the terrible close (vv.7–9, against Edom and Babylon, "blessed shall he be who dashes your little ones") is the *rage of the violated* — read morally as 109's frame: the fury *voiced to God*, the raw cry of the traumatised, committed to God's justice rather than enacted. I do *not* treat the curse-content as an inner-being finding; the finding is the honest grief and the vowed memory. The verse records how real the wound was.
