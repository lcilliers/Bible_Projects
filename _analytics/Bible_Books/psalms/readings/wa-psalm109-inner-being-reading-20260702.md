# Psalm 109 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 109:1–31 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked; aheb-fix build). Complete chapter (6 vv. backfilled with the fixed full-text logic). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **Lens:** the finding is the inner-being **operation**, God-relation as arena (`feedback_lens_is_inner_being_process_not_god_relation`).
- **The wronged self that will not retaliate but *gives itself to prayer*; the stricken heart.** Psalm 109 is the great imprecatory psalm — a long, terrible curse (vv.6–20). But its *inner-being* findings are not the curse-content; they are the operations of the **wronged self**: love repaid with hatred, met by **giving oneself to prayer** rather than revenge (vv.4–5); the **poor, needy, *stricken* heart** (v.22); and the self **physically wasted by grief and scorn** (vv.23–25).

## The reading

**I. Love repaid with hatred — met by *giving oneself to prayer* (vv.4–5).**
"In return for my **love** (H0160, M05) they accuse me, but **I give myself to prayer**. So they reward me evil for good, and **hatred** (H8135, M06) for my love" (vv.4–5). The gem-finding: to being *accused in return for love*, the self's response is not counter-attack but **prayer** — Hebrew literally "I *am* prayer" *(stated)*. The operation: the wronged inner being **converts its grievance into *prayer*** — where love has been repaid with hatred, the self does not retaliate in kind but *turns the whole matter Godward*, becoming prayer itself *(inferred: "in return for my love they accuse… but I give myself to prayer" makes the self's move a *handing-over to God* rather than revenge — the injustice is prayed, not avenged; this is the frame that makes even the fierce imprecation an act committed to God's court, cf. Rom 12:19, Ps 35:13)*.

**II. The poor, needy, *stricken* heart (v.22).**
"For I am **poor and needy**, and my **heart** (H3820, M47) is **stricken** (H2490 — pierced/wounded) within me" (v.22). The gem-finding: the self names itself *poor and needy*, its **heart *wounded within*** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being under sustained assault is **pierced at its core** — not merely troubled but *wounded within*, a heart stricken through *(inferred: "my heart is stricken within me" locates the injury *inside* — the persecution has reached the heart itself; the self's poverty/neediness is the honest confession of its emptied condition, the very ground on which it appeals; cf. Ps 34:18 "the brokenhearted," 69:20 "reproach has broken my heart")*.

**III. The self physically wasted by grief and scorn (vv.23–25).**
"I am gone like a **shadow** at evening; I am **shaken off** like a locust. My **knees are weak** through fasting; my **body** (H1320, the flesh — M47) has become **gaunt**… I am an object of **scorn** (H2781, M06) to my accusers; when they see me, they **wag their heads**" (vv.23–25). The finding: grief has **wasted the body** — knees weak, flesh gaunt, the self a fading shadow — and the scorn is *felt*, the wagging heads registering as reproach *(stated)*. The operation: inner distress **writes itself on the body** and the self **absorbs the contempt of others** — the fasting-worn flesh and the felt scorn are the outward form of a self ground down *(inferred: "knees weak… body gaunt… an object of scorn… they wag their heads" shows the affliction embodied and the mockery internalised — the self diminished to a shadow, cf. Ps 102:3–11, 22:6–7)*.

## What Psalm 109 says about the inner being
- **The wronged self *becomes prayer* rather than retaliating (vv.4–5).** Love repaid with hatred is met not by counter-attack but by handing the whole matter to God — the self *is* prayer; the grievance is committed to God's court, not avenged in kind.
- **The persecuted heart is *stricken within* (v.22).** The assault reaches the core — a heart *pierced/wounded* inside; the self's confessed poverty and neediness is the honest ground of its appeal.
- **Inner distress *writes itself on the body* and *absorbs others' scorn* (vv.23–25).** Grief wastes the flesh gaunt, the self fades like a shadow, and the mockery is felt as reproach — affliction embodied and contempt internalised.

## Honest notes
- **Scope honesty — the imprecation:** the long curse (vv.6–20, "may his days be few… may his children be fatherless") is aimed at the enemy; read morally it is *what the wronged self prays*, and its inner-being significance is precisely that — the rage is **committed to God in prayer** (v.4's frame), not enacted by the self's own hand. I have *not* treated the curse-content as inner-being findings; the finding is the self's recourse to prayer.
- **v.31 "he stands at the right hand of the needy one"** resolves the psalm — the answered hope of the stricken self; the arena of the plea.
