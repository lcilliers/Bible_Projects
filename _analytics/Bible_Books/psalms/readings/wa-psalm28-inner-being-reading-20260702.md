- **The psalm of the dread of divine silence — answered by "he has heard."** Its spine: the inner being **cannot live if God is deaf** ("if you be silent to me, I become like those who go down to the pit," v.1); the whole psalm pivots on being **heard** (v.6, *"he has heard the voice of my pleas"*), and the answered cry issues in a heart that **trusts → is helped → exults** (v.7). ## The reading

**I. The dread of God's silence — worse than the enemies (v.1).**
"To you, O LORD, I **call** (H7121, M37); my **rock**, be not **deaf** (H2814, M42) to me, lest, if you be **silent** (H2790, M33) to me, I become like those who go down to the **pit**." The inner being's deepest fear here is **not the enemies but God's non-response** *(stated)*. Divine silence is equated with **death** — "if you be silent… I become like those who go down to the pit." **The inner being's life depends on being heard; God's silence is itself a kind of dying** *(inferred: the unanswered cry is set equal to descent to the pit)*. God is named "my **rock**" — the stable ground under the dread *(stated)*.

**II. The lifted hands — the whole self oriented to the holy place (v.2).**
"Hear the voice of my **pleas for mercy** (H8469, M21, *tachanun*)… when I **lift up my hands** toward your most **holy** (H6944, M22) sanctuary." The inner being's plea is **embodied** — hands lifted toward God's dwelling. The whole self (voice, body, longing) **reaches toward the holy place** *(stated)*; the inner cry takes bodily posture.

**III. The wicked whose lips and heart contradict (vv.3–5).**
"Do not drag me off with the **wicked** (H7563, M10)… who speak **peace** (H7965, M33, *shalom*) with their neighbours **while evil** (H7451, M27) is in their **hearts** (H3824, the seat — M47)" (v.3). The gem: the wicked are defined by a **split** — *peace on the lips, evil in the heart* *(stated)*. The **heart is the true content**, and their speech **conceals** rather than reveals it (the "double heart" of Ps 12, inverted). The inner being pleads **not to be grouped with these heart-deceivers** *(inferred: "do not drag me off *with* them" fears the shared verdict)*. And the root of their condition is an inner failure: "Because they do **not regard** (H0995, M15, *bin* — discern/consider) the **works of the LORD**… he will tear them down" (v.5) — their downfall is grounded in a **failure to perceive God's action** *(stated)*; not-regarding is the inward blindness beneath the outward evil (cf. Ps 10:11).

**IV. The pivot — "he has heard" (v.6).**
"**Blessed** (H1288, M39) be the LORD! For he has **heard** (H8085, M41) the voice of my pleas for mercy." The hinge: the **dread of silence** (v.1) is answered by "**he has heard**" *(stated)*. The inner being moves in one verse from fearing the pit to blessing God — and the turn is on **being heard**, the same word Ps 22:24 turned on. **The answered cry is the resolution** *(inferred: v.6 directly reverses v.1's dread)*.

**V. The heart's cycle — trust → helped → exult → thanks (v.7).**
"The LORD is my **strength** (H5797, M23) and my **shield**; in him my **heart** (H3820, M47) **trusts** (H0982, M19), and I am **helped**; my heart **exults** (H5937), and with my song I **give thanks** (H3034, M22)." A compact inner sequence: the heart **trusts** → is **helped** → **exults** → **gives thanks** *(stated)*. The help *follows* the trust ("my heart trusts, **and** I am helped"), and the experienced help *issues* in exultation *(inferred: the order runs trust → help → joy)*. The **heart** is named twice — it is both the *organ of trust* and the *organ of exultation*; the whole cycle from confidence to joy runs through the inner being *(inferred)*.

**VI. The personal strength widens to the people (vv.8–9).**
"The LORD is the **strength** (H5797, M23) of his people… the saving **refuge** (H4581, M19, *maoz*) of his anointed. Oh, **save** your people and **bless** (H1288, M39) your heritage! Be their **shepherd** and **carry** them forever" (vv.8–9). The "**my** strength" of v.7 becomes "the strength of **his people**" (v.8) — the personal rescue **overflows into intercession for the whole community** *(stated)*. The inner being that was heard now prays that the people be shepherded and **carried forever** — its own experience of being upheld enlarged to a prayer for all *(inferred; the shepherd/carry image ties to Ps 23)*.

## What Psalm 28 says about the inner being
- **The inner being cannot live under God's silence (v.1).** Its deepest dread is not the enemy but the unanswered cry — "if you be silent… I become like those who go down to the pit." Being heard is life; silence is a kind of death.
- **The plea is embodied (v.2).** Lifted hands toward the sanctuary — the whole self, body and longing, orients to the holy place.
- **The heart is the true content, and speech can conceal it (v.3).** The wicked "speak peace… while evil is in their hearts" — the inner being's real state may contradict its words; the double heart.
- **The wicked's root is inner non-perception (v.5).** They "do not regard the works of the LORD" — a failure of attention/discernment underlies the outward evil.
- **Everything pivots on "he has heard" (v.6).** The dread of silence is answered; the inner being turns from the pit to blessing in a single verse.
- **The heart's cycle runs trust → helped → exult → thanks (v.7).** Help follows trust and issues in joy; the heart is both the organ of trust and of exultation.
- **The rescue widens (vv.8–9).** "My strength" becomes "the strength of his people"; being upheld overflows into praying the whole flock be carried forever.
