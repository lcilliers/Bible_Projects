## The reading

**I. The fork at hearing God's voice — heed or harden (vv.7–8).**
"**Today, if you hear** (H8085) his voice, **do not harden** (*qashah*) your **hearts** (H3824, the seat — M47), as at Meribah… when your fathers put me to the **test**" (vv.7–8). The gem-finding: hearing God's voice presents the inner being with a **fork** — to *heed* or to *harden* *(stated)*. The operation: the heart can **stiffen itself against the very word it hears** — hardening is the heart's *refusal to be moved* by what it has just heard *(inferred: "if you hear his voice, do *not harden* your hearts" makes the hardening a response *to* the hearing — the heart setting itself against the word; contrast Ps 85's listening posture, 81's given-over refusal)*. And the **"today"** presses the urgency — the fork is *now*, at *this* hearing *(stated)*.

**II. The erring heart that does not know God's ways (vv.10–11).**
"They are a people who **go astray** (*taah*) **in their heart** (H3820, M47), and they **have not known** my ways… I swore in my wrath, 'They shall **not enter my rest**'" (vv.10–11). The finding: the inner root of straying is a **heart that *errs*** — those who "go astray *in their heart*" do **not know** God's ways *(stated)*. The operation: the **heart's inner wandering → ignorance of God's ways → exclusion from rest** *(inferred: "go astray in their heart… have not known my ways… shall not enter my rest" chains the erring heart to the lost knowledge and the forfeited rest — the hardening/wandering heart cannot enter the rest)*.

## What Psalm 95 says about the inner being
- **Hearing God's voice presents a *fork* — heed or harden (vv.7–8).** The heart can stiffen itself against the very word it hears; hardening is the refusal to be moved by what it has just heard. The "today" presses the fork into the present.
- **The *erring heart* forfeits the knowledge of God's ways and his rest (vv.10–11).** Inner wandering — "going astray in the heart" — issues in not knowing God's ways and in exclusion from rest. The heart that wanders cannot enter.
