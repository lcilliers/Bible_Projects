## The reading

**I. The enemies' *one heart* in conspiracy (v.5).**
"For they **conspire** (H3289, M15, *yaats* — take counsel, devise) **with one heart** (H3820, the seat — M47, *leb* — 'one accord'); against you they make a **covenant** (H1285, M44)" (v.5). The finding: the wicked's hostility is a **united inner disposition** — a *single heart* devising against God *(stated)*. The operation: the conspiring inner beings are **welded into one heart** — a corporate unison of malice, bound by a mutual covenant *(inferred: "conspire with one heart" makes the hostility a shared, unified inner posture, not scattered individual grudges)*.

**II. The redemptive aim of shame — "that they may seek your name" (v.16).**
"Fill their faces with **shame** (H7036, M07), **that they may seek** (H1245, M41) your name, O LORD" (v.16). The gem-finding: even the imprecation carries a **redemptive purpose** — the shame is aimed at the enemies' eventual **seeking** of God *(stated)*. The operation: shame is **not the end but the means** — "that they may *seek* your name" — the humbling of the hostile inner being is directed toward its **turning to God** *(inferred: the "shame… that they may seek" makes the shame instrumental to the enemies' seeking/knowing, v.18; the imprecation bends toward their conversion)*.

## What Psalm 83 says about the inner being
- **Hostility can be a *united heart* (v.5).** The conspiring wicked are welded into "one heart" — a corporate unison of malice bound by covenant, not scattered grudges.
- **Shame can be aimed at *seeking* (v.16).** Even the imprecation carries a redemptive purpose — the humbling of the hostile self is directed toward its turning to seek God's name; shame the means, seeking the end.
