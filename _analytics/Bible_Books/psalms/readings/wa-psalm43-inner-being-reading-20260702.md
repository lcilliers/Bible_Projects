- **The resolution of the dialogical soul.** Where Ps 42 left the inner being *disoriented* — flooded, downcast, addressing itself — Ps 43 supplies the **answer to that disorientation**: the soul asks for **light and truth to *lead* it home** (v.3), names God as its **exceeding joy** (v.4), and repeats the self-command to **hope** (v.5), now with a destination.

## The reading

**I. Light and truth as guides for the disoriented self (v.3).**
"Send out your **light** (H0216) and your **truth** (H0571, M13, *emet*); let **them lead** (H5148, *nachah*) me; let them bring me to your holy hill and to your dwelling" (v.3). The operation: the inner being, unable to find its own way out of the flood of Ps 42, asks that **light and truth be *sent* as guides** — personified, *they* lead it home *(stated)*. The finding: the disoriented soul is led **not by its own navigation but by an externalised light and truth God dispatches** — guidance is *received*, and it is what re-orients the self toward God's presence *(inferred: "let *them* lead me… bring me" makes light/truth the agents, the self the led)*. This directly answers Ps 42's "deep calls to deep" — the flooded inner being needs a light not its own to find the way *(inferred)*.

**II. God as the *exceeding joy* — the apex of gladness (v.4).**
"Then I will go to the altar of God, to God **my exceeding joy** (H8057, M04 — *simchat gili*, 'the gladness of my rejoicing'), and I will **praise** (H3034, M22) you" (v.4). The finding: God is named as the inner being's **superlative joy** — not *a* joy among others but "the *gladness of my joy*," the joy-of-my-joy, the **apex and source of the self's gladness** *(stated: the Hebrew stacks joy-words — the gladness of my rejoicing)*. The disoriented, thirsting soul of Ps 42 finds at the end of the guidance not merely relief but its **highest gladness located in God himself** *(inferred: the destination of the leading is God-as-exceeding-joy)*.

**III. The self-dialogue resolved (v.5).**
"Why are you **cast down**, O my **soul**… Hope (H3176, M18) in God; for I shall again praise him" (v.5). The refrain returns a third time (42:5, 11; 43:5) — but now **after** the request for guidance and the naming of God as exceeding joy, so the self-command to **hope** rests on a **destination reached in prospect** *(inferred: the same refrain, now anchored by vv.3–4's altar and joy)*. The finding: the inner being's **self-government (Ps 42) completes in being led and re-joyed** — the "I" that counselled the downcast "soul" now has light, truth, and the altar to point it to *(inferred)*.

## What Psalm 43 says about the inner being
- **The disoriented inner being is *led*, not self-navigating (v.3).** It asks for light and truth to be sent as guides; re-orientation is received, and it is what brings the flooded soul home. (The answer to Ps 42's "deep calls to deep.")
- **God is the inner being's *exceeding* joy (v.4).** Not a joy among others but "the gladness of my rejoicing" — the apex and source of the self's gladness, located in God himself.
- **Self-government completes in guidance and re-joy (v.5).** The self-command to hope (Ps 42's refrain) now rests on a destination — light, truth, the altar, and God-as-joy give the "hope in God" its content.
