## Psa 21:1-11

**asked** — Psa 21:4  ·  `H7592:ask#1`

_Story_

When the king wants life, he asks God for it plainly, and he receives far more than he asked - not just life but length of days without end. The psalm shows this as a movement of the will: a deliberate request. It does not tell us what stirred the request inside him, nor does it lodge it in any one part of him; the whole person simply asks. There is no urgency described, no measure of how hard he asked, and no note of how he felt afterward - only that he asked for life and life was given. What matters is the pairing itself: a request met, and met beyond all measure.

_Reading_

The inner act here is asking (H7592), whose sense (101) is 'asked life, received it' and whose type (102) is volition - a movement of the will, not a feeling or reflex. No source is recorded (103 absent): the psalm does not trace what within the king generated the petition, so the asking stands as a bare act of will with no named internal origin. It is not organ-located (seat 104 = none) - the willing is not lodged in heart or any faculty but is a whole-self movement. The bearer (105) is the king. The operation (106) is a completed transaction: he asked life of God and received it, length of days forever - a request answered beyond measure. The target (107) is 'asking-and-receiving' itself, so the reading fixes on the coupling of petition to grant rather than on an external object. Manner is none (108): the text marks no mode, no urgency or ceremony to the asking. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are all unrecorded - the passage does not weigh the force of the request, adds no qualification, and names no inner after-state; the will simply asks and is met. Coupling (112) is 'asked-life', binding the act to its content: what is asked is life, and life defines the movement. No prohibition (113): this willing is neither restrained nor forbidden. The discovery (114, v4) confirms the shape - a specific petition (life) overfulfilled. Role (115) is characteristic: the asking is a true feature of the inner being. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - the act sits within the inner life as a state of the willing self, even as God is the arena it reaches toward.

**deaf** — Psa 38:13  ·  `H2795:deaf#1`

_Story_

Surrounded by people plotting against him and spreading lies, the psalmist deliberately acts like a man who is deaf and mute - he simply will not hear their charges or answer them. This is a choice, not a disability; nothing in his body has failed. The psalm does not explain what inside him produced this restraint, and it does not measure how hard it is to keep. His refusal to listen and his refusal to speak are really one act: he shuts the whole exchange out. He holds his silence and leaves his defense to God.

_Reading_

This deafness is not a defect but a chosen posture. Sense (101) is 'like a deaf, mute man I do not answer'; type (102) is volition - the deafness is willed, an act, not an affliction. No source is recorded (103 absent): the passage does not trace what inside the psalmist produces the silence, leaving the will's decision unexplained at its root. Seat is none (104): the deafness is not organ-located - it is not the ear that fails but the self that chooses, a whole-person stance. Bearer (105) is the psalmist. The operation (106) is that the self becomes like a deaf man who does not hear and a mute who does not open his mouth - a deliberate silence held before accusers. Target (107) is 'chosen-silence': the aim is the silence itself, a refusal to answer. Manner is none (108): no mode of the silence is marked - not its tone, not its bearing. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: the text does not measure how total the silence is, adds no qualification, and names no inner consequence - the outcome is left with God (per the discovery). Coupling (112) is 'deaf-and-mute', binding not-hearing to not-speaking as one act: the refusal to hear and the refusal to answer are a single movement. No prohibition (113): the silence is not commanded or forbidden. Discovery (114, v13-14): though slandered, the interior keeps silence and leaves its case to God. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - a state of the inner self, adopted within, with the accusers as the arena it refuses to engage.

**hear** — Psa 51:8  ·  `H8085:hear#1`

_Story_

The penitent aches to hear joy and gladness again - not to make it himself, but to be told it. He does not yet have this word; the whole psalm shows him longing for it. The passage does not spell out what part of him does the longing or how intensely he feels it; it simply shows the desire straining toward a word not yet spoken. What he wants to hear is the news of pardon - and that news is tied to something almost physical: the bones God broke would themselves rejoice. His sorrow is turning, reaching toward hope.

_Reading_

Here hearing is longed for, not yet had. Sense (101) is shama, 'let me hear joy and gladness'; type (102) is action. No source (103 absent): the passage does not name the inner faculty behind the longing. Seat is none (104): the hearing is not organ-located - it is not framed as an ear's function but as the whole penitent's yearning. Bearer (105) is the penitent. Operation (106) is 'long to hear' - the act is desire straining toward a word not yet spoken. Target (107) is 'joy and gladness', a paired span: what he wants to hear is the announcement that restores gladness. Manner none (108): no mode marked. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent - the text does not weigh the longing's force, qualifies it no further, and names no achieved inner result; it stays a plea. Coupling (112), also a paired span, binds the hearing to the broken bones rejoicing - the word longed for is the pardon that would make the very bones God crushed rejoice. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the plea to be told the word of pardon that turns sorrow toward hope. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - the operation sits within the inner life as a longing state, reaching toward God for the word.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from the item's other readings: this is a penitent's private longing to hear the word of pardon that restores joy (bearer = the penitent; locus internal), whereas the wicked readings (58:5, 59:7) are refusal and presumption, and the God-oriented readings (81:5, 85:8, 95:7) hear God's own voice directly.

## Psa 58:1-5

> To the choirmaster : according to Do Not Destroy . A Miktam of David . Psa 58:1 Do you indeed decree what is right , you gods ? Do you judge the children of man uprightly ? Psa 58:2 No , in your hearts you devise wrongs ; your hands deal out violence on earth . Psa 58:3 The wicked are estranged from the womb ; they go astray from birth , speaking lies . Psa 58:4 They have venom like the venom of a serpent , like the deaf adder that stops its ear , Psa 58:5 so that it does not hear the voice of charmers or of the cunning enchanter . Psa 58:7 Let them vanish like water that runs away; when he aims his arrows, let them be blunted.

**hear** — Psa 58:5  ·  `H8085:hear#2`

_Story_

The wicked are like a snake that plugs its own ears so no charmer's voice can reach it. Their refusal to listen is not a passing act but a fixed condition of who they are. The psalm does not explain where this deafness comes from inside them; it simply belongs to them. And it is deliberate - they stop their own ears on purpose, shutting out every appeal and every correction. Their unwillingness to hear is bound up with their poison: they cannot be talked out of it, because they will not let the words in.

_Reading_

This is the descent read at full weight: hearing refused. Sense (101) is shama, 'does not hear the charmers'; but type (102) is status, not action - the not-hearing is a settled condition of the wicked, a fixed state rather than a single deed. No source (103 absent): the passage does not trace where this deafness originates inside them, presenting it as simply constitutive. Seat none (104): not organ-located - the ear is 'stopped', but the deafness is of the whole self, not a failed organ. Bearer (105) is the wicked, imaged as the deaf adder. Operation (106) is 'refuse to hear / heed' - an active shutting-out. Target (107) is the voice of charmers, the cunning enchanter - every appeal and correction. Manner (108) is here present and vivid: 'stopping its ear, willfully deaf' - the mode is deliberate self-closure. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: the text does not grade the deafness, adds no further qualifier, and names no inner consequence beyond the condition itself. Coupling (112), a paired span, binds the deafness to 'the incorrigibility of their venom' - the refusal to hear is one with their poison; they cannot be charmed out of it. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the willful, self-stopped deafness of the wicked, deaf to all correction and appeal. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - a state of the wicked inner being, with the charmers' voice as the arena it seals itself against.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from the item's other readings by being a settled state (type = status) of willful, self-stopped deafness in the wicked - refusal rather than reception. Unlike 59:7 (a presumption that none hears), here the refusal has a marked manner (stopping the ear) and is coupled to incorrigible venom.

## Psa 59:7-13

**hear** — Psa 59:7  ·  `H8085:hear#3`

_Story_

The psalmist's enemies snarl their threats and think, 'Who will hear us?' - convinced no one is listening, not even God. This is not a stray thought but their settled outlook. The psalm does not say what bred this attitude in them, and it names no one they imagine hearing - that emptiness is the point: they assume there is no listener at all. But there is one. The God they think is absent hears them, and answers their arrogance with laughter.

_Reading_

The inner act here is a presumption about hearing. Sense (101) is shama, 'Who will hear us?'; type (102) is status - a settled assumption, the enemies' standing frame of mind, not a discrete deed. No source (103 absent): the passage does not name what breeds this presumption within them. Seat none (104): not organ-located. Bearer (105) is the enemies, presuming. Operation (106): 'presume none hears' - they act on the conviction that no one, not even God, marks their words. Target (107) is explicitly none: the reading finds no object of their hearing - fittingly, since their whole point is that there is no hearer; the silence in this dimension is itself the finding, the void where a listener should be. Manner (108) is none: no mode marked. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: the presumption is not graded, not further qualified, and no inner after-state is recorded. Coupling (112), a paired span, binds their presumption to God's mocking laughter (v8) - the assumption that none hears is answered by the One who does, with derision. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the brazen presumption of the wicked, practical atheism, which God answers with laughter. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - a state of the enemies' inner being, oriented toward a hearer they deny.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from the item's other readings: a settled presumption (type = status) that no one hears, with an explicitly empty target and empty manner - the very void of a listener is the finding. Unlike 58:5's active self-stopping deafness, this is a denial that any hearer exists at all, answered by God's derision.

**heard** — Psa 62:11  ·  `H8085:heard#1`

_Story_

The psalmist has heard God speak, and what he heard he holds as doubly sure: God said it once, but he has taken it in twice over. What he heard is two things held together - that all power belongs to God, and that faithful love belongs to him too. The psalm does not lodge this hearing in any one part of him; his whole self simply receives it. Unlike his other moments of hearing, this one is not tied to any further action - it stands on its own. And because both truths are true at once, his soul can rest in silence before God.

_Reading_

Here hearing is attentive reception. Sense (101) is shama, 'twice have I heard this'; type (102) is action - a real taking-in. No source (103 absent): the passage does not name the inner faculty of reception. Seat none (104): not organ-located; the whole self receives. Bearer (105) is the psalmist. Operation (106): 'hear / take in' - the word is received and held. Target (107), a paired span, is 'that power and steadfast love belong to God' - two truths, taken together. Manner (108), present and a paired span, is 'once God spoke, twice heard' - the mode is a numerical intensification, one speaking received as doubly certain. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: no separate grading, no further qualifier, no named inner after-state - though the whole psalm's silent trust flows from this hearing. Coupling (112) is none: unlike the other readings, this hearing is not bound to a paired act - the finding stands alone, the bare reception of the two great truths. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the two truths that ground all trust - power AND steadfast love both belong to God - so the soul may safely be silent before him. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is external:god - the operation is oriented toward God, whose word is received.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from the item's other 'heard' readings: this is the inward reception of God's own twofold word (locus external:god, and uniquely no coupling), whereas 66:8 causes praise to resound outward, 78:3 receives handed-down tradition, 92:11 hears the enemies' doom, and 132:6 is a report that prompts a quest.

**heard** — Psa 66:8  ·  `H8085:heard#2`

_Story_

Here the peoples are told to make God's praise heard - to send the sound of it out so everyone can catch it. This is not about taking a sound in but about giving one out, letting the praise ring abroad. The psalm does not say what stirs this inside them; it simply calls them to do it together. And making his praise heard is the same thing as blessing him - the two are one act. It is worship made public, carried to every ear.

_Reading_

This 'hearing' runs outward - causing to be heard. Sense (101) is shama in its causative reach, 'let the sound of his praise be heard'; type (102) is action. No source (103 absent): the passage does not name an inner origin for this making-audible. Seat none (104): not organ-located. Bearer (105) is the peoples - a corporate inner impulse. Operation (106): 'make heard / cause to resound' - not receiving a sound but producing one, sending praise abroad. Target (107), a paired span, is 'the sound of God's praise' - what they cause to be heard. Manner none (108): no mode marked. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: the loudness is not graded, no further qualifier, no named inner after-state. Coupling (112), a paired span, binds the making-heard to 'blessing God' - the sounding of praise is one with blessing him. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the peoples causing his praise to be heard abroad - the public voicing of blessing. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is external:god - oriented toward God as the one praised.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from the item's other 'heard' readings: this is the outward, causative sense - making praise resound and be heard abroad (coupled with blessing God), unlike the inward receptions of 62:11 and 78:3, the hearing of judgment in 92:11, or the report that prompts a quest in 132:6.

**hear** — Psa 66:16  ·  `H8085:hear#4`

_Story_

The psalmist calls out to everyone who fears God: come and listen, and I will tell you what God has done for me. The hearing here is an invitation to attend to one person's testimony. The passage does not dwell on what part of the hearers does the listening; it just gathers them in. Earlier the psalm said 'come and see' what God has done; now it says 'come and hear' - the same move, turning a private mercy into public witness. One soul's rescue becomes something the whole gathering takes in.

_Reading_

Hearing here is summoned attention to testimony. Sense (101) is shama, 'Come and hear, all you who fear God'; type (102) is action. No source (103 absent): no inner origin named for the summoned hearing. Seat none (104): not organ-located. Bearer (105) is all who fear God, summoned. Operation (106): 'come and hear' - the invited act of listening to another's account. Target (107): 'what God has done for the psalmist's soul' - the object is personal testimony. Manner none (108): no mode marked. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: not graded, not further qualified, no named inner after-state. Coupling (112), a paired span, binds this to the God-fearers being summoned and parallels 'come and see' (v5) - private mercy now made public witness. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the invitation to the God-fearing to listen to what God did for one soul. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is external:god - oriented toward God's deed as the thing heard.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from the item's other 'hear' readings: this is a summons to attend to human testimony about God's deed (bearer = God-fearers summoned, target = what God did for one soul), whereas 51:8 is a private longing to hear pardon, 58:5 and 59:7 are the wicked's refusal and presumption, and 81/85/95 hear God's own voice directly.

## Psa 78:4-6

> A Maskil of Asaph . Psa 78:1 Give ear , O my people , to my teaching ; incline your ears to the words of my mouth ! Psa 78:3 things that we have heard and known, that our fathers have told us. Psa 78:4 We will not hide them from their children , but tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the Lord , and his might , and the wonders that he has done . Psa 78:5 He established a testimony in Jacob and appointed a law in Israel , which he commanded our fathers to teach to their children , Psa 78:6 that the next generation might know them, the children yet unborn , and arise and tell them to their children , Psa 78:7 so that they should set their hope in God and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments;

**Give ear** — Psa 78:1  ·  `H0238:giveear#1`

_Story_

The teacher opens by calling the people to give ear - to lean in and pay attention to his instruction. This is a deliberate turning of attention toward what is being taught: the story of what God has done. The psalm does not say what stirs the people to listen; it simply summons them. And their listening is tied to a chain of handing-on - what the fathers told is now passed to the next generation.
