## Psa 24:3-10

> Psa 24:3 Who shall ascend the hill of the Lord ? And who shall stand in his holy place ? Psa 24:4 He who has clean hands and a pure heart , who does not lift up his soul to what is false and does not swear deceitfully . Psa 24:5 He will receive blessing from the Lord and righteousness from the God of his salvation . Psa 24:6 Such is the generation of those who seek him, who seek the face of the God of Jacob . Selah Psa 24:7 Lift up your heads , O gates ! And be lifted up , O ancient doors , that the King of glory may come in . Psa 24:8 Who is this King of glory ? The Lord , strong and mighty , the Lord , mighty in battle ! Psa 24:9 Lift up your heads , O gates ! And lift them up , O ancient doors , that the King of glory may come in . Psa 24:10 Who is this King of glory ? The Lord of hosts , he is the King of glory ! Selah

**lift up** — Psa 24:4  ·  `H5375:liftup#1`

_Story_

This song asks who is fit to climb God's holy hill, and answers: the person whose inner life will not sell itself to a lie. The word for lifting up is turned around — instead of raising his soul toward something false, this worshipper refuses to. Nothing outside him forces the choice; it comes from his own settled will, and it is not felt in any one part of the body but is simply who he is inside. He will not pour his devotion into an empty idol, and he will not swear to back a falsehood. The picture is of a heart kept whole and single by everything it declines to give itself to.

_Reading_

Here nasa ('lift up', sense 101) appears in its rare negated form: the sense recorded is 'the soul not lifted to falsehood', and the type (102) is volition — this is not a bodily act but a movement of the will. The bearer (105) is the worshipper who may ascend the hill, and the operation (106) is a refusal: 'he does not lift up his soul to what is false or swear deceitfully — the self refuses to devote itself to an idol or a lie.' The target (107) is therefore given as undivided-devotion: what the refusal secures is a whole, single-hearted directedness. Coupling (112) ties the lift to 'soul-not-to-false', binding the will's motion to the object it declines. No source is recorded (103 present:false): the passage names no external cause pushing the soul — the movement issues from the interior's own governance. Seat is 'none' (104): this operation is not lodged in an organ; it is a state of the whole self, confirmed by locus (116) internal:ib-state. Manner is 'none' (108) — the refusal is stated without a described mode. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113 present:false): the reader measures no degree of the will's resolve, adds no qualifier, traces no downstream consequence, and — notably — the refusal is framed as the worshipper's own characteristic (115) rather than an imposed command, so no prohibition dimension is recorded even though the content is a 'does not'. The discovery (114) crystallises it: the operation is 'a refusal of misdirected devotion: the interior will not give itself to emptiness or back a lie with an oath.' Read for the inner being, lifting the soul is here the very capacity to devote, exercised in the negative — the clean-handed, pure-hearted person is defined by where the soul is withheld.

*Why this reading differs:* Alone among the ten nasa 'lift up' readings this one is negated (type = volition, not action): the soul is defined by refusing to be lifted to falsehood, so its target is undivided-devotion secured by withholding, rather than a positive raising of soul, hands, eyes or cup toward God.

## Psa 25:1-3

> Of David . Psa 25:1 To you, O Lord , I lift up my soul . Psa 25:2 O my God , in you I trust ; let me not be put to shame ; let not my enemies exult over me . Psa 25:3 Indeed , none who wait for you shall be put to shame ; they shall be ashamed who are wantonly treacherous . Psa 25:4 Make me to know your ways, O Lord; teach me your paths.

**lift up** — Psa 25:1  ·  `H5375:liftup#2`

_Story_

David begins his prayer by lifting his whole inner self up to God, like a man holding out everything he is with open hands. It is not just a gesture or a mood but the deep offering of himself, and it flows from his own trust rather than being triggered by anything outside him. This lifting is not felt in one part of him; it is the whole person turned upward and handed over. The prayer that follows rests on this opening act of self-surrender. From the very first line, he gives himself away to the God he trusts.

_Reading_

The acrostic opens with nasa in its fullest interior sense (101): 'lift up my soul to you'. The type (102) is affect — an emotional, devotional lifting of the whole self, not a mere gesture. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the operation (106) is that 'the self lifts up its soul to God — the whole inner self raised toward him as the psalm opens.' The target (107) is self-offering: the lifting hands the person over. Coupling (112) is 'lift-up-soul', welding the act to its object, the soul itself. No source is recorded (103 present:false): the psalm names no prior cause; the movement is the psalmist's own initiating act of trust (note v2, 'in you I trust'). Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) is internal:ib-state — this is not organ-located but a condition of the entire interior. Manner is 'none' (108): no mode of the lifting is described. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): the reader records no measured degree, no narrowing qualifier, and does not trace what the offering produces — the psalm states the self-surrender without quantifying or resolving it. Role (115) is characteristic: this defines the psalmist. The discovery (114) reads it as the acrostic 'handing the self over' from its first line. For the inner being, lifting up the soul is here the primary act of devotion — the whole self raised and given to God as the ground of the prayer that follows.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from the hand- and eye-liftings (63:4, 119:48, 121:1, 123:1, 134:2) in that the whole soul, not a body-part, is raised; and from the parallel soul-lifting at 86:4 in its type (affect here vs action there), reading the act as devotional feeling. Its target, self-offering, marks the positive counterpart to 24:4's negated withholding.

**bear** — Psa 55:12  ·  `H5375:bear#1`

_Story_

The poet says something surprising about his own strength: if an enemy were mocking him, he could take it — he could brace himself and carry the insult, or simply keep out of the man's way. He has that kind of inner toughness for open hostility. But the pain here is not from an enemy at all; it is from a close friend who has turned on him. And against a trusted hand there is no bracing. The very strength that could have shouldered a foe's contempt collapses, because it was never built to carry betrayal.

_Reading_

Here nasa carries its 'bear / endure' sense (101): 'then I could bear it'. The type (102) is action — a capacity exercised. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the operation (106) is to 'bear / endure (hypothetically)': the endurance is offered as a condition contrary to fact. The target (107) is a genuine pair (item_type pair, spans 280052->280051): 'an enemy's taunt' — what could be borne is a known foe's reproach. Coupling (112) is also a pair (280052->280057): the bearing is set beside 'hiding from an open adversary' — endurance and evasion are the two things open enmity would have permitted. No source is recorded (103 present:false): no external cause is named for the capacity to endure; it is the interior's own resilience. Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) internal:ib-state — the endurance is a state of the whole self, not organ-lodged. Manner is 'none' (108); intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): the reader records no measured degree of the endurance, no qualifier, and does not trace its outcome — because the outcome is precisely what the verse denies (it is not an enemy, so he cannot bear it). Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) is the sharp finding: nasa here is 'the capacity to shoulder a known foe's reproach — a strength that the betrayal of a friend dissolves, for there is no bracing against a trusted hand.' Read for the inner being, bearing is a real interior strength, but the passage exposes its limit: the self can carry open hostility and cannot carry treachery from a companion. The absence of any recorded effect underlines that the endurance never actually engages — betrayal has disarmed it.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the four 'bear' readings this and 89:50 are endurance of reproach, while 126:6 (twice) is carrying a physical load. This one is uniquely hypothetical (a strength that does NOT engage, because the wound is a friend's, not a foe's), whereas 89:50 is an actual bearing of insult lodged in the heart.

**violated** — Psa 55:20  ·  `H2490:violat#1`

_Story_

The betrayer in this psalm does something worse than attack — he desecrates. He had sworn friendship, and now he tramples that sacred promise underfoot, stretching out his hand against the very people he had bound himself to. The wound and the words come from one poisoned source inside him: his speech is smooth as butter while war fills his heart. Nothing outside him made him do it; the treachery is simply who he has become. This is the poem's unflinching look at a friend's inner life gone rotten — trust turned into a drawn sword.

_Reading_

This reading tracks the dark movement of the betrayer. The sense (101) is chalal, 'profane / violate': 'he violated his covenant'. Type (102) is action. The bearer (105) is the betrayer, and the operation (106) is to 'profane / break'. The target (107) is a pair (spans 280130->280132): 'his covenant' — the sworn bond itself is what is desecrated. Coupling (112) is another pair (280130->280126): the violation is welded to 'the hand stretched out against his friends' (v20a), so the inner faithlessness and the outward aggression are one motion. No source is recorded (103 present:false): the passage names no external cause driving the treachery — it issues from the betrayer's own interior, exposed in v21 as a heart at war behind smooth speech. Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) internal:ib-state: the profaning is not organ-located but a state of the whole self. Manner is 'none' (108); intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): the reader measures no degree, adds no qualifier, and traces no consequence here (the passage's judgement in v23 is God's separate act, not recorded as this operation's effect); and though the deed is covenant-breaking, no prohibition dimension is recorded — it is read as the betrayer's characteristic (115), what he is, not a rule he transgresses. The discovery (114) names it: 'the treachery of breaking a sworn covenant of friendship — faithlessness at its most defiling, the trust desecrated.' Read for the inner being with no valence bias, this is the interior of the traitor rendered fully: a self that can profane a sacred bond, its smooth words drawn swords, treated with the same descriptive weight as the psalms of trust.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the two 'violate' (chalal) readings, this is the profaning of a personal covenant of friendship by a human betrayer (target = his covenant, coupled to the hand against friends), whereas 89:31 is David's children violating God's statutes — a shift of bearer (friend vs sons) and target (a friendship-bond vs God's ordinances).

## Psa 63:1-11

> A Psalm of David , when he was in the wilderness of Judah . Psa 63:1 O God , you are my God ; earnestly I seek you; my soul thirsts for you; my flesh faints for you, as in a dry and weary land where there is no water . Psa 63:2 So I have looked upon you in the sanctuary , beholding your power and glory . Psa 63:3 Because your steadfast love is better than life , my lips will praise you . Psa 63:4 So I will bless you as long as I live ; in your name I will lift up my hands . Psa 63:5 My soul will be satisfied as with fat and rich food , and my mouth will praise you with joyful lips , Psa 63:6 when I remember you upon my bed , and meditate on you in the watches of the night ; Psa 63:7 for you have been my help , and in the shadow of your wings I will sing for joy . Psa 63:8 My soul clings to you; your right hand upholds me. Psa 63:9 But those who seek to destroy my life shall go down into the depths of the earth ; Psa 63:10 they shall be given over to the power of the sword ; they shall be a portion for jackals . Psa 63:11 But the king shall rejoice in God ; all who swear by him shall exult , for the mouths of liars will be stopped .

**lift up** — Psa 63:4  ·  `H5375:liftup#3`

_Story_

In a dry, waterless wilderness, thirsting for God, David turns his longing into a gesture: he lifts his hands in worship, in God's own name. Here the lifting is something you can see — it lives in the hands and stretches outward toward God, not just inside the heart. It rises out of a soul already full of love and praise, and it belongs to a lifelong habit of blessing him. The inward devotion has become a bodily act. His whole self, hands and heart together, reaches up toward the God he cannot stop praising.

_Reading_

Here nasa is the bodily posture of prayer: sense (101) 'lift up (the hands)', type (102) action. Uniquely in this family the seat (104) is filled — 'the hands' — so this lifting IS organ-located, and correspondingly the locus (116) is external:god: the movement reaches outward toward God rather than resting as an internal state. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, the operation (106) is to 'lift up (the hands in worship)', and the target (107) is 'to God, in his name'. Manner (108) is filled too — 'in God's name' — the mode under which the hands rise. Coupling (112) is a pair (281058->281054): the raised hands are welded to 'the gesture of the lifelong blessing' (v4a, 'so I will bless you as long as I live'). No source is recorded (103 present:false): the psalm names no external trigger; the lifting flows from the thirsting, love-driven soul of vv1-3. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): no degree, qualifier, or downstream result is recorded — the worship is stated, not measured. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) reads the raised hands as 'the outward act of a heart lifted to God, worship enacted with the whole self.' For the inner being, this is the interior movement of blessing taking bodily form: the seat in the hands and the external locus show devotion breaking out of the self and into gesture, God being the arena the whole person reaches toward.

*Why this reading differs:* This is the only 'lift up' reading with a filled seat ('the hands') and a filled manner ('in God's name'), and its locus is external:god rather than internal:ib-state. It differs from the soul-liftings (25:1, 86:4, 143:8) by being an outward bodily gesture, and shares the hand-lifting motif with 119:48 and 134:2.

## Psa 75:4-10

> Psa 75:4 I say to the boastful , ‘Do not boast ,’ and to the wicked , ‘Do not lift up your horn ; Psa 75:5 do not lift up your horn on high , or speak with haughty neck .’” Psa 75:6 For not from the east or from the west and not from the wilderness comes lifting up , Psa 75:7 but it is God who executes judgment , putting down one and lifting up another . Psa 75:9 But I will declare it forever ; I will sing praises to the God of Jacob . Psa 75:10 All the horns of the wicked I will cut off , but the horns of the righteous shall be lifted up .

**lift up** — Psa 75:4  ·  `H7311:liftup#1`

_Story_

To the arrogant and the wicked the psalm says plainly: stop raising your horn. Lifting the horn was the proud swagger of self-made strength, chin up, neck stiff, boasting with the mouth what the posture already declared. This pride comes from nowhere but inside them — it is simply what they are. And the psalm sets a hard limit on it: no one lifts himself up; it is God who lowers one man and raises another. Here the study looks squarely at the interior of pride, the very opposite of the humble soul lifted to God, and finds it forbidden because exaltation was never ours to hand ourselves.

_Reading_

This reading uses a different verb, rum ('lift up / exalt', sense 101), and turns the family's motif toward pride. Type (102) is action. The bearer (105) is the wicked, and the operation (106) is to 'lift up the horn (forbidden self-exaltation)'. The target (107) is a pair (282722->282723): 'the horn (symbol of strength/pride)' — the self-promoting emblem raised. Manner (108) is filled: 'on high, with haughty neck', the arrogant mode of the gesture. Coupling (112) is a pair (282722->282729): the raised horn is welded to 'speaking with haughty neck' (v5) — proud posture and proud speech are one movement. No source is recorded (103 present:false): the passage names no cause outside the wicked; the exaltation wells up from their own interior. Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) internal:ib-state — the pride is a state of the whole self, not organ-lodged. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): notably, though the verse is a command ('Do not lift up your horn'), the reader records no prohibition dimension — the self-exaltation is read as the wicked's characteristic (115), the interior disposition being addressed, and no measured degree, qualifier, or effect is traced (the counter-movement, God's putting down and lifting up in v7, is a separate divine act). The discovery (114) names it: 'the arrogant self-exaltation God forbids... self-promotion the wicked must abandon, for lifting up is God's to give (v7).' Read with no valence bias, this is the inner being of pride laid bare and given full weight: a self that raises its own strength on high — the dark mirror of the psalter's upward devotion, and forbidden precisely because exaltation belongs to God.

*Why this reading differs:* The two rum 'lift up' readings diverge sharply: here the wicked's forbidden self-exaltation (bearer = the wicked, manner = haughty, a descent/pride movement), against 110:7 where the same verb marks the king's God-given triumph (bearer = the king, an ascent). The difference — condemned pride vs granted victory — is the finding: lifting up is illegitimate when self-taken, legitimate when God-given.

## Psa 86:2-7

**lift up** — Psa 86:4  ·  `H5375:liftup#4`

_Story_

A man who calls himself poor and needy prays for gladness, and the reason he gives is that he lifts up his whole soul to God. His inner self reaches upward, out toward God, and that reaching is the same motion as his longing for joy — he raises his heart to the only one who can lighten it. Nothing pushes him to do this from outside; it rises out of his own need and devotion. This is what dependence looks like from the inside: the whole self stretched up to God, asking to be made glad.

_Reading_

Here nasa lifts the soul again: sense (101) 'lift up (nasa)', with the operation (106) 'lift up the soul'. Type (102) is action. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, the target (107) is 'to God', and the locus (116) is external:god — the soul's raising is read as directed outward toward God. Coupling (112) welds the lift to 'the soul gladdened' (v4a, 'Gladden the soul of your servant'): the lifting of the soul upward and the plea for its gladdening are bound together, so the upward reach is also a reach for joy. No source is recorded (103 present:false): no external cause is named; the movement is the servant's own devotion, grounded in his need (v1, 'poor and needy'). Seat is 'none' (104): not organ-located. Manner is 'none' (108); intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): the reader records no degree, qualifier, or outcome — the raising is stated as an act of the whole self, not measured. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) names it 'the aspiration of the whole self toward God, devotion as upward reach.' For the inner being, lifting the soul here is dependence turned upward: the needy servant raises his whole interior to God as the ground of his plea, and the lifting is inseparable from his longing for the gladness only God can give.

*Why this reading differs:* Like 25:1 and 143:8 this raises the whole soul to God, but its recorded type is action (not affect) and its locus is external:god (not internal:ib-state), and its distinctive coupling binds the lifting to the plea to have the soul 'gladdened' — devotion driven by need for joy rather than pure self-offering.

## Psa 89:12-14

> Psa 89:12 The north and the south , you have created them; Tabor and Hermon joyously praise your name . Psa 89:13 You have a mighty arm ; strong is your hand , high your right hand . Psa 89:14 Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne ; steadfast love and faithfulness go before you . Psa 89:15 Blessed are the people who know the festal shout, who walk, O Lord, in the light of your face,

**shout** — Psa 89:15  ·  `H8643:shout#1`

_Story_

The happy people in this psalm are the ones who know the festal shout — the great glad cry that hails God as their king. It bursts out of them not because anyone commands it but because they know him; the joy inside simply has to be voiced. And this shout is not empty noise: it belongs together with a life walked in the light of God's face. To know the shout is to walk in his presence and be glad. Here a whole community's inner joy overflows into a single jubilant cry to their King.

_Reading_

This reading breaks the lift/bear pattern with teruah, the 'festal shout' (sense 101), type (102) action. The bearer (105) is 'the people', the operation (106) is to 'raise the festal shout', and the target (107) is 'to God the king'. No source is recorded (103 present:false): the passage names no external trigger — the acclamation rises from the people's own knowledge of God (v15, 'Blessed are the people who know the festal shout'). Seat is 'none' (104): the shout is not organ-located but is the whole people's act. Manner is 'none' (108); intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): no degree, qualifier, or downstream result is recorded — the acclamation is stated, not measured. Two dimensions carry values that read as cross-references rather than descriptions: coupling (112) is recorded as 'external:god' and locus (116) as 'paired with knowing and walking in the light' — that is, the shout is welded to its Godward direction and set beside the interior knowing and the walking 'in the light of your face' (v15). This means the jubilant cry is not a bare noise but the outward face of an inner state: the people who KNOW the shout are those whose walk is lit by God's presence. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) reads it as 'the joyful acclamation of God as king, worship as jubilant cry.' For the inner being, the shout is joy breaking into voice — an interior gladness and knowledge of God-as-king that cannot stay silent, the corporate counterpart to the individual soul lifted in the other readings.

_Story_

**lift up** — Psa 143:8  ·  `H5375:liftup#10`

_Story_

By the end of this desperate prayer, the poet's spirit is fainting and his heart is appalled — he feels crushed, close to the grave. And yet, out of that collapse, he lifts his whole soul up to God. He raises his failing inner self as his one offering, the very reason he dares to ask God to show him the way and save his life. It is the same handing-over of the soul that a calmer psalm makes in peace, but here it is made from the edge of despair. Even emptied and exhausted, he turns everything he has left upward, trusting God with the whole of himself.

_Reading_

The family's last reading returns to lifting the soul: sense (101) 'lift up the soul', and — like 25:1 — type (102) is affect, a devotional lifting of the whole inner self.
