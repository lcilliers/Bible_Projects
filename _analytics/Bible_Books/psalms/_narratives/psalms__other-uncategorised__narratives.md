## Psa 8:4-7

> Psa 8:4 what is man that you are mindful of him, and the son of man that you care for him ? Psa 8:5 Yet you have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings and crowned him with glory and honor . Psa 8:6 You have given him dominion over the works of your hands ; you have put all things under his feet , Psa 8:7 all sheep and oxen , and also the beasts of the field , Psa 8:9 O Lord , our Lord , how majestic is your name in all the earth !

**dominion** — Psa 8:6  ·  `H4910:dominion#1`

_Story_

Looking up at the vast sky, the psalmist asks why God would bother with a creature as small as a human being. The surprising answer is that people have been handed real authority over the world and everything in it. This was not earned or seized; it was simply given, placed in human hands from above. It is not a passing feeling but a settled standing that belongs to the whole person. And with the honour comes a task: to be crowned is also to be made a ruler, answerable for the world entrusted to us.

_Reading_

The inner being here reckons with its own dignity. Contemplating 'what is man,' the interior registers that mankind has been GIVEN DOMINION (sense 101, mashal) over the works of God's hands. This is read as a state (102), not a self-initiated act - and fittingly the source (103) goes unrecorded: dominion is conferred, not self-generated; it arrives from outside as gift. No seat (104: none) lodges it in an organ - it is a standing of the whole person, whose locus is internal:ib-state (116). The bearer is mankind (105); the operation (106) is the human vocation contemplated - the crowned creature is also a ruler, responsibility folded into the honour - with target vocation itself (107), coupled as given-dominion (112). Manner (108: none) leaves uncoloured how it is held; intensity, specifier and effect are all silent (109/110/111), the psalm dwelling on the fact of the charge rather than its degree or its outworking. No prohibition (113). The finding is a characteristic (115): dominion as an inner reckoning with a calling.

**unjust** — Psa 43:1  ·  `H5766:unjust#1`

_Story_

The psalmist pleads to be rescued from a man he calls unjust. What stands out is that this injustice is not described as a single wrong the man commits, but as the kind of person he simply is - a crookedness woven through his whole character. The passage does not say where it came from or what it is doing at that exact moment; it just names it. And it never travels alone: the unjust man is paired with a deceitful one, so that dishonesty and injustice form a single portrait of the enemy the psalmist wants to escape.

_Reading_

Here the inner being appears as the fixed character of the enemy: avlah, INJUSTICE / unrighteousness (sense 101), read as a status (102) rather than an action - a condition of the man, not a deed caught in motion. Accordingly operation (106: none), target (107: none) and manner (108: none) are all explicitly empty: the passage does not show this injustice doing anything in the moment; it names what the man is. Source (103) is unrecorded - no origin is given for the perversity; it simply constitutes him. No seat (104: none) locates it in an organ; it pervades the person (locus internal:ib-state, 116). The bearer is the unjust man (105) from whom the psalmist begs deliverance, and the single coupling pairs him with the deceitful man (112) - injustice keeping company with deceit, the two-fold portrait of the 'ungodly people.' Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all silent (109-113): the reading refuses to embellish, registering a character rather than its measure. A characteristic (115).

## Psa 48:4-6

> Psa 48:4 For behold , the kings assembled ; they came on together . Psa 48:5 As soon as they saw it, they were astounded ; they were in panic ; they took to flight . Psa 48:6 Trembling took hold of them there , anguish as of a woman in labor . Psa 48:7 By the east wind you shattered the ships of Tarshish.

**astounded** — Psa 48:5  ·  `H8539:astound#1`

_Story_

Enemy kings gather and march against God's city, confident. But the moment they see it, something breaks inside them. They are stunned - not by anything they did to themselves, but by a power greater than they can face, the same power that will soon shatter their ships. The shock does not stay still: amazement tips into panic, panic into trembling, and trembling drives them to flee. The passage looks straight into the fear of God's foes and reports it plainly - dread that starts in the eyes and empties out into headlong flight.

_Reading_

This reading opens the interior of the enemy - and refuses to soften it. The assembled kings (bearer 105) are ASTOUNDED (sense 101, tamah), an action (102) of the mind stunned. Uniquely in this family a source (103) is named: God's terrifying power that shatters (v7), so the collapse is not self-caused but triggered from outside - the interior seized by what it beholds. The operation (106) is simply to be astounded, its target the sight of Zion (107); no seat (104: none) confines it to an organ (locus internal:ib-state, 116). Manner is unrecorded (108: none), yet the coupling (112) pairs the amazement with panic - the first move of a terror-sequence (astounded, panic, trembling, anguish) the discovery (114) traces. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111): the text names the shock without grading it. No prohibition (113). A characteristic (115): the foe's inner overthrow read with the same weight as any believer's wonder - dread that begins in the eyes and ends in flight.

**approve** — Psa 49:13  ·  `H7521:approv#1`

_Story_

A rich man boasts of his wealth and lives as though he will never die. He does not; he perishes like an animal. Yet the striking thing is what the watching crowd does: they applaud. Generation after generation admires the very boasting that leads to the grave, endorsing the folly instead of seeing through it. The passage does not say why people do this or how deeply they feel it - only that this approval keeps the folly alive, and that the same capacity to admire could just as easily be turned toward what actually lasts.

_Reading_

The inner being here is the crowd's endorsement of folly. The people who follow (bearer 105) APPROVE (sense 101, ratsah - be pleased with), an action (102) directed at their boasts (target 107) - the vaunting of the doomed rich. Source (103) is unrecorded: no cause is named for the approval; it simply perpetuates itself down the generations. No seat (104: none) fixes it in an organ (locus internal:ib-state, 116); manner (108: none) is uncoloured. The coupling (112) marks the approvers as belonging to the foolishly confident - the endorsement binds the crowd to the folly it admires. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111): the psalm registers the approval without measuring how strong or with what result, though the surrounding verses supply the irony - those approved perish like beasts. No prohibition (113). Note the range of ratsah across this family (contrast H7521:pleas, consenting to a thief; H7521:hold, cherishing Zion's stones): here the same faculty of glad approval is bent onto boasting. A characteristic (115).

**Truly** — Psa 49:7  ·  `H6299:truly#1`

_Story_

The psalm confronts a hard fact about money and death. No matter how rich a person is, he cannot buy back his own life or anyone else's; there is no price high enough to pay off the grave. The word 'truly' underlines it - this is stated as flat, unarguable reality. What matters here is precisely what cannot be done: the wealthy imagine their riches make them safe, but death takes rich and poor alike. The passage lets that impossibility stand on its own, a leveling truth that strips away every boast.

_Reading_

This reading turns on an impossibility. The sense (101) is padah, to RANSOM / redeem - but negated: no man can. It is an action (102) whose bearer is 'no man' (105), able to do nothing; the operation (106) is ransom, explicitly negated, its target another - a brother (107). The manner (108) is emphatic, carried by the opening 'truly.' Crucially the prohibition dimension is present and marked negated (113): 'NO man can ransom another' - the passage's force lies in what cannot be done. Source (103) is unrecorded, and coupling is none (112) - the impotence stands alone, unattached. No seat (104: none) locates it (locus internal:ib-state, 116); intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111). The discovery (114) names the point: wealth is powerless before death; no human price buys off the grave, the great equaliser the rich forget. A characteristic (115): a truth the interior must reckon with - the limit that levels pomp.

**adulterers** — Psa 50:18  ·  `H5003:adulterer#1`

_Story_

God turns to accuse the wicked man who recites his laws while living against them. One charge is the crowd he chooses: he delights in thieves and keeps easy company with adulterers. The point is not a single act pinned to a single moment, but the kind of people he is at home among - the immoral are his fellowship. A person is revealed by the company he keeps, and this man has thrown in his lot with the unfaithful. His corruption spreads by association, caught from those he chooses to stand beside.

_Reading_

The inner being here is exposed through the company it keeps. The sense (101) is naaph, ADULTERERS / to commit adultery, an action (102) whose bearer is the adulterers themselves - the wicked's chosen company (105). The operation (106) is to commit adultery, but the target (107: none) is left empty: the reading does not fix an object; the point is not a particular act but a shared condition. Source (103) is unrecorded; no seat (104: none) locates it (locus internal:ib-state, 116); manner (108: none) is uncoloured. The one coupling (112) names the mechanism: this is the company the wicked keeps - fellowship with the immoral. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111). No prohibition (113), though the whole indictment is God's charge against the wicked who mouth his covenant while consorting with thieves and adulterers. The discovery (114) calls it corruption by association. A characteristic (115): the interior known by whom it runs with.

**pleased** — Psa 50:18  ·  `H7521:pleas#1`

_Story_

The same accusation against the wicked man reaches deeper here. When he sees a thief at work, he is glad - his heart takes the thief's side. This is the inner approval underneath the outward friendships: first the heart consents to the wrong, then the company follows. The word for being pleased is the very one used for delighting in God's ways, which makes the twist sharper - the same power of approval that should welcome good is here bent toward theft and betrayal. It is complicity from the inside out.

_Reading_

Distinct from the adulterers he keeps company with, this reading isolates the inward consent itself. The sense (101) is ratsah - to be PLEASED with, to consent - an action (102) whose bearer is the wicked (105) and whose target is the thief (107). The operation (106) is to be pleased / consent: seeing a thief, the heart approves. Source (103) is unrecorded; no seat (104: none) fixes it (locus internal:ib-state, 116); manner (108: none) is uncoloured. The coupling (112) pairs this consent with keeping company with adulterers - the two verses of the indictment holding together, inward approval and outward fellowship. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111); no prohibition (113). The discovery (114) names it inner complicity with evil, and marks the irony against ratsah's proper use (approving God's ways). Preserving the distinction from H5003:adulterer in the same verse: adultery names the company, this names the heart that consents to it. A characteristic (115).

**willing** — Psa 51:12  ·  `H5081:will#1`

_Story_

In his prayer for forgiveness, David asks not just to be cleansed but to be changed inside. He begs for a willing spirit - an eager, generous, ready readiness to obey, the opposite of grudging duty. This is not a passing mood but a settled state seated in the very spirit of a person, and he cannot manufacture it himself; he asks God to uphold him with it. It comes bound together with restored joy: the forgiven heart is both glad and glad to obey. What he wants is an inner disposition that gladly says yes.

_Reading_

In the great penitential prayer the inner being is asked for a specific disposition. The sense (101) is nadib - WILLING, free, noble, the root of freewill offerings - read as a status (102), a condition of the self rather than a single act. Uniquely here a seat (104) is named: the spirit; and the locus (116) is internal:spirit, the deepest register of the person. The bearer is the penitent (105); the operation (106) is to be upheld by it - the willing spirit sustains him - with target none (107), since it is not aimed outward but holds him steady. Source (103) is unrecorded: the disposition is asked of God ('uphold me'), not self-produced. Manner (108: none) is uncoloured. The coupling (112) pairs it with the restored joy of salvation - readiness and joy together as the fruit of pardon. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111); no prohibition (113). The discovery (114) names it a free, generous, glad obedience, second of the psalm's three spirit-states. A characteristic (115).

**corrupt** — Psa 53:1  ·  `H7843:corrupt#1`

_Story_

The fool tells himself there is no God, and the result works its way through everything he is. He does not just do bad things; he becomes rotten, spoiled all the way through. The passage widens this from one fool to the whole human race - looking down from heaven, God finds no one who is good, not even one. Denying God is not shown as a harmless private opinion; it curdles into a corruption that fills the life with abominable wrong. The inner denial and the outward rot are one movement.

_Reading_

This reads the interior of the godless as a settled ruin. The sense (101) is shachat - to be CORRUPT, act ruinously, spoil - a status (102), a condition following the fool's denial of God 'in his heart.' The bearer is the fools / mankind (105); the operation (106) is to be corrupt / act ruinously, but target (107: none) is empty - corruption here is not aimed at an object; it is what they have become. Source (103) is unrecorded, though the passage's logic supplies it (the heart that says 'no God'); no seat (104: none) fixes it (locus internal:ib-state, 116); manner (108: none) is uncoloured. The coupling (112) pairs corruption with abominable iniquity, and the discovery (114) notes it is restated in v3 (alach) - the whole race gone rotten, none doing good. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111); no prohibition (113). A characteristic (115): moral rot as the consequence-shape of practical atheism - not merely wrong deeds but a spoiled condition of the person.

**work** — Psa 53:4  ·  `H6466:work#1`

_Story_

The wicked in this psalm do not stumble into wrong now and then; they work at it, the way a person works a trade. Evil is their craft, something they practise and produce. And it goes hand in hand with a chosen blindness - they 'have no knowledge,' not because they cannot know God but because they will not. The same ordinary word for doing and making is used elsewhere for doing no wrong at all; here it is bent to the steady manufacture of harm. Evildoing has become their occupation.

_Reading_

Here evil is not a lapse but a trade. The sense (101) is paal - to WORK, practise, make - an action (102) whose bearer is the evildoers (105) and whose target is evil, aven (107) - a coupling of verb to object that welds the doing to the thing done. The operation (106) is to work / practise: iniquity as a craft plied. Source (103) is unrecorded; no seat (104: none) fixes it (locus internal:ib-state, 116); manner (108: none) is uncoloured. A second coupling (112) pairs the working of evil with having no knowledge - the discovery (114) sharpening this into a settled practice joined to a wilful ignorance of God. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111); no prohibition (113). Preserve the distinction from H6466:do (Psa 119:3), where the same verb paal describes doing NO wrong: the identical faculty of doing is here turned to manufacturing iniquity. A characteristic (115): evildoing as vocation, not accident.

**trouble** — Psa 55:10  ·  `H5999:troubl#1`

_Story_

The psalmist looks at his city and sees its whole mood poisoned. Day and night, prowling its walls, are violence and strife; inside, trouble and wrongdoing have settled in like permanent residents, and oppression and fraud never leave the market. This is not one crime but an atmosphere - harm that wearies and wounds, soaked into the streets. The passage does not single out a victim or an act; it names the climate of a place given over to the wicked. The same word can mean honest weariness or legal cruelty; here it is the grinding mischief that fills a corrupt town.

_Reading_

The inner life of a whole city is read as its atmosphere. The sense (101) is amal - TROUBLE, toil-mischief, wearying harm - a status (102) filling the city, not a single deed. The bearer is the city's wicked (105); operation (106: none) and target (107: none) are both empty - the reading does not show trouble acting on anyone in the moment; it names a pervading condition. Source (103) is unrecorded; no seat (104: none) fixes it (locus internal:ib-state, 116); manner (108: none) is uncoloured. The one coupling (112) pairs trouble with iniquity - the twin residents that never depart the marketplace. Intensity, specifier and effect are silent (109-111); no prohibition (113). The discovery (114) names it the harmful toil the wicked inflict - wrong that wearies and wounds. Note amal's range across this family (H5999:toil, life's weary labour; H5999:injustic, legalised oppression): here it is civic mischief, wrong soaked into a place. A characteristic (115).

**hide** — Psa 55:12  ·  `H5641:hide#1`

_Story_

The psalmist works out why this betrayal cuts so deep. If an open enemy had insulted him, he could have borne it, or simply kept out of the man's way - you can hide from a foe you can see coming. But this attacker was a close friend, a companion who worshipped beside him. Against that there is no hiding, because the betrayer was already inside his defences. The passage measures the pain precisely by naming the escape that is no longer available: the very intimacy that made the friendship sweet is what leaves him exposed.

_Reading_

This reading is a defence that never gets to happen. The sense (101) is sathar - to HIDE oneself - an action (102) whose bearer is the psalmist (105) and whose operation (106) is to take cover, but only hypothetically: 'then I could hide.'
