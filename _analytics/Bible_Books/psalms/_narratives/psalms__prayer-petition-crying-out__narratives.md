**cried** — Psa 3:4  ·  `H7121:cri#1`

_Story_

Voices all around him insist that God will not save him, and in reply the man simply lifts his voice and cries out loud to the LORD — and is answered from God's holy hill. The cry pours straight out of a soul under siege; nothing is said about where inside him it begins or what part of him makes it. Its whole force is just the reaching upward. It is not weighed or measured, and no inner change is tallied afterward; the point is that he cried and was heard. This crying is treated as a real feature of his inner life, and nothing forbids it.

_Reading_

The characteristic is a cry-aloud lifted to the LORD (sense), read as affect (type) — an inner state discharging as voice rather than a reasoned act of will. It is borne by the psalmist (bearer) and located wholly inside the inner state (locus internal:ib-state). Under the triple taunt that there is no salvation for him in God, the operation is the interior going vocal upward: the besieged self answers the verdict of 'no help' by appealing to the only helper, and is answered from the holy hill. Its target is the crying-out itself, welded to the act of crying aloud (coupling). No source (present:false) names a faculty behind the cry — it rises without a stated origin; the seat is none, so this is not a heart- or throat-act but an undifferentiated movement of the whole self; manner is none, and intensity, specifier and effect are all unrecorded — the reading grades no force and books no resulting inner change, so the outcome is carried by the answer, not by an itemised effect. No prohibition attaches: crying aloud is not a forbidden movement. Role marks it a genuine characteristic. Grounding: v4 'I cried aloud to the LORD, and he answered me.'

*Why this reading differs:* Of the three readings of this cried-item, this is the cry answered directly from the holy hill against a taunt of 'no help' — the accent falls on the immediacy of the reply to a soul told God will not act.
