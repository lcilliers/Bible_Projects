## Psa 8:4-7

> Psa 8:4 what is man that you are mindful of him, and the son of man that you care for him ? Psa 8:5 Yet you have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings and crowned him with glory and honor . Psa 8:6 You have given him dominion over the works of your hands ; you have put all things under his feet , Psa 8:7 all sheep and oxen , and also the beasts of the field , Psa 8:9 O Lord , our Lord , how majestic is your name in all the earth !

**mindful** — Psa 8:4  ·  `H2142:mindful#1`

_Story_

Standing under a night sky full of stars, the poet is struck by how tiny a human being is against all that vastness - and yet God keeps human beings in mind and cares for them. The poem gives no reason for this wonder except the sky itself; the feeling simply rises when he looks up. It is not lodged in any one part of him but seizes the whole person as a kind of humbled amazement. Nothing is said about how strongly he feels it or what he then does; the wonder is left standing on its own. What the passage shows is a self shrinking in astonishment precisely because it discovers it is noticed.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is being 'mindful of frail man' and the type (102) is cognition: the reading treats mindfulness here not as an emotion but as an act of the mind holding another in view. Uniquely, the mind held is God's, and the wonder is the psalmist's; the bearer (105) is the psalmist and the operation (106) is that, confronted with the vast heavens, the self marvels that God is mindful of and cares for so small a creature as man. Source (103) is absent (present:false): the text supplies no prior cause for the wonder beyond the sight of the sky itself, so the movement reads as spontaneous, triggered by contemplation rather than by any inner antecedent. Seat (104) is explicitly 'none' - the operation is not lodged in any organ (no heart, no kidneys); it is a whole-self astonishment, not an organ-function. The target (107) is 'humbled-wonder' and the coupling (112) is the very question 'why-mindful-of-man', so the process runs: contemplation of vastness -> awareness of own smallness -> astonishment at being noticed -> humbled wonder. Manner (108) is 'none' and intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are all absent: the reading records no stated style, no measured degree, no downstream consequence - the wonder is left as a standing interior posture rather than issuing in an act. The discovery note (114) fixes it in v4: the interior weighs its own smallness against the star-filled sky and is astonished at being noticed at all. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state and role (115) is characteristic: this is a genuine inner-being movement, God's mindfulness the arena the self measures itself against, and the finding is the self-diminishment-in-wonder that God's attention provokes.

**remember** — Psa 22:27  ·  `H2142:remember#1`

_Story_

Here the poet imagines that the rescue of one suffering man will ripple outward until people everywhere call God to mind and turn to worship him. This remembering is less a cold act of recall than a warm turning of the whole self back toward God. It is carried not by one person but by all the nations, so the private deliverance becomes a public homecoming. The poem does not measure how deeply they feel it or what follows; it simply names remembering and turning as a single movement. The striking thing is that memory here is the very doorway to conversion.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'the nations remember and worship' and the type (102) is affect - notably NOT cognition here: the remembering is read as an affective turning, not a bare recollection. The bearer (105) is 'the nations' (not the psalmist), which is the distinctive mark of this reading among the 'remember' set: the interior movement is universalised, carried by all peoples rather than by the single sufferer. Source (103) is absent: no inner cause is named; the operation (106) supplies the external trigger - the one man's vindication spreading to the peoples so that all the ends of the earth remember and turn to the LORD. Seat (104) is 'none' - the turning is not organ-located but a whole-people reorientation. Target (107) is 'universal-turning' and coupling (112) is 'nations-remember': the process runs rescue-of-the-one -> memory awakened in the many -> turning and worship. Manner (108) is 'none', and intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) are all absent: the reading does not measure the fervour or trace a consequence beyond the worship itself. The discovery note (114) reads v27 as the operation universalising the psalm: the one man's rescue draws the nations' interior back to God. Role (115) is characteristic and locus (116) internal:ib-state: within the human inner life this is memory functioning as the hinge of conversion - to remember God is to turn to him, the two named as one motion.

*Why this reading differs:* Against the other 'remember' readings (mostly the psalmist recalling God in distress), this one is borne by 'the nations', typed as affect not cognition, and located as universal-turning: the bearer shifts from the single sufferer to all peoples, and remembrance becomes corporate conversion rather than private recall.

**remember** — Psa 25:7  ·  `H2142:remember#2`

_Story_

The poet begs God not to hold the sins of his younger years against him, but to keep him in mind through love instead. It is the prayer of a person conscious of guilt, asking that the old record be wiped and that mercy, not failure, be the way God sees him. Nothing is said about how intense the feeling is or what comes of it; the plea is simply left with God. The heart of it is a wish for a kind of chosen forgetting - to be remembered kindly and forgotten in one's faults.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'remember not my youth's sins' and the type (102) is cognition. This reading is distinctive because the operation (106) is a plea about God's remembering, not the psalmist's own: the self asks God NOT to remember the sins of its youth but to remember it by steadfast love - a penitent appeal for a divine forgetting. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the movement is inward petition directed outward to God. Source (103) is absent: no prior inner state is named as cause; the penitence stands as the given posture. Seat (104) is 'none' - not organ-located; it is the whole penitent self appealing. Target (107) is 'penitence' and coupling (112) is 'remember-not-sins': the process runs guilt-consciousness -> plea for the record to be blotted -> request that love, not sin, be the lens of God's memory. Manner (108) is 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) all absent - no degree, no consequence traced, the appeal left open. The discovery note (114) reads v7 as a penitent's plea: let the record of old sin be blotted, and let love be the lens instead. Role (115) is characteristic, locus (116) internal:ib-state: as an inner-being movement this is memory used morally - the self petitioning for selective divine memory, wanting to be remembered under mercy and forgotten under judgement.

*Why this reading differs:* Unlike the readings where the psalmist himself remembers, here the psalmist asks GOD to remember-not: remembrance is inverted into a petition for divine forgetting of sin, the target being penitence rather than recall of God's deeds.

**never** — Psa 30:6  ·  `H5769:never#1`

_Story_

Looking back, the poet remembers how, when life was easy, he told himself nothing could ever shake him. It was the quiet over-confidence that good times breed - security mistaken for permanence, with no deeper cause than comfort itself. The poem does not spell out here how the fall came, only names the smugness for what it was. It is an honest confession of a heart that took ease for granted and assumed it would always stand.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'in prosperity, I shall never be moved' and the type (102) is cognition: the reading treats the confident 'never' as a thought the self once held. The bearer (105) is the psalmist and the operation (106) is that the self recalls that in its ease it presumed it would never be moved - the complacency of good times, now confessed. Source (103) is absent: the text names no deeper cause than the prosperity itself, so the false security reads as bred simply by comfort. Seat (104) is 'none' - the complacency is not organ-located; it is a whole-self settled assumption. Target (107) is 'complacency' and coupling (112) is 'never-be-moved': the process runs prosperity -> lulled interior -> presumption of permanence -> (implicitly, by the psalm's turn) exposure when God hides his face. Manner (108) is 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) all absent - the reading does not grade the complacency or trace its collapse here, though the surrounding verses supply it. The discovery note (114) reads v6 as naming a past over-confidence: prosperity had lulled the interior into a false sense of permanence. Role (115) is characteristic, locus (116) internal:ib-state. Read without valence-bias, this is a dark inner movement narrated honestly: security misread as self-sufficiency, the 'never' of a heart that mistook God's favour for its own unshakeable standing.

**forgotten** — Psa 31:12  ·  `H7911:forgotten#1`

_Story_

The poet feels as though everyone has forgotten him, the way people forget the dead - he has become like a jar that is broken and thrown out. No reason is given; it is simply the crushing sense of having dropped completely out of other people's minds, useless and discarded. This is different from merely being avoided: it is the feeling of being erased altogether. The passage shows that to be forgotten can be lived as a kind of death.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'forgotten like one who is dead' and the type (102) is state - not an action but a condition suffered. This is the distinctive note of the reading: the psalmist is the object, not the agent, of forgetting. The bearer (105) is the psalmist and the operation (106) is that the self has been forgotten like a dead man, become like a broken vessel - the sense of being erased and discarded. Source (103) is absent: no cause is named for the erasure; it is simply the social fact pressing on the interior. Seat (104) is 'none' - the felt erasure is not organ-located but a whole-self experience of oblivion. Target (107) is 'erasure' and coupling (112) is 'forgotten-like-dead': the process runs being-forgotten-by-others -> inward sense of having dropped out of memory -> self-perception as useless, a broken vessel. Manner (108) is 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) all absent - the reading does not grade the desolation or name a consequence, leaving it as raw condition. The discovery note (114) distinguishes it from being merely shunned: this is the sense of having dropped out of memory entirely, useless and cast aside. Role (115) is characteristic, locus (116) internal:ib-state. As an inner-being movement this is the passive face of the memory-family: to be forgotten is experienced as a kind of death, the self annihilated in others' minds.

**remember** — Psa 42:4  ·  `H2142:remember#3`

_Story_

Far from the temple, the poet remembers how he used to walk with the festival crowds to God's house amid shouts of joy - and the memory is bittersweet, remembered as he pours out his grief. The recollection is deliberate, but it does not comfort; recalling that lost happiness only deepens the pain of being cut off now. The poem names no cause and traces no outcome, just the act of remembering bound to the weeping. Here memory cuts rather than heals.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'remember (zakar - these things)' and the type (102) is action: a deliberate recollection. The bearer (105) is David and the operation (106) is simply 'remember', but its object is set by the target (107): the past worship in the throng. The distinctive mark here is the manner (108), which is not 'none' but a genuine pairing - 'pouring out his soul' (item_type pair, spanning the recollection) - so the memory is enacted amid grief, not in calm. Source (103) is absent and coupling (112) is 'none': the reading declines to name an inner cause or a further inner link, isolating the bare act of recall bound only to the outpouring. Seat (104) is 'none' - not organ-located. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) all absent: no degree or consequence recorded, though the note supplies the emotional colour. The discovery note (114) reads it as memory of past worship deepening present grief: this zakar (first of two, vv4,6) is the recollection of festal joy that now pains the exile. Role (115) is characteristic, locus (116) internal:ib-state. As an inner-being movement, memory here works against the self: recalling former nearness to God sharpens the ache of present distance - remembrance as wound.

*Why this reading differs:* This first zakar of Psalm 42 differs from its pair in v6: here the object is past worship (locus internal:ib-state) and the memory WOUNDS - the manner 'pouring out his soul' binds recall to grief; in v6 the object is God himself and memory turns redemptive.

**remember** — Psa 42:6  ·  `H2142:remember#4`

_Story_

From a distant land, cast down and low, the poet turns and calls God himself to mind. Unlike his earlier memory of happier days, this remembering reaches out toward God, and it steadies rather than saddens him - it is his anchor when everything feels far away. It rises straight out of his downcast state, the sinking soul turning upward. The same act of memory that had deepened his grief now, aimed at God, becomes his hope.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'remember (zakar - you, from afar)' and the type (102) is action. The bearer (105) is David; the operation (106) is 'remember' and the target (107) is God himself ('you') - this is the decisive difference from its pair in v4, where the object was past worship. The locus (116) is external:god (not internal:ib-state), marking that the memory reaches out toward God as its object. Manner (108) is 'from the land of Jordan and Hermon' - memory exercised in exile, at the far edge of the land. Source (103) is absent, but coupling (112) is a pairing, 'driven by the cast-down soul' (span-linked to the downcast self of v5-6): so the process runs dejection -> the cast-down soul turning -> Godward remembrance from the far country. Seat (104) is 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) all absent - no degree or outcome measured. The discovery note (114) reads it as memory turned Godward from exile: the redemptive remembering of God himself from the far-off land, hope's anchor in dejection. Role (115) is characteristic, locus external:god. As an inner-being movement this is memory reversing the wound of v4: the same faculty that sharpened grief now, aimed at God rather than at lost joy, becomes the tether of hope.

*Why this reading differs:* Paired with v4: the object shifts from past worship to God himself and the locus from internal:ib-state to external:god, so the SAME faculty that wounded in v4 here becomes redemptive - memory Godward is hope, memory of lost joy is grief. **forgotten** — Psa 44:17  ·  `H7911:forgotten#2`

_Story_

Struck down and shamed, the whole people protest that they have done nothing to deserve it: they have not forgotten God, nor been false to their agreement with him. The remembering here is loyalty held firm - a corporate refusal to abandon God even while he seems to have abandoned them. It is stated flatly as a denial: we have NOT forgotten. This is the anguish of the faithful who suffer anyway, holding up their unbroken memory as proof of their innocence.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'forget (shakach - not forgotten you)' and the type (102) is action. Distinctively, this reading carries an explicit prohibition dimension (113): 'negated (we have NOT forgotten you)' - the only place a prohibition flag fires in this set. The bearer (105) is 'the nation' and the operation (106) is 'forget (negated)', targeting God ('you', 107). Locus (116) is external:god. Source (103) is absent; seat (104) is 'none' - the fidelity is not organ-located but a corporate posture. Coupling (112) is a pairing, 'paired with not being false to covenant' (span-linked), so the process runs undeserved suffering -> protest of innocence -> the double denial: we have not forgotten you AND we have not broken covenant. Manner (108) is 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) all absent - the reading does not grade the loyalty or name a consequence. The discovery note (114) reads it as the protest of innocence: undeserved suffering despite fidelity, the nation insisting it has not abandoned God - the anguish of the faithful afflicted. Role (115) is characteristic, locus external:god. As an inner-being movement this is memory as maintained fidelity under undeserved blows: not-forgetting held up as evidence against the apparent verdict of their suffering.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the 'forgotten' readings this alone fires a prohibition flag (explicit negation) and is borne corporately by 'the nation': forgetting is denied as a claim of innocence, contrasting the passive erasure of Psa 31:12 and the hypothetical apostasy of the paired v20.

**forgotten** — Psa 44:20  ·  `H7911:forgotten#3`

_Story_

The people put a hard question to themselves: if we HAD forgotten God, or turned to some foreign god, wouldn't God know? He sees the secrets of every heart. Here forgetting is imagined rather than admitted - a test they are sure they would pass, since God can read them through and through. It is the opposite of the real forgetting elsewhere in the poem: an offered self-examination, confident that the searcher of hearts would find them loyal.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'forget (shakach - if we had forgotten God)' and the type (102) is action. The distinctive mark is that the bearer (105) is 'the nation (hypothetically)' and the operation (106) is 'forget (hypothetical)': this is forgetting entertained as a supposition, not confessed or denied outright but imagined as a test case. The target (107) is 'the name of our God'; locus (116) external:god.
