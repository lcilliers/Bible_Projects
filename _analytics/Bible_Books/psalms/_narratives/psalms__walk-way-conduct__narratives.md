## Psa 1:1-6

> Psa 1:1 Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of the wicked , nor stands in the way of sinners , nor sits in the seat of scoffers ; Psa 1:2 but his delight is in the law of the Lord , and on his law he meditates day and night . Psa 1:3 He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season , and its leaf does not wither . In all that he does , he prospers . Psa 1:4 The wicked are not so , but are like chaff that the wind drives away. Psa 1:5 Therefore the wicked will not stand in the judgment , nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous ; Psa 1:6 for the Lord knows the way of the righteous , but the way of the wicked will perish .

**walk** — Psa 1:1  ·  `H1980:walk#1`

_Story_

The very first line of the Psalter draws a picture of a happy man, and it starts not with what he does but with what he refuses to do: he will not so much as fall into step with the counsel of the wicked. This is a choice of the will, the mildest possible involvement declined at the door. Walking alongside is the easiest, most casual kind of company, and it is exactly this that he turns down - the first boundary of the inner life, drawn before any wrong is ever committed. The passage does not say where the refusal comes from or measure how firm it is; it simply reports the declining as the man's own settled character, a boundary kept quietly inside him.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is halak read negatively - 'Blessed is the man who WALKS not in the counsel of the wicked': walking named only to be refused. Type (102) is volition: not a felt state but an act of the will, a conduct chosen. Source (103) is absent - the reading does not trace where this refusal springs from; it reports the declining, not its origin. Seat (104) is none: the operation is not organ-located; it is the whole man setting a boundary, not a faculty switching on. Bearer (105) is the blessed man (inferred, to_span 275344): the interior of the one Psalm 1 pronounces happy. Operation (106) is the core event - 'the first refusal: he does not even walk in step with the counsel of the wicked, declining the casual first association.' Target (107) is refusal-1 (inferred): what the walk aims at is the refusal itself, the first boundary drawn. Manner (108) is none: no mode is marked, the declining stated flatly. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), and prohibition (113) are all absent: no degree is scored, nothing narrows it, no downstream result is booked, and it is not framed as a forbidding but as the blessed man's own choice. Coupling (112) is 'walk-not' (inferred): the reading is welded to its own negation - this walk exists only as a walk-declined, the refusal being the whole of it. Discovery (114) names the placement: the mildest, most casual involvement, walking-alongside, is already refused; the interior sets its first boundary. Role (115) is characteristic: a standing trait of the blessed man. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - the refusal sits wholly inside, a set of the inner life. The full process: from no named source and no organ-seat, the blessed man's will declines even to fall into step with wicked counsel - the gentlest first association refused, an inward boundary drawn before any deed, held as a settled trait of the inner life.

*Why this reading differs:* First of eighteen distinct walk-readings and the opening one in a three-step descent Psalm 1 refuses - walk (v1a), stand (v1b), sit (v1c). This reading is the mildest rung: casual walking-alongside declined. It differs from every later walk-reading in being purely negative and purely a boundary - no God-ward target yet, only refusal-1; the positive, God-oriented walks come later in the family.

**stands** — Psa 1:1  ·  `H5975:stand#1`

_Story_

The happy man's refusals in Psalm 1 come in stages, and this is the second, sterner one. It is one thing to decline to walk alongside the wicked; it is more to refuse to stop and stand where sinners stand - to take up a position among them, to settle in. That is what he will not do. Standing is more committed than walking past, and so the boundary of the inner life deepens here from merely declining company to refusing to plant one's feet in it. Again the passage gives no source and no measure; it simply shows a man whose settled character will not let him take his stand among wrongdoers.

_Reading_

Target (107) is refusal-2 (inferred): the aim is the second boundary. Manner (108) is none. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), and prohibition (113) are all absent - no degree, no narrowing, no booked result, and it reads as the man's own declining, not a decree. The full process: from no named source and no seat, the blessed man's will declines to halt and take up a position among sinners - the boundary of v1a deepened, passing-by hardening into a refusal to settle, an inward stance held as a trait of the inner life.

**walk** — Psa 15:2  ·  `H1980:walk#2`

_Story_

Psalm 15 asks who may live in God's presence, and the answer begins with how a person walks through ordinary life: blamelessly, doing what is right. Here walking is not one act but the whole shape of a life - a way of living with no crookedness in it. This consistent uprightness is the very first qualification for dwelling with God; the life itself is the credential. The passage does not explain where such integrity comes from or grade how much of it is needed; it simply binds the walk and the blamelessness together as one thing and holds it up as an inner condition that fits a person to stand on God's holy hill.

Bearer (105) is the righteous (inferred, to_span 274617): the one who may dwell on God's holy hill. Operation (106) is 'the one who may dwell walks with integrity and does what is right - a whole manner of life without crookedness.' Target (107) is integrity-of-walk (inferred): the aim is a life of consistent uprightness. Manner (108) is none: no mode marked. The full process: from no named source and no seat, the one who would dwell with God walks blamelessly and does right - a whole undivided manner of life, integrity and walk welded together, held as an inner condition that qualifies the person for God's presence.

*Why this reading differs:* One of eighteen walk-readings; here the accent is on walking as the qualification to dwell with God - integrity of the whole life. It differs from Psalm 1's refusals (negative boundaries) by being wholly positive: not what the walker avoids but the undivided uprightness he embodies. The target has moved from mere refusal to integrity-of-walk, though the locus is still internal.

**become corrupt** — Psa 53:3  ·  `H0444:becomecorrupt#1`

_Story_

Psalm 53 pronounces a bleak verdict over the whole human race: together they have become corrupt. The word pictures something gone sour, like milk left to spoil - a nature turned bad, not a single wrong act. It is a condition people are in, not something aimed at a victim, and it is not one person's failing but a rottenness shared by all at once, none of them exempt. The psalm had already said this of the fool at the start; now it spreads the same corruption across everyone.
