## Psa 26:1-12

Psa 26:5 I hate the assembly of evildoers , and I will not sit with the wicked . Psa 26:6 I wash my hands in innocence and go around your altar , O Lord , Psa 26:7 proclaiming thanksgiving aloud , and telling all your wondrous deeds . Psa 26:8 O Lord , I love the habitation of your house and the place where your glory dwells . **test** — Psa 26:2  ·  `H6884:test#1`

_Story_

Here a man does something unusual: instead of dreading God's scrutiny, he asks for it. He invites God to prove him, try him, and test his heart and mind. This is entirely an act of his own will, turned back on himself - he is not testing anyone else but laying his own inner life open. The text ties the request to his heart, the thing he wants examined, yet it names no fear, no reluctance, and no single part of him as the place it comes from; the quiet is the point. Nothing is said about how hard the test will be or how it turns out - only that he willingly offers himself. And that willingness is itself the trait on display: only someone confident in his own integrity would ask to be searched.

_Reading_

This is a rare inward movement in the family: the self does not resist testing but summons it. The sense (D101 sense, 7696132) is an invitation to God to test the heart; the type (D102 type, 7696133) is volition - not a deed done to another but an act of the will offered up. The bearer (D105 bearer, 7696135) is the psalmist himself, and the operation (D106 operation, 7696136) is precisely the self inviting God to prove, try and test its heart and mind, a confidence willing to be assayed. The target (D107 target, 7696137) is not an enemy but 'confident-self-submission': the movement turns back on the self. The locus (D116 locus, 7696140) confirms this, internal:ib-state - the whole transaction happens inside the inner life even though God is the one addressed. No seat is recorded (D104 seat, 7696134 = none): the offering of heart and mind is not tied to a single organ, a real silence given the heart-language, yet the coupling (D112 coupling, 7696139) binds the operation to 'test-my-heart', so the heart is the thing laid open. Manner is none (D108 manner, 7696138): the psalmist names no dread or reluctance, and that silence itself reads as the calm of integrity. Source is absent (D103), and intensity, specifier and effect are absent (D109/D110/D111), with no prohibition (D113): the text records only the bare, willed act of self-exposure, not its degree or outcome. The discovery note (D114 discovery, 7696141) draws the point out: only an integrity sure of itself asks to be tested. The role (D115 role, 7696142) is characteristic - this willingness-to-be-searched is itself a trait of the inner being. The absences matter as much as the content: no coercion, no resistance, no dread, no stated result - testing here is desired, not endured.

## Psa 54:1-7

**looked** — Psa 54:7  ·  `H7200:look#1`

_Story_

After being rescued, the psalmist says his own eye has looked in triumph on his enemies. This is an outward act - his gaze rests on the people who had hunted his life, now brought down. But the reason he can look this way is not his own power: it is because God put an end to them. So the triumphant look is really the fruit of God's rescue, not personal revenge - the quiet confidence of a man who has seen justice done, not the sneer of someone gloating. The text doesn't dwell on how intense the look is or forbid it; it simply records the vindicated eye of the delivered.

_Reading_

The looking here (D101 sense, 7653358) is raah in its triumphant register - 'my eye has looked in triumph on my enemies'. The type (D102 type, 7653359) is action, and unlike the self-offering of Psa 26 the movement now points outward: the seat (D104 seat, 7653360) is the eye, the bearer (D105 bearer, 7653361) the psalmist, the operation (D106 operation, 7653362) a gaze in triumph, the target (D107 target, 7653363) his enemies. The locus (D116 locus, 7653367) is external:person - the inner state discharges itself upon other people. Crucially the source (D103 source, 7653366) is not the psalmist's own strength but God's act: because God put an end to the enemies (v5). The manner (D108 manner, 7653364) is 'in triumph / vindication', and the coupling (D112 coupling, 7653365) makes the gaze the fruit of God's deliverance - the eye's look is an effect with a divine cause. The discovery note (D114 discovery, 7653368) guards against misreading: this is not gloating cruelty but the settled assurance of God's justice done. Intensity, specifier and effect are absent (D109/D110/D111), and there is no prohibition (D113): the text does not measure the intensity of the look or forbid it, leaving it a bare, vindicated beholding. The role (D115 role, 7653369) is characteristic. Read without valence-bias, the finding is honest about a hard thing - the delivered man looks with satisfaction on his fallen foes - while grounding that satisfaction in God's justice rather than personal spite.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the three 'look' readings this one is retrospective and accomplished: the deliverance has already happened (God HAS put an end to the enemies), so the eye looks back in settled triumph on defeated foes - the source dimension records an achieved cause. Contrast look#2 (Psa 59:10), where the same triumphant gaze is still anticipated ('God WILL let me look') and that source is absent, and look#3 (Psa 63:2), where the target shifts entirely from enemies to God beheld in the sanctuary. Same verb, but here the eye rests on people in the past tense of vindication.

**looked** — Psa 59:10  ·  `H7200:look#2`

_Story_

Here the psalmist looks ahead: he says God will let him look in triumph on his enemies. It is the same kind of gaze as elsewhere - the eye resting in triumph on those who oppose him - but this time it hasn't happened yet. There is no accomplished rescue behind it; instead it rests on the confidence that God, in his steadfast love, is coming to meet him. So the triumphant look is something he expects, not something he already holds. The text sets no limit on it and does not measure it - it simply voices the sure hope that the moment of vindication will arrive.

_Reading_

As in Psa 54, the looking (D101 sense, 7655220) is raah in triumph on enemies, the type (D102 type, 7655221) action, the seat (D104 seat, 7655222) the eye, the bearer (D105 bearer, 7655223) the psalmist, the operation (D106 operation, 7655224) a look in triumph, the target (D107 target, 7655225) his enemies, and the locus (D116 locus, 7655228) external:person. The manner (D108 manner, 7655226) is 'in triumph, by God's grant', and the coupling (D112 coupling, 7655227) grounds it in the God who meets him in his steadfast love (v10). But note the divergence from Psa 54:7: here the source is absent (D103, present:false) - no accomplished cause is recorded, because the deliverance has not yet happened; the gaze is anticipated, not remembered. The discovery note (D114 discovery, 7655229) confirms it: 'God WILL let me look' - the vindicated gaze the delivered psalmist expects. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (D109/D110/D111/D113): the future look is asserted without measure or limit. The role (D115 role, 7655230) is characteristic. The finding lies in the difference: the same trait of triumphant beholding operates in the mode of confident expectation rather than settled fact - trust that the look will come because God's love is coming to meet him.

*Why this reading differs:* This 'look' reading is anticipatory where look#1 (Psa 54:7) is accomplished. The key difference is the source dimension: Psa 54:7 records an achieved cause (God HAS put an end to the enemies), but here that source is absent because the looking is still future ('God WILL let me look'). The trait is identical, yet it operates in the mode of expectant trust rather than settled triumph. Both differ from look#3 (Psa 63:2), where the gaze turns from enemies to God.

## Psa 63:1-11

> A Psalm of David , when he was in the wilderness of Judah . Psa 63:11 But the king shall rejoice in God ; all who swear by him shall exult , for the mouths of liars will be stopped .

**looked** — Psa 63:2  ·  `H7200:look#3`

_Story_

This time the looking is turned upward, not outward at enemies. The psalmist remembers gazing on God in the sanctuary, beholding his power and glory. And this looking is set against a raging thirst - in a dry and weary land his soul longs for God, and this vision is the very thing that answers that longing. Unlike the triumphant glance at defeated foes, this gaze isn't pinned to the eye or to any single part of him; it is the whole hungry soul feeding on what it sees. Nothing limits or measures it - it is simply the sight a longing person lives on, even in the desert.

_Reading_

This is the decisive variation within 'look'. The sense (D101 sense, 7656422) is again raah - 'I have looked upon you in the sanctuary' - and the type (D102 type, 7656423) is action, but the target (D107 target, 7656427) is no longer enemies: it is God himself, his power and glory, in the sanctuary. The locus (D116 locus, 7656430) is external:god, not external:person. The bearer (D105 bearer, 7656425) is the psalmist; the operation (D106 operation, 7656426) is to look upon / gaze; the manner (D108 manner, 7656428) is 'beholding his power and glory'. The seat is none (D104 seat, 7656424): this contemplative gaze is not localised in the eye as the triumphant look was - a telling silence, for the beholding of God is not tied to the physical organ. The coupling (D112 coupling, 7656429) is the richest note: this vision is the one that answers the thirst, tying the looking back to the desert-longing of v1 ('my soul thirsts for you'). Source is absent (D103), and intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (D109/D110/D111/D113): the contemplation is recorded without cause, measure or limit. The discovery note (D114 discovery, 7656431) calls it the vision the longing soul feeds on even in the wilderness. Role (D115 role, 7656432) is characteristic. The finding: the same verb that beholds fallen enemies here beholds God, and the movement is satisfied desire rather than vindication - the inner being's gaze turned upward to feed on what it lacks.

*Why this reading differs:* This is the sharpest divergence among the three 'look' readings. Where look#1 (Psa 54:7) and look#2 (Psa 59:10) turn the triumphant eye outward on enemies (locus external:person), here the target and locus shift to God himself (external:god), and the manner changes from triumph/vindication to contemplative beholding that answers a thirst. The same verb carries an entirely different inner movement - satisfied longing rather than vindication - and drops the eye-seat the other two share.

## Psa 64:1-6

> To the choirmaster . A Psalm of David . Psa 64:1 Hear my voice , O God , in my complaint ; preserve my life from dread of the enemy . Psa 64:2 Hide me from the secret plots of the wicked , from the throng of evildoers , Psa 64:3 who whet their tongues like swords , who aim bitter words like arrows , Psa 64:4 shooting from ambush at the blameless , shooting at him suddenly and without fear . Psa 64:5 They hold fast to their evil purpose ; they talk of laying snares secretly , thinking , “ Who can see them ?” Psa 64:6 They search out injustice , saying, “We have accomplished a diligent search .” For the inward mind and heart of a man are deep .

**see** — Psa 64:5  ·  `H7200:see#1`

_Story_

This is the dark side of seeing. The wicked, plotting in secret, tell themselves, 'Who can see us?' It is not something they do but a settled state of mind - a confidence, deep inside them, that no one is watching. That presumption is the hidden engine behind their secret traps. And the seeing they deny has no real object at all: they have simply ruled it out, blinding themselves to the fact that God sees and searches even the deepest heart. It is the same word used elsewhere for beholding God, but turned inside out - a self-assured belief that they act unobserved.

_Reading_

This reading inverts the family: seeing appears here as a denied seeing. The sense (D101 sense, 7656759) is raah in the mouth of the wicked - 'Who can see them?' The type (D102 type, 7656760) is status, not action - a settled condition of mind rather than a deed, itself a finding against the active looking elsewhere. The bearer (D105 bearer, 7656762) is the wicked, presuming; the operation (D106 operation, 7656763) is to presume none can see; the locus (D116 locus, 7656767) is internal:ib-state - a movement wholly inside the wicked's interior. Read with no valence-bias (directive 5), this is the descent: the inner being's self-blinding confidence that its scheming is unobserved. The coupling (D112 coupling, 7656766) binds it to the secret snares - the presumption is the interior engine behind the outward plots. Strikingly, target, seat and manner are all 'none' (D107 target 7656764 / D104 seat 7656761 / D108 manner 7656765): the wicked's imagined non-seeing has no object, no organ, no mode - the whole point is that the seeing they deny is empty, aimed at nothing, because they have wrongly ruled it out. Source is absent (D103), and intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (D109/D110/D111/D113). The discovery note (D114 discovery, 7656768) links it to the godless 'who will hear us?' of Ps 59:7 and to God who nonetheless sees and searches the deepest heart (v6-7). Role (D115 role, 7656769) is characteristic. The finding: the same verb that elsewhere beholds God or enemies is here a seeing the wicked cancel in their own minds - the interior presumption of impunity.

*Why this reading differs:* The two 'see' readings are opposites. Here (Psa 64:5) seeing is denied and interior: the wicked presume 'who can see them?' - a status of mind (type status), located inside the inner being (internal:ib-state), with no object, seat or manner because the seeing is cancelled. In see#2 (Psa 66:5) seeing is commanded and outward: 'Come and see what God has done' - an action directed at God's deeds (external:god). The same verb runs from godless presumption of impunity to the summons to behold God's works.

**see** — Psa 66:5  ·  `H7200:see#2`

_Story_

Here seeing is an invitation shouted to everyone: 'Come and see what God has done.' It is not one person's private gaze but a call to a whole crowd to look at God's works - and the reason for the call is that those works are awesome. This summons to see runs alongside a matching call later to 'come and hear': looking and listening are the two ways people are brought to witness what God has done. Nothing narrows it to the physical eye or sets any mood; it is simply the open call to behold. It is the bright mirror image of the wicked who told themselves no one could see them.

_Reading_

Here seeing is a summons. The sense (D101 sense, 7658270) is raah - 'Come and see what God has done' - and the type (D102 type, 7658271) is action, a called-for beholding. The bearer (D105 bearer, 7658273) is 'all who are summoned' - not the psalmist alone but a corporate 'come', the widest bearer in the family. The operation (D106 operation, 7658274) is to come and see / behold; the target (D107 target, 7658275) is what God has done; the locus (D116 locus, 7658279) is external:god. The source (D103 source, 7658278) grounds the summons: God is awesome in his deeds (v5) - the seeing is warranted by the works themselves. The coupling (D112 coupling, 7658277) pairs the call to see with the call to 'come and hear' (v16): sight and hearing are the two doors into witnessing God's acts. Seat and manner are none (D104 seat 7658272 / D108 manner 7658276): the beholding is not localised in the eye and no attitude is specified - it is the bare summons to look. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (D109/D110/D111/D113). The discovery note (D114 discovery, 7658280) calls it the corporate counterpart to 'come and hear'. Role (D115 role, 7658281) is characteristic. The finding: seeing here is outward-turned and communal, an invitation for the inner being to be arrested by God's deeds - the exact opposite of the wicked's denied seeing in Psa 64.

*Why this reading differs:* Opposite to see#1 (Psa 64:5): there seeing is denied, interior and objectless (the wicked presuming none can see); here it is commanded, corporate and outward - an action (not a status) borne by 'all who are summoned' and directed at God's deeds (external:god), warranted by a stated source (God's awesome works). The same verb moves from presumed impunity to a public summons to behold God.

## Psa 78:17-22

> Psa 78:17 Yet they sinned still more against him, rebelling against the Most High in the desert . Psa 78:18 They tested God in their heart by demanding the food they craved . Psa 78:19 They spoke against God , saying , “ Can God spread a table in the wilderness ? Psa 78:20 He struck the rock so that water gushed out and streams overflowed . Can he also give bread or provide meat for his people ?” Psa 78:21 Therefore , when the Lord heard , he was full of wrath ; a fire was kindled against Jacob ; his anger rose against Israel , Psa 78:22 because they did not believe in God and did not trust his saving power .

**tested** — Psa 78:18  ·  `H5254:test#1`

_Story_

Now the testing runs the wrong way. In the wilderness the people put God to the test - not offering themselves to be examined, but daring to try God. And what drove it was appetite: deep in their hearts they demanded the food they craved, and made their craving a test of whether God could provide. The whole thing is powered by hunger turned into a challenge. It is the mirror image of a person humbly inviting God to search him; this is presumption, the inner self demanding proof on the terms of its own appetite.

_Reading_

This begins the run of five 'test' (nasah) readings, all descent. The sense (D101 sense, 7664043) is to test / put to proof; the type (D102 type, 7664044) action; the bearer (D105 bearer, 7664046) 'they', the wilderness generation; the operation (D106 operation, 7664047) to test / try; the target (D107 target, 7664048) God; the locus (D116 locus, 7664051) external:god. This is the inverse of Psa 26:2: there the psalmist invited God to test him; here men presume to test God. The coupling (D112 coupling, 7664050) is decisive - the testing is paired with the heart and the craving: 'they tested God in their heart by demanding the food they craved' (D114 discovery, 7664052). So the interior engine is appetite: the test is born of the belly. The seat is none (D104 seat, 7664045) - though the testing is 'in their heart', the row records no organ-seat, a real silence given the heart-language; the coupling, not the seat, carries the heart. Manner is none (D108 manner, 7664049): no mode is named beyond the demand itself. Source, intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (D103/D109/D110/D111/D113). Role (D115 role, 7664053) is characteristic. The finding: testing God here is presumption driven by craving - the inner being demanding proof on its own appetite's terms.

*Why this reading differs:* First of five 'test' (nasah) readings and the template for the descent. Here the testing is driven by craving - the coupling ties it to the heart and the demanded food. This reading's distinctive is motive: appetite as the engine of the test.
