- **Lens:** the inner-being **operation** is the finding; wealth/toil/God-relation is the arena.

## ECC-02-A · Ecc 2:1–11 · [D, multi] — the self-experimenting, appetite-indulging heart; pleasure tested and found empty

**The reading.** This is the book's great *experiment on the self*, and the experimenter and the subject are the same person. "I said in my **heart**, 'Come now, I will **test** you with **pleasure**; enjoy yourself'" (v.1) — the self deliberately turns its own interior into a laboratory *(stated)*. Everything the appetite could want is supplied: houses, vineyards, gardens, pools, slaves, herds, silver and gold, singers (vv.4–8). The crucial inner-being line: "**whatever my eyes desired** I did not keep from them; I **kept my heart from no pleasure**, for my heart **found pleasure** in all my toil" (v.10) — desire is not denied but *fully granted*, the eyes given everything they reach for. And the verdict, after total indulgence: "behold, **all was vanity** and a striving after wind, and there was nothing to be gained" (v.11). The finding is the **appetite that is satisfied and still comes up empty** — the self discovers experimentally that having-everything does not fill it *(inferred: the experiment is designed to test whether pleasure can be the soul's rest; the answer is no — the heart that withheld nothing still found "nothing to be gained")*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** The substrate is strong here. *heart* (H3820, *leb*) is correctly seated (`seat=heart`) as the bearer of both the test (v.1) and the finding of pleasure (v.10). *desired* (H7592, v.10) and *found pleasure* (H8056, v.10) are correctly `characteristic` — the desiring and delighting operations the reading rests on. *body* (H1320, v.3) carries `seat=flesh` — right, "cheer my **body** with wine" is the fleshly seat. Note the recurring **`seat=spirit` on H7307 ("striving after wind," v.11)**: the *ruach* here is elemental wind in an idiom, not the human spirit — the same lemma-level seat mis-fire flagged in ch.1, to discount. Pairs otherwise sensible (the construct *of wine*, *of toil* correctly specifier-tagged).

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The self as its own experimental subject** — "I will test *you*": the interior split into examiner and examined, a reflexive structure distinct from mere introspection (the self *runs a trial* on itself). (2) **Wisdom retained *inside* the indulgence** — "my heart still **guiding me with wisdom**" (v.3): the experiment is conducted *soberly*, not lost in it; the observing self never fully dissolves into the pleasure it tests — which is precisely why it can report the emptiness.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the appetite fully indulged yet unfilled* (new — the sharpest form of the never-satisfied thread); *the self-experimenting / self-testing heart* (new — Ecclesiastes' distinctive reflexive move); *desire (the eyes that reach)* (shared w/ the Psalter's desire cluster); *the sober observer inside the experience* (new).

## ECC-02-B · Ecc 2:12–17 · [D, multi] — the hatred of life; the leveling of the wise and the fool

**The reading.** The self grants wisdom its real superiority — "there is more gain in **wisdom** than in **folly**, as there is more gain in **light** than in **darkness**; the wise person has his **eyes in his head**, but the fool **walks in darkness**" (vv.13–14). Then the finding that overturns it: "**the same event happens to all** of them" (v.14) — wise and fool arrive at the one death and the one forgetting (v.16). Out of this comes a raw inner-being state, named without hedging: "**so I hated life**, because what is done under the sun was grievous to me" (v.17) *(stated)*. The operation is **the collapse of meaning into the hatred of life** — not depression as mood but a *reasoned revulsion*: if the best thing the self can be (wise) ends identically to the worst (fool), then the ground under the self's striving gives way *(inferred: the hatred is the felt cost of the leveling; the observing self of 2:1–11, having proven pleasure empty, now finds even wisdom's advantage cancelled at the grave)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *hated* (H8130, v.17) is correctly `characteristic` with the reading's object — "hated **life**" (target H2416, sound). *wise/wisdom* and *folly/madness* correctly carried; the *leb* self-address of v.15 ("I said in my heart") again correctly seated. The reading is clean off the substrate; no material mis-tag beyond the *ruach*-wind seat at v.17.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Hatred directed at *life itself*, not at God or circumstance** — the object of the revulsion is the self's own existence-under-the-sun; a distinct and startling inner-being datum (the Psalter's laments cry *out* of trouble; here the self recoils from *living as such*). It is honest despair held open, not yet resolved — the book will not answer it with argument but with the joy-refrain (2:24).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the hatred of life (meaning's collapse at the grave's leveling)* (new — Ecclesiastes' darkest inner state); *the wise/fool distinction felt and then cancelled* (new).

## ECC-02-C · Ecc 2:18–23 · [D, multi] — the restless, despairing heart over toil

**The reading.** The revulsion narrows onto labour: "**I hated all my toil**… seeing that I must **leave it** to the man who will come after me — and who knows whether he will be **wise or a fool**?" (vv.18–19). The self cannot even secure the meaning of its own work, since an unknown heir will inherit it. So "I **gave my heart up to despair** over all the toil" (v.20) *(stated)*. The unit's gem is the somatic close: "all his days are full of **sorrow**, and his work is a **vexation**. Even in the **night his heart does not rest**" (v.23). The finding is **the toiling heart that cannot rest even in sleep** — anxiety that has colonised the one place meant for release; the interior churns through the night *(inferred: the despair of v.20 becomes the sleeplessness of v.23 — the same restless heart the Psalter answers with "he gives to his beloved sleep," here left un-answered under the sun)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *hated* (H8130, v.18, target *all*), *despair* (H2976, v.20, target *heart*), *sorrow* (H4341), *vexation* (H3708) all correctly `characteristic` — the grief-cluster the reading names. *heart* (H3820) seated throughout; "his heart does not rest" (v.23) carries the `prohibition=forbidden` neg-particle stamp — here the negation is real ("does *not* rest"), so the stamp is not spurious, though it spreads to adjacent tokens (a mild over-reach). The reading is well-supported.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The heir-anxiety** — a specific inner-being operation: dread not of losing one's work but of *not controlling who receives it and whether they deserve it* (v.19); the self's meaning held hostage to an unknown other. (2) **Night as the exposure of the unrested heart** — "even in the night": the finding that daytime toil's vexation *does not switch off*, isolating the sleepless interior as the true site of the vanity.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the sleepless, unrested heart* (new — kin to but darker than the Psalter's restless soul, here without the "he gives sleep" resolution); *heir-anxiety (meaning surrendered to an unknown successor)* (new); *despair as a deliberate act of the heart ("gave my heart up to despair")* (new).

## ECC-02-D · Ecc 2:24–26 · [T, thread] — joy/enjoyment as the gift of God (first sounding of the refrain)

**The reading.** Against everything just said, the first sounding of the refrain that will recur seven times and carry the book's one positive answer: "There is nothing **better** for a person than that he should **eat and drink and find enjoyment** in his toil. This also, I saw, is **from the hand of God**" (v.24). The finding is **enjoyment received as gift, not achieved as gain** — the very toil that produced despair (2:18–23) becomes, when taken *as given*, a site of enjoyment *(stated)*. Crucially the enjoyment is not a different activity from the toil but the *same* activity received differently — "find enjoyment **in his toil**." And it is not self-generated: "apart from him who can eat or who can have enjoyment?" (v.25); "to the one who **pleases him** God has given wisdom and knowledge and **joy**" (v.26). The operation is **the reframing of the same life as gift** — the inner shift from grasping-for-gain to receiving-with-joy *(inferred: the refrain does not solve the vanity by argument; it relocates the self's posture from acquisition to reception, which is where joy becomes possible)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *joy* (H8057, v.26), *enjoyment/better/pleases* (H2896/H8056 family) correctly `characteristic`. *given* (H5414, v.26) correctly carries the gift-operation (targets *wisdom*, *knowledge*). The theological weight sits in the phrase "from the hand of God," correctly `standalone`. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Joy as a *capacity granted*, not a mood chosen** — "apart from him who can *have enjoyment*?" (v.25): the ability to enjoy is itself the gift, a subtle and important inner-being finding (later made explicit in 6:2, where wealth is given but the *power to enjoy* withheld). The refrain's joy is thus not stoic resignation but a *received competence* of the soul.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *joy/enjoyment as received gift (the refrain)* (new thread — the book's one positive answer; recurs 3:12–13, 3:22, 5:18–20, 8:15, 9:7–10, 11:7–9); *the capacity-to-enjoy as itself a gift* (new — the hidden precision under the refrain).
