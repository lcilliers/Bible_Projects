# Proverbs 9 — inner-being unit readings (Phase-2 meaning synthesis)

- **Source:** `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 (v1.1 enriched) for Pro 9:1–18, per `segment_unit`. Grounded; *stated* | *inferred*. Lens: the inner-being **operation**. (Closes the ch 1–9 discourses; two rival banquets — Wisdom and Folly.)

---

**PRO-09-A · 9:1–6 · [D] — the summons to leave simplicity and live.** Wisdom's feast: "Whoever is **simple**, let him **turn in** here!… **Leave your simple ways, and live, and walk in the way of insight**" (vv.4,6). The finding: the call is to **abandon a settled inner condition** — "*leave* your simple ways" — as the price of life *(stated)*. The operation: **the self must renounce its own simplicity** — simplicity here is not innocence but a *chosen ease* the self is asked to *walk out of*; turning toward wisdom means turning *from* a former self *(inferred; cross-book with the "leave/turn" that begins the wise life)*. *Validation:* *Leave→target=ways*, way→*of insight*. *Surfaced:* renouncing one's own simplicity (new).

**PRO-09-B · 9:7–9 · [S, multi] — the response to reproof reveals the heart.** The chapter's gem: "**Reprove a scoffer, and he will hate you; reprove a wise man, and he will love you.** Give instruction to a wise man, and he will be **still wiser**" (vv.8–9). The finding: **the same reproof divides two hearts** — one *hates* the reprover, the other *loves* him *(stated)*. The operation: **the reaction to correction is the diagnostic of the inner being** — teachability is not measured by knowledge but by whether reproof is met with *hatred* or *love*; and the teachable heart *compounds* ("still wiser") while the closed heart only hardens *(inferred; the master teachability datum, cross-book with Ps 141:5 "let a righteous man strike me… it is a kindness")*. *Validation:* *increase→target=learning*; the aheb (love, 9:8) now tags characteristic under the v1.1/aheb rule — the *love* of the reproved wise. *Hidden:* **correction as the test that sorts the heart** (love-vs-hate of the reprover). *Surfaced:* teachability as the diagnostic of the heart (★ cornerstone, cross-book w/ Ps 141:5).

**PRO-09-C · 9:10–12 · [S] — the fear of the LORD, the beginning of wisdom.** "**The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom**, and the knowledge of the Holy One is insight" (v.10). The motto restated at the discourses' close (bookending 1:7). The finding: reverent fear is the **root** of the whole wise inner life *(stated)*. *Validation:* *fear→specifier=of Lord*; *added→target=life*. *Surfaced:* fear of the LORD (★ shared cornerstone — re-attested, bookends ch 1).

**PRO-09-D · 9:13–18 · [D] — the counterfeit allure of Folly.** Folly, Wisdom's mirror-image, calls from the same "highest places": "**Stolen water is sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant.** But he does not know that the **dead are there**, that her guests are in the **depths of Sheol**" (vv.17–18). The finding: Folly's appeal is the **sweetness of the illicit** — *stolen*, *secret* — and its victim, like the naive of ch 7, **does not know** the end *(stated)*. The operation: **the allure of the forbidden precisely *because* it is forbidden** ("stolen… secret" are the selling points), working on a self that will not look to the end *(inferred; the counterfeit feast set exactly against Wisdom's, so the whole discourse-section closes on a *choice of two tables* — two loves, two ends)*. *Validation:* *woman→of Folly*, *bread→of secret*, *depths→of Sheol*. *Hidden:* **the forbidden desired *as* forbidden** (illicitness as the attraction). *Surfaced:* the counterfeit allure / the sweetness of the illicit (new); ruin-through-not-knowing (shared w/ 7:23).
