## 1. The narrative  ·  *the observations, read as one movement*
Ruthlessness (*perek*) is the **harsh, merciless crushing of a weaker or subordinate party** (#39) — its object is *always* the weaker (#47). It is **not an act of its own but a manner**: it couples to another operation (here enslavement) and gives it its cruel quality (#48, #50). It does not arrive whole — it is **emergent**, growing and escalating as oppression deepens (#107).

Its **sources** run from the surface to the depths: outwardly it springs from the oppressor's **dread of a perceived threat to power** (#40); beneath that lies the **heart's evil thoughts** (#42), its **deceit** (#109), and a heart **incurably sick** (#110). Its God-appointed **restraint is the fear of God** — not a narrow brake on cruelty but a governance of the whole conduct toward others (#41).

It never stands alone — it **cohabits** with other inner-being evils (#108). And it is **condemned**: the Levitical law forbids ruling *perek* over a brother (#49). The same word Egypt used becomes the word Israel is forbidden to use, and which Ezekiel turns back on Israel's own shepherds (#51).
