## JER-no-wife-refuge · Jeremiah 16:1–21 · [D, multi] — the costly sign-bearing life; God as strength, stronghold, refuge

**The reading.** Jeremiah's *life itself* is made a sign of the coming desolation: he is forbidden to marry and have children (v.2), forbidden to enter "**the house of mourning**" to grieve the dead (v.5), forbidden to enter "**the house of feasting**" to celebrate (v.8) — every ordinary human comfort withheld as a living parable. Behind the people's coming exile is the old failure: "**every one of you follows his stubborn, evil will, refusing to listen to me**" (v.12). Yet the chapter closes in the prophet's confession of trust: "**O LORD, my strength and my stronghold, my refuge in the day of trouble, to you shall the nations come… and say: 'Our fathers have inherited nothing but lies… Can man make for himself gods? Such are not gods!'**" (vv.19–20). The finding is **a *vocation so costly it renounces marriage, mourning, and feasting* — the prophet's whole life bent into a sign — held together by a confession of *God as "strength, stronghold, refuge"*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior asked to *forgo ordinary human goods* (family, shared grief, shared joy) for the sake of the calling — its personal life *emptied* to become a message — and *sustained* not by those forgone comforts but by *God himself* as its "strength, stronghold, and refuge"** *(inferred: the threefold renunciation (vv.2,5,8) is extraordinary — no wife, no attendance at funerals, no attendance at feasts: the finding is that a calling can demand the *surrender of ordinary human comforts* — even the basic solidarities of shared grief and shared joy — so that the prophet's very *loneliness and abstention* preach (his empty life embodies the coming end of "the voice of mirth and the voice of the bridegroom", v.9); the interior here bears a cost that goes beyond words into the shape of a whole life; and the sustaining counterweight is the confession of vv.19–20 — "**O LORD, MY STRENGTH and my STRONGHOLD, my REFUGE in the day of trouble**": the finding is that the self stripped of ordinary supports finds its support *in God directly* — when family, fellowship, and celebration are withheld, God himself becomes the strength, the fortress, the shelter (the same triple refuge as Ps 18:2); the renunciation is bearable only because the One for whom it is made is *himself* the refuge that the renounced things could only have partially given; and the contrast is drawn — the nations will confess their inherited "lies" and self-made "gods" that "are not gods" (v.20), while the prophet's refuge is the living God — so the costly emptying is not loss into a void but exchange of lesser comforts for the ultimate one)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the costly sign-bearing life (marriage, mourning, and feasting renounced — the whole life bent into a message)* (new — a gem: a calling that demands the surrender of ordinary human comforts, the empty life itself preaching, w/ 16:2-9, Ezek 24:16-18, 1 Cor 7:29-31); *the self sustained by God when ordinary supports are withheld ("my strength and my stronghold, my refuge in the day of trouble")* (thread — a gem: God himself the support when family/fellowship/joy are stripped away, w/ 16:19, Ps 18:2, 46:1); *the exchange of lesser comforts for the ultimate refuge (the renounced goods given only partially what God gives fully)* (new — the emptying as exchange, not void, w/ 16:19-20); *self-made gods that are not gods ("can man make for himself gods? such are not gods")* (thread — w/ 16:20, 10:5, Isa 44:9-20).
