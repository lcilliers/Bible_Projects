## JER-covenant-lamb · Jeremiah 11:1–23 · [D, multi] — the innocent lamb unaware; committing the cause to the heart-testing God

**The reading.** After the covenant-lawsuit — Judah "**walked in the stubbornness of his evil heart**" (v.8) — the chapter turns personal: Jeremiah's own townsmen at Anathoth plot to kill him. Here begins the first of his *confessions*: "**But I was like a gentle lamb led to the slaughter. I did not know it was against me they devised schemes**" (v.19). His response is not revenge but *entrustment*: "**O LORD of hosts, who judges righteously, who tests the heart and the mind, let me see your vengeance upon them, for to you have I committed my cause**" (v.20). The finding is **the wronged *innocent* — "a gentle lamb", unaware of the plot against him — *committing his cause* to "the God who tests the heart and the mind" rather than avenging himself** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior betrayed and endangered *handing over* its case to God: rather than plotting back or despairing, Jeremiah *commits* ("rolls onto", galah) his cause to the one "**who tests the heart and the mind**" — trusting that the God who *reads the interior* will judge rightly — so vindication is relinquished to God, not seized by the self** *(inferred: v.19's "**gentle lamb led to the slaughter**" images an interior *innocent and unsuspecting* — Jeremiah "did not know" the schemes, the vulnerability of the guileless among the treacherous; and his response (v.20) is the confessions' recurring inner-being posture — "**to you have I committed my cause**": the finding is that the betrayed interior's mature move is *entrustment* — to *hand over* the matter of one's vindication to God rather than to manage it oneself (revenge, self-defence, bitterness); and the ground of the entrustment is precisely that God "**tests the heart and the mind**" (v.20, boneh libbot ukelayot — examines the innermost self): the finding is that trusting God with one's cause rests on trusting that he *sees the interior truly* — both the plotter's hidden malice and the victim's real innocence — so justice can be safely left to the one who reads hearts; the "let me see your vengeance" is raw and un-sanitised (the confessions do not pretend to serenity), yet it is *committed to God*, not enacted by the self)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the innocent unaware ("like a gentle lamb led to the slaughter… I did not know" — the guileless vulnerable among the treacherous)* (thread — a gem: the unsuspecting integrity of the innocent, w/ 11:19, Isa 53:7, Ps 44:22); *committing the cause to God ("to you have I committed my cause" — the betrayed interior handing over its vindication rather than avenging itself)* (thread — a gem: entrustment instead of self-managed revenge, w/ 11:20, 20:12, Rom 12:19, 1 Pet 2:23); *trusting the God who tests the heart ("who tests the heart and the mind" — justice safely left to the one who reads the interior)* (thread — a gem: entrustment grounded in God's true sight of the heart, w/ 11:20, 17:10, Ps 7:9); *the raw un-sanitised plea ("let me see your vengeance" — committed to God, not enacted by the self)* (new — the confessions' honest, un-serene prayer, w/ 11:20, 20:12).
