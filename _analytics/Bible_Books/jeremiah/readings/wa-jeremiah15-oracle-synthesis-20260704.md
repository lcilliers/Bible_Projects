## JER-woe-is-me-return · Jeremiah 15:1–21 · [D, multi] — eating God's words as delight; the isolation of the calling; the raw complaint; the prophet called to return

**The reading.** The third confession is the rawest yet. Jeremiah laments his very existence: "**Woe is me, my mother, that you bore me, a man of strife and contention to the whole land!**" (v.10). He recalls the joy of God's word: "**Your words were found, and I ate them, and your words became to me a joy and the delight of my heart, for I am called by your name, O LORD**" (v.16). He names the cost — isolation: "**I sat alone, because your hand was upon me, for you had filled me with indignation**" (v.17). Then he accuses God directly: "**Why is my pain unceasing, my wound incurable, refusing to be healed? Will you be to me like a deceitful brook, like waters that fail?**" (v.18). God's answer is stunning — the prophet himself is called to *repent*: "**If you return, I will restore you, and you shall stand before me. If you utter what is precious, and not what is worthless, you shall be as my mouth**" (v.19); and re-commissioned, "**I am with you to save you**" (v.20). The finding is **the word of God *eaten as joy and heart-delight*, the *isolation* it costs, a *raw accusation* (God as a "deceitful brook"), and God's answer calling the *prophet himself to return*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior of the suffering servant of the word laid bare — its *nourishment* ("I ate your words… the delight of my heart"), its *loneliness* ("I sat alone"), and its *breaking-point* where it accuses God of failing it ("like waters that fail") — met not by rebuke but by a *call to the complainer himself to repent* of his worthless words and return, with the promise of renewed presence** *(inferred: three inner-being movements. First, "**I ate them, and your words became… the delight of my heart**" (v.16): the finding is that God's word can be the interior's deepest *nourishment and joy* — internalised ("ate"), it becomes *gladness*, not duty (the same as Ps 119); the word feeds the self. Second, the *cost* — "**I sat ALONE, because your hand was upon me**" (v.17): the finding is that a genuine calling can *isolate* — the hand of God that fills one with his concern also sets one apart from ordinary fellowship ("did not sit with revelers"); the joy of the word and the loneliness of the vocation coexist. Third, and most striking, the *complaint and its answer* — Jeremiah accuses God of being "**a deceitful brook, waters that fail**" (v.18, the bitterest thing a thirsty man can say — the stream you counted on has dried up), and God's reply is not "how dare you" but "**IF YOU RETURN, I will restore you**" (v.19): the finding is that even the *prophet* — the faithful sufferer — needs to *repent*, to sort "the precious from the worthless" in his own words, and that God's restoration is offered to him on the same terms as to everyone (return); the confession is honest enough to show the man of God himself faltering into bitter accusation, and grace enough to show God calling him back rather than casting him off — "I am with you to save you" (v.20))*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *God's word eaten as joy and heart-delight ("I ate them, and your words became the delight of my heart" — the word as the interior's nourishment and gladness, not duty)* (thread — a gem: the word internalised becomes joy, w/ 15:16, Ps 119:103, Ezek 3:3, Job 23:12); *the isolation of the calling ("I sat alone, because your hand was upon me" — the vocation that sets one apart from ordinary fellowship)* (new — a gem: joy of the word and loneliness of the calling coexisting, w/ 15:17, Ps 102:7); *the raw accusation against God ("will you be to me like a deceitful brook, like waters that fail?" — the faithful sufferer's bitter complaint)* (thread — a gem: the honest breaking-point where even the man of God accuses him, w/ 15:18, 20:7, Job 6:15-20); *the prophet himself called to return ("if you return, I will restore you… utter what is precious, not worthless" — grace calling the complainer to repent, not casting him off)* (new — a gem: even the faithful sufferer needs to return, restored on the same terms, w/ 15:19, Rev 2:5).
