## JER-drought-hope · Jeremiah 14:1–22 · [D, multi] — hope set on God amid deserved judgment; "do not leave us"; the prophet's tears

**The reading.** In a devastating drought, the people pray a remarkable prayer — *owning* their guilt yet *appealing* to God's own name: "**Though our iniquities testify against us, act, O LORD, for your name's sake; for our backslidings are many; we have sinned against you. O you hope of Israel, its saviour in time of trouble, why should you be like a stranger in the land?… Yet you, O LORD, are in the midst of us, and we are called by your name; do not leave us**" (vv.7–9). The prophet weeps: "**let my eyes run down with tears night and day… for the virgin daughter of my people is shattered**" (v.17). The chapter closes with hope pinned on God alone: "**Are there any among the false gods of the nations that can bring rain?… Are you not he, O LORD our God? We set our hope on you, for you do all these things**" (v.22). The finding is **a prayer that *acknowledges deserved judgment yet appeals to God's name and presence* ("do not leave us"), setting its *whole hope on God alone* ("we set our hope on you")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior praying from *within* its guilt — not denying the judgment is deserved ("our iniquities testify against us… we have sinned") but appealing *past its own worthiness* to *God's character and presence* ("for your name's sake"; "you are in the midst of us; do not leave us") — and locating its *entire hope* in God as the only one who can give what is needed (rain, salvation)** *(inferred: the prayer's structure is instructive — it *fully concedes* the guilt ("our backslidings are many; we have sinned", v.7) yet does not therefore despair; instead it *pivots to God's own name*: "act **for your name's sake**" (v.7): the finding is that a heart praying from acknowledged guilt can still appeal — not on its own merit (there is none) but on *God's* stake in his people ("we are called by your name", v.9); and the plea "**do not leave us**" (v.9) names the interior's deepest fear (God as "a stranger… a traveler who turns aside for a night", v.8 — present but detached) and asks for the opposite — *committed presence*; and the closing "**we set our hope on you**" (v.22) locates hope by *elimination* — the idols cannot bring rain, only the LORD can, so the interior's hope is fixed on the sole One able to give: the finding is that hope, at its truest, is *not optimism about outcomes* but a *settled reliance on the only One who can act* — pinned to God's character and ability precisely when one's own case is hopeless)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *praying from within acknowledged guilt ("our iniquities testify against us… yet act, O LORD, for your name's sake" — conceding the judgment is deserved yet appealing past one's worthiness to God's name)* (thread — a gem: the guilty heart appealing on God's stake, not its own merit, w/ 14:7, Dan 9:18-19, Ps 25:11, 79:9); *"do not leave us" — the fear of God as a detached stranger answered by asking for committed presence* (new — a gem: the interior's deepest fear (God present but detached) met by the plea for real nearness, w/ 14:8-9, Exod 33:15); *hope set on God alone by elimination ("are there any among the idols that can bring rain?… we set our hope on you" — hope fixed on the only One able to act)* (thread — a gem: hope as settled reliance on the sole One who can give, not optimism about outcomes, w/ 14:22, 17:13, Ps 130:5-7); *the prophet's ceaseless tears ("let my eyes run down with tears night and day")* (thread — w/ 14:17, 9:1, 13:17).
