## JER-buy-the-field · Jeremiah 32:1–44 · [D, multi] — hope enacted against all appearances; nothing too hard for God; one heart, the fear of God put within

**The reading.** Imprisoned in the besieged city (vv.2–3), Jeremiah is told to *buy a field* at Anathoth — and he does, signing and sealing the deed before witnesses (vv.9–12), a deliberate *act of hope* in God's promise that "**houses and fields and vineyards shall again be bought in this land**" (v.15). His prayer wrestles with the paradox: "**Ah, Lord GOD!… Nothing is too hard for you**" (v.17), yet "**the city is given into the hands of the Chaldeans… and you have said to me, 'Buy the field'**?" (vv.24–25). God answers: "**Behold, I am the LORD, the God of all flesh. Is anything too hard for me?**" (v.27). And the promise reaches the interior: "**I will give them one heart and one way, that they may fear me forever… I will put the fear of me in their hearts, that they may not turn from me. I will rejoice in doing them good… with all my heart and all my soul**" (vv.39–41). The finding is **hope *enacted* — buying a field in a doomed city — resting on "**nothing is too hard for God**"; and God's promise to *put the fear of himself in their hearts* so they "may not turn", *rejoicing* to do them good** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's *trust made visible in action*: Jeremiah does not merely *say* he hopes but *invests* — buying land that is, to all appearances, worthless (occupied by the enemy) — because God has spoken; faith that *acts against the visible evidence*; and the ground is "nothing too hard for God"; and the promised inner-work — God putting "**the fear of me in their hearts**" — secures the very *not-turning* that the deceitful heart could never manage on its own, done by a God who "**rejoices**" to do good "with all my heart and all my soul"** *(inferred: the field-purchase (vv.9–15) is *enacted hope* — the finding is that genuine trust in God's promise *takes concrete, costly action* consistent with that promise, even when every visible circumstance contradicts it: Jeremiah buys a field in a city about to fall, "signs and seals the deed", because the God who promised restoration cannot lie — faith is not mere sentiment but *investment against the evidence*; and his honest prayer (vv.24–25) holds the tension *openly* — "nothing is too hard for you… *yet* the city is given to the Chaldeans, and you say buy the field?" — the finding is that faith may voice its bafflement while still obeying; and God's answer, "**is anything too hard for me?**" (v.27), grounds the hope in *omnipotence*; then the deepest note is the interior-promise of vv.39–40 — "**I will give them ONE HEART and one way… I will put THE FEAR OF ME IN THEIR HEARTS, that they may NOT TURN from me**": the finding is that the not-turning the book has shown to be *impossible* for the deceitful, backsliding heart (the leopard's spots, 13:23) is *accomplished by God* — he *puts his own fear within* so the heart *cannot* turn away (the same inner-work as the new covenant, 31:33); and it is done by a God who "**rejoices** to do them good… with all my heart and all my soul" (v.41): the finding is that God's good-doing is not grudging but *whole-hearted delight* — he pursues his people's good with his whole self)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *enacted hope against the evidence (buying a field in the doomed city — trust that takes concrete costly action consistent with God's promise despite contrary appearances)* (thread — a gem: faith as investment against the visible evidence, not mere sentiment, w/ 32:9-15, Heb 11:1,8-10, Rom 4:18-21); *honest faith holding the tension ("nothing is too hard for you… yet the city is given… and you say buy the field?" — voicing bafflement while obeying)* (new — a gem: faith that questions openly yet acts, w/ 32:17,24-25, Mark 9:24); *"nothing too hard for God" (hope grounded in omnipotence)* (thread — w/ 32:17,27, Gen 18:14, Luke 1:37); *God putting his own fear within so the heart cannot turn ("I will put the fear of me in their hearts, that they may not turn from me" — the not-turning accomplished by God, not the self)* (thread — a gem: the impossible perseverance done by God's inner-work, w/ 32:39-40, 31:33, 24:7, Phil 1:6); *God rejoicing whole-heartedly to do good ("I will rejoice in doing them good… with all my heart and all my soul")* (new — a gem: God's good-doing as whole-hearted delight, not grudging, w/ 32:41, Zeph 3:17, Mic 7:18).
