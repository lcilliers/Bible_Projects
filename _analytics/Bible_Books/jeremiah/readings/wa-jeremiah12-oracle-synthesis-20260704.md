## JER-why-wicked-prosper · Jeremiah 12:1–17 · [D, multi] — the complaint of the prospering wicked; the toughening answer; God's own grief

**The reading.** The second confession opens with the ancient wound: "**Righteous are you, O LORD, when I complain to you; yet I would plead my case before you. Why does the way of the wicked prosper? Why do all who are treacherous thrive?**" (v.1). Jeremiah notes the hypocrites' interior: "**you are near in their mouth and far from their heart**" (v.2). God's answer is not consolation but a *summons to be toughened*: "**If you have raced with men on foot, and they have wearied you, how will you compete with horses? And if in a safe land you are so trusting, what will you do in the thicket of the Jordan?**" (v.5). And God discloses his *own* grief: "**I have forsaken my house; I have abandoned my heritage; I have given the beloved of my soul into the hands of her enemies**" (v.7). The finding is **the honest complaint "**why does the way of the wicked prosper?**" — met not by an explanation but by a *call to grow stronger* ("how will you compete with horses?") — and God's own *grieved* heart over the judgment he must bring** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior *permitted to argue* with God ("I would plead my case", opening with "righteous are you" — the complaint framed *inside* faith), and *answered* not with the resolution it wanted but with a *challenge to endure harder things* — the racing-with-horses answer that treats the present distress as *training* for worse to come; and the disclosure that God himself is *grieved*, giving up "the beloved of my soul", so the sufferer is not alone in his anguish** *(inferred: v.1's complaint is a model of *faithful protest* — Jeremiah does not conclude against God ("**RIGHTEOUS are you, O LORD**") yet insists on pleading his case: the finding is that the interior may bring its hardest question ("why do the wicked prosper?") *to* God, honestly, without it being unbelief — the complaint is lodged *within* the relationship, not against it; and God's answer (v.5) is startling and instructive — instead of *explaining* the prosperity of the wicked, he *raises the stakes*: "if footmen have wearied you, how will you race horses?" — the finding is that God sometimes answers the interior's "why?" not with an explanation but with a *summons to greater strength*, treating the present trial as *preparation* for harder ones (the answer reframes the complaint as a training-question: are you being toughened or worn down?); and v.7 opens an unexpected window — "**the beloved of my soul**" given to her enemies: the finding is that the God who must judge is himself *grieved* by it, so the prophet's anguish over his people is *shared* by God — the sufferer complains to a God who is *also* suffering the loss)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *faithful protest ("righteous are you… yet I would plead my case: why does the way of the wicked prosper?" — the hardest question lodged inside faith, not against it)* (thread — a gem: honest complaint that does not conclude against God, w/ 12:1, Hab 1:2-4, Ps 73:1-3, Job 21); *near in the mouth, far from the heart (the hypocrite's interior — God on the lips, absent from the heart)* (thread — w/ 12:2, Isa 29:13, Matt 15:8); *the toughening answer ("how will you compete with horses?" — the "why?" met not with explanation but a summons to greater strength, the trial as training)* (new — a gem: God reframing the complaint as a training-question, w/ 12:5, Heb 12:1-11); *God's own grief ("the beloved of my soul given to her enemies" — the judging God himself grieved; the sufferer's anguish shared by God)* (new — a gem: the interior complains to a God who also suffers the loss, w/ 12:7, Hos 11:8, Luke 19:41).
