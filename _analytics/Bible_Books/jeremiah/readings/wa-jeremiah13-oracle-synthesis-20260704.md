## JER-loincloth-cling · Jeremiah 13:1–27 · [D, multi] — made to cling to God; pride spoiling; the leopard's spots

**The reading.** Jeremiah wears a linen loincloth (worn against the skin), hides it by the Euphrates, and finds it later "**spoiled… good for nothing**" (v.7). The meaning: "**As the loincloth clings to the waist of a man, so I made the whole house of Israel and the whole house of Judah cling to me, declares the LORD, that they might be for me a people, a name, a praise, and a glory, but they would not listen**" (v.11); "**even so will I spoil the pride of Judah**" (v.9). The prophet grieves: "**if you will not listen, my soul will weep in secret for your pride**" (v.17). And the diagnosis of habituated evil: "**Can the Ethiopian change his skin or the leopard his spots? Then also you can do good who are accustomed to do evil**" (v.23). The finding is **the intimacy God *intended* — a people "made to *cling* to him" like a garment worn close, for his "praise and glory" — *refused* through *pride*; and the sobering law that a self *accustomed to evil* can no more do good than a leopard change its spots** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure of God's *design for the interior* — to be worn *close against him*, clinging like an intimate garment, existing "for his glory" — and the tragedy of that intimacy *spoiled by pride*; and the exposure of *habituation* — evil practised long enough becomes *second nature*, so ingrained the self *cannot reform itself* ("accustomed to do evil")** *(inferred: the loincloth-image (v.11) is a startling picture of *intended intimacy* — "**as the loincloth CLINGS to the waist… so I made [them] CLING TO ME**": the finding is that God designed the interior to be worn *close against himself*, in the most intimate proximity, "**for a people, a name, a praise, and a glory**" — the self's very purpose is nearness to God and being his adornment; and the ruin of this is *pride* ("I will spoil the **pride**", v.9) — the swollen self will not be worn close, and so, like the buried loincloth, rots "good for nothing" (the intimacy refused leaves the self useless to its purpose); and v.23 names the deeper bind — "**can the leopard change his spots? then also you can do good who are ACCUSTOMED to do evil**": the finding is that evil *habituated* becomes *constitutional* — practised so long it is now the self's "spots", as unchangeable by mere effort as skin-colour; the finding is not that change is impossible absolutely (God can give a new heart) but that *self*-reform is impossible for a will grooved by long habit — the accustomed evildoer cannot lift himself out; the prophet's response is *secret weeping* over their pride (v.17) — grief, not contempt, over an interior that has made itself unable to return)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *made to cling to God (the interior designed to be worn close against him, for his praise and glory)* (new — a gem: the self's purpose is intimate nearness to God, clinging like a garment, w/ 13:11, Deut 10:20, Ps 63:8); *intimacy spoiled by pride ("I will spoil the pride… but they would not listen" — the swollen self refusing to be worn close, rotting useless)* (thread — a gem: pride ruining the intended intimacy, w/ 13:9,15,17); *habituated evil as second nature ("can the leopard change his spots? then also you can do good who are accustomed to do evil" — evil practised long enough becomes constitutional, beyond self-reform)* (new — a gem: the accustomed will cannot lift itself out, w/ 13:23, Rom 7:18, Ezek 36:26); *secret weeping over pride ("my soul will weep in secret for your pride" — grief, not contempt, over the self made unable to return)* (thread — w/ 13:17, 9:1, Luke 19:41).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** made to cling to God; intimacy spoiled by pride; habituated evil as second nature; secret weeping over pride. Chapter 13 discloses the interior's designed *intimacy* — worn close against God like a garment — ruined by pride, and the bind of *habituated evil* that a self cannot reform on its own.
