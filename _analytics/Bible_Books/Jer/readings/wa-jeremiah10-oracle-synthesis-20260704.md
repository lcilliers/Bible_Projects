## JER-idols-vs-living-God · Jeremiah 10:1–25 · [D, multi] — dead carried idols vs the living God; God as portion; the way of man not in himself

**The reading.** Jeremiah mocks the idols and exalts the living God: idols "**are like scarecrows in a cucumber field, and they cannot speak; they have to be carried, for they cannot walk. Do not be afraid of them, for they cannot do evil, neither is it in them to do good**" (v.5). By contrast: "**There is none like you, O LORD… the LORD is the true God; he is the living God and the everlasting King**" (vv.6,10); idols are "**worthless, a work of delusion**" (v.15), while "**the LORD is the portion of Jacob… the LORD of hosts is his name**" (v.16). Then the prophet's submission and a profound confession of human limitation: "**Truly this is an affliction, and I must bear it**" (v.19); "**I know, O LORD, that the way of man is not in himself, that it is not in man who walks to direct his steps. Correct me, O LORD, but in justice; not in your anger**" (vv.23–24). The finding is **dead idols (that "*must be carried*" and can do neither harm nor good) set against the *living God* who is the soul's "*portion*" — and the humbling confession that "**the way of man is not in himself**", closing in a plea for *measured correction*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the contrast of two objects of trust by their *capacity*: the idol is *inert* — carried, speechless, powerless for evil or good — a dead thing the self must prop up; the LORD is *living* — the active, true God who is the interior's *"portion"* (its allotted inheritance and treasure); and flowing from this, the interior's *honest self-limitation* — "the way of man is not in himself… not in man to direct his steps" — the confession that the self *cannot navigate its own life*, met not with despair but with a prayer to be *corrected in justice, not anger*** *(inferred: the idol-satire (v.5) makes an inner-being point by contrast — the idol "**must be carried**… **cannot do evil, neither… good**": the finding is that a false god is a *burden the worshipper sustains* (the god you carry, as Isa 46), utterly *inert*, while the LORD is "the **living** God" (v.10) who is himself the worshipper's "**portion**" (v.16, cheleq — one's allotted share, treasure, inheritance): the finding is that the interior's ultimate good is not a possession it holds but a living Person who is its *portion* — the difference between a dead thing you carry and a living God who carries and satisfies you; and then vv.23–24 turn inward with rare humility — "**the way of man is NOT IN HIMSELF, that it is not in man who walks to DIRECT HIS STEPS**": the finding is a fundamental confession of *creaturely limitation* — the self does not have within itself the resource to *direct its own life*, to know or secure its own way (the negation of autonomy); and this confession does not collapse into despair but into *dependence and submission* — "**correct me, O LORD, but in justice, not in your anger**" (v.24): the finding is that the self that admits it cannot direct itself *asks to be directed and corrected* by God, and even asks for the correction to be *measured* ("in justice… not… bring me to nothing") — a trust that welcomes discipline while pleading it be proportionate; the idol-worshipper carries a dead god and cannot direct his steps; the true worshipper is carried by the living God and submits his steps to be corrected)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the dead idol carried vs the living God who is one's portion (idols "must be carried… cannot do good or evil" vs "the living God… the portion of Jacob")* (thread — a gem: the god you prop up vs the living Person who is your treasure, w/ 10:5,10,16, Isa 46:1-4, Ps 73:26, 16:5); *"the way of man is not in himself" (the creaturely confession that the self cannot direct its own steps — the negation of autonomy)* (new — a gem: the interior's honest self-limitation, w/ 10:23, Prov 16:9, 20:24, Ps 37:23); *dependence welcoming correction ("correct me, O LORD, but in justice, not in your anger" — the self that cannot self-direct asks to be directed, pleading measured discipline)* (new — a gem: submission that welcomes proportionate correction, not despair, w/ 10:24, Ps 6:1, 38:1); *bearing affliction submissively ("this is an affliction, and I must bear it")* (thread — w/ 10:19, Lam 3:27-28).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the dead idol carried vs the living God who is one's portion; "the way of man is not in himself"; dependence welcoming correction; bearing affliction submissively. Chapter 10 sets the inert idol (a burden the self props up) against the living God who is the soul's *portion*, and confesses that the self cannot direct its own steps — asking instead to be corrected in measure.
- **Book-level note (Jeremiah batch 2, chs 7–10):** the movement exposes *false securities of the interior* and names the true good. Its findings: *religion as talisman* — the temple trusted as a charm while the heart stays stubborn (7); the *disabled return-reflex* and the missing self-question "what have I done?", with the balm that stands available yet un-applied (8); the *one legitimate boast* — knowing God rather than boasting in wisdom, might, or riches (9); and the *dead idol vs the living God who is one's portion*, closing in the confession that "the way of man is not in himself" (10). Master-findings: **worship divorced from the heart is a charm, not a relationship**; **repentance hinges on the self-question "what have I done?"**; **the interior's one true boast is knowing God**; and **the self cannot direct its own steps — it is made to be carried and corrected by the living God.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the deceitful heart (→ 17:9); knowing God (← 9:24); the fountain/portion (← 2:13; Ps 16:5); the weeping prophet (→ 13:17, 14:17); measured correction (→ 30:11).
