## JER-scroll-burned · Jeremiah 36:1–32 · [D, multi] — the word burned yet indestructible; the fearless rejection vs the trembling hearers

**The reading.** Jeremiah dictates a scroll of God's words, hoping "**it may be that the house of Judah will hear… so that every one may turn from his evil way, and that I may forgive**" (v.3). When the officials hear it read, "**they turned one to another in fear**" (v.16). But the king Jehoiakim, in his winter house by the fire, receives it differently: "**As Jehudi read three or four columns, the king would cut them off with a knife and throw them into the fire… until the entire scroll was consumed**" (v.23); "**Yet neither the king nor any of his servants who heard all these words was afraid, nor did they tear their garments**" (v.24). God's response: "**Take another scroll and write on it all the former words**" (v.28) — and "**many similar words were added**" (v.32). The finding is **the word of God *burned* by an *un-frightened, unmoved* king (against the *trembling* officials) — yet *indestructible*, rewritten and *enlarged*** *(stated)*. The operation is **two interiors before the same word: the officials who "**turned to one another in fear**" (the word *landing*, producing the appropriate tremor), and the king who is "**not afraid**" and *destroys* the word he cannot bear to hear — the fearless heart that thinks it can *end* God's word by burning the page; and the futility of it: the word *cannot be destroyed* — it is simply *rewritten and augmented*, so the attack on it only *adds* to it** *(inferred: the chapter contrasts two responses to the read word. The officials "**turned one to another IN FEAR**" (v.16) — the finding is that a rightly-functioning conscience *trembles* at God's word (the fear that could lead to repentance); against them, Jehoiakim's "**not afraid, nor did they tear their garments**" (v.24) is the terminal hardness — the same shamelessness as "did not know how to blush" (6:15): the finding is that the gravest state before God's word is *not to be moved by it* — to hear pronounced judgment and feel *no tremor*, so complete is the heart's insulation; and Jehoiakim goes further, *physically destroying* the word column by column — the finding is the futile arrogance of imagining one can *abolish God's word* by silencing its medium (cutting and burning the page); and God's reply exposes the futility — "**take ANOTHER scroll**… and many similar words were **added**" (vv.28,32): the finding is that God's word is *indestructible* — the attempt to destroy it not only fails but *enlarges* it (the burned scroll replaced by a fuller one); the fearless heart can burn the page but cannot touch the word, and its rebellion only multiplies the testimony against it)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the trembling conscience vs the unmoved heart (the officials "turned to one another in fear" vs the king "not afraid, nor did they tear their garments")* (thread — a gem: the rightly-functioning conscience trembles at God's word; the gravest state is to hear judgment and feel nothing, w/ 36:16,24, 6:15, 2 Kgs 22:11-13, Isa 66:2); *the futile arrogance of destroying the word ("the king would cut them off… and throw them into the fire" — imagining one can abolish God's word by silencing its medium)* (new — a gem: burning the page cannot touch the word, w/ 36:23, Ps 2:1-4); *the word indestructible and enlarged ("take another scroll… and many similar words were added" — the attack on the word only multiplying it)* (new — a gem: God's word cannot be destroyed; rebellion against it augments the testimony, w/ 36:28,32, Isa 40:8, Matt 24:35).
