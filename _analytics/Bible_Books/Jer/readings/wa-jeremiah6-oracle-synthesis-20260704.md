## JER-ancient-paths · Jeremiah 6:1–30 · [D, multi] — the ancient paths and rest for the soul, refused; the shameless who cannot blush

**The reading.** Amid the gathering siege, God issues a tender invitation that the people reject: "**Stand by the roads, and look, and ask for the ancient paths, where the good way is; and walk in it, and find rest for your souls. But they said, 'We will not walk in it'**" (v.16). Their prophets and priests "**have healed the wound of my people lightly, saying, 'Peace, peace,' when there is no peace**" (v.14); and their consciences are dead: "**Were they ashamed when they committed abomination? No, they were not at all ashamed; they did not know how to blush**" (v.15). Their ears "**cannot listen**… the word of the LORD is to them an object of scorn; they take no pleasure in it" (v.10); and the refining fails: "**in vain the refining goes on, for the wicked are not removed**" (v.29). The finding is **the offer of *rest for the soul* on the *ancient good way*, flatly *refused* ("we will not walk in it") — and a conscience so dead it "**did not know how to blush**"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior offered its truest good — "**rest for your souls**" on the tested "ancient paths" — and *choosing against it* ("we will not walk in it"); and a *shamelessness* so complete that the capacity to *blush* (the body's involuntary confession of wrong) is gone, joined to a word-deafness that finds God's word "an object of scorn" and takes "no pleasure in it"** *(inferred: v.16 is one of Scripture's tenderest inner-being invitations — "**ask for the ANCIENT PATHS… the GOOD WAY… and find REST for your souls**": the finding is that the deepest rest of the interior is found on a *proven, well-worn way* (the "ancient paths" — the tested road of covenant faithfulness), and that this rest is *offered* not imposed — but the interior can *refuse its own rest*: "**but they said, We will not walk in it**"; the finding is that the self can knowingly turn from the very thing that would give it rest, preferring its own way to soul-rest (the tragedy of a freedom used against itself); and v.15 names the terminal corruption — "**they did not know how to BLUSH**": the finding is that shame is a *God-given interior alarm* (the flush that involuntarily confesses wrong), and its *loss* is a deep degradation — a conscience so seared it can do abomination and feel no heat in the face; the "blush" is the body's honesty, and its absence marks an interior that has lost even involuntary self-awareness of wrong (the shamelessness of Zeph 3:5); and the failed refining (v.29, "in vain the refining goes on") completes it — the ordinary furnace of discipline cannot purify a metal this corrupt, "the wicked are not removed": the finding is that a sufficiently hardened, shameless, rest-refusing interior becomes *unrefinable* by ordinary means)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *rest for the soul on the ancient good way, refused ("ask for the ancient paths… find rest for your souls. But they said, We will not walk in it")* (thread — a gem: the interior refusing its own truest rest, freedom used against itself, w/ 6:16, Matt 11:28-29, Isa 30:15); *the conscience that cannot blush ("they did not know how to blush" — shame as a God-given interior alarm, its loss a deep degradation)* (new — a gem: the flush of shame as the body's honesty, its absence marking a seared conscience, w/ 6:15, 8:12, Zeph 3:5, 1 Tim 4:2); *word-deafness and scorn ("the word of the LORD is an object of scorn; they take no pleasure in it")* (thread — the interior that finds no delight in God's word, w/ 6:10, Ps 1:2 inverted); *the unrefinable interior ("in vain the refining goes on, for the wicked are not removed")* (new — a heart too corrupt for the ordinary furnace, w/ 6:29).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** rest for the soul on the ancient good way, refused; the conscience that cannot blush; word-deafness and scorn; the unrefinable interior. Chapter 6 closes batch 1 with the tender offer of *soul-rest* on the ancient paths, flatly refused — by a conscience so dead it can no longer blush.
- **Book-level note (Jeremiah batch 1, chs 1–6):** the opening movement joins the *prophet's interior* (known and consecrated before birth, his fear met by presence, his own heart breaking, 1; 4:19) with the *great indictment of the people's interior* — the *two evils* of forsaking the fountain for broken cisterns (2:13), the *self-deceiving* "I am innocent" (2:35), the *open door of return* on the sole condition of *acknowledging guilt* (3:12-13), the demand to *circumcise the heart* and evict its lodging thoughts (4:4,14), the *correction-proof hardness* and heart that will not fear (5:3,24), and the *refused rest* on the ancient paths with the conscience that *cannot blush* (6:15-16). Master-findings: **the fountain vs the broken cisterns**; **repentance is honest self-acknowledgement, not self-repair**; **the decisive renewal is of the heart (circumcised, washed, ploughed)**; and **the gravest state is the self-hardened, shameless, rest-refusing interior.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the fountain/cisterns (→ 17:13); the deceitful heart (→ 17:9); circumcised/new heart (→ 31:33, the new covenant); the weeping prophet (→ 8:18, 9:1); return / seek with the whole heart (→ 29:13).
