## JER-broken-liberty · Jeremiah 34:1–22 · [D, multi] — the reneged repentance; the good act undone; liberty withheld recoiling

**The reading.** Under siege, Zedekiah and the people make a covenant to free their Hebrew slaves — and do it: "**You recently repented and did what was right in my eyes by proclaiming liberty**… **and you made a covenant before me in the house that is called by my name**" (v.15). But then, "**you turned around and profaned my name when each of you took back his male and female slaves**" (v.16). God's verdict plays on the word: "**You have not obeyed me by proclaiming liberty… behold, I proclaim to you liberty — to the sword, to pestilence, and to famine**" (v.17). The finding is **a *genuine repentance done and then undone* — a right act (freeing the slaves) *reversed* — and the recoil that *liberty withheld from others* becomes *liberty to death* for the withholders** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that *begins* to obey and then *retracts* — a real turning ("you recently repented and did what was right") followed by a *turning-back* ("you turned around and took back") — showing that a good act *begun is not yet a good act completed*, and that a reneged obedience *profanes God's name* more than never obeying; and the ironic recoil ties the punishment to the sin (you denied liberty, so you receive "liberty" to sword and famine)** *(inferred: the tragedy of vv.15–16 is *the reneged good* — the finding is that the interior can *genuinely begin* to do right ("did what was right in my eyes") and then *undo* it, and the undoing is worse than the never-doing because it "**profanes my name**" (the covenant was made "before me, in the house called by my name", so its reversal drags God's name into the betrayal); the finding is that *half-repentance retracted* is a distinct and grave sin — the good impulse followed by the self-interested reversal (once the immediate pressure of the siege eased, they took the slaves back); and the punishment fits with grim irony — "you would not proclaim **liberty**, so I proclaim you **liberty** — to the sword" (v.17): the finding is that the mercy one *withholds* from others is *withheld from oneself* — the interior that would not free its neighbours is not itself set free (the same measure-for-measure as Matt 18, the unforgiving servant); the reneged liberty recoils exactly)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the reneged good / repentance undone ("you recently repented and did what was right… but then you turned around" — a right act begun and reversed, worse than never done)* (thread — a gem: half-repentance retracted profaning God's name, w/ 34:15-16, Luke 9:62, 2 Pet 2:20-21); *mercy withheld recoiling on the withholder ("you would not proclaim liberty… I proclaim you liberty to the sword" — the freedom denied to others denied to oneself)* (new — a gem: measure-for-measure, the un-freeing self left un-free, w/ 34:17, Matt 18:32-35, Jas 2:13); *the good act completed, not merely begun (obedience begun is not yet obedience)* (new — w/ 34:16).
