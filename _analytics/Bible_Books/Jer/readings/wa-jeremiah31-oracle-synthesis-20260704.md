# Jeremiah 31 — inner-being oracle readings (Phase-2 meaning synthesis)

- **Source:** Phase-1 lexicals per oracle unit (provenance `jeremiah-oracle-segmentation-batch6-v1-20260704`). Cited; grounded; *stated* | *inferred*. The chapter carries the book's two greatest inner-being gems — everlasting love (31:3) and the new covenant (31:31–34).

---

## JER-everlasting-love · Jeremiah 31:1–26 · [D, multi] — everlasting love; the father-heart that yearns; mourning turned to joy; the weary soul satisfied

**The reading.** God discloses the ground of all restoration: "**I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have continued my faithfulness to you**" (v.3). He is a *father*: "**I am a father to Israel, and Ephraim is my firstborn**" (v.9). He promises the reversal of grief: "**I will turn their mourning into joy; I will comfort them, and give them gladness for sorrow**" (v.13). To Rachel "**weeping for her children… refuses to be comforted**" (v.15), he says: "**Keep your voice from weeping… there is hope for your future… your children shall come back**" (vv.16–17). He overhears Ephraim's repentance — "**bring me back that I may be restored… I was ashamed**" (vv.18–19) — and answers with a father's ache: "**Is Ephraim my dear son?… my heart yearns for him; I will surely have mercy on him**" (v.20). And he promises, "**I will satisfy the weary soul, and every languishing soul I will replenish**" (v.25). The finding is **an *everlasting love* as the root of faithfulness, a *father-heart that yearns* over the repentant, *mourning turned to joy*, and the *weary soul satisfied*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure of the *motive* beneath God's whole dealing with the interior — not obligation or reluctance but *everlasting love* ("I have loved you with an everlasting love") — a love that *yearns* like a father's over a returning child (God's "heart yearns" — the visceral inward stirring), that *turns mourning into joy* rather than merely ending sorrow, and that *satisfies* the weary and languishing soul at its point of exhaustion** *(inferred: v.3 is the wellspring of the whole book — "**I have loved you with an EVERLASTING love; THEREFORE I have continued my faithfulness**": the finding is that God's faithfulness is *downstream of his love* — he keeps on with his people *because* he loves them with a love that has no beginning or end ('olam — everlasting/from-of-old); the interior's security rests not on its own lovability (it has none, per the whole indictment) but on a love that *precedes and outlasts* everything; and v.20 lets us see God's *interior* — "**my HEART YEARNS for him**" (hamu me'ay — my inward parts churn/moan for him): the finding is that God is not a detached judge but a *yearning father* whose very insides ache over the repentant child, "as often as I speak against him, I do remember him still" — even his rebuke does not extinguish his tenderness; and the movement of vv.18–20 models *received* repentance — Ephraim's honest "you disciplined me… bring me back" (v.18, owning the discipline and asking to be restored) meets God's overflowing mercy; and the promises are *affective* — "**turn mourning into JOY**… gladness for sorrow" (v.13), "**satisfy the WEARY soul**" (v.25): the finding is that God's restoration reaches the *feelings* — it does not merely fix circumstances but *converts sorrow to joy* and *replenishes* the exhausted interior at the exact point of its languishing)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *everlasting love as the root of faithfulness ("I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have continued my faithfulness" — God's persistence downstream of a love that precedes and outlasts all)* (thread — a gem: the interior's security in a love with no beginning or end, not in its own lovability, w/ 31:3, Rom 8:38-39, Isa 54:8, Eph 2:4-5); *the father-heart that yearns ("my heart yearns for him… I will surely have mercy" — God's inward parts aching over the repentant child)* (new — a gem: God not a detached judge but a yearning father whose insides churn with tenderness, even in rebuke, w/ 31:20, Hos 11:8, Luke 15:20); *received repentance ("you disciplined me… bring me back that I may be restored… I was ashamed" — honest owning of discipline meeting overflowing mercy)* (thread — w/ 31:18-19, Ps 51, Luke 15:18-21); *mourning turned to joy and the weary soul satisfied ("I will turn their mourning into joy… satisfy the weary soul" — restoration reaching the feelings, converting sorrow, replenishing exhaustion)* (thread — a gem: God's healing reaches the affections, not just circumstances, w/ 31:13,25, Ps 30:11, Isa 61:3, Matt 11:28).

## JER-new-covenant · Jeremiah 31:27–40 · [D, multi] — the law written on the heart; all shall know God; sins remembered no more

**The reading.** After affirming individual responsibility ("**everyone shall die for his own iniquity**", v.30), God announces the covenant that answers the whole book's diagnosis of the incurable heart: "**Behold, the days are coming… when I will make a new covenant… not like the covenant that I made with their fathers… my covenant that they broke. For this is the covenant… I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts. And I will be their God, and they shall be my people. And no longer shall each one teach his neighbour… saying, 'Know the LORD,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest… For I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more**" (vv.31–34). The finding is **a *new covenant* in which the law is *written on the heart* (internalised, not merely external), the *knowledge of God* is *universal and direct* ("all shall know me"), and *sin is forgiven and no longer remembered*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the divine remedy for the interior the book has diagnosed as *deceitful and un-turnable* (17:9): not another external law to be broken but a *law written inside the heart* — so that obedience flows from a *transformed interior* rather than being imposed from without; a *direct, personal knowing* of God available to all, not mediated by teachers; and a *forgiveness so complete* that God "remembers the sin no more" — the guilt not merely covered but erased from the divine memory** *(inferred: the new covenant (vv.31–34) is the answer to everything the book has exposed. First, "**I will WRITE it on their HEARTS**": the finding is that the fundamental change is *internalisation* — the old covenant was "written on tablets" outside and the heart broke it (v.32), but the new writes the law *inside*, on the heart itself, so that God's will becomes the interior's own disposition rather than an external demand (the remedy for the "deceitful heart" and the "stubborn evil heart" — the heart itself is rewritten); obedience becomes *organic*, flowing from a transformed interior, not effortful conformity to an outside rule. Second, "**they shall ALL KNOW me, from the least to the greatest**": the finding is that the *knowledge of God* — the one legitimate boast (9:24) — becomes *direct and universal*, no longer the preserve of priests and teachers but the possession of every member, "from the least to the greatest"; the interior has *immediate access* to God. Third, "**I will FORGIVE their iniquity, and REMEMBER their sin NO MORE**": the finding is that the ground of the whole thing is a *forgiveness so total* it reaches even God's memory — the guilt is not merely pardoned but "remembered no more", erased, so the interior stands before God with no residue of its record; and the order matters — the forgiveness ("for I will forgive", v.34) is the *foundation* on which the written-heart and the knowing rest: grace first, then the transformed and knowing interior)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the law written on the heart (internalisation — God's will becoming the interior's own disposition, not an external demand)* (thread — a gem, the answer to the deceitful un-turnable heart: obedience organic, flowing from a rewritten heart, w/ 31:33, 17:9, 24:7, Ezek 36:26-27, 2 Cor 3:3); *the universal, direct knowledge of God ("they shall all know me, from the least to the greatest" — immediate access, no longer mediated by teachers)* (new — a gem: knowing God the possession of every member, not a preserve, w/ 31:34, 9:24, 1 John 2:27, Heb 8:11); *forgiveness that reaches God's memory ("I will forgive their iniquity, and remember their sin no more" — guilt not covered but erased from the divine memory)* (thread — a gem: total forgiveness, the interior standing with no residue of its record, w/ 31:34, Isa 43:25, Ps 103:12, Heb 8:12); *grace as the foundation (the forgiveness the ground on which the written-heart and the knowing rest)* (new — w/ 31:34, "for I will forgive").

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** everlasting love as the root of faithfulness; the father-heart that yearns; received repentance; mourning turned to joy; the law written on the heart; the universal knowledge of God; forgiveness that reaches God's memory; grace as the foundation. Chapter 31 is the book's summit — the *everlasting love* and *yearning father-heart* beneath all restoration, and the *new covenant* that rewrites the deceitful heart, makes God known to all, and forgets sin.
- **Threads:** everlasting love (← 30:17; w/ Rom 8:38-39); the yearning father (w/ Hos 11:8, Luke 15:20); the written heart / new covenant (← 17:9, 24:7, 4:4; → 32:39-40; w/ Ezek 36:26, Heb 8); knowing God (← 9:24); sin remembered no more (→ 33:8; w/ Isa 43:25).
