## JER-heart-deceitful-trust · Jeremiah 17:1–27 · [D, multi] — the two trusts; the deceitful heart searched by God; heal me

**The reading.** The chapter opens with sin *engraved on the interior*: "**The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron; with a point of diamond it is engraved on the tablet of their heart**" (v.1). Then the two ways, defined by *what the heart trusts*: "**Cursed is the man who trusts in man and makes flesh his strength, whose heart turns away from the LORD. He is like a shrub in the desert… in an uninhabited salt land**" (vv.5–6); "**Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD… He is like a tree planted by water, that sends out its roots by the stream, and does not fear when heat comes… is not anxious in the year of drought**" (vv.7–8). Then the great diagnosis: "**The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick; who can understand it? 'I the LORD search the heart and test the mind'**" (vv.9–10). And the prophet's plea: "**Heal me, O LORD, and I shall be healed**" (v.14). The finding is **two interiors distinguished by their *trust* — the man trusting *flesh* (a desert shrub) and the man trusting *the LORD* (a water-rooted tree) — and the *deceitful, unsearchable heart* that only *God* can read and heal** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's stability shown to depend entirely on the *object of its trust*: trust in "flesh" (man, self, the created) leaves it a *stunted shrub in salt land* — exposed, fruitless, unwatered; trust in "the LORD" makes it a *tree by the stream* — rooted, *unafraid in heat*, *un-anxious in drought*, fruitful regardless of conditions; and beneath the choice lies the *deceitful heart* — so twisted it deceives even *itself* ("who can understand it?"), knowable only to the God who "searches the heart", and healable only by him** *(inferred: vv.5–8 draw the definitive contrast of the interior by its *trust-object* — the finding is that the self's *stability, fruitfulness, and freedom from fear* are functions of *what it trusts*: the one who "makes flesh his strength" (v.5, trusting the human, the created, the self) is a "shrub in the desert" — *stunted and exposed*, in "a salt land" where nothing grows; the one who "trusts in the LORD" is "a tree planted by water" whose *roots reach the stream*, so that "**when heat comes**" and "**in the year of drought**" it does *not fear* and is *not anxious* but *still bears fruit* (vv.7–8): the finding is that trust in God produces an interior *un-shaken by adverse circumstances* — the tree's roots reach past the surface weather to a constant source, so heat and drought (the trials that wither the shrub) cannot touch its life; and vv.9–10 supply the reason such trust cannot be self-generated — "**the heart is deceitful above all things… who can understand it?**": the finding is that the heart is *not transparent even to itself* — it deceives its own owner, so twisted and sick that no self-examination can fully know it ("who can understand it?"), and *only God* "searches the heart" (v.10) and can *heal* it (v.14, "heal me, O LORD, and I shall be healed"); so the two trusts and the deceitful heart belong together — the self cannot be trusted (deceitful), therefore the self must not be the object of trust (cursed shrub), therefore trust must rest on the LORD (blessed tree), and the deceitful heart itself must be searched and healed by God, not by its own inspection)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the two trusts — flesh vs the LORD ("cursed is the man who trusts in man… blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD" — the interior's stability a function of its trust-object)* (thread — a gem: what the self trusts determines whether it is a stunted shrub or a water-rooted tree, w/ 17:5-8, Ps 1, Prov 3:5); *trust in God as freedom from fear in adversity (the tree "does not fear when heat comes… not anxious in the year of drought" — roots past the surface weather to a constant source)* (new — a gem: the God-trusting interior un-shaken by the trials that wither the self-truster, w/ 17:8, Ps 46, Isa 26:3); *the deceitful, self-opaque heart ("the heart is deceitful above all things… who can understand it?" — the heart that deceives even its own owner)* (thread — a gem: the interior not transparent to itself, beyond self-examination, w/ 17:9, Ps 19:12, Jer 2:35); *only God searches and heals the heart ("I the LORD search the heart… heal me, O LORD, and I shall be healed" — the heart knowable and curable only by God)* (thread — a gem: the deceitful heart must be searched and healed by God, not by its own inspection, w/ 17:10,14, Ps 139:23-24, Ezek 36:26).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the two trusts — flesh vs the LORD; trust in God as freedom from fear in adversity; the deceitful, self-opaque heart; only God searches and heals the heart. Chapter 17 is batch 3's summit — the interior is a stunted shrub or a water-rooted tree depending on *what it trusts*, and its deceitful, self-opaque heart can be known and healed only by God.
- **Book-level note (Jeremiah batch 3, chs 11–17):** the movement joins the *prophet's confessions* — the betrayed lamb committing his cause (11), the "why do the wicked prosper?" met by the toughening answer (12), the raw "deceitful brook" complaint answered by a call to the prophet's own return (15), the sign-bearing life sustained by God as refuge (16) — with the great *diagnoses of the heart*: made to *cling* to God but spoiled by pride and *habituated evil* (13); praying *from within guilt* on God's name (14); and the summit — the *two trusts* (shrub vs tree) and the *deceitful heart* that only God can search and heal (17). Master-findings: **the interior is designed to cling to God**; **its stability is a function of what it trusts (flesh withers, God-trust bears fruit through drought)**; **the heart is deceitful even to itself and healable only by God**; and **even the faithful sufferer is called to return.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the deceitful heart / new heart (→ 31:33); the fountain of living water (← 2:13; 17:13); committing one's cause (→ 20:12); trust (Ps 1; Isa 26:3); the confessions (→ 20:7-18).
