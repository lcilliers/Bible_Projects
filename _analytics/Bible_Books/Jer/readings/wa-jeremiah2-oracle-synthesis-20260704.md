## JER-fountain-cisterns · Jeremiah 2:1–37 · [D, multi] — forsaking the fountain for broken cisterns; the self-deceiving "I am innocent"

**The reading.** God recalls a lost first love — "**I remember the devotion of your youth, your love as a bride**" (v.2) — and asks the wounded question, "**What wrong did your fathers find in me that they went far from me?**" (v.5). At the centre is the book's most famous diagnosis: "**My people have committed two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living waters, and hewed out cisterns for themselves, broken cisterns that can hold no water**" (v.13). The apostasy is willful ("**the fear of me is not in you**", v.19), un-washable ("**though you wash yourself with lye… the stain of your guilt is still before me**", v.22), and *self-deceived*: "**How can you say, 'I am not unclean'?**… you say, 'I am innocent; surely his anger has turned from me'**" (vv.23,35). The finding is **the *two evils* — *forsaking the living fountain* and *hewing out broken cisterns* — joined to a *self-deceiving denial* ("I am innocent… I have not sinned")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's fundamental mis-transaction: abandoning the *inexhaustible living source* (God, "the fountain of living waters") to *manufacture its own* sources of life ("cisterns… hewed out for themselves") which *cannot hold water* — a double folly (leaving the full for the empty, the given for the self-made) — compounded by a *denial* that cannot see its own stain** *(inferred: v.13's twin images are the book's inner-being key. The *first* evil is *forsaking the fountain* — turning from the living, self-replenishing source of life; the *second*, deeper evil is the *positive labour of self-supply* — "**hewed out cisterns FOR THEMSELVES**", the exhausting work of digging one's own reservoirs, which "**can hold no water**": the finding is that the God-less interior does not merely *lack* the source but *replaces* it with self-constructed substitutes, and the tragedy is doubled — it leaves what is *living and free* (a fountain that flows on its own) for what is *dead and laborious and leaky* (a cistern one must dig and that drains away); this is the exact anatomy of misplaced desire (the interior toiling to supply itself with what only God gives freely, and failing); and the second finding is the *self-deception* that seals it — "**I am innocent… I have not sinned**" (v.35): the finding is that the forsaking interior *cannot see its own guilt* ("how can you say, I am not unclean?", v.23) — the stain is "before God" but invisible to the self, so the very heart that has dug leaky cisterns insists it is fine; the denial is not mere dishonesty but a *blindness* the apostasy produces)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *forsaking the living fountain for self-hewn broken cisterns ("they have forsaken me, the fountain of living waters, and hewed out cisterns… that can hold no water")* (thread — a gem, the book's central diagnosis: leaving the living free source for laborious self-made substitutes that leak, w/ 2:13, 17:13, John 4:14, Isa 55:1-2); *the double folly (leaving the full for the empty, the given for the self-supplied)* (new — a gem: the exhausting labour of self-supply replacing the freely-flowing source, w/ 2:13, Hag 1:6); *self-deceiving denial of guilt ("I am innocent… I have not sinned" — the stain before God but invisible to the self)* (thread — a gem: apostasy producing a blindness to its own guilt, w/ 2:22-23,35, 1 John 1:8, Rev 3:17); *the lost first love ("the devotion of your youth… but my people have forgotten me")* (thread — w/ 2:2,32, Rev 2:4).
