## JER-return-acknowledge · Jeremiah 3:1–25 · [D, multi] — the call to return; acknowledging guilt; God's readiness to heal faithlessness

**The reading.** By every human law the adulterous wife could not return (v.1), yet God pleads, "**would you return to me?**" The summons is astonishing in its mercy: "**Return, faithless Israel… I will not look on you in anger, for I am merciful… I will not be angry forever. Only acknowledge your guilt, that you rebelled against the LORD your God**" (vv.12–13); "**Return, O faithless children… I will heal your faithlessness**" (v.22). But God exposes a counterfeit: Judah "**did not return to me with her whole heart, but in pretense**" (v.10). The finding is **an *open door of return* — offered even to the deeply unfaithful, conditioned only on *honest acknowledgement of guilt* ("only acknowledge") — and God's readiness to *"heal faithlessness itself"*, against a *pretended* return that is not "with the whole heart"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior invited back on the gentlest possible terms — not "make yourself worthy" but simply "**acknowledge your guilt**" — so that the single required act is *honest self-admission*, not self-repair; and the counterfeit is exposed as a *return "in pretense"* — the outward motion of repentance without the "whole heart", which God sees through** *(inferred: the mercy of vv.12–13 is startling — after chapters of "whoredom", God's word is not "you have gone too far" but "**RETURN**… I am merciful… **only acknowledge your guilt**": the finding is that the door of return stands open even to the grievously unfaithful, and the *sole condition* is *honest acknowledgement* — not penance, not proven reform, but simply *owning* the guilt ("that you rebelled"); the finding is that what God requires to begin restoration is *truthfulness about oneself*, the interior's admission of its real state (the opposite of ch.2's "I am innocent"); and the depth of the offer is "**I will heal your FAITHLESSNESS**" (v.22) — God offers to cure not just the acts but the *disposition* to stray (the "bent to backsliding", as Hos 14:4); yet the passage guards against a *counterfeit* — "she did not return… with her whole heart, but **in pretense**" (v.10): the finding is that a return can be *outwardly performed and inwardly absent* — the motions of repentance without the whole heart — and God distinguishes the two; the acknowledgement he asks is not a formula but a *whole-hearted* owning; the chapter closes with the model true response: "we have sinned against the LORD our God… let us lie down in our shame" (vv.24–25) — the honest self-admission that opens the door)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the open door of return on the gentlest terms ("Return… only acknowledge your guilt" — restoration conditioned solely on honest self-admission, not self-repair)* (thread — a gem: the sole condition is truthfulness about oneself, w/ 3:12-13, 1 John 1:9, Luke 15:18-20, Ps 32:5); *God's readiness to heal faithlessness itself ("I will heal your faithlessness" — curing the disposition to stray, not just the acts)* (thread — a gem: the healing of the bent to backslide, w/ 3:22, Hos 14:4); *the pretended return ("not with her whole heart, but in pretense" — the motions of repentance without the whole heart, which God sees through)* (new — a gem: outward return without inward reality, w/ 3:10, 29:13, Joel 2:13); *honest self-admission as the true response ("we have sinned… let us lie down in our shame")* (thread — w/ 3:24-25, contrast 2:35).
