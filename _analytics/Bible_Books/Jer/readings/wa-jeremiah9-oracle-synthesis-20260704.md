## JER-boast-knowing-God · Jeremiah 9:1–26 · [D, multi] — the one legitimate boast: knowing God; the weeping prophet; refusing to know God

**The reading.** The chapter opens in the prophet's tears: "**Oh that my head were waters, and my eyes a fountain of tears, that I might weep day and night for the slain of the daughter of my people!**" (v.1). Society has dissolved into deceit — "**they bend their tongue like a bow… they do not know me**" (v.3); "**they refuse to know me**" (v.6). Then the chapter's summit, which relocates all human glory: "**Let not the wise man boast in his wisdom, let not the mighty man boast in his might, let not the rich man boast in his riches, but let him who boasts boast in this, that he understands and knows me, that I am the LORD who practices steadfast love, justice, and righteousness in the earth. For in these things I delight**" (vv.23–24). And the closing diagnosis: all are "**uncircumcised in heart**" (v.26). The finding is **the *one legitimate boast* — not wisdom, might, or riches, but *knowing God* — set against a people who "**refuse to know**" him, and framed by the *prophet's weeping*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's *ground of worth and glory* relocated: the three great human securities (intellect, power, wealth) are *disqualified* as objects of boasting, and the *only* worthy boast is *knowing God* — a *relational* knowing of his character (steadfast love, justice, righteousness) — so that the self's true riches are not what it *has or achieves* but *whom it knows*; and this is set against a people who *actively "refuse to know"* God, whose hearts are "uncircumcised"** *(inferred: vv.23–24 are one of Scripture's clearest statements about where the interior may rightly find its glory — the finding is that the three things the self most naturally *boasts in* — "wisdom… might… riches" (the achievements and possessions that seem to constitute worth) — are all *disqualified*; not because they are worthless but because they are the *wrong ground* of the self's confidence; and the *one* legitimate boast is "**that he understands and KNOWS me**" (v.24): the finding is that the interior's true glory is *relational, not proprietary* — it lies not in what one has attained but in *whom one knows*, and specifically in knowing God's *character* ("steadfast love, justice, righteousness… in these I delight"); to *know God* — and to know that these are the things he *delights* in — is the only boast that will hold, because it rests the self on an inexhaustible Other rather than on its own perishable achievements; and this is sharpened by contrast — the people "**refuse to know me**" (v.6), "uncircumcised in heart" (v.26): the finding is that the very knowing which is the one worthy boast is what the corrupt interior *declines* — it would rather boast in its wisdom/might/riches than *know God*; and framing it all is the prophet's *weeping* (v.1) — the one who does know God grieving over those who refuse to)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the one legitimate boast — knowing God ("let not the wise/mighty/rich boast… but let him who boasts boast that he knows me")* (thread — a gem: the interior's true glory relocated from what it achieves to whom it knows, resting on an inexhaustible Other, w/ 9:23-24, 1 Cor 1:31, Phil 3:8, Ps 20:7); *knowing God as relational, not proprietary (knowing his character — steadfast love, justice, righteousness — in which he delights)* (new — a gem: worth in relationship, not possession, w/ 9:24, John 17:3); *refusing to know God ("they refuse to know me… uncircumcised in heart" — the very knowing that is the one worthy boast, declined)* (thread — a gem: the corrupt interior would rather boast in its achievements than know God, w/ 9:6, 4:22, Hos 4:6); *the weeping prophet ("oh that my head were waters… that I might weep" — the one who knows God grieving over those who refuse to)* (thread — a gem: the cost of knowing God amid those who refuse, w/ 9:1, 8:18, 13:17, Luke 19:41).
