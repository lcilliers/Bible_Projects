## JER-rechabites-obedience · Jeremiah 35:1–19 · [D, multi] — consistent multi-generational obedience as a rebuke to disobedience toward God

**The reading.** Jeremiah offers the Rechabites wine, and they refuse: "**We will drink no wine, for Jonadab the son of Rechab, our father, commanded us**… **We have obeyed the voice of Jonadab… in all that he commanded us**" (vv.6,8) — a command kept for *generations*. God turns them into a living rebuke: "**The command that Jonadab… gave to his sons, to drink no wine, has been kept… for they have obeyed their father's command. I have spoken to you persistently, but you have not listened**" (v.14); "**the sons of Jonadab have kept the command their father gave them, but this people has not obeyed me**" (v.16). The finding is **the Rechabites' *consistent, multi-generational obedience* to a mere human ancestor's word — held up as a *rebuke* to a nation that will not obey the *living God*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the contrast of two obediences: a *human* command (a forefather's, about a small matter — no wine) kept *faithfully across generations*, against the *divine* command (God's, about everything) *persistently refused* — so the Rechabites' fidelity *shames* the interior that reserves such steadfastness for lesser loyalties while spurning the greatest** *(inferred: the point is comparative and pointed — the Rechabites obey "**Jonadab our father**", a human ancestor, in a *minor* matter (abstaining from wine, living in tents), and they do so *for generations without lapse*; the finding is that people are *capable* of remarkable, sustained faithfulness — the interior *can* hold a commitment consistently over a lifetime and beyond — which makes the *refusal* to obey God all the more damning: the same heart that keeps a family tradition unbroken "will not listen" to the God who made it (v.14); the finding is that *disobedience to God is not from incapacity* — we prove by our steadfast loyalties elsewhere (to a family code, a cause, a discipline) that we *can* be faithful; the failure is not of ability but of *will and priority* — reserving our fidelity for lesser things while spurning the greatest claim; the Rechabites are commended and given a lasting reward (v.19), not because their rule was itself God's command, but because their *spirit of faithful obedience* is what God longs to find in his people)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *sustained faithfulness proving capacity ("the sons of Jonadab have kept the command… but this people has not obeyed me" — the human steadfastness that shames disobedience to God)* (thread — a gem: disobedience to God is not from incapacity — our faithful loyalties elsewhere prove we can be faithful, w/ 35:14-16, Luke 16:8, Matt 21:31); *fidelity reserved for lesser loyalties (the interior steadfast to a family code yet spurning the living God — a failure of priority, not ability)* (new — a gem: our capacity for faithfulness misdirected to lesser claims, w/ 35:6-16); *the spirit of obedience commended ("because you have obeyed… Jonadab shall never lack a man to stand before me" — the faithful heart rewarded)* (thread — w/ 35:18-19).
