## JER-broken-flask · Jeremiah 19:1–15 · [D, multi] — the fired flask broken past mending; the point of no return; the stiffened neck

**The reading.** Where chapter 18 showed soft clay *reworked*, chapter 19 shows a *fired* vessel *shattered*. Jeremiah takes "**a potter's earthenware flask**" (v.1) to the Valley of Slaughter, and after pronouncing judgment, "**you shall break the flask… and shall say, 'So will I break this people and this city, as one breaks a potter's vessel, so that it can never be mended'**" (vv.10–11). The cause is the same hardening: "**they have stiffened their neck, refusing to hear my words**" (v.15). The finding is **the *fired flask broken irreparably* ("so that it can never be mended") — the dark counterpart to the reworkable clay — marking a *point past re-forming*, reached by a *stiffened neck* that "refused to hear"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the sober disclosure that the interior which *will not be reworked* (18:12) eventually passes from *soft clay* to *fired pottery* — from the state where it can be re-formed to the state where it can only be *broken*; the difference is *time and hardening*: the neck "stiffened" long enough becomes a vessel set hard, and set hard, it "can never be mended"** *(inferred: the two potter-signs are deliberately paired and must be read together. Chapter 18's clay is *soft, on the wheel* — spoiled but *reworkable* ("reworked into another vessel"); chapter 19's flask is *fired, finished* — and when it fails, it can only be *smashed* ("**can never be mended**", v.11): the finding is that there is a *movement* in the hardening interior from the *malleable* to the *set* — a heart that repeatedly refuses the potter's hand does not stay soft forever but *fires hard*, passing a threshold past which re-formation is no longer possible; the "stiffened neck, refusing to hear" (v.15) is the process that fires the clay; the finding is a solemn warning about the *urgency* of turning — the same self is, at one stage, re-formable clay (grace's invitation, 18) and at a later stage, a brittle vessel fit only for breaking (judgment's finality, 19) — and the transition is made by *persistent refusal to hear*; the interior does not remain indefinitely open; hardening, prolonged, becomes irreversible)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the fired flask broken past mending ("so will I break this people… that it can never be mended" — the interior passed from reworkable clay to set pottery fit only for breaking)* (thread — a gem: the movement from malleable to irreparable, the dark counterpart to ch.18, w/ 19:11, Prov 29:1, Heb 6:4-6); *hardening as a threshold-crossing process ("they have stiffened their neck, refusing to hear" — the persistent refusal that fires the clay hard)* (new — a gem: prolonged refusal moving the self past re-formation, w/ 19:15, 7:26, Zech 7:11-12); *the urgency of turning while still soft (the two potter-signs paired — grace's invitation before the clay fires)* (new — the solemn warning about time, w/ 18:4/19:11, 2 Cor 6:2).
