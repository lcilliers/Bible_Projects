## JER-potter-clay · Jeremiah 18:1–23 · [D, multi] — the reworkable clay; God's dealing conditional on turning; the defiant self-will

**The reading.** At the potter's house Jeremiah watches: "**the vessel he was making of clay was spoiled in the potter's hand, and he reworked it into another vessel, as it seemed good to the potter**" (v.4). The lesson: "**like the clay in the potter's hand, so are you in my hand, O house of Israel**" (v.6) — and God's dealing is *responsive*: "**if that nation… turns from its evil, I will relent of the disaster… if it does evil… I will relent of the good**" (vv.7–10). So the summons: "**Return, every one from his evil way**" (v.11). But the people answer with flat defiance: "**That is in vain! We will follow our own plans, and will every one act according to the stubbornness of his evil heart**" (v.12). The finding is **the interior as *clay in the potter's hand* — *reworkable* when spoiled, its future *conditional on turning* — met by a *defiant self-will* that says "we will follow our own plans"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure that the self is *malleable in God's hands* — spoiled clay *reworked*, not discarded — and that God's dealings are *not fixed fate but responsive* to the interior's *turning* ("if it turns… I will relent"); against which stands the *hardened autonomy* that refuses the potter's hand — "we will follow our own plans… the stubbornness of his evil heart"** *(inferred: the potter-image (vv.4,6) makes two inner-being points. First, the self is *clay* — pliable, *re-formable*: the "spoiled" vessel is not thrown away but "**reworked into another vessel**" — the finding is that the interior, marred, remains *workable* in God's hands, capable of being made again (grace as re-formation, not scrapping); second, and crucially, vv.7–10 attach a *conditionality* — God's declared intentions (to pluck up or to build) are *responsive to the interior's response*: "if that nation **turns**… I will relent" — the finding is that the future is not sealed fate but genuinely *hinges on the heart's turning*, so repentance is never futile (God stands ready to "relent"); against this open, hopeful picture stands v.12's chilling refusal — "**That is in vain! We will follow OUR OWN PLANS**": the finding is that the defiant self *rejects the potter's hand altogether* — it will not be clay, insisting on its "own plans" and "the stubbornness of its evil heart"; the tragedy is not that God will not rework them but that they *will not be reworked* — the clay that hardens itself against the wheel; the imprecatory close (vv.19-23) shows even the prophet's interior strained to the point of praying against his plotters — the confessions do not sanitise his anguish)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the self as reworkable clay (the spoiled vessel "reworked into another" — the interior marred yet re-formable in God's hands)* (thread — a gem: grace as re-formation, not scrapping, w/ 18:4,6, Isa 64:8, Rom 9:20-21); *God's dealings conditional on turning ("if that nation turns… I will relent" — the future hinging on the heart's response, so repentance is never futile)* (new — a gem: not sealed fate but responsive to turning, w/ 18:7-10, Jonah 3:10, Ezek 18:21-23); *the defiant self that will not be clay ("we will follow our own plans… the stubbornness of his evil heart" — the interior refusing the potter's hand)* (thread — a gem: hardened autonomy rejecting re-formation, w/ 18:12, 2:25, 7:24); *the strained imprecatory heart (the prophet's anguish praying against his plotters)* (thread — the confessions' un-sanitised rawness, w/ 18:19-23, 20:12).
