## JER-incurable-healed · Jeremiah 30:1–24 · [D, multi] — the incurable wound healed by God; care for the un-cared-for outcast; measured discipline

**The reading.** The Book of Consolation opens over a "**time of distress for Jacob; yet he shall be saved out of it**" (v.7), with a tender "**fear not, O Jacob my servant… for I am with you to save you… I will discipline you in just measure, and I will by no means leave you unpunished**" (vv.10–11). God then names the wound as beyond all human remedy: "**Your hurt is incurable, and your wound is grievous. There is none to uphold your cause, no medicine for your wound, no healing for you… all your lovers have forgotten you**" (vv.12–14). And precisely there, the reversal: "**For I will restore health to you, and your wounds I will heal, declares the LORD, because they have called you an outcast: 'It is Zion, for whom no one cares!'**" (v.17). The finding is **a wound *humanly incurable* — "no medicine… no healing" — *healed by God*, and God's care aroused *precisely because no one else cares* ("Zion, for whom no one cares!")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior brought to the point where its condition is *beyond all self- and human-help* ("your hurt is incurable… none to uphold your cause"), so that healing can come *only* from God — and the disclosure that God's compassion is *drawn* by the very abandonment of the sufferer: the label "outcast, for whom no one cares" is what *moves* him to heal** *(inferred: the sequence is the inner-being key — God *first* insists the wound is "**incurable… no healing for you**" (vv.12–13): the finding is that there is a mercy in being brought to the end of all remedies — the interior must sometimes be shown that its wound is *beyond every human and self-cure* before it will receive the only cure that works (God's); the "incurable" diagnosis is not despair but the clearing away of false hopes ("all your lovers have forgotten you", v.14 — the substitutes fail); and then the reversal (v.17) is grounded in a *stunning motive* — "**because they have called you an outcast: 'Zion, for whom NO ONE CARES!'**": the finding is that God's compassion is *aroused by the sufferer's very abandonment* — the fact that "no one cares" is precisely what moves *him* to care; the interior at its most forsaken, written off by everyone, is the object of God's particular tenderness — the "for whom no one cares" is exactly whom he heals; and even the discipline is measured — "**in just measure**… but I will by no means leave you unpunished" (v.11): the finding is that God's severity toward his own is *proportioned*, never destroying ("of you I will not make a full end"), so the wound, though grievous, is within a love that heals)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the humanly-incurable wound healed by God ("your wound is incurable… no healing for you" — YET "I will heal you" — brought to the end of all remedies so only God's cure remains)* (thread — a gem: the mercy of being shown one's wound is beyond every self- and human-cure, w/ 30:12-17, 8:22, 2 Cor 1:9, Mark 5:26-29); *God's compassion aroused by the sufferer's abandonment ("because they called you an outcast, for whom no one cares" — the very being-forsaken that moves God to heal)* (new — a gem: the one whom no one cares for is exactly whom God cares for, w/ 30:17, Ps 27:10, 142:4-5, Isa 62:4); *the failing substitutes ("all your lovers have forgotten you" — the false helpers proving empty at the point of need)* (thread — w/ 30:14, 2:13); *measured discipline ("I will discipline you in just measure… not make a full end" — severity proportioned within a healing love)* (thread — a gem: God's correction never destroying his own, w/ 30:11, 10:24, 46:28).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the humanly-incurable wound healed by God; God's compassion aroused by the sufferer's abandonment; the failing substitutes; measured discipline. Chapter 30 opens the Consolation by bringing the interior to the end of all remedies — "no healing for you" — and there healing it, its very abandonment ("for whom no one cares") the thing that moves God's care.
- **Threads:** the incurable wound healed (← 8:22; → 33:6); God's care for the forsaken (w/ Ps 27:10, Isa 62:4); measured discipline (← 10:24; → 46:28); everlasting love (→ 31:3).
