## JER-plans-seek-whole-heart · Jeremiah 29:1–32 · [D, multi] — seeking the welfare of the place of exile; plans for a future and hope; seeking God with the whole heart

**The reading.** Jeremiah writes to the exiles in Babylon a word against despair and false hope alike: "**Build houses and live in them; plant gardens and eat their produce… But seek the welfare of the city where I have sent you into exile, and pray to the LORD on its behalf, for in its welfare you will find your welfare**" (vv.5–7). He warns against the diviners' comforting lies (vv.8–9), then gives the promise: "**For I know the plans I have for you, declares the LORD, plans for welfare and not for evil, to give you a future and a hope. Then you will call upon me and come and pray to me, and I will hear you. You will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart**" (vv.11–13). The finding is **the interior in exile called to *flourish and seek the welfare of even its captor-city* ("in its welfare you will find your welfare") — assured of God's *good plan* ("a future and a hope") — and promised that it *will find God when it seeks him with the whole heart*** *(stated)*. The operation is **three inner-being movements: (1) the self displaced into an unwanted place told not to *withdraw in bitterness* but to *invest and bless* — "seek the welfare of the city", binding its own good to the good of even its captors; (2) the anxious heart steadied by the assurance that God's *plans* toward it are *good* ("welfare, not evil… a future and a hope"), even in exile; (3) the seeking heart promised a *real finding* — "you will seek me and find me, when you seek me with the whole heart"** *(inferred: three findings. First, vv.5–7 confront the *interior of the displaced* — the exile's temptation is to live in suspended bitterness, refusing to invest in a hated place; God says the opposite: "**build… plant… SEEK THE WELFARE of the city**… **in ITS welfare you will find YOUR welfare**": the finding is that the interior thrives not by resentful withdrawal but by *investing in and blessing the place it did not choose* — even praying for its captors — binding its own flourishing to the common good (the seed of "love your enemies"). Second, v.11's "**I know the plans I have for you… for welfare and not for evil, to give you a future and a hope**" steadies the *anxious* interior — the finding is that God's disposition toward his people, *even in the exile he sent*, is *benevolent and purposeful*: the discipline is not the abandonment of a good plan but part of it, so the heart may trust that its future is held by One who intends "welfare… a future and a hope". Third, vv.12–13 promise the *seeking heart a finding* — "**you will SEEK me and FIND me, when you seek me WITH ALL YOUR HEART**": the finding is that God is *findable* — not hidden from the earnest — on one condition, *whole-heartedness*; the seeking that finds is not casual or divided but "with all the heart" (the antidote to the "pretense" return of 3:10); and strikingly this whole-hearted seeking-and-finding is promised *in Babylon*, in the very place of exile — the interior can find God fully even in displacement, provided it seeks him wholly)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *investing in the unchosen place, not withdrawing in bitterness ("seek the welfare of the city… in its welfare you will find your welfare" — the displaced interior blessing even its captors)* (new — a gem: thriving by investment and blessing, binding one's good to the common good, w/ 29:5-7, Matt 5:44, 1 Tim 2:2); *God's good and purposeful plan even in exile ("I know the plans I have for you… for welfare, a future and a hope" — the discipline part of a benevolent purpose)* (thread — a gem: God's benevolent disposition steadying the anxious heart, w/ 29:11, Rom 8:28, Lam 3:22-23); *the seeking heart's real finding, conditioned on whole-heartedness ("you will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart")* (thread — a gem: God findable to the earnest, the whole-hearted seeking that finds, w/ 29:13, Deut 4:29, Jer 3:10 contrast, Matt 7:7); *God findable even in the place of exile (whole-hearted seeking finds him in displacement)* (new — w/ 29:12-14).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** investing in the unchosen place, not withdrawing in bitterness; God's good and purposeful plan even in exile; the seeking heart's real finding conditioned on whole-heartedness; God findable even in exile. Chapter 29 speaks to the displaced interior — flourish and bless where you did not choose to be, trust God's good plan, and seek him whole-heartedly, for he is found by such even in Babylon.
- **Book-level note (Jeremiah batch 5, chs 21–29):** the movement joins *judgment on false leadership* with *the true good of the interior*. Its findings: *justice to the poor is to know God*, and prosperity deafens (21–22); the false shepherds vs the Branch who is "the LORD is our righteousness" (23:1-8); the *self-projected word* vs the true word "like fire", before a God from whom none can hide (23:9-40); the God-given "*heart to know me*" from which whole-hearted return flows (24); the *long-spurned word* and universal accountability (25); the prophet's *composure on trial* (26); the *seduction of the comforting lie* and Jeremiah's honest "Amen" (27–28); and the exile's charge to *flourish, trust God's good plan, and seek him with the whole heart* (29). Master-findings: **knowing God is enacted in justice to the weak**; **the capacity to know God is God's gift**; **the true word burns while the congenial lie soothes**; and **God is found by whole-hearted seeking, even in exile.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the given/new heart (→ 31:33); whole-hearted seeking (← 3:10, 24:7); the true word (← 20:9); the Branch / the LORD our righteousness (→ 33:16).
