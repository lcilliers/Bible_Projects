## HOS-sow-wind · Hos 8:1–14 · [D, multi] — self-destroying autonomy; forgetting the Maker

**The reading.** The oracle's proverb frames it: "for they **sow the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind**" (v.7). The autonomy behind it: "they **made kings, but not through me**. They set up princes, but I knew it not. With their silver and gold they **made idols for their own destruction**" (v.4). And the root: "**Israel has forgotten his Maker** and built palaces" (v.14). The finding is **an autonomy that acts without God — self-made kings, self-made gods — recoiling as self-destruction, because the Maker himself is forgotten** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self that constructs its own supports (rulers, idols, fortresses) apart from God and reaps ruin** *(inferred: "sow the wind, reap the whirlwind" (v.7) names the *amplifying recoil* of autonomous action — emptiness sown returns as a storm; and the taproot is v.14, "Israel has forgotten his Maker" — the interior that no longer references its origin builds frantically (palaces, fortified cities) on nothing; the finding is that forgetting the Maker is the source-fault from which self-made religion and politics grow, and that such self-supply is self-defeating; this "forgot his Maker" is the same fullness-forgetting that recurs at 13:6 and echoes Ecclesiastes' "remember your Creator")*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *self-destroying autonomy (self-made kings and idols recoiling as ruin)* (new — sow-wind/reap-whirlwind); *forgetting the Maker as the source-fault* (thread — w/ 13:6, Ecc 12:1's "remember your Creator" inverted); *frantic self-supply built on nothing* (new).
