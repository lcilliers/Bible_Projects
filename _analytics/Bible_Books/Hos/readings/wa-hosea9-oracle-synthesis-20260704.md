## HOS-days-punishment · Hos 9:1–17 · [D, multi] — becoming what you love; the days of reckoning

**The reading.** Amid the exile-oracle sits one of Scripture's deepest inner-being laws: "they came to **Baal-peor** and consecrated themselves to the thing of shame, and **became detestable like the thing they loved**" (v.10). Around it, the reckoning: "the **days of punishment have come**" (v.7); "**I will love them no more**" (v.15); and the terrible "**Woe to them when I depart from them!**" (v.12). The finding is **the formation-law — the self becomes like what it loves and worships** — set inside the sorrow of a God whose love is at last withdrawn *(stated)*. The operation is **worship as self-transformation: consecrating oneself to an object *conforms* the self to it** *(inferred: "became detestable like the thing they loved" (v.10) is the same law as Ps 115:8 ("those who make idols become like them") — the finding that *love conforms the lover to the beloved*; devotion is never neutral, it *shapes* the interior into the likeness of its object, so loving a "thing of shame" makes the self shameful; this is the positive betrothal-telos (2:20, "you shall know the LORD") turned dark — as knowing/loving God would form them into his likeness, loving Baal deforms them into its; and the withdrawal ("I will love them no more", "when I depart", vv.12,15) is the gravest note — not new judgment but the felt *absence* of the God whose presence was their life)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the formation-law — becoming like what you love/worship* (thread — w/ Ps 115:8; the study's candidate organising law; the dark mirror of 2:20's "know the LORD"); *worship as self-transformation (devotion conforms the self to its object)* (new); *the withdrawn love / departed God as the gravest loss* (thread — w/ 5:15, Lam).
