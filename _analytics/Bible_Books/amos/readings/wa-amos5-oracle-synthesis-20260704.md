## AMOS-seek-and-live · Amos 5:1–27 · [D, multi] — seek God and live; justice over ritual; the misjudged day of the LORD

**The reading.** Amid a lament over fallen Israel, the summons repeats like a heartbeat: "**Seek me and live**" (v.4); "**Seek the LORD and live**" (v.6); "**Seek good, and not evil, that you may live… Hate evil, and love good, and establish justice in the gate**" (vv.14–15). It exposes those who "**turn justice to wormwood**" and "**hate him who reproves in the gate**" (vv.7,10). Then the searing word on worship: "**I hate, I despise your feasts… Take away from me the noise of your songs… But let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream**" (vv.21–24). And a warning to those who long for God's day: "**Woe to you who desire the day of the LORD! … It is darkness, and not light**" (v.18). The finding is **the repeated summons to *"seek God and live"* — bound to *seeking good, hating evil, loving good* — over against a *ritual worship God hates* because it lacks the "justice [that] roll[s] down like waters"** *(stated)*. The operation is **life relocated in *seeking God* (and, inseparably, in *loving good and establishing justice*) — the interior's "living" is a *seeking*; while worship divorced from justice is not neutral but *hateful* to God ("I hate, I despise your feasts"), because the flood of song without the flood of justice is a counterfeit** *(inferred: the refrain "**seek me and LIVE**" (vv.4,6,14) makes *seeking* the very condition of life — the finding is that the interior *lives* by *seeking God*, an active, directional reaching (the opposite of the un-returned heart of ch.4); and "seek me" is immediately glossed as "**seek GOOD, and not evil… HATE evil, and LOVE good, and establish justice**" (vv.14–15): the finding is that seeking God and seeking good are *one movement* — one cannot seek God while loving evil; the affections (hate evil, love good) are reoriented as part of the seeking; and the great contrast (vv.21–24) exposes the counterfeit — God "**hates**" the feasts, songs, and offerings *because* they flow while justice does not: "let **justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream**" (v.24) sets the *torrent of justice* against the *noise of songs* — the finding is that worship is measured by whether the same heart that sings also *does justice*; ritual without justice is not half-worship but *anti-worship*, hateful to God; finally v.18 corrects a presumptuous longing — those who "**desire the day of the LORD**" imagining it their vindication are warned "it is **darkness, not light**": the finding is that the interior can crave God's coming while unready for it, mistaking judgment for reward)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *life as seeking God ("seek me and live" — the interior lives by an active, directional reaching for God)* (thread — a gem: seeking as the condition of life, w/ 5:4,6,14, Deut 4:29, Jer 29:13); *seeking God and loving good as one movement ("seek good… hate evil, love good, establish justice" — the affections reoriented as part of the seeking)* (new — a gem: one cannot seek God while loving evil, w/ 5:14-15, Rom 12:9, Ps 97:10); *worship without justice as anti-worship ("I hate your feasts… but let justice roll down like waters" — the torrent of justice against the noise of songs)* (thread — a gem: worship measured by whether the singing heart also does justice, w/ 5:21-24, Isa 1:11-17, 58, Mic 6:8); *craving God's day while unready ("woe to you who desire the day of the LORD… it is darkness" — mistaking judgment for reward)* (new — w/ 5:18-20, Mal 3:2).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** life as seeking God; seeking God and loving good as one movement; worship without justice as anti-worship; craving God's day while unready. Chapter 5 is the heart of Amos — "seek me and live", inseparable from loving good and letting "justice roll down like waters", against a ritual worship God *hates* for its justice-lessness.
- **Threads:** seek and live (← 4:6 "did not return"; w/ Deut 4:29); justice over ritual (← Isa 1:11-17; → Mic 6:8); the day of the LORD as darkness (→ 8:9; w/ Joel 2:2, Zeph 1:15).
