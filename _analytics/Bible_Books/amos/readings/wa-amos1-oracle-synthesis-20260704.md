## AMOS-three-and-four · Amos 1:2–2:16 · [D, multi] — the settled cruelty of the nations; Israel's oppression of the poor as covenant sin

**The reading.** Amos circles the nations with a drumbeat refrain — "**For three transgressions of [Damascus / Gaza / Tyre / Edom / Ammon / Moab], and for four, I will not revoke the punishment**" — naming atrocities of settled cruelty: Edom "**pursued his brother with the sword and cast off all pity, and his anger tore perpetually, and he kept his wrath forever**" (1:11); Ammon "**ripped open pregnant women… that they might enlarge their border**" (1:13). The circle tightens onto Judah ("**they have rejected the law of the LORD**", 2:4) and finally Israel itself: "**they sell the righteous for silver, and the needy for a pair of sandals — those who trample the head of the poor into the dust**" (2:6–7). The finding is **entrenched, *hardened* cruelty — anger "kept forever", pity "cast off" — and, at the climax, Israel's *oppression of the poor* (selling, trampling) named as the covenant's breaking-point** *(stated)*. The operation is **the exposure of a cruelty that has become *settled disposition* (Edom keeping "his wrath forever" — not a flash of anger but a nursed, perpetual one) — and the turning of the indictment onto God's own people, whose sin is located precisely in *how they treat the weak* ("trample the head of the poor"), so that the interior's true state is read off its dealings with the vulnerable** *(inferred: two inner-being notes. First, Edom's sin (1:11) is diagnosed as an *interior habit* — "his anger **tore perpetually**, and he **kept** his wrath **forever**": the finding is that the deepest cruelty is not the outburst but the *nursed grudge*, anger cultivated and kept until it becomes a permanent posture ("cast off all pity" — the deliberate extinguishing of compassion); this is cruelty as a settled state of the heart, not a lapse. Second, the rhetorical trap of the cycle — the hearer nods along as the *foreign* nations are condemned, then the same formula lands on Judah and *Israel* (2:4–6): the finding is that Israel's sin is measured by the *identical* standard, and its content is *oppression of the poor* ("sell the righteous… trample the head of the poor into the dust", 2:6–7) — the covenant people's interior is judged not by its religious privilege but by its *treatment of the vulnerable*; the God who condemns Ammon's atrocities condemns Israel's economic cruelty in the same breath, because both are the heart's contempt for the weak)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *cruelty as nursed, settled disposition (Edom's anger "kept forever", pity "cast off" — the grudge cultivated into a permanent posture)* (thread — a gem: the deepest cruelty is the nursed grudge, not the outburst, w/ 1:11, Eph 4:26-27, Lev 19:18); *the interior judged by its treatment of the weak ("trample the head of the poor into the dust" — covenant sin located in oppression)* (thread — a gem: the heart's state read off its dealings with the vulnerable, w/ 2:6-7, 5:11-12, 8:4-6, Mic 3:2-3); *the same standard for insider and outsider (the cycle turning from the nations onto Israel)* (new — privilege no exemption, w/ 3:2).
