## AMOS-plumbline-intercede · Amos 7:1–17 · [D, multi] — the prophet's intercession; the plumb line; courage under threat

**The reading.** Amos is shown two impending judgments — locusts, then fire — and each time he *intercedes*: "**O Lord GOD, please forgive! How can Jacob stand? He is so small!**" (v.2); "**O Lord GOD, please cease!**" (v.5) — and "**the LORD relented**" (vv.3,6). Then a third vision, un-intercedable: "**the Lord was standing beside a wall built with a plumb line… 'Behold, I am setting a plumb line in the midst of my people Israel'**" (vv.7–8). When Amaziah the priest orders him to stop prophesying and flee (vv.12–13), Amos answers with his commission: "**I was no prophet… but the LORD took me from following the flock, and the LORD said to me, 'Go, prophesy to my people Israel'**" (vv.14–15). The finding is **the prophet's *intercession* that stays judgment ("please forgive… the LORD relented"), the *plumb line* held to the people's heart, and the *courage* to keep speaking under threat, grounded in a commission he did not seek** *(stated)*. The operation is **the intercessor's interior — moved to plead for a people under judgment ("how can Jacob stand? he is so small"), and able to move God to relent — set beside the *measuring standard* (the plumb line, the objective test held against a crooked wall) and the *courage* of one who speaks not from ambition but from being *taken and sent*** *(inferred: three inner-being notes. First, Amos's *intercession* (vv.2,5) — the same prophet who thunders judgment *pleads* for mercy the moment he sees the blow coming ("please forgive! he is so small!"): the finding is that the true prophet's heart is *for* the people, not against them — his severity and his compassion are one, and his plea *moves God* ("the LORD relented", the intercessor genuinely affecting the outcome, as Moses did); the announcer of doom is the first to beg it be averted. Second, the *plumb line* (vv.7–8) — an objective *standard* dropped against the wall of the people: the finding is that judgment is not arbitrary but *measured* — God holds a true vertical against the crooked interior, and the crookedness is exposed by the *standard*, not by mood. Third, Amos's reply to Amaziah (vv.14–15) — under pressure to be silent, he grounds his courage not in himself ("I was no prophet") but in his *commission* ("the LORD **took** me… and **said**, Go"): the finding is that the courage to speak unwelcome truth rests on *being sent* — the interior stands firm under threat because its mandate is not its own, so it cannot be revoked by a priest or a king)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the intercessor's compassion ("please forgive! how can Jacob stand?" — the announcer of judgment the first to plead for mercy)* (thread — a gem: the true prophet's heart is for the people; severity and compassion one, w/ 7:2,5, Exod 32:11-14, Jer 18:20); *intercession that moves God ("the LORD relented" — the pleading interior genuinely affecting the outcome)* (new — a gem: prayer that alters the course, w/ 7:3,6, Gen 18:22-33); *the plumb line as objective standard (judgment measured against a true vertical, not arbitrary)* (new — a gem: the crooked interior exposed by the standard, w/ 7:7-8, 2 Kgs 21:13); *courage grounded in commission ("the LORD took me… Go, prophesy" — firmness under threat rests on being sent, not on self)* (thread — a gem: the mandate that cannot be revoked by human authority, w/ 7:14-15, Jer 1:7-8, Acts 4:19-20).
