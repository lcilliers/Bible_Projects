## HOS-forgot-me · Hos 13:1–16 · [D, multi] — fullness that forgets; the ransom from Death

**The reading.** The oracle names the mechanism of apostasy with precision: "it was I who **knew you** in the wilderness… but when they had grazed, they **became full**; they were **filled**, and their **heart was lifted up; therefore they forgot me**" (vv.5–6). Yet even here the promise breaks in: "**I shall ransom them from the power of Sheol; I shall redeem them from Death. O Death, where are your plagues?**" (v.14). The finding is **satisfaction as the road to forgetting God — a full, lifted-up heart forgets its source — set against a God who would ransom even from Death** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that forgets God precisely when it is *satisfied*, its heart lifted up by plenty** *(inferred: vv.5–6 give the exact anatomy — God *knew* them in the *wilderness* (in need); then fullness came, "their heart was lifted up (rum), therefore they forgot me": the finding is that *prosperity, not affliction, is the great forgetter* — the satisfied, self-exalted heart loses its grip on the God it depended on when hungry (the identical warning as Deut 8:11–14, "lest… you forget the LORD", and the counterpart to Ecc 12:1 "remember your Creator" / Hos 8:14 "forgot his Maker"); and against this self-caused ruin, v.14's astonishing gospel-note — a God who ransoms from Sheol and taunts Death — the redeeming love that outreaches even the grave; the finding pairs the deepest human fault (fullness-forgetting) with the deepest divine grace (ransom from Death))*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *fullness/satisfaction as the road to forgetting God (the lifted-up heart)* (thread — prosperity as the great forgetter, w/ Deut 8, Ecc 12:1, Hos 8:14); *ransom from Death (redeeming love outreaching the grave)* (new — w/ Job 19:25, the go'el; 1 Cor 15:55 quotes v.14). **Shared:** forgetting the Maker (8:14).
