## JON-psalm · Jonah 2:1–10 · [D, multi] — the drowning self's cry and thanksgiving

**The reading.** From inside the fish Jonah prays a psalm of the depths: "**I called out to the LORD, out of my distress, and he answered me; out of the belly of Sheol I cried, and you heard my voice**" (v.2); "**when my life was fainting away, I remembered the LORD**, and my prayer came to you" (v.7); "those who **pay regard to vain idols forsake their hope of steadfast love**" (v.8); "**Salvation belongs to the LORD!**" (v.9). The finding is **the cry of a self at the bottom — driven to remember God when life fails, ending in the confession that salvation is God's alone** *(stated)*. The operation is **the drowning interior turning back to God from the depths, and grasping that rescue is not self-made** *(inferred: the psalm's movement is from *distress* to *remembering* to *thanksgiving* — "when my life was fainting away, I *remembered* the LORD" (v.7): the extremity that finally turns the fleeing self back Godward (the same distress-driven return as Hos 5:15); and the two theological hinges are inner-being data — v.8, "those who regard vain idols *forsake their hope of chesed*" (idolatry is the abandonment of steadfast love), and v.9, "salvation belongs to the LORD" (yeshuah is God's, not the self's); the finding is the irony the book will exploit — Jonah *knows and sings* that salvation is God's free gift to the perishing, yet in ch.4 resents that same salvation given to Nineveh; the man who was himself rescued from the pit begrudges rescue to others; the psalm is genuine but its singer has not yet let its truth reshape his heart)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the drowning self's cry from the depths (distress → remembering → thanksgiving)* (new — the distress-driven return, w/ Hos 5:15, Ps 130); *salvation belongs to the LORD (rescue not self-made)* (new — the confession Jonah sings but does not yet live); *idolatry as forsaking one's hope of steadfast love* (new).
