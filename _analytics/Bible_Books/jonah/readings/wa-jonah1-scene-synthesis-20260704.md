# Jonah 1 — inner-being scene readings (Phase-2 meaning synthesis)

- **Source:** Phase-1 lexicals read per **narrative scene** (`segment_unit`, provenance `jonah-scene-segmentation-v1-20260704`). Jonah is **narrative prose** (not oracle/poetry) — processed as scenes, the same segmentation machinery. Cited; grounded; *stated* | *inferred*.
- **Lens:** the inner-being **operation** the scene stages; Jonah's story is, throughout, a study of the *reluctant, resentful interior* against God's compassion.

---

## JON-flight · Jonah 1:1–3 · [D, multi] — the interior fleeing its calling and God's presence

**The reading.** Called to Nineveh, "**Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD**… and went down into it… **away from the presence of the LORD**" (v.3). The finding is **the self running from its commission — and from God's very face** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior in flight, its every motion *downward*** *(inferred: the doubled "from the presence of the LORD" (mi-liphne YHWH) makes the flight not merely from a task but from *God himself* — an attempt to get beyond his face; and the narrative traces a *descent* — down to Joppa, down into the ship (and soon down into sleep, down into the sea, down to the roots of the mountains): the finding is that fleeing one's calling is a *going-down*, a self-lowering away from God; and it is telling that Jonah's flight is driven, as ch.4 reveals, not by fear but by *not wanting God to be merciful* — the interior runs because it disagrees with God's grace)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the self fleeing its calling and God's presence* (thread — the reluctant prophet, → 4:2); *flight as a downward descent* (new — running from God as a self-lowering).

---

## JON-storm · Jonah 1:4–17 · [D, multi] — the sleeping prophet vs the praying pagans

**The reading.** In the storm, the roles invert: the pagan "**mariners were afraid, and each cried out to his god**" (v.5), while "**Jonah had gone down… and was fast asleep**" (v.5); the captain must rouse him — "**What do you mean, you sleeper? Arise, call out to your god!**" (v.6). Jonah confesses, "I **fear the LORD**… who made the sea" (v.9) — a creed his conduct contradicts. When he is thrown in and the sea stills, "the men **feared the LORD exceedingly**, and they offered a sacrifice" (v.16). The finding is **the outsiders more spiritually responsive than the prophet — the pagans pray and come to fear the LORD while the called one sleeps** *(stated)*. The operation is **the deadened interior of the fleeing prophet, set against the awakening interior of the pagan sailors** *(inferred: the contrast is the scene's whole point — the *sailors* cry out, deliberate, and end by *fearing the LORD and sacrificing* (a conversion, v.16), while *Jonah* sleeps through the crisis his own flight caused; the finding is a sharp inner-being irony — the one who *says* "I fear the LORD" (v.9) is asleep to him, while those who did not know him come to fear him; the responsiveness of the "outsider" exposes the numbness of the insider; and Jonah's willingness to be thrown overboard (v.12) is an odd nobility — he would rather die than obey, a first glimpse of the death-wish that recurs at 4:3,8)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the responsive outsider vs the numb insider (pagans fear the LORD, the prophet sleeps)* (new — a sharp irony; the "insider" asleep to God); *the creed contradicted by conduct ("I fear the LORD" while fleeing him)* (new); *the death-preference over obedience* (thread — → 4:3,8).
