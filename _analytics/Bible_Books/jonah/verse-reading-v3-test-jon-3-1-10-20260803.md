## T1–T5 — Lexical meaning, by verse

### Jon 3:1

**Reading:** The word of the LORD came-to-be (Qal wayyiqtol, `HVqw3ms`, on the root *hayah*) to Jonah, a second time, saying [the speech that follows]. "Word" is the grammatical subject of the coming-to-be event; the LORD is named as the word's source (construct "word-of-LORD"); Jonah is the goal ("to Jonah"); "saying" (Qal infinitive construct + lamed) is the standard formula introducing the direct speech that follows in v2.

**Flags:**
- **T2** — `H1961` (hayah) stepGloss reads flatly "to be," but the operative sense here is the lexicon's own "to happen, fall out, occur, take place, come about, come to pass" — the formulaic prophetic-word-event sense, not a static "to be." Stated directly in the lexical range, not inferred.
- **T5** — This verse is the book's own repeat of its opening commissioning formula (cf. Jon 1:1), explicitly marked "the second time." That marker is itself the genre-conventional signal that this is a renewed commission after the flight/deliverance of chs. 1–2 — recorded so it isn't read as incidental repetition.

### Jon 3:2

**Reading:** Three Qal imperatives, 2ms (`HVqv2ms`), address Jonah in sequence: Arise, go to Nineveh ("that great city"), call out against it. The message clause that follows — "the message that I tell you" — does not itself supply the message's words; H1696G ("tell") is a Qal **participle** (`HVqrmsa`), not a finite past/future verb.

**Flags:**
- **T3** — The participle on "tell" carries a durative, in-the-moment force: "the message that I am [in the act of] speaking to you," not "I will tell" or "I have told." This is the load-bearing morph correction for the verse — the English present-tense gloss happens to land close to this, but the grammatical basis is the participle, not tense choice.
- **T4** — No referent crux; "you" (2ms) is unambiguously Jonah throughout, matching the imperatives.
- **T5** — The commission (v2) defers the actual proclamation content to v4 ("Yet forty days…"). This gap between being commissioned and being given the words is a structural/genre feature (delayed disclosure), recorded so it is not read as an omission.

### Jon 3:3

**Reading:** Jonah arose and went to Nineveh (Qal wayyiqtol 3ms ×2), "according to the word of the LORD" — compliance with v2's commands. A disjunctive aside follows: "Now Nineveh was a city great to/for God [idiom], three days' journey [in extent]."

**Flags:**
- **T1/T2** — Row 8's surface gloss is "exceedingly," but its Strong's tag is `H0430G` (Elohim, "God") + `H9005` (lamed, "to/for") — not an intensifier lexeme. This is the Hebrew superlative idiom "great to/of God" (construct + lamed + Elohim), not a separate Hebrew adverb. H0430G's own lexical range is entirely "the Deity" entries — no intensifier sense exists in H0430G itself; the superlative force comes from the construction, not from a hidden sense of "God." Flagged so the row isn't read as if Elohim is functioning as an independent participant here.
- **T3** — "was" (`H1961`, `HVqp3fs`, Qal **perfect**, not wayyiqtol) breaks the wayyiqtol narrative chain that carries "arose"/"went" — this grammatical shift is what produces the English "Now…" as a background/circumstantial aside, not a further narrated event. Stated from the morph, not assumed from translation style.
- **T5** — The city-size aside ("three days' journey") is a stock scene-setting device; recorded because it sets up the scale problem the narrative exploits in v4 (a one-day proclamation inside a three-day city).

### Jon 3:4

**Reading:** Jonah began (Hiphil wayyiqtol 3ms) to go into the city, one day's journey [in]; he called out and said (two Qal wayyiqtol 3ms verbs functioning as one reporting unit, "called out, saying"): "Yet forty days, and Nineveh [is] overthrown."

**Flags:**
- **T2** — `H2490C` ("began") stepGloss doubles as "to profane/begin"; its full lexical range is dominated by profane/defile senses (Niphal/Piel/Pual). The "begin" sense is specifically the Hiphil entry (confirmed by morph `HVhw3ms`), stated directly for that stem — not an inference from context, and not connected to the profane-sense cluster that otherwise fills the entry.
- **T3** — "overthrown" (`H2015`, `HVNrfsa`) is a **Niphal passive participle**, feminine singular (agreeing with Nineveh) — not a finite future-tense verb. Per the technique doc's own caution on participles, this reads as a durative/imminent state ("[is on the verge of being] overthrown") rather than a flat prediction — a nuance the participle carries that a finite yiqtol "will be overthrown" would not.
- **T5** — The oracle itself (v4b) is bare and unconditional — five Hebrew words, no stated "unless you repent" clause. This absence is genre-relevant: a reader familiar with conditional judgment-oracle form might expect the condition stated here; it is not — the conditionality only becomes visible in v9 ("who knows") and is confirmed retroactively by v10. Recorded per T5 rather than passed over.

### Jon 3:5

**Reading:** The people of Nineveh (a collective, `H0582`) believed God (Hiphil wayyiqtol 3mp), called for a fast, and put on sackcloth (both Qal wayyiqtol 3mp) — "from their greatest to their least." Corporate, immediate, city-wide response; no individual (the king) yet named.

**Flags:**
- **T2** — `H0539` ("believed") stepGloss reads "be faithful," but the operative sense is the Hiphil-specific entry "to stand firm, to trust, to be certain, to believe in" (morph confirms `HVhw3mp`) — distinct from the Qal/Niphal "support, confirm, be established" senses that dominate the rest of the entry. Stated directly for this stem.
- **T2** — "God" here is `H0430G` (Elohim, bare plural noun form, `HNcmpa`) — not `H3068` (YHWH), which is the form used for the LORD's own speech in vv1 and 3. The text itself marks this shift (different Strong's codes) between the LORD who commissions and the generic "God" the Ninevites perceive/respond to — recorded as a lexical fact, not smoothed into a single referent.
- **T5** — "From the greatest to the least" is a stock merism marking totality of response (no exceptions); recorded as doing that structural work rather than read as incidental phrasing.

### Jon 3:6

**Reading:** The word reached the king of Nineveh (introduced here for the first time, `H4428G`). A tight chain of the king's own individual acts follows, all Qal/Hiphil wayyiqtol 3ms: arose from his throne, removed his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, sat in ashes.

**Flags:**
- **T2** — `H5674C` ("removed") stepGloss "to pass: bring"; full range dominated by "to pass over/through/by." The causative "bring/carry/take away" sense is specifically Hiphil (morph confirms `HVhw3ms`); with "his robe" as object, the operative sense is "caused [his robe] to pass off him" = "took off" — the lexicon's own stated causative sense for this stem, not a free translation.
- **T3** — "covered" (`H3680`, `HVqw3ms`) is **Qal active**, not a Niphal/Hithpael reflexive form, even though the entry lists reflexive senses ("cover oneself") elsewhere. The reflexive force here is carried by the prepositional chain ("upon him," `H5921A`+`H9033`) rather than by the verb stem — grammatically "he covered [sackcloth] upon himself," an active-transitive construction. This distinction matters for T3's cross-verse comparison — see v8's flag, where the population's "covered" is a different stem/mood entirely.
- **T4** — The king is never named anywhere in Jonah — a genuine open naming gap, but not a T4 referent crux (no competing candidates for who "he" is; every 3ms form in the verse points unambiguously to "the king"). Recorded as an observation, not resolved as if it were an ambiguity.
- **T5** — The four-part sequence (arise → remove robe → cover with sackcloth → sit in ashes) is a stock royal mourning/humiliation ritual formula; recorded so it is read as one conventional ritual unit, not four unrelated actions.

### Jon 3:7

**Reading:** He (the king, continued from v6) issued a proclamation and had it published throughout Nineveh: "By the decree of the king and his nobles/great-ones [lit. "his great-ones," built on the ordinary adjective "great," not a distinct noble-title lexeme]: let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste anything; let them not feed or drink water…"

**Flags:**
- **T1** — Row 6's surface "nobles" is literally `H1419A` ("great," the same root used for "great city" in vv2–3) + "and" — "his great-ones," not a separate title-word. Flagged so the term isn't read as an unrelated court-title lexeme.
- **T3** — "taste"/"feed"/"drink" are all jussive-form 3mp (`HVqj3mp`) — jussive mood, matching the decree's own imperatival force ("let them not…"), not simple future statements. This is a mood distinction from the morph, not just from the English "let."
- **T5** — Extending the fast/mourning to animals ("man and beast… herd nor flock") goes beyond a typical human-only fasting convention; recorded explicitly as the text's own marked excess (total civic mourning, human and animal alike) rather than passed over as incidental detail.

### Jon 3:8

**Reading:** [Decree continues] "…but let man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and let them call out mightily to God. Let everyone/each turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in their hands."

**Flags:**
- **T2** — `H0376I` ("everyone") stepGloss "man," but its lexical range explicitly includes "each (adjective)… whosoever" — the distributive "each one" sense used here is lexicon-stated directly, not inferred from the plural context. Flagged because the base gloss "man" alone would mislead toward a specific singular male rather than "each person," which the clause needs.
- **T3** — "covered" here is `HVtu3mp` — a distinct stem/mood from the king's own Qal active "covered [sackcloth upon] himself" in v6 (`HVqw3ms`). Here the decree commands the populace to *be* covered/cover-themselves (a jussive reflexive-passive), grammatically different from the king's individual active act even though both render as "covered … with sackcloth" in English. Flagged so the king's self-initiated act (v6) is not read as grammatically identical to the population's commanded one (v8).
- **T3** — Row 14 "his" (`H9028`) is morph-tagged `HSp3mp` — third person **masculine plural** ("their"), not singular — yet the printed ESV above the table reads "his hands" (singular). Per T3 (morph decides, not the English), the reading is "the violence that is in **their** hands," matching the distributive "everyone… each" already established two words earlier. Flagged as a direct correction to the surface translation, not a smoothing-over.
- **T4** — Not a referent crux (both "his way"/3ms suffix and "their hands"/3mp suffix plainly point to the same distributive populace), but an internal grammatical-number variation within one clause about the same subject — recorded rather than passed over silently.
- **T5** — "Turn from his evil way and from violence" pairs a general reorientation with one specific, named sin (violence/*hamas*) — both conventional elements of a repentance call; the specificity of "violence" is the text's own chosen concretization, recorded rather than read as generic phrasing.

### Jon 3:9

**Reading:** "Who knows? God may turn and relent, and turn from his burning anger, so that we do not perish." A three-verb chain (turn/relent/turn) describes an uncertain hoped-for divine action, followed by a 1cp purpose clause.

**Flags:**
- **T2** — `H2740` ("fierce [anger]") — the stepGloss itself states this term is "always used of God's anger," i.e., lexically restricted to divine anger, not a generic anger-word. Named explicitly per T2 rather than treated as an ordinary intensity term.
- **T3** — "knows" (`H3045`, `HVqrmsa`) is a Qal active participle ("who [is] knowing") — a stative/durative form matching the rhetorical-question idiom, not a punctual past/future. The two occurrences of "turn" (`H7725O`) carry two different morph tags in the row data — `HVqi3ms` (first) and `HVqq3ms` (second) — though both render "turn" in English; flagged as a form the source data itself distinguishes, recorded rather than treated as two identical repetitions, pending fuller morph-code confirmation. "Perish" (`H0006`, `HVqi1cp`) confirms the 1cp ("we") grammatical subject.
- **T4** — Referent crux on "we": the row data fixes the *person* (1cp) but not *who* is included. Three grammatically live readings: (a) the king speaking for himself and his court; (b) the king voicing the fate of the whole city, on behalf of the fast he has just decreed for everyone (vv7–8); (c) a general proverbial "we" (humanity at risk). Reading (b) is adopted here as primary, given the immediate context of a city-wide decree; (a) and (c) are kept on record as grammatically possible but contextually weaker — not eliminated, only not adopted.
- **T5** — "Who knows?" is a recognized OT idiom of hope offered without presumption of guaranteed mercy; recorded as doing exactly that conventional work here, not read as rhetorical filler.

### Jon 3:10

**Reading:** God saw their deed(s) — [lit. a noun phrase, "the deed/thing-they-did," not a finite verb clause in that row] — namely that they turned (Qal **perfect** 3cp, subordinate/background) from their evil way; God relented (Niphal wayyiqtol 3ms, main narrative line resumes) of the disaster that he had said (Piel perfect 3ms) he would do to them, and he did not do it (Qal perfect 3ms).

**Flags:**
- **T1** — Row 2's surface gloss "they did" reads as if a finite clause, but its actual Strong's/morph is `H4639K` (*ma'aseh*, "deed, work, thing done" — a noun, `HNcmpc`, construct) + `H0853` (definite object marker): literally "[he saw] the-deeds-of…," a construct noun phrase as the object of "saw," not an independent verb clause. The finite content-verb ("turned") is supplied by the *next* row. Flagged so the row isn't mistaken for a second finite clause.
- **T2** — `H7451` appears under two different sub-entries in this passage: `H7451I` ("evil," vv8–9, the ethical/moral sense — "evil, injury, wrong; evil (ethical)") vs. `H7451C` here ("disaster" — the harm/calamity sense, same base root). The lexical data itself preserves a deliberate wordplay: the Ninevites' moral "evil way" (turned from) and God's threatened "disaster" (relented of) share one root, tagged by two different sub-senses — named explicitly rather than flattened into one gloss.
- **T3** — "turned" (`H7725O`, `HVqp3cp`, **perfect**, not wayyiqtol) is grammatically a subordinate/background clause within the object of "saw" ("[the deeds] that they had turned…"), not a separate main-line event — matching the English "how they turned" as explanatory, not narrated in its own right. "Said" (`H1696G`, `HVpp3ms`) is **Piel perfect**, not Qal — the lexicon lists "(Piel) to speak; to promise" as a distinct sense from Qal's plainer "to speak, declare, converse"; this ties God's earlier oracle (v4) to a formally spoken/declared register, stated from the stem, not assumed.
- **T4** — "them" (`H9038`, 3mp, row 13) is unambiguous — the anaphoric chain runs back to "the people of Nineveh" (v5) via "they turned" here; no live crux.
- **T5** — This verse is the resolution-formula of an averted-judgment oracle ("God saw… God relented… he did not do it") — a recognized structural close. Recorded as closing the repentance-oracle unit opened at v4, and as the verse that retroactively confirms v4's oracle was implicitly conditional even though v4 itself stated no condition (per the T5 flag there) — the conditionality is demonstrated narratively here, not stated propositionally anywhere in the passage.

## T6–T9 — Inner-being relevance stamps

Applied only after T1–T5 were complete for the whole passage (vv1–10), per the technique doc's phase gating. Stamps are per the researcher's own clarifications recorded in the technique doc/session log: *IB* = human words only; non-human agents (LORD/God) are stamped *Agent*, not *IB*; a word can carry multiple stamps; stamping is preliminary and does not determine which *Agent* affects which *IB* via which *Process*.

**Two scoping calls made in applying T6–T9, flagged here rather than silently decided (see Self-checking below for full discussion):**
1. Where a human subject is encoded only through pro-drop verb morphology (person/number on the verb, no separate pronoun row) — e.g. "we" in v9's `perish`, "they" in v10's `turned` — this pass does **not** add an *IB* stamp to that verb row; only rows whose own surface lexeme is a human noun, human proper name, or a free-standing pronoun row are stamped *IB*. The verb still receives *action*.
2. *Process* (T8, "state/condition/faculty words tied to an IB") is applied here to physical/ritual objects that mark the IB's own bodily state or condition (garment worn/removed, posture, location sat in, body part) — e.g. throne, robe, sackcloth, ashes, hands, way, violence, disaster (as a threatened fate) — but **not** to communicative content/objects produced by an IB (message, decree, proclamation, deed). This is a broad reading of T8; both scoping calls are judgment calls, not settled by the doc's text alone.

## Self-checking

Confirmed T1–T9 received due consideration for all ten verses. Two items are surfaced explicitly rather than silently resolved, per T4's own discipline:

1. **Pro-drop subject / IB-stamp scope (open methodological question).** T6 says to stamp every *word* that explicitly points to a human being. Hebrew is pro-drop: several finite verbs carry their human subject only via person/number morphology (no separate pronoun row) — e.g. v9 `perish` (1cp = "we"), v10 `turned` (3cp = "they"). The technique doc does not say whether such a verb row itself qualifies as an "IB word" in addition to its *action* stamp. This pass adopted the narrower reading (surface-lexeme-driven: only human nouns/names/free-standing pronoun rows get *IB*) for consistency, and applied it throughout — but this is a judgment call the doc doesn't settle, not a rule read off it. Flagging for confirmation rather than treating it as decided.
2. **Broadened *Process* scope (judgment call).** T8's own text ("relates to any IB… includes state/condition/faculty words") is broad enough to cover physical/ritual objects marking an IB's bodily state (sackcloth, ashes, throne, robe, hands) and a threatened fate-noun (disaster), which this pass stamped *Process*; it excluded spoken/decreed content-objects (message, decree, proclamation, deed) from *Process* on the reasoning that those are produced *by* an IB rather than being a condition *of* one. Both halves of that line are this pass's own reading of a genuinely broad instruction, not confirmed by the researcher — flagged rather than presented as settled.
3. **T4 referent crux** (v9 "we") — three readings enumerated, one adopted with reasons given, two kept on record (see Jon 3:9 above).
4. **T3 morph-vs-translation divergence** actively caught and corrected, not smoothed: v3's "exceedingly" (idiom, not a lexeme), v4's participle "overthrown" (durative/imminent, not flat future), v8's "their hands" vs. the ESV's "his hands," v8's distinct covering-stem vs. v6's, v10's "they did" (a noun phrase, not a verb clause).
5. **T5 genre-conventional elements** recorded for all ten verses (formula repetition, deferred proclamation content, scene-setting aside, oracle brevity/no stated condition, merism, mourning-ritual sequence, fast extended to animals, repentance-call components, the "who knows" idiom, and the averted-judgment resolution formula that retroactively confirms v4's implicit conditionality) — none passed over silently.

This check covers the intelligent reading of the verses themselves (T1–T9 compliance); it is not a check on any phenomenon or movement judgment, which is a separate later step's output.
