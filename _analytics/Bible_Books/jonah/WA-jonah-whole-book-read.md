## Closing synthesis

**What recurred across the whole book.** Four threads run the full length of Jonah, confirmed only by reading all four chapters together: (1) **the LORD's/God's explicit agency brackets every chapter without exception** (1:1-2/1:4/1:17 → 2:10 → 3:1-2/3:10 → 4:6-8/4:10-11) — the single most consistent structural spine in the book; (2) **a whole-book alternation of whose interior is given voice** — chs. 1 and 3 foreground outsiders (the sailors; Nineveh) while Jonah stays silent in his own action, chs. 2 and 4 foreground Jonah's own extended speech; (3) **every human party who directly encounters God's power responds with ritual/cultic action** — the sailors (1:16), Jonah (2:9), and Nineveh (3:5-8), a three-instance pattern; (4) **Q12 (divine mirroring) surfaced three separate, textually-anchored instances**, each via a different shared root — "turn" (3:8-10), "anger" (4:1-2/4:9), "pity" (4:10-11) — confirming this is a genuine, deliberate feature of Jonah's own composition, not an imported reading.

**What never resolved.** Three questions the book itself never answers, at any point: who calms the sea at 1:15 (contrast the explicit agency at 1:4); whether the lot at 1:7 is divinely directed or left to chance; and whether Jonah's grievance by ch. 4's end is really about Nineveh's sparing, his own lost comfort, or both entangled (4:2-3 vs. 4:8-9 give different stated causes for the identical death-wish, and the text never adjudicates between them).

**What's left genuinely open past the last chapter.** The book's own final word (4:11) is an unanswered question to Jonah, and by extension to the reader — no repentance, resolution, or change in him is narrated. This instrument does not supply one either, per Part B.1: the silence is recorded as the book's own chosen ending, not a gap to be filled. Two further items are explicitly deferred beyond this single-book read: whether Q12's shared-vocabulary mirroring recurs as densely in other books of this corpus (EQ15), and whether that eventual cross-book comparison will change how Jonah's own three instances (turn/anger/pity) should be weighted relative to the rest of the corpus.
