## JOEL-locust-lament · Joel 1:2–20 · [D, multi] — devastation as a summons to lament; the whole creature turned toward God

**The reading.** A locust plague has stripped the land bare — "**what the cutting locust left, the swarming locust has eaten**" (v.4) — and with the crops goes every comfort: "**gladness dries up from the children of man**" (v.12). The prophet turns the catastrophe into a summons: "**Consecrate a fast; call a solemn assembly… and cry out to the LORD**" (v.14). And it ends in a cry that draws in even the animals: "**To you, O LORD, I call… Even the beasts of the field pant for you because the water brooks are dried up**" (vv.19–20). The finding is **catastrophe *stripping every comfort* ("gladness dries up") and thereby *driving the creature to cry to God* — "to you, O LORD, I call"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's *dependence exposed* by loss: when the props of gladness are stripped (harvest, wine, oil, joy), the self is turned *toward God* as its only remaining recourse — the emptying becomes a summons, and even the dumb beasts "pant" Godward, as though creation's instinct in extremity is to look up** *(inferred: the movement of the chapter is from *devastation* to *God-ward cry* — the locust has taken not just food but "**gladness**" (v.12, the whole ground of human cheer), and the fitting response is not despair but *lament directed to God* ("cry out to the LORD", v.14; "to you, O LORD, I call", v.19); the finding is that stripped comfort can *turn the interior toward God* — loss exposing the dependence that plenty concealed; and the closing image is striking — "**even the beasts of the field pant for you**" (v.20): the finding is that the instinct of the creature in extremity is *upward* — when the brooks dry, the beasts pant Godward, a picture of the interior's deepest reflex under loss being to seek its Maker; the catastrophe is thus read not merely as judgment but as a *call to return*, the emptying that makes room for the cry)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *loss exposing dependence (stripped comfort turning the creature toward God — "gladness dries up… to you, O LORD, I call")* (thread — a gem: catastrophe driving the interior to its only recourse, w/ 1:12-19, Ps 42:1, Hos 5:15); *lament directed to God (catastrophe turned into a God-ward cry, not despair)* (thread — w/ 1:14,19, Lam 2:19); *the creature's upward reflex in extremity ("even the beasts pant for you" — the instinct under loss is to seek the Maker)* (new — a gem: creation's Godward reflex when the brooks dry, w/ 1:20, Ps 104:21,27).
