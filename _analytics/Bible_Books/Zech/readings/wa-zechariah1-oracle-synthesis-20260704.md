## ZECH-return-jealous · Zechariah 1:1–21 · [D, multi] — return and I will return; God's jealous mercy toward Zion

**The reading.** The book opens with a reciprocal summons: "**Return to me, says the LORD of hosts, and I will return to you**" (v.3). The fathers "**did not hear or pay attention**" (v.4), yet in the end "**they repented**" (v.6). In the night vision the angel pleads, "**O LORD of hosts, how long will you have no mercy on Jerusalem?**" (v.12), and God answers "**gracious and comforting words**" (v.13): "**I am exceedingly jealous for Jerusalem… I have returned to Jerusalem with mercy**" (vv.14,16). The finding is **the *reciprocal turn* — "return to me, and I will return to you" — grounded in a God "exceedingly jealous" for his people, who returns "with mercy"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's return met, step for step, by God's return — repentance not a lonely reaching toward a distant God but a movement *answered* ("I will return to you") — and the whole framed by God's *jealous love* (a possessive, ardent care for his own) that longs to show mercy** *(inferred: v.3's "**return to me, and I will return to you**" is the inner-being key — the finding is that repentance is *reciprocal*: the turning of the heart toward God is met by God's turning toward it, so the movement is not a desperate effort to reach an aloof deity but a step *into* a relationship God is eager to resume; and the ground is God's *jealousy* — "**I am exceedingly jealous for Jerusalem**" (v.14): the finding is that God's stance toward his people is not indifference but an ardent, possessive love (qana — jealous zeal), which is precisely why he "returns with mercy"; the "how long?" of the angel (v.12) voices the waiting heart's ache, and it is answered with "gracious and comforting words" — the finding is that the God who summons the return is himself *straining toward* his people, jealous for them and quick with mercy, so the invitation "return to me" rests on a love already reaching back)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the reciprocal turn ("return to me, and I will return to you" — repentance met step for step by God's return)* (thread — a gem: turning to God answered by God's turning, not a lonely reaching, w/ 1:3, Mal 3:7, Jas 4:8, Luke 15:20); *God's jealous love for his people ("I am exceedingly jealous for Jerusalem… I have returned with mercy")* (thread — a gem: ardent possessive care as the ground of mercy, w/ 1:14-16, 8:2, Exod 34:14); *the ache of "how long?" answered by comforting words (the waiting heart met with grace)* (new — w/ 1:12-13, Ps 13, Hab 2:1).
