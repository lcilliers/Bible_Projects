## ZECH-pierced-mourning · Zechariah 12:1–14 · [D, multi] — the spirit of grace and mercy; the mourning look on the pierced one

**The reading.** The oracle opens by naming God as the one "**who stretched out the heavens and founded the earth and formed the spirit of man within him**" (v.1). After promising to defend Jerusalem, God discloses an interior work: "**I will pour out on the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy, so that, when they look on me, on him whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for an only child, and weep bitterly over him, as one weeps over a firstborn**" (v.10). The land mourns "**each family by itself**" (v.12). The finding is **a *God-given "spirit of grace and pleas for mercy"* producing a *mourning look* upon "**him whom they have pierced**" — repentance as *grief* over the one their sin wounded** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure that true repentance is itself *God-given* ("I will **pour out** a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy") — not self-generated remorse but a gifted disposition — and that its form is a *look* that becomes *mourning*: gazing on "him whom they have pierced" and grieving "as for an only child", the interior broken open by seeing what its sin has done to the one it wounded** *(inferred: v.10 is the chapter's inner-being heart, and it holds two profound findings. First, repentance is *God-given* — "**I will POUR OUT… a SPIRIT OF GRACE and pleas for mercy**": the finding is that the very capacity to mourn one's sin rightly is a *gift poured out by God* (the same outpouring language as the Spirit of Joel 2:28, Zech 4:6) — genuine contrition is not manufactured by the will but *granted*, a "spirit of grace" that produces "pleas for mercy"; the interior does not work itself up to repentance but *receives* the disposition to repent. Second, the form of this repentance is a *grieving look* — "**when they LOOK ON… him whom they have PIERCED, they shall MOURN**": the finding is that the deepest repentance is not fear of punishment but *grief over the wounded one* — seeing "him whom they have pierced" (the one their sin has hurt) and weeping "as one mourns for an only child" (the most desolating human grief); repentance here is *relational sorrow* — not "I will be punished" but "look what I have done to him" — the heart broken by the sight of the beloved it has wounded; and God identifies himself *with* the pierced one ("they look on ME, on him whom they have pierced") — the finding is that sin's wounding reaches *God himself*, and the healing of the interior comes through the anguished gaze upon the one it pierced)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *repentance as a God-given gift ("I will pour out a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy" — genuine contrition granted, not self-manufactured)* (thread — a gem: the capacity to mourn one's sin rightly is poured out by God, w/ 12:10, Joel 2:28, Acts 5:31, 2 Tim 2:25); *repentance as a grieving look on the pierced one ("when they look on him whom they have pierced, they shall mourn as for an only child")* (new — a gem: the deepest repentance is relational sorrow over the wounded one, not fear of punishment, w/ 12:10, John 19:37, Ps 51:4); *sin's wounding reaching God himself ("they look on me, on him whom they have pierced")* (new — a gem: the pierced one is God; sin wounds God, and healing comes through the anguished gaze, w/ 12:10, 63:9-10); *mourning "as for an only child" (the desolating depth of true contrition)* (thread — w/ 12:10, Amos 8:10).
