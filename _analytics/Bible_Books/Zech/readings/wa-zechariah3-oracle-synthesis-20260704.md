## ZECH-filthy-garments · Zechariah 3:1–10 · [D, multi] — the accused sinner cleansed; iniquity removed and pure vestments given

**The reading.** A courtroom scene: "**Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD, and Satan standing at his right hand to accuse him**" (v.1). God silences the accuser: "**The LORD rebuke you, O Satan!… Is not this a brand plucked from the fire?**" (v.2). Joshua stands "**clothed with filthy garments**" (v.3), and the word comes: "**Remove the filthy garments from him… Behold, I have taken your iniquity away from you, and I will clothe you with pure vestments**" (v.4); a "**clean turban**" is set on his head (v.5). And the promise: "**I will remove the iniquity of this land in a single day**" (v.9). The finding is **the *accused, filthy-clad* sinner *cleansed by God's initiative* — the accuser rebuked, the "filthy garments" (his guilt) *removed*, and *pure vestments* freely put on him** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior stained with guilt ("filthy garments"), *accused* and defenceless before the accuser, met not by its own self-justification but by *God's act* — the removal of the iniquity and the clothing in what it could never provide — so that acquittal and cleansing are *done to* the sinner, not achieved by him** *(inferred: the scene is a drama of the guilty interior — Joshua stands "**clothed with filthy garments**" (v.3, the visible shame of his iniquity) while "Satan" (the accuser) presses the case against him (v.1); the finding is that the guilty self has *no defence of its own* — it stands filthy and silent while accused; but the verdict comes from *outside* — "**the LORD REBUKE you, O Satan**" (v.2): the accuser is silenced not by the sinner's merit but by God's choice ("the LORD who has chosen Jerusalem"); and the cleansing is entirely *God's initiative and act* — "**I have TAKEN your iniquity away… I WILL CLOTHE you**" (v.4, both verbs God's, done *to* Joshua): the finding is that the removal of guilt and the gift of a new standing ("pure vestments", "clean turban") are *received, not earned* — the interior contributes only its filth; the "**brand plucked from the fire**" (v.2) images the self as barely-rescued, unworthy, yet claimed; and the whole is the pattern of grace toward the accused conscience — the guilty, silenced sinner re-clothed by God, its iniquity removed "in a single day" (v.9))*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the guilty self defenceless before the accuser (Joshua "clothed with filthy garments", Satan accusing — the interior with no defence of its own)* (thread — a gem: the accused conscience standing filthy and silent, w/ 3:1-3, Rom 3:19, Rev 12:10); *the accuser silenced by God's choice, not the sinner's merit ("the LORD rebuke you, O Satan… who has chosen Jerusalem")* (new — a gem: acquittal grounded in God's election, not self-justification, w/ 3:2, Rom 8:33-34); *iniquity removed and pure vestments given by God's act ("I have taken your iniquity away… I will clothe you" — cleansing done to the sinner, not achieved by him)* (thread — a gem: guilt removed and new standing received, not earned, w/ 3:4-5, Isa 61:10, Luke 15:22); *the brand plucked from the fire (the self barely-rescued, unworthy, yet claimed)* (new — w/ 3:2, Amos 4:11, Jude 23).
