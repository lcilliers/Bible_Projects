## ZECH-fasting-for-self · Zechariah 7:1–14 · [D, multi] — religion for self vs for God; the diamond-hard heart

**The reading.** Asked whether they should keep mourning-fasts, God probes the *motive*: "**When you fasted and mourned… for these seventy years, was it for me that you fasted? And when you eat and when you drink, do you not eat for yourselves and drink for yourselves?**" (vv.5–6). The true worship the "former prophets" had urged is recalled: "**Render true judgments, show kindness and mercy to one another, do not oppress the widow, the fatherless, the sojourner, or the poor, and let none of you devise evil against another in your heart**" (vv.9–10). But "**they refused to pay attention and turned a stubborn shoulder… They made their hearts diamond-hard lest they should hear**" (vv.11–12). The finding is **religion done *"for yourselves"* rather than *"for me"* — self-referential worship — exposed against true religion (*mercy, justice, no evil devised in the heart*), and the *self-hardened heart* that refuses to hear** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interrogation of *motive*: fasting and feasting alike can be *self-directed* ("for yourselves"), a religion that revolves around the worshipper rather than God — and the true worship God seeks is *mercy and justice toward the vulnerable* plus a heart that *does not devise evil*; and the reason the word does not land is a *deliberately hardened heart* ("diamond-hard… lest they should hear")** *(inferred: v.5's question "**was it FOR ME that you fasted?**" cuts to the inner-being root — the finding is that religious acts (fasting, mourning, even feasting) can be *self-referential*, performed "for yourselves" — for one's own sense of piety, tradition, or comfort — rather than genuinely *for God*; the same act (abstaining or eating) is worship or self-service depending on its *Godward orientation*; and the true worship recalled (vv.9–10) is strikingly *ethical and interior* — "kindness and mercy… do not oppress… **do not devise evil in your heart**": the finding is that God measures worship by *mercy done* and by the *heart's inner intentions* (not devising evil), not by ritual observance; and the tragic obstacle is *self-hardening* — "**they made their hearts DIAMOND-HARD** (shamir — adamant/flint) lest they should hear" (v.12): the finding is that the heart can *deliberately* render itself impervious to God's word — hardness here is not passive dullness but an *active self-armouring against hearing*, "lest they should hear"; and the recoil is exact — "as I called and they would not hear, so they called and I would not hear" (v.13): the self-hardened heart eventually meets a God who answers its deafness with silence)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *worship for self vs for God ("was it for me that you fasted? do you not eat for yourselves?" — religious acts as self-referential vs Godward)* (thread — a gem: the same act worship or self-service by its orientation, w/ 7:5-6, Isa 58:3, Matt 6:1-18); *true worship as mercy and inner integrity ("show kindness and mercy… do not devise evil in your heart" — worship measured by mercy done and the heart's intentions)* (thread — a gem, w/ 7:9-10, Mic 6:8, Amos 5:24, Zech 8:16-17); *the self-hardened heart ("they made their hearts diamond-hard lest they should hear" — active self-armouring against God's word)* (new — a gem: hardness as deliberate imperviousness, not passive dullness, w/ 7:11-12, Heb 3:15, Acts 7:51); *the recoil of deafness ("as I called and they would not hear, so they called and I would not hear")* (thread — w/ 7:13, Pro 1:28).
