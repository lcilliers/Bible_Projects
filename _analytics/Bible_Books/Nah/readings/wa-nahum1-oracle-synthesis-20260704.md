## NAHUM-good-refuge-avenging · Nahum 1:1–15 · [D, multi] — God avenging to his adversaries yet a stronghold to those who take refuge

**The reading.** The book opens with a fierce portrait of God's character: "**The LORD is a jealous and avenging God… slow to anger and great in power, and the LORD will by no means clear the guilty**" (vv.2–3). His coming shakes creation — "**Who can stand before his indignation? Who can endure the heat of his anger?**" (v.6). Then, at the centre, the pivot: "**The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble; he knows those who take refuge in him**" (v.7). It ends with good news: "**Behold, upon the mountains, the feet of him who brings good news, who publishes peace!**" (v.15). The finding is **the *same* God who is *avenging* and unendurable to his adversaries ("who can stand?") is *"good, a stronghold… who knows those who take refuge in him"*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the two faces of one God held together: to the "adversaries" his power is *terror* ("the heat of his anger… who can stand?"), to those who "take refuge in him" the *same* power is *shelter* ("a stronghold in the day of trouble") — the difference is not in God but in the interior's *relationship* to him; and even his vengeance is bounded by "slow to anger", his character not capricious but measured** *(inferred: the chapter deliberately places terror and refuge side by side — "who can endure the heat of his anger?" (v.6) immediately followed by "**the LORD is GOOD, a STRONGHOLD… he knows those who take refuge in him**" (v.7): the finding is that God's overwhelming power is experienced oppositely by two interiors — as *unendurable wrath* by those set against him, as *reliable shelter* by those who "take refuge" (chasah — flee to him for protection); the same day of the LORD is terror or refuge according to *whose one is* (as in Joel 3:16, Isa 8:14); and the tender phrase "**he KNOWS those who take refuge in him**" (v.7) discloses that the fearsome God is *personally attentive* to those who trust — his knowing is intimate, not merely cognitive; and his avenging is framed by "**slow to anger**" (v.3, the Exod 34:6 self-revelation) — the finding is that even God's wrath is *patient* and *measured*, not the caprice of a tyrant, so that his power is safe for those who shelter under it precisely because his character is good)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the same power terror or refuge by relationship ("who can stand before his indignation?" vs "a stronghold… he knows those who take refuge in him")* (thread — a gem: God's power experienced oppositely by two interiors, difference in the heart not in God, w/ 1:6-7, Joel 3:16, Isa 8:14); *the fearsome God personally attentive to those who trust ("he knows those who take refuge in him")* (new — a gem: intimate knowing, not merely cognitive, of the sheltering interior, w/ 1:7, Ps 1:6, John 10:14); *wrath that is patient and measured ("slow to anger and great in power" — vengeance bounded by good character, not caprice)* (thread — w/ 1:3, Exod 34:6, making his power safe for those who shelter).
