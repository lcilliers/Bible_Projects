## JOS-14-blessing-ephraim-manasseh · Genesis 48:1–22 · [the younger over the elder — now knowingly] — the dim-eyed patriarch, the crossed hands, and "I know, my son, I know"

**The passage.** Jacob summoned his **strength** (chazaq) (48:2); "God Almighty **blessed** (barakh) me at Luz" (48:3); "'make you fruitful and **multiply** (ravah)… **give** (natan) this land'" (48:4); "bring them that I may **bless** (barakh) them… sons God has **given** (natan)" (48:9); his eyes **dim** (kaved), he **could** (yakhol) not see (48:10); he **blessed** (barakh) Joseph (48:15); "the angel who redeemed me… **bless** (barakh) the boys… let them grow into a **multitude** (rov)" (48:16); Joseph, **displeased** (raa), tried to **move** (sur) the hand (48:17); "I **know** (yada), my son… the younger shall be **greater** (gadal)" (48:19); he **blessed** (barakh) them (48:20); "I have **given** (natan) you one mountain slope" (48:22).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **chazaq (strength) — the dying man *rallies* (48:2).** "Israel summoned his **strength** (chazaq) and sat up in bed" *(stated)* — chazaq, *strengthen*: the gem is the *dying father rallying* to bless — the interior *mustering its last strength for the blessing* (← the revived spirit 45:27); the blessing *worth the effort of the failing body* *(inferred: chazaq = the dying man rallying to bless)*.
- **barakh (blessed) — the *foundational blessing recalled* (48:3).** "'God Almighty appeared to me at Luz and **blessed** (barakh) me'" *(stated)* — barakh, *bless*: Jacob *grounds the coming blessing in God's own blessing to him* (at Bethel/Luz, 28; 35) — the interior *passing on what it received* *(inferred: barakh = the God-given blessing recalled as the ground)*.
- **ravah (multiply) + natan (give) — the *Abrahamic promise* rehearsed (48:4).** "'make you fruitful and **multiply** (ravah)… **give** (natan) this land for an everlasting possession'" *(stated)*. **ravah** — *multiply*; **natan** — the *land given*: the *covenant promise* (increase + land, ← 12:2; 17:8) — the interior *rehearsing the promise it hands on* *(inferred: ravah/natan = the covenant increase and land promise rehearsed)*.
- **barakh (bless) + natan (given) — the *intent to bless the God-given sons* (48:9).** *barakh/natan distinct:* "'sons God has **given** (natan) me'… 'bring them that I may **bless** (barakh) them'" *(stated)*. **natan** — the sons *God's gift*; **barakh** — the *intent* to bless: Joseph names his sons *God's gift* (← Ephraim/Manasseh's naming 41:51–52), and Jacob *purposes to bless them* *(inferred: barakh/natan = the God-given sons, the intent to bless)*.
- **kaved (dim) + yakhol (could not) — the *dim eyes* — the ch-27 echo (48:10).** "the eyes of Israel were **dim** (kaved) with age, he **could** (yakhol) not see" *(stated)*. **kaved** — *heavy/dull*; **yakhol** — *could not*: the gem is the *echo* — **Jacob's dim eyes replay Isaac's** ("his eyes were dim so he could not see", 27:1) — the *blind-father-blessing scene returns*; but where *blind Isaac was **deceived*** into blessing the younger, *dim-eyed Jacob will bless the younger **knowingly** (48:19)*. The interior *at the same threshold as the deception it once worked* — now *reversed* *(inferred: kaved/yakhol = the dim eyes echoing Isaac — the blind-blessing scene, now to be done knowingly)*.
- **barakh (blessed) — the *blessing on Joseph* through the God of the fathers (48:15).** *barakh distinct from 48:3,9:* "he **blessed** (barakh) Joseph: 'the God before whom my fathers walked, the God who has been my **shepherd** all my life'" *(stated)* — the gem: Jacob names God his ***shepherd all his life*** (the mature faith of a life *now seen as shepherded* — the grasping, grieving pilgrim *looking back on a life kept*); the interior *confessing God's lifelong faithfulness* as it blesses *(inferred: barakh = the blessing grounded in God the lifelong shepherd)*.
- **rov (multitude) + barakh (bless) — the *redeemer invoked* (48:16).** *barakh distinct:* "'the angel who **redeemed** me from all **evil**, **bless** (barakh) the boys… let them grow into a **multitude** (rov)'" *(stated)*. **barakh** — the *invocation*; **rov** — *abundance/multitude*: the gem is *"the angel who redeemed me from all evil"* — Jacob names God his ***redeemer*** (the first "redeem" of a person in Scripture) — the interior *reading its whole hard life as redeemed from evil*; and asks the boys *grow into a multitude* *(inferred: rov/barakh = the redeemer-God invoked to bless the boys into a multitude)*.
- **raa (displeased) + sur (move) — Joseph's *correction attempt* (48:17).** "it **displeased** (raa) Joseph… he took his father's hand to **move** (sur) it to Manasseh" *(stated)*. **raa** — *be evil/bad* (in his eyes); **sur** — *remove/move*: the gem is *Joseph's expectation of order* — the *firstborn* should get the right hand; he *tries to correct* the crossed hands. The interior *protecting precedence* — natural expectation vs the deliberate inversion *(inferred: raa/sur = Joseph's displeasure and correction of the crossed hands — the expectation of order)*.
- **yada (know) + gadal (greater) — the *deliberate, knowing inversion* (48:19).** "'I **know** (yada), my son, I **know**… the younger shall be **greater** (gadal)'" *(stated)*. The **central gem**: **yada** — *"I know"* (doubled) — Jacob blesses the younger *knowingly and deliberately* — the *opposite of blind Isaac*, who was *deceived* into it (27); **gadal** — the *younger greater*: the *family pattern* (younger over elder — Isaac/Ishmael, Jacob/Esau, now Ephraim/Manasseh) now *consciously enacted* by the man who *stole* the elder's blessing by deceiving his blind father. Grace *running against precedence*, at last *done in the open*, *knowing* *(inferred: yada/gadal = the deliberate, knowing inversion — the younger blessed greater, the deception of ch27 reversed)*.
- **barakh (blessed) — the *naming-blessing* (48:20).** *barakh distinct:* "he **blessed** (barakh) them: 'by you Israel will bless — God make you as Ephraim and Manasseh'" *(stated)* — the gem: the boys become the *paradigm of blessing* for Israel — the interior *making them the model blessing* (Ephraim *before* Manasseh — the inversion *fixed*) *(inferred: barakh = the naming-blessing — the boys made Israel's blessing-paradigm)*.
- **natan (given) — the *extra portion* to Joseph (48:22).** *natan distinct from 48:4,9:* "'I have **given** (natan) you one mountain slope, rather than your brothers'" *(stated)* — natan, *give*: the gem is the *double-portion to Joseph* (the firstborn's share, via his two sons counted as tribes) — the interior *granting Joseph the firstborn's inheritance* (the favoured son honoured) *(inferred: natan = the extra portion — Joseph given the firstborn's double share)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **the dying father rallies to bless (chazaq 48:2 → barakh 48:3 → ravah/natan 48:4 → barakh/natan 48:9):** Jacob *musters his strength*, *grounds the blessing in God's blessing to him*, *rehearses the covenant promise*, and *purposes to bless the God-given sons*.
2. **the dim eyes — the ch-27 scene returns (kaved/yakhol 48:10 → barakh 48:15 → rov/barakh 48:16):** *dim-eyed* (as blind Isaac), Jacob *blesses* — naming God his *lifelong shepherd* and *redeemer from all evil* — his *mature faith* looking back on a life *kept*.
3. **the knowing inversion (raa/sur 48:17 → yada/gadal 48:19 → barakh 48:20):** Joseph, *displeased*, *tries to correct* the crossed hands; but Jacob *"I know, my son, I know"* — *deliberately* blesses the younger greater — the *deception of ch27 reversed* (knowing, not deceived); the boys made *Israel's blessing-paradigm*.
4. **the extra portion and the land (natan 48:22):** Joseph *given the firstborn's double share* — and (48:21) *"God will bring you to the land of your fathers"* — the *pull to the land* held to the end.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** chazaq (the dying man *rallying* to bless); barakh ×5 (God's blessing *recalled* / the *intent* / the blessing *on Joseph* — God the shepherd / the *redeemer invoked* / the *naming-blessing*); ravah/natan (the *covenant promise* rehearsed); natan ×3 (the land *given* / the sons *God's gift* / the *extra portion* to Joseph); **kaved/yakhol (the *dim eyes* echoing blind Isaac — the ch-27 scene returns)**; rov (the boys grown to a *multitude*); raa/sur (Joseph's *displeasure* and correction — the expectation of order); **yada/gadal (the *deliberate, knowing inversion* — "I know, my son" — the younger greater, ch27 reversed)**.
- **Whose interior (multi-interior):** **Jacob** — the *dying faith* (God his lifelong *shepherd* and *redeemer from all evil* — the mature reckoning of a hard life *kept*) and the *deliberate, knowing inversion* (younger over elder, done in the open); **Joseph** — the *expectation of order* (displeased at the crossed hands); **God** — the *shepherd, redeemer, blesser*. The knowing inversion is the pivot.
- **Threads:** **the younger-over-elder pattern, now knowing** (yada/gadal 48:19 ← the oracle 25:23; Jacob's *stolen* blessing 27 — the man who *deceived* his blind father now *deliberately* blesses the younger; the deception *reversed*); the **dim-eyed father** (kaved 48:10 ← Isaac 27:1 — the blind-blessing scene *replayed and inverted*); **God as shepherd/redeemer** (barakh 48:15–16 — Jacob's *mature faith*, the first personal "redeem"); the **covenant promise** (ravah/natan 48:4 ← 17:8; 28:3); the **pull to the land** (48:21 ← 47:29 — "God will bring you to the land"); the **favoured Joseph** honoured (natan 48:22 — the double portion).
- **Guarded against:** lumping the five barakh or three natan (each a distinct beat); missing the **ch-27 echo** (kaved 48:10 ← 27:1) and its *reversal* (the deliberate, *knowing* blessing of the younger — not the deceived one); reading the inversion as arbitrary when Jacob names it *knowing* ("I know, my son") — grace against precedence *consciously* enacted; over-reading "the angel who redeemed me" (48:16) into later angelology/Christology over the *redeemer-God* Jacob confesses; treating Joseph's displeasure (48:17) as faithlessness when it is the *natural expectation of the firstborn's precedence* (overruled by the father's knowing choice).
