## JAC-12-flight · Genesis 31:1–21 · [the grievance, the God-reading of the wealth, and the stealthy departure] — the sons' envy, Jacob's case to his wives, and the flight by deception

**The passage.** Jacob **heard** (shama) Laban's sons — "he has taken all our father's, gained all this **wealth** (kavod)" (31:1); Laban's face changed; the LORD says return; Jacob **called** (qara) Rachel and Leah to the field (31:4); "you **know** (yada) I **served** (avad) your father with all my **strength** (koach)" (31:6); "he cheated me… but God did not permit him to **harm** (ra) me" (31:7); "God has taken your father's livestock and **given** (natan) them to me" (31:9); the dream of the angel of Bethel; the wives — "are we not **regarded** (chashav) as foreigners? he sold us" (31:15); "all the **wealth** (osher) God took is ours… do what God said" (31:16); Jacob fled, "**tricked** (lev) Laban" by not telling him (31:20).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **shama (heard) — the *resentment overheard* (31:1a).** "Jacob **heard** (shama) that Laban's sons were saying…" *(stated)* — shama, *hear*: the interior *catches the turning mood* — as he once was warned of Esau's threat by report (27:42), now he *hears the brothers-in-law's grievance*; the *trigger* of the departure *(inferred: shama = the resentment overheard, prompting flight)*.
- **kavod (wealth) — the gain seen by others as *their loss* (31:1b).** "he has gained all this **wealth** (kavod)" *(stated)* — kavod, *weight / glory / wealth*: the sons name Jacob's prosperity as *heavy gain stripped from their father*; the interior of the *envious* (the grasped wealth *provoking* resentment, as it did around Isaac, 26:14) *(inferred: kavod = the weighty gain that provokes the sons' envy)*.
- **qara (called) — the *private summons* for the decision (31:4).** "Jacob sent and **called** (qara) Rachel and Leah to the field" *(stated)* — qara, *call-to*: the interior *gathers his wives privately* (in the field, away from Laban) for the momentous choice — a *deliberate, secret* consultation; the departure planned in confidence *(inferred: qara = the private summons to decide the flight)*.
- **yada (know) + avad (served) + koach (strength) — the *wholehearted labour* pleaded (31:6).** "you **know** (yada) I have **served** (avad) your father with all my **strength** (koach)" *(stated)*. **yada** — the wives' *knowledge* invoked; **avad** — the *service*; **koach** — *with all my strength*: the gem is the *wholeheartedness* — Jacob pleads he *gave his full power* to the labour; the interior *asserting its unstinting service* against the charge of gain-by-theft *(inferred: yada/avad/koach = the full-strength service pleaded)*.
- **ra (harm) — the *evil intent restrained by God* (31:7).** "he cheated me and changed my wages ten times, but God did not permit him to **harm** (ra) me" *(stated)* — ra, *be evil / do harm*: the interior *reads a providence* — Laban *meant evil* (the ten changes) but *God restrained it*; the same *God-restrains-the-harmer* motif as Abimelech (20:6). Jacob *credits God's guarding* over his own cunning *(inferred: ra = Laban's evil intent, restrained by God)*.
- **natan (given) — the *cunning re-read as God's gift* (31:9).** "God has taken your father's livestock and **given** (natan) them to me" *(stated)* — the gem: **natan** — Jacob *re-narrates the breeding-scheme* (JAC-11) as **God's giving** — the flocks he *built by craft* are now confessed as *taken-from-Laban-and-given-by-God*; the interior *theologises its prosperity*, crediting the gift to God (and the dream, 31:10–13, backs it — "I have seen all Laban does to you"). Whether piety or self-justification, the *stated* reading is *God gave it* *(inferred: natan = the flocks re-read as God's gift, not Jacob's trick)*.
- **chashav (regarded) — the wives *counted as foreigners* (31:15).** "are we not **regarded** (chashav) by him as foreigners? he has sold us and devoured our money" *(stated)* — chashav, *reckon / count / regard*: the gem is the *daughters' wound* — their own father *reckons them as outsiders*, having *sold* them (the bride-service) and *consumed the price*; the interior of the *disinherited daughters*, siding against Laban *(inferred: chashav = the daughters reckoned as foreigners by their father)*.
- **osher (wealth) — the wealth *claimed as theirs* by right (31:16).** "all the **wealth** (osher) God has taken from our father belongs to us and our children" *(stated)* — osher, *riches*: the wives *claim the wealth* as *rightfully theirs* (restitution for the devoured bride-price) and *endorse the flight* — "do what God said"; the interior *aligning with Jacob and God against the father* *(inferred: osher = the wealth claimed as the daughters' due)*.
- **lev (tricked) — the departure *by deception* (31:20).** "Jacob **tricked** (lev) Laban the Aramean by not telling him he intended to flee" *(stated)* — the gem is the idiom: **lev — *heart/mind*** — "Jacob *stole the heart of* Laban" (ganav-lev = *deceive*): even the *God-commanded* return (31:3,13) is *executed by stealth*; the interior *deceives again* — the grasping/deceiving pattern *persists* into the departure (and Rachel *steals* the household gods, 31:19). The deceiver *leaves as he lived* *(inferred: lev = the heart-stealing deception of the secret flight)*.
- **(31:19 — Rachel steals the household gods, no non-T2 span.)** Flagged: "Rachel **stole** her father's household gods" carries no flagged span, but it *doubles* the departure's theme — *theft and stealth run in the family*; the wife *steals the teraphim* as the husband *steals the heart*; the interior's *acquisitive stealth* shared. Read as context, anchored by lev (31:20) *(no-span context; the parallel theft)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **the mood turns and God calls (shama/kavod 31:1 → qara 31:4):** Jacob *overhears the sons' envy* of his *wealth*, sees Laban's face changed, and (God having said "return") *privately summons his wives* to decide.
2. **the case: full service, God's protection, God's gift (yada/avad/koach 31:6 → ra 31:7 → natan 31:9):** Jacob pleads his *wholehearted labour*, reads Laban's *evil intent as restrained by God*, and *re-narrates his prospering as God's giving* — the interior *theologising the wealth it built by craft*.
3. **the wives side against their father (chashav 31:15 → osher 31:16):** feeling *reckoned as foreigners and sold*, they *claim the wealth as their due* and *endorse the flight* — the daughters' wound turned to alliance.
4. **the stealthy departure (lev 31:20 → [Rachel's theft 31:19]):** the God-commanded return is *carried out by deception* — Jacob *steals Laban's heart* (deceives), Rachel *steals the gods*; the interior *leaves as it lived* — obedience to God *entangled with the old stealth*.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** shama (the *resentment overheard*); kavod (the *weighty gain* provoking the sons' envy); qara (the *private summons* to decide); yada/avad/koach (the *full-strength service* pleaded); ra (Laban's *evil intent restrained by God*); **natan (the *cunning re-read as God's gift*)**; chashav (the daughters *reckoned as foreigners* and sold); osher (the wealth *claimed as their due*); **lev (the *heart-stealing deception* of the flight — the idiom)**; Rachel's *parallel theft* of the gods (no-span context).
- **Whose interior:** the **human** interiors *aggrieved and theologising* (Jacob — full service, God's gift, yet fleeing by stealth), *wounded and allied* (the daughters — sold, foreignised, claiming their due), and *acquisitive* (Rachel's theft); the **divine** interior *restraining the harmer and calling the fugitive home* (the Bethel-God keeping the vow, 31:13).
- **Threads:** *God gave it* (natan 31:9 ← the breeding scheme 30:43 — Jacob's own theological re-reading of his craft); *God restrains the harmer* (ra 31:7 ← 20:6 Abimelech); the Bethel vow kept (31:13 ← 28:20–22 — God returns him as promised); the persisting deception (lev 31:20 ← the deceiver deceived JAC-09; → the confrontation where Laban pursues, JAC-13); envy at grasped wealth (kavod 31:1 ← 26:14 qana around Isaac); Rachel's theft (→ Jacob's rash oath "let the thief die", 31:32, JAC-13).
- **Guarded against:** deciding *for* the reader whether Jacob's "God gave it" (31:9) is piety or self-justification — I read only that he *states* it, and that it *re-frames* the craft of JAC-11 (the tension left open); flattening the lev-idiom into plain "tricked" and missing that the *deception-word* frames even the God-commanded return; importing a verdict on Rachel's theft (the text merely *reports* it; its consequence comes at 31:32); treating koach as a throwaway ("all my strength") when it *stresses the wholeheartedness* Jacob pleads; reading the wives' endorsement as pure faith when it is also *claim to their devoured inheritance* (chashav/osher — both are there).
