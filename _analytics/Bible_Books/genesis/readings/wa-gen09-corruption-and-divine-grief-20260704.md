## GEN-09-corruption-and-divine-grief · Genesis 6:1–8 · [two hearts] — the covetous seeing that takes, the heart that forms only evil, the God who grieves to his heart, and the first grace

**The passage.** "The sons of God **saw** that the daughters of man were **attractive** (tov), and they took… any they **chose** (bachar)" (6:2); "My Spirit shall not **abide** (din) in man forever, for he is **flesh** (basar)" (6:3); "every **intention** (yetser) of the **thoughts** (machashavah) of his **heart** (lev) was only **evil** (raah) continually" (6:5); "the LORD **regretted**… and it **grieved** (atsav) him to his **heart** (lev)" (6:6); "I am **sorry** (nacham) that I have made them" (6:7); "but Noah found **favor** (chen)" (6:8).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **tov (attractive) — the appraisal-word turned covetous seeing (6:2).** The finding is "the sons of God **saw** that the daughters were **attractive** (tov)… and took" *(stated)*. The operation is the *same tov* that was God's verdict over creation (GEN-01) and the trees' "good for food" (2:9), now the object of a **grasping look that takes** — the exact pattern of the fall (3:6: "the woman *saw* the tree was *tov*… and took") replayed: *saw → good → took*. The gem is that the creational appraisal-word has become the trigger of covetous seizure, at cosmic scale *(inferred: the inversion pattern again — tov, God's word of goodness, drives the grasping)*.
- **bachar (choose) — unrestrained elective taking (6:2).** The finding is "they took as their wives **any they chose** (bachar)" *(stated)*. The operation is **choice exercised as pure appetite** — the first *bachar* in Scripture, and it is *unbounded* ("any they chose"); electing-and-taking without limit *(inferred: distinct from tov's *seeing* — bachar is the *willing/taking* that follows)*.
- **din (abide/strive) — the Spirit's contention with flesh *limited* (6:3).** The finding is "My Spirit shall not **abide** (din) in man forever" *(stated)*. The operation is the divine Spirit's striving/abiding with man **set a limit** — God will not endlessly contend with corrupt man; the days are numbered *(inferred)*.
- **basar (flesh) — flesh as *frail, mortal* nature (6:3).** The finding is "for he is **flesh** (basar)" *(stated)*. The operation is basar in a *new sense* — not the material/shared-substance/one-flesh of GEN-03 (2:21,23,24) but **man's weak, mortal, sin-prone creatureliness**, the reason the Spirit will not abide forever *(inferred: keep apart from GEN-03's basar — here it names the frailty that limits the Spirit)*.
- **6:5 — the anatomy of the corrupt heart (four operations stacked).** The finding is "every **intention** (yetser) of the **thoughts** (machashavah) of his **heart** (lev) was only **evil** (raah) continually" *(stated)*. Kept apart: **lev (heart)** — the **first "heart" in Scripture** as the inner *seat*, the source of the intentions; **yetser (intention/forming)** — the heart's *forming/framing* faculty, and its root echoes *yatsar* ("form", 2:7): the God who *formed* man now sees man's heart *forming* only evil — the formed become a former-of-evil; **machashavah (thoughts/plots)** — the *devising/scheming* the heart produces; **raah (evil)** — the *total* content, "only evil continually". The gem is that this is a *complete corruption of the inner forming-faculty*: the seat (lev) frames (yetser) devisings (machashavah) wholly and ceaselessly evil (raah) *(inferred: the interior's generative core is turned entirely to evil; the first named "heart" is a heart that forms only evil)*.
- **nacham (regretted) — God's *sorrow/regret* (6:6).** The finding is "the LORD **regretted** (nacham) that he had made man" *(stated)*. The operation is the **divine interior disclosed as regret** — and the gem is the word: *nacham* is the *same* word as Noah's naming (5:29, "he shall bring us **relief/comfort**"): there the human *hope for comfort*, here God's *sorrow*. The one root bridges the two interiors — man longs for nacham, God feels nacham *(inferred: the narrator states God's inside; this is a stated divine-interior finding, not inferred onto God)*.
- **atsav (grieved) — the human curse-pain now felt *by God* (6:6).** The finding is "it **grieved** (atsav) him to his **heart** (lev)" *(stated)*. The operation is grief in God — and the gem is that *atsav* shares the root of *itstsavon/etsev*, the **pain/toil the curse laid on man** (3:16–17, GEN-05): the very pain-word of the human sentence now describes **God's own grief**. God *shares the atsav* he decreed — the divine interior *hurt* by what man has become *(inferred: the pain of the curse is now reciprocal — felt in the divine heart)*.
- **lev (heart) #2 — the *grieved divine* heart against the *evil human* heart (6:6).** The finding is "grieved him to his **heart** (lev)" *(stated)*. The gem is the *same word lev*, one verse apart: at 6:5 the **human** heart is "only evil"; at 6:6 the **divine** heart is "grieved". The passage sets the two hearts side by side — the corrupt human interior and the wounded divine interior, named identically *(inferred: this juxtaposition is the passage's centre — do not read lev generically; the two hearts are the point)*.
- **nacham (sorry) #2 — the regret restated as the ground of judgment (6:7).** The finding is "I am **sorry** (nacham) that I have made them" *(stated)*. The operation is the divine regret *restated* — now the stated *reason* for the blotting-out; distinct occurrence, deepening 6:6 *(inferred)*.
- **chen (favor) — the first grace, at the nadir (6:8).** The finding is "but Noah found **favor** (chen) in the eyes of the LORD" *(stated)*. The operation is **the first "grace/favor" in Scripture**, appearing at the *darkest* moment — against total corruption and divine grief, *one man is looked on with favor*. The gem is the placement: chen enters the world precisely where judgment is decreed — unmerited favor as the counter-note *(inferred: the "but" turns the whole passage)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **saw → tov → took → chose (6:2) ← 3:6:** the fall's grasping pattern (saw good, took) recurs at cosmic scale, tov again the trigger, bachar the unbounded taking — the inversion of the creational appraisal-word into covetous seizure.
2. **the corrupt forming-faculty (6:5) ← yatsar (2:7):** the God who *formed* (yatsar) man finds man's heart *forming* (yetser) only evil — the making inverted; the interior's generative core (lev → yetser → machashavah) turned wholly to raah.
3. **two hearts, one word (lev 6:5 / 6:6):** the evil human heart and the grieved divine heart, juxtaposed — the passage's centre. The corruption of the one *produces the grief of the other*.
4. **the pain-word reciprocated (atsav 6:6 ← itstsavon 3:16–17):** the curse's pain, laid on man, now *felt by God* — the divine interior shares the atsav of the sentence it passed.
5. **nacham bridges the two interiors (5:29 / 6:6–7):** man's *hope for comfort* and God's *sorrow* are the same word — the human longing and the divine grief meet in nacham.
6. **chen turns it (6:8):** at the point of total corruption and divine regret, the first *grace* appears — the "but Noah found favor" that carries the flood narrative forward.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** tov as *covetous seeing-that-takes* (← 3:6 pattern); bachar as *unbounded elective taking*; din — the Spirit's *limited* striving; basar as *frail mortal flesh* (distinct from GEN-03's basar); the 6:5 anatomy — first **lev** (heart) as the seat, yetser (forming, echoing yatsar 2:7), machashavah (devising), raah (total evil); **nacham** as God's *regret* (← 5:29 human comfort-hope); **atsav** as God's *grief* (← 3:16–17 human pain-root); the **two hearts** (lev, 6:5 evil / 6:6 grieved); nacham restated (6:7); **chen** — the *first grace*, at the nadir.
- **Whose interior:** *both* — the **human** interior in *total corruption* (the heart forming only evil) and, disclosed directly, the **divine** interior in *grief and regret*; joined by the "but" of *grace* to one man.
- **Threads:** tov (← 3:6 saw-good-took; the inversion pattern); yetser ← yatsar (2:7); nacham (← 5:29 → the flood's outcome); atsav ← itstsavon/etsev (3:16–17); lev (first here → the heart carried through the whole study); chen (→ Noah's righteousness, GEN-10 6:9; grace before the flood).
- **Guarded against:** reading God's grief/regret as mere anthropomorphism to be explained away (the verse *states* God's inside — it is the datum); lumping basar's frail-flesh sense with GEN-03's; flattening the two lev into one generic "heart" (the juxtaposition is the gem); merging nacham's comfort- and sorrow-senses; importing later doctrines of total depravity or common grace beyond what 6:5 and 6:8 state.
