## EZE-egypt-nile-mine · Ezekiel 29:1–32:32 · [D, multi] — crediting oneself with the gift received; the towering self felled for its pride

**The reading.** The Egypt oracles turn on Pharaoh's self-crediting boast: "**the great dragon that lies in the midst of his streams, that says, 'My Nile is my own; I made it for myself'**" (29:3), repeated — "**Because you said, 'The Nile is mine, and I made it'**" (29:9). The allegory of the great cedar (Assyria, ch.31) makes the point universal: a tree "**of towering height, its top among the clouds**" (31:3), so magnificent that "**all the trees of Eden envied it**" (31:9), yet — "**because it towered high… and its heart was proud of its height**" (31:10) — it is "**cast down to Sheol**". And Pharaoh: "**You consider yourself a lion of the nations, but you are like a dragon**" (32:2); "**they shall bring to ruin the pride of Egypt**" (32:12). The finding is **the interior *crediting itself with what it only received* — "**the Nile is mine, I made it**" — and the *towering self* whose "**heart was proud of its height**" *felled*** *(stated)*. The operation is **two forms of pride: the *false attribution* of Pharaoh — claiming to have *made* the Nile (the very source of Egypt's life, a gift) as his own creation ("I made it for myself"); and the *self-exaltation* of the cedar — a greatness real and God-given ("I made it beautiful", 31:9) that becomes ruin when "**its heart was proud of its height**", so the height that was gift becomes the ground of a pride that fells it** *(inferred: 29:3's "**the Nile is MINE, and I MADE IT for myself**" is a precise inner-being sin — *false attribution* — the finding is that the proud self *credits itself with what it merely received*: the Nile (Egypt's lifeline, entirely a gift of nature/God) is claimed as Pharaoh's *own creation* ("I made it"), the ultimate self-crediting delusion (as Assyria's "by my hand… by my wisdom", Isa 10:13, or Nebuchadnezzar's "great Babylon which *I* have built", Dan 4:30); the finding is that pride's root is this *mis-attribution* — taking the gift as one's own achievement, forgetting the Giver; and the cedar-allegory (31) universalises it — the finding is that *real, God-given greatness* ("I made it beautiful, all the trees of Eden envied it", 31:9) is precisely what *breeds* the deadly pride when "**its heart was proud of its height**" (31:10): the height that God gave becomes, in the proud heart, the ground of self-exaltation, and so it is "cast down"; the finding is that the loftier the gift, the greater the temptation to be "proud of one's height", so the tallest trees fall hardest — and the lesson is deliberately *general* ("that no trees by the waters may grow to towering height… for they are all given over to death", 31:14): all towering self-exaltation, however magnificent, ends in the pit)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *false attribution — crediting oneself with the gift ("the Nile is mine, I made it for myself" — claiming to have made what one only received)* (thread — a gem: pride's root is mis-attributing the gift to oneself, forgetting the Giver, w/ 29:3,9, Isa 10:13, Dan 4:30, Deut 8:17); *God-given greatness breeding pride ("its heart was proud of its height" — the loftiness that was gift become the ground of self-exaltation)* (thread — a gem: real greatness the occasion of deadly pride; the tallest trees fall hardest, w/ 31:10, 28:17, Prov 16:18); *the towering self felled ("cast down to Sheol… bring to ruin the pride of Egypt" — self-exaltation, however magnificent, ending in the pit)* (thread — w/ 31:10-18, 32:12, Isa 14:15); *self-inflating self-image ("you consider yourself a lion… but you are like a dragon" — the gap between the proud self-estimate and the truth)* (new — w/ 32:2, Obad 3).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** false attribution — crediting oneself with the gift; God-given greatness breeding pride; the towering self felled; self-inflating self-image. Chapters 29–32 anatomise pride as *false attribution* ("the Nile is mine, I made it") and *self-exaltation* (the towering cedar "proud of its height", felled) — all lofty self-magnification ending in the pit.
- **Book-level note (Ezekiel batch 5, chs 25–32):** the nations-oracles judge the *interiors* behind the nations: *malicious gloating* over another's fall and *nursed enmity* (25); *self-deifying pride* and the *gift-bred pride* that corrupts wisdom for splendour (26–28); and *false attribution* — crediting oneself with the gift — with the *towering self felled* (29–32). Master-findings: **God weighs the soul's gloating and grudge apart from any overt act**; **the summit of pride is self-deification, forgetting one is "but a man"**; **the deepest gifts (beauty, wisdom, greatness) most tempt the heart to pride**; and **crediting oneself with what one only received is pride's root.**
- **Threads:** gloating / enmity (← Obad; 25); self-deification (Isa 14; 28:2); false attribution (Isa 10:13, Dan 4:30); pride felled (Prov 16:18).
