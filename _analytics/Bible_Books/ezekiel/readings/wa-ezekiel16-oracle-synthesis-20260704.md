## EZE-faithless-bride · Ezekiel 16:1–63 · [D, multi] — the foundling love; trust in one's beauty/gifts; the never-satisfied whoring; the shame that mercy produces

**The reading.** The chapter tells Jerusalem's story as a *rescued foundling*. An unwanted newborn is cast out "**wallowing in your blood**", and God passes by: "**I said to you in your blood, 'Live!'**" (v.6); he raises her, and when she is grown, "**I spread the corner of my garment over you… I made my vow to you and entered into a covenant with you**" (v.8), adorning her until "**your renown went forth among the nations because of your beauty, for it was perfect through the splendour that I had bestowed on you**" (v.14). But then: "**you trusted in your beauty and played the whore… you were not satisfied**" (vv.15,28), even using "**my gold… which I had given you**" to make idols (v.17). Her sister Sodom's guilt is named: "**pride, excess of food, and prosperous ease, but did not aid the poor and needy**" (v.49). And "**How sick is your heart**" (v.30). Yet the ending turns: "**I will remember my covenant with you… I will establish… an everlasting covenant… that you may remember and be ashamed… when I atone for you for all that you have done**" (vv.60,63). The finding is **the *foundling love* that gave the self its very life and beauty, *betrayed* by a heart that "**trusted in its beauty**" and was "**never satisfied**" — and a mercy that *restores* while producing *shame* ("that you may remember and be ashamed… when I atone")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the anatomy of ingratitude and its cure: the self owes God *everything* (life, growth, adornment, renown — all "bestowed"), yet *turns the very gifts against the Giver* — "trusted in your beauty" (making the gift its own glory), used "my gold" for idols (spending God's gifts on rivals to him); the disorder is *insatiable* ("not satisfied" though it multiplied its lovers); and the redemption comes not through the bride's merit but through God *remembering his covenant* and *atoning* — a grace that produces not pride but *shame*, a humbled, silenced gratitude** *(inferred: three inner-being findings. First, the *foundling love* (vv.6–14) — the self's very *existence and worth* are pure gift: it was a dying, unwanted infant to whom God said "**Live!**", and everything it later boasts (beauty, renown) was "**bestowed**" by him; the finding is that the interior owns *nothing* of itself that it did not receive, so all self-glory is stolen glory. Second, the betrayal is *turning the gift against the Giver* — "**you TRUSTED IN YOUR BEAUTY**" (v.15, making God's gift the object of self-confidence) and used "**my gold**" for idols (v.17): the finding is the precise mechanism of ingratitude — the gifts meant to point *to* the Giver become the self's *own glory* and are spent on his rivals; and the appetite is *insatiable* — "**not satisfied**" (vv.28–29) however much it whores (the bottomless quality of misdirected desire); Sodom's guilt (v.49) names the same in social form — "pride, ease, and no aid to the poor", the comfortable self curved in on itself. Third, the *shame that mercy produces* (v.63) — God's atonement does not leave the bride proud but "**ashamed… never to open your mouth again**": the finding is that *being forgiven* rightly produces not entitlement but a *humbled, silenced shame* — the memory of what one was and did, met by grace, closes the boasting mouth; grace received truly *humbles*, it does not inflate)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the foundling love — the self's very life and worth pure gift ("I said to you… Live!"… "perfect through the splendour I had bestowed" — the interior owning nothing it did not receive)* (thread — a gem: all self-glory is stolen glory, w/ 16:6-14, 1 Cor 4:7, Deut 8:17, Hos 2:8); *trusting the gift instead of the Giver ("you trusted in your beauty… made idols of my gold" — the gifts turned into self-glory and spent on God's rivals)* (thread — a gem: the mechanism of ingratitude, the gift meant to point to the Giver becoming the self's boast, w/ 16:15,17, Rom 1:21-23); *insatiable misdirected desire ("you were not satisfied" — the bottomless appetite of the straying heart)* (thread — a gem: misdirected desire never filled, w/ 16:28-29, Eccl 5:10, Jer 2:13); *Sodom's guilt — comfortable self-absorption ("pride, excess of food, prosperous ease, but did not aid the poor")* (new — a gem: the guilt of ease curved inward, w/ 16:49, Amos 6:1-6, Luke 16:19-21); *the shame that mercy produces ("that you may remember and be ashamed… when I atone for you" — grace humbling, not inflating, closing the boasting mouth)* (new — a gem: being forgiven produces humbled silence, not entitlement, w/ 16:60-63, Rom 3:19, Luke 7:47).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the foundling love; trusting the gift instead of the Giver; insatiable misdirected desire; Sodom's guilt of comfortable self-absorption; the shame that mercy produces. Chapter 16 tells the interior's story as a rescued foundling who trusted her God-given beauty, whored insatiably, and is restored by an atoning grace that leaves her humbled and ashamed, not proud.
