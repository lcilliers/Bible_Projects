# Worked example — narrative inner-being method on Genesis 3:1–13

- **Date:** 2026-07-04
- **Purpose:** demonstrate (not assert) the corrected method: spans → verse-bounded lexicals → **individual** operations → **interactive** operations between spans, with **distinctions preserved** (never lumping repeated glosses). The story is used only to see the operations more sharply; the operations are the object.
- **Scope:** one passage, the evidence spans actually in `wa_verse_records` (non-T2). This is a method proof for review, not a filed reading.

## 2. Individual span-operations (read off *this* verse, not the gloss)

- **arum (3:1) — "crafty":** the gloss is neutral "prudent," but the verse-bounded operation is *prudence bent to subvert* — intelligence operating as cunning. **Note the lexical resonance held for §3:** the serpent is *arum* (prudent/crafty) and will dangle *sakal* (to-make-prudent/wise, 3:6) as the bait — the prudent one markets prudence.
- **yada (3:5) — God "knows":** knowing predicated of *God*, deployed by the serpent as *knowledge-God-withholds* — an operation of **suspicion-seeding**: God's knowing reframed as God's self-interest.
- **tov (3:5) — "good" (in "good and evil"):** the *moral* good, the content of the god-like knowing promised.
- **ta'avah / chamad / sakal (3:6) — the triple appraisal:** three distinct faculties fire on one object — *appetite* (delight to eyes), *coveting* (desired), *aspiration* (to make wise). Not one "desire"; three operations stacked (see §3).
- **natan (3:6) — the woman "gave":** an operation that **crosses persons** — the transgression *transmitted* to another; giving as drawing-in.
- **yada (3:7) — they "knew" (nakedness):** knowing predicated of *the couple*, and its content is *shame*, not deity — an operation of **self-exposure**.
- **shama (3:8) — "heard" God walking:** perception of God's presence that triggers **flight** (they hid).
- **qara (3:9) — God "called":** the *pursuing* operation — God moves toward the hidden self.
- **shama (3:10) — "I heard you":** the *same* hearing now *confessed*, and welded to fear.
- **yare (3:10) — "afraid":** the gloss says "revere," but the verse-bounded operation is **dread-and-concealment**, not reverence — the affect that *produces hiding*.
- **natan (3:12) — "the woman you gave… she gave":** giving repurposed as **blame-shift**.

---

## 3. Interactive operations — the web *between* spans (the point)

This is what a gloss list cannot show and what "grouping stuff together" destroys:

1. **arum → sakal (3:1 → 3:6): the bait matches the baiter.** The *prudent* serpent offers *prudence* ("to make one wise"). The operation is a lure keyed to the very faculty the tempter embodies — craftiness selling wisdom. Lumping H6175 and H7919A as "prudence" would erase that this is one prudence *weaponising* another.
2. **yada(3:5) → yada(3:7): the promised knowing inverts into its opposite.** The serpent promises knowing that makes one *like God* (3:5); what actually arrives is knowing one is *naked* (3:7) — the **same lemma, antithetical operations**: exaltation-knowledge promised, shame-knowledge delivered. The operation is *self-defeating fulfilment* — the promise comes true and ruins. This distinction is the passage's central gem, and it lives *only* in refusing to merge the two yada spans.
3. **perception → desire → aspiration → hand (within 3:6):** tov-appraisal (saw good) feeds ta'avah (delight) feeds chamad (coveting) feeds sakal (aspiration) feeds the act (took) feeds **natan** (gave). A single interior sequence, faculties handing off to each other — the anatomy of one transgression, not one "desire operation."
4. **natan(3:6) → natan(3:12): gift becomes alibi.** In 3:6 giving *spreads* the act to the man; in 3:12 the man turns *give* into accusation — and aims it past the woman at **God**: "the woman *you gave* me." The **same operation** runs first as transmission, then as blame-deflection that indicts the Giver. Merge the two natan and the whole reversal (God's good gift → the sinner's excuse) vanishes.
5. **shama(3:8) → qara(3:9) → shama(3:10) + yare(3:10): the birth of hiding-from-God.** Hearing God (3:8) now triggers flight; God's *calling* (3:9) pursues; the self's *hearing* (3:10) is confessed and **fused with fear** (yare) — a new operation-compound that did not exist before the act: *God-presence → dread → concealment*. The reverence-gloss on yare is exactly the wrong label; the verse builds *terror*, and it is interactive — hearing and fearing weld into one hiding.

---

## 4. What this demonstrates about the method

- **The operation is read off the span's lexical in *its* verse**, never off the gloss. (yare glossed "revere" = the operation *dread*; the gloss would have misled.)
- **Repeats are never lumped.** yada, shama, natan each carry *different* operations at each occurrence — and the *difference between the occurrences* is itself the finding (promised-knowing vs shame-knowing; gift vs alibi).
- **A passage is a web, not a point.** Gen 3:1–13 yields ~11 individual span-operations and ~5 interactive ones; the interactions (bait-matches-baiter, knowing-inverts, gift-becomes-alibi, birth-of-hiding) are the richest yield and are invisible at gloss level.
- **The story serves the operations.** Nothing above is a plot summary; the narrative is used only to let each operation and interaction show itself.
- **"Why are they different?" is the engine.** Every gem here came from asking why two same-gloss spans are *not* the same operation.

---

## 5. Implication for scoping

The scene boundary is set so the **interactions** are complete (3:1–13 holds the whole temptation→act→exposure→interrogation web; cutting at 3:7 would sever the hiding/blame operations). So scenes are bounded by *where the operation-web closes*, anchored on the evidence spans — not by chapter and not by single verses.
