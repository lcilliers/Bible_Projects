## EZE-watchman · Ezekiel 3:16–27 · [D, multi] — the watchman's responsibility; delivering one's own soul by warning; the bound and mute prophet

**The reading.** After seven days God commissions Ezekiel afresh: "**I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel. Whenever you hear a word from my mouth, you shall give them warning from me**" (v.17). The charge is weighted with *responsibility*: if the watchman fails to warn the wicked, "**that wicked person shall die for his iniquity, but his blood I will require at your hand**" (v.18); but "**if you warn the wicked, and he does not turn… he shall die for his iniquity, but you will have delivered your soul**" (v.19). Then, strangely, God binds him and makes him *mute* — "**I will make your tongue cling to the roof of your mouth, so that you shall be mute**" — until "**when I speak with you, I will open your mouth**" (vv.26–27). The finding is **the *watchman's charge* — accountable to *warn*, so that failing to warn makes him *bloodguilty*, while warning *delivers his own soul* (regardless of the hearer's response) — and the *mute* prophet who speaks only when God opens his mouth** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior burdened with *responsibility for warning others*: the watchman is answerable not for whether they *heed* but for whether he *warns* — his own soul is "delivered" by faithful warning even if the hearer perishes, and imperilled ("blood required at your hand") by silence; and the paradox of the *mute* prophet — bound and speechless *except* when God gives the word — locating the whole authority of his speech in God's initiative** *(inferred: the watchman-image (vv.17–21) defines a specific inner-being weight — *responsibility for the warning, not the outcome*: the finding is that the one who knows God's word is *accountable to speak it* — his "soul is delivered" (v.19) by *faithfully warning*, whether or not the hearer turns; the result is God's and the hearer's, but the *warning* is the watchman's charge, and *silence* makes him complicit in the death he could have spoken against ("his blood I will require at your hand"); the finding is that to know a saving truth and withhold it is a grave failure, while to speak it faithfully *clears* one's own conscience even when it is rejected — the responsibility is *to warn*, and it is discharged by warning; and the strange muteness (vv.26–27) adds a counter-weight — the watchman who is bidden to speak is also made *unable to speak* except when God opens his mouth: the finding is that the prophet's authority is *entirely derivative* — he does not speak at will but *only God's given word*, so his silence and his speech are both under God's hand; the interior is thus held in tension — *bound* to warn yet *bound* from speaking his own words, answerable to declare God's word and forbidden to substitute his own)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the watchman accountable to warn, not for the outcome ("if you give no warning… his blood I will require at your hand; if you warn… you will have delivered your soul" — responsible for the warning, the result God's and the hearer's)* (thread — a gem: to know a saving truth and withhold it is grave failure; faithful warning clears the conscience even when rejected, w/ 3:17-21, 33:1-9, Acts 20:26-27, 1 Cor 9:16); *delivering one's own soul by faithfulness regardless of response (the watchman's soul secured by warning, not by success)* (new — a gem: faithfulness, not results, discharges the charge, w/ 3:19,21, Ezek 33:9); *the derivative authority of the mute prophet ("mute… until I open your mouth" — speaking only God's given word, not his own)* (new — a gem: the prophet bound to warn yet bound from his own words, authority wholly derivative, w/ 3:26-27, Num 22:38, John 12:49).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the watchman accountable to warn, not for the outcome; delivering one's own soul by faithfulness regardless of response; the derivative authority of the mute prophet. Chapter 3 lays the watchman's charge — answerable to *warn* (his soul delivered by faithfulness, imperilled by silence) — and makes him mute except when God gives the word.
- **Book-level note (Ezekiel batch 1, chs 1–3):** the call joins three inner-being fields — the *glory that prostrates* the seer (1), the *word internalised* (eaten, sweet-though-woeful, borne at real cost) and the *hardened face* given for a hard people (2–3:15), and the *watchman's responsibility* to warn (3:16-27). Master-findings: **the sight of God's glory floors the beholder**; **the messenger must first eat the word — take it inside — and even so bears it at cost**; and **to know God's word is to be accountable to warn, delivering one's soul by faithfulness, not results.**
- **Threads:** the glory (← 1:28; → 8-11, 43); the word eaten (← 2:8-3:3); the watchman (→ 33:1-9); the prophet's cost (← 3:14; → 24).
