## EZE-two-sisters · Ezekiel 23:1–49 · [D, multi] — escalating lust; self-disgust that does not stop the sin; the cup

**The reading.** Two sisters, Oholah (Samaria) and Oholibah (Jerusalem), "**played the whore in Egypt… in their youth**" (v.3) and, though they "**became mine**" (v.4), kept lusting after Assyria, Babylon, and Egypt. The younger is "**more corrupt than her sister in her lust**" (v.11). A telling detail marks the *interior* dynamics: after Babylon defiled her, "**she turned from them in disgust**" (v.17) — yet "**she increased her whoring, remembering the days of her youth**" (v.19). The end is the shared cup: "**you shall drink your sister's cup that is deep and large… a cup of horror and desolation**" (vv.32–33). The finding is **a lust that *recoils into self-disgust* — "turned from them in disgust" — and *yet does not stop*, "increasing her whoring" even after the disgust** *(stated)*. The operation is **the *addictive* structure of misdirected desire: the interior *tastes its object, is disgusted by it* ("turned in disgust"), and *still returns to it and increases* — the craving *outlasting* the revulsion, so that even the experience of the sin's ugliness does not free the will from it; the disgust and the pursuit *coexist*, the mark of a bondage the self cannot break by mere distaste** *(inferred: v.17–19 exposes a profound inner-being dynamic often overlooked — Oholibah is "**defiled**", then "**turned from them in DISGUST**", and *then* "**INCREASED her whoring**": the finding is that *misdirected desire can be addictive in a way that survives even self-disgust* — the interior may *loathe* what it does the moment after doing it ("turned in disgust"), and *still be driven back to it*; the revulsion does not liberate the will, because the *craving* runs deeper than the distaste; this is the anatomy of a *bondage* — the self both *hates* its sin and *cannot stop*, the disgust and the compulsion held together (the very condition Paul names, "the evil I do not want is what I keep on doing", Rom 7:19); the finding is that *feeling disgust at one's sin is not the same as being freed from it* — a person can be genuinely repulsed by their own behaviour and yet remain enslaved to it, because deliverance requires more than distaste (it requires the new heart, 36:26); and the deepening ("increased… remembering the days of her youth", v.19) shows the *escalating* character — the craving, unbroken, does not stay level but *grows*, remembering and returning to earlier indulgence; the shared "cup" (v.32) names the recoil — the sin, un-stopped, comes back as a "cup of horror" one must drink)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *lust that recoils into self-disgust yet does not stop ("she turned from them in disgust… yet she increased her whoring" — the craving outlasting the revulsion)* (new — a gem: the addictive structure of misdirected desire; disgust and compulsion coexisting, distaste not equalling deliverance, w/ 23:17-19, Rom 7:15-24, 2 Pet 2:22); *feeling disgust at one's sin not the same as being freed from it (the self both hating its sin and unable to stop — bondage requiring the new heart, not mere distaste)* (new — a gem: deliverance beyond revulsion, w/ 23:17-19, 36:26, John 8:34); *the escalating craving ("she increased her whoring, remembering the days of her youth" — the unbroken desire growing, not staying level)* (thread — w/ 23:11,19); *the recoil-cup ("you shall drink your sister's cup… of horror" — the un-stopped sin returning as a cup one must drink)* (thread — w/ 23:32-33, Jer 25:15).
