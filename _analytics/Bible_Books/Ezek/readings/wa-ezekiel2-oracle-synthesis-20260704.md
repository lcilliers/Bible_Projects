## EZE-eat-the-scroll · Ezekiel 2:1–3:15 · [D, multi] — the word internalised (sweet though full of woe); the hardened face given; the cost of bearing it

**The reading.** Raised to his feet by the Spirit, Ezekiel is sent to "**a rebellious house… impudent and stubborn**" (2:3–4) with the charge, "**be not afraid of them, nor be dismayed at their looks**" (2:6). He is handed a scroll "**written on the front and on the back… words of lamentation and mourning and woe**" (2:10) and told to *eat* it: "**Son of man, feed your belly with this scroll… Then I ate it, and it was in my mouth as sweet as honey**" (3:3). For the hard-faced audience God gives him a matching hardness: "**I have made your forehead… like emery harder than flint**" (3:9), and bids him "**receive [my words] in your heart**" (3:10). The bearing of it costs him: "**I went in bitterness in the heat of my spirit… and I sat there overwhelmed among them seven days**" (3:14–15). The finding is **the word of God *eaten* — internalised so wholly it becomes part of the self, and *sweet* even though its content is "lamentation and woe" — with a *hardened face* given for a hard audience, and a real *bitterness and overwhelming* in bearing it** *(stated)*. The operation is **the prophet's interior *formed by the word*: he does not merely *carry* a message but *eats* it — takes it *inside*, into "belly" and "heart" — so that the word becomes his own substance; and the paradox that the word is *sweet* to taste (the delight of receiving God's own word, as Jer 15:16) even though its message is *woe*; and the equipping — a "forehead harder than flint" *given* to match the stubborn faces he must confront; and the honest *cost* — even so equipped, he goes "in bitterness" and sits "overwhelmed" for seven days** *(inferred: the *eating* of the scroll (3:1–3) is the inner-being center — the finding is that God's word is meant to be *internalised*, not merely handled: Ezekiel *ingests* it ("feed your belly… fill your stomach"), so it passes *into* him and becomes part of who he is — the messenger must first be *inwardly filled* with the word before he can speak it; and the *sweetness* is a striking note — "**as sweet as honey**" (3:3) though the scroll's content is "lamentation and mourning and woe" (2:10): the finding is that *receiving God's word is itself sweet* — a delight to the one who loves God — even when the word's *message* is grievous; the sweetness is in the *word as God's* (the intimacy of being given his very words), not in its cheerful content (as Jer 15:16, "your words became the delight of my heart"; yet cf. Rev 10:10, sweet in the mouth, bitter in the belly); and the *hardened forehead* (3:8–9) is grace for the task — God does not send the sensitive prophet defenceless against "briers, thorns, scorpions" but *makes his face hard* to match theirs, an equipping of resolve; yet the honesty of 3:14–15 completes it — even the God-hardened, word-filled prophet goes "**in BITTERNESS**… OVERWHELMED seven days": the finding is that bearing God's heavy word is *genuinely costly* — the equipping does not remove the grief; the prophet is sweet-fed and flint-faced and *still* bitter and overwhelmed, the interior carrying a weight that the calling does not lighten)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the word internalised, eaten ("eat this scroll… feed your belly… receive my words in your heart" — the messenger inwardly filled with the word before speaking it)* (thread — a gem: God's word ingested, becoming the self's substance, not merely handled, w/ 3:1-3,10, Jer 15:16, Ps 119:11, Col 3:16); *the word sweet though its message is woe ("words of lamentation and woe"… "sweet as honey" — receiving God's word itself sweet even when grievous)* (new — a gem: the sweetness in the word as God's, not in its content, w/ 2:10/3:3, Jer 15:16, Rev 10:9-10, Ps 19:10); *the hardened face given for the task ("your forehead like emery harder than flint" — resolve equipped by God to match the stubborn audience)* (new — a gem: God hardening the sensitive messenger for the hard hearers, w/ 3:8-9, Isa 50:7, Jer 1:18); *the real cost of bearing the word ("I went in bitterness… overwhelmed seven days" — the grief the equipping does not remove)* (thread — a gem: the sweet-fed, flint-faced prophet still bitter and overwhelmed, w/ 3:14-15, Jer 20:9,14, Dan 8:27).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the word internalised; the word sweet though its message is woe; the hardened face given; the real cost of bearing the word. Chapters 2–3 form the prophet by the word — eaten and made his own substance, sweet even in its woe, his face hardened for the task, yet leaving him bitter and overwhelmed.
- **Threads:** the word internalised / eaten (← Jer 15:16; w/ Rev 10:9-10, Ps 119:11); the hardened face (w/ Isa 50:7, Jer 1:18); the cost of the word (← Jer 20:9; → Eze 24); the rebellious house / hard heart (→ 3:7, 11:19).
