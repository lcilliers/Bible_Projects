## EZE-idols-in-the-heart · Ezekiel 14:1–15:8 · [D, multi] — idols set up in the heart; each saves only himself by his own righteousness

**The reading.** Elders come to "inquire" of God, but he exposes their hidden state: "**These men have taken their idols into their hearts, and set the stumbling block of their iniquity before their faces**" (14:3); "**Any one… who takes his idols into his heart… and yet comes to the prophet**" will be answered by God himself, "**that I may lay hold of the hearts of the house of Israel, who are all estranged from me through their idols**" (14:4–5). The summons: "**Repent and turn away from your idols**" (14:6). Then, on a land under judgment, "**even if these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job, were in it, they would deliver but their own lives by their righteousness**" (14:14). The finding is **idolatry located *in the heart itself* ("idols into their hearts", before any outward image) — and the *non-transferability* of righteousness (even the most righteous "deliver but their own lives")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure that the *real* idol is *inward* — "taken into the heart", set up "before the face" as the thing one actually looks to — so that a person can *outwardly inquire of God* while *inwardly enthroning an idol*; and the sober principle that *righteousness is personal and non-transferable* — no one's virtue, however great, can *cover* another's guilt** *(inferred: 14:3's "**idols INTO THEIR HEARTS**" is the inner-being key — the finding is that idolatry is fundamentally a matter of the *interior*: the idol is not primarily the statue but the thing *enthroned in the heart* and "set before the face" (the object one's inner gaze actually fixes on); a person can come to worship God *outwardly* while an idol occupies the heart *inwardly* — the divided worship God sees straight through ("all estranged from me through their idols", 14:5); the finding is that the decisive question is not what one professes but what one's heart is *set on*; and the cure is inward — "**repent and turn away from your idols**" (14:6), a turning of the heart's fixation; the second finding (14:14) is the *non-transferability of righteousness* — even "Noah, Daniel, and Job" (three bywords of righteousness) could "deliver but their own lives": the finding is that no one's goodness is a *credit* transferable to another — each soul stands or falls on its own relation to God (setting up ch.18's individual responsibility); righteousness is *personal*, not a communal asset one can lean on)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *idols enthroned in the heart ("taken their idols into their hearts… before their faces" — idolatry primarily inward, the thing the heart is set on)* (thread — a gem: the real idol is inward; one can worship God outwardly while an idol occupies the heart, w/ 14:3-5, Col 3:5, Matt 6:24, 1 John 5:21); *the heart's fixation as the decisive question ("all estranged from me through their idols" — not what one professes but what the heart is set on)* (new — a gem: divided worship God sees through, w/ 14:5, Matt 15:8); *turning the heart's fixation ("repent and turn away from your idols" — the inward cure)* (thread — w/ 14:6); *the non-transferability of righteousness ("Noah, Daniel, and Job… would deliver but their own lives" — no one's goodness credited to another; each soul stands on its own)* (new — a gem: righteousness personal, not a communal asset to lean on, w/ 14:14,20, Eze 18:20, Ps 49:7).

## Chapter-level notes
