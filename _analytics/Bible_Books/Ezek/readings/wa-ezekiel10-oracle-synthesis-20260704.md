## EZE-glory-departs-heart-of-flesh · Ezekiel 10:1–11:25 · [D, multi] — the glory withdrawing; God a sanctuary in exile; the heart of stone replaced by a heart of flesh

**The reading.** The vision Ezekiel most dreads unfolds in slow stages: "**the glory of the LORD went up from the cherub to the threshold of the house**" (10:4); then "**the glory of the LORD went out from the threshold… and stood over the cherubim**" (10:18); then it rose to "**the east gate**" (10:19); and finally "**the glory of the LORD went up from the midst of the city and stood on the mountain that is on the east side of the city**" (11:23) — God's presence *leaving* his temple by degrees. Yet a word of grace comes to the exiles: "**though I scattered them among the countries, yet I have been a sanctuary to them for a while in the countries where they have gone**" (11:16); and the great promise: "**I will give them one heart, and a new spirit I will put within them. I will remove the heart of stone from their flesh and give them a heart of flesh**" (11:19). The finding is **the *glory departing* the defiled temple by stages — yet God himself becoming "**a sanctuary**" to the exiles — and the promise to *replace the heart of stone with a heart of flesh*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the sober disclosure that God's presence *withdraws* from persistent defilement (the glory does not flee instantly but leaves *reluctantly, by stages*, as if grieving to go) — balanced by the astonishing grace that the departed glory does not *abandon* his people but becomes "**a sanctuary**" to them *in exile* (presence relocated from the building to the people); and the deepest promise, that the *hard, unfeeling heart* (the stone) will be *removed* and a *responsive, tender heart* (flesh) *given*** *(inferred: the *staged departure* of the glory (10:4,18,19; 11:23) is deliberately *slow* — threshold, cherubim, east gate, mountain: the finding is that God's presence *withdraws with reluctance*, lingering at each step as if loath to leave, so the departure itself testifies to a grieving love (he does not slam the door but backs away step by step); and yet — crucially — the withdrawal is *not abandonment*: "**I have been a SANCTUARY to them… in the countries where they have gone**" (11:16): the finding is that when the *building* is defiled and forsaken, God himself becomes the *sanctuary* — his presence relocated from the temple to the *people* wherever they are (the seed of "the temple is his people", the presence not bound to a place, as ch.1's mobile throne showed); and the summit is 11:19 — "**a HEART OF STONE… a HEART OF FLESH**": the finding is Ezekiel's parallel to Jeremiah's new covenant — the fundamental problem is a *heart of stone* (hard, unfeeling, unresponsive to God), and the fundamental remedy is *transplant*, not repair — God *removes* the stone heart and *gives* a heart of "flesh" (soft, tender, responsive), along with "one heart" (undivided) and "a new spirit"; the finding is that the interior's decisive transformation is *received, not achieved* — the stone cannot soften itself; God must give the new heart)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the glory withdrawing by reluctant stages (threshold, cherubim, east gate, mountain — God's presence leaving persistent defilement slowly, as if grieving to go)* (thread — a gem: the reluctant departure testifying to a grieving love, w/ 10:4,18-19, 11:23, Hos 11:8); *God himself a sanctuary in exile ("I have been a sanctuary to them… in the countries where they have gone" — presence relocated from the building to the people)* (new — a gem: the presence not bound to a place, God the sanctuary of the scattered, w/ 11:16, 1:1-3, John 4:21-24); *the heart of stone replaced by a heart of flesh ("I will remove the heart of stone… and give them a heart of flesh" — the hard unresponsive heart transplanted, not repaired)* (thread — a gem, Ezekiel's parallel to the new covenant: the decisive transformation received, not achieved; the stone cannot soften itself, w/ 11:19, 36:26, Jer 31:33, 2 Cor 3:3); *"one heart and a new spirit" (the undivided, God-given interior)* (thread — w/ 11:19, 36:26).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the glory withdrawing by reluctant stages; God himself a sanctuary in exile; the heart of stone replaced by a heart of flesh; "one heart and a new spirit". Chapters 10–11 show the glory *leaving* the defiled temple by grieving degrees — yet God becoming a *sanctuary* to the exiles, and promising to transplant the heart of stone for a heart of flesh.
- **Book-level note (Ezekiel batch 2, chs 4–11):** the movement joins *judgment enacted and grieved* with the *seed of the great promise*. Its findings: the prophet's *body made the sermon* and God's *un-pitying justice* revealed as *wounded love* ("broken over their whoring heart", 6:9); the *hidden idolatry* that thinks "the LORD does not see" against the *grieving remnant* marked to be spared (8-9); and the *glory departing* the defiled temple, yet God a *sanctuary in exile* and the promise of a *heart of flesh* (10-11). Master-findings: **God's severity is the anguish of a betrayed love**; **hidden sin rests on the lie that God is not watching, while grief over sin marks the spared**; and **the hard heart of stone can only be transplanted by God, not repaired by the self.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the new heart / heart of flesh (→ 36:26; ← Jer 31:33); God's presence in exile (→ 43, the returning glory); wounded love beneath judgment (6:9; → 18:23, 33:11); grief over sin (9:4).
