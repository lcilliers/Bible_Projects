## EZE-sign-siege · Ezekiel 4:1–7:27 · [D, multi] — the prophet's body made a sign; un-pitying justice; God broken over the whoring heart

**The reading.** Ezekiel is made to *enact* the siege in his own body — an iron griddle set as a wall, cords binding him so "**you cannot turn from one side to the other**" (4:8), siege-rations eaten in the city's sight. The judgment oracles are severe: "**my eye will not spare you, nor will I have pity**" (7:4,9); "**I, even I, am against you**" (5:8). Yet within the severity a wound is disclosed — the survivors "**will remember me among the nations… how I have been broken over their whoring heart that has departed from me, and over their eyes that go whoring after their idols**" (6:9). The finding is **the prophet's *body made a message* (bound, bearing the siege), God's *un-pitying justice* ("my eye will not spare"), and beneath it God's *grief* — "**I have been broken over their whoring heart**"** *(stated)*. The operation is **two inner-being disclosures: the prophet whose *very body is conscripted* into the message (his physical constraint and privation *are* the sermon), and — most importantly — the *wounded love beneath the severe justice*: God's "eye will not spare", yet he says he "**has been BROKEN**" over the heart that left him, so the unsparing judgment is the act of a God who is himself *heartbroken* by the unfaithfulness** *(inferred: the sign-acts (4:1–8) make the prophet's *body* the medium — bound with cords, immobile, eating measured rations: the finding is that the message can require the *whole self*, not just the voice — Ezekiel's constrained, suffering body *is* the word, the calling reaching into his physical existence (as ch.24 will demand even his grief); and the severe refrain "**my eye will not spare, nor will I have pity**" (5:11, 7:4,9) could seem to portray a merciless God — but 6:9 pierces that reading: "**how I HAVE BEEN BROKEN over their WHORING HEART**" (the God who judges is the God who is *wounded* — "broken", shavar, shattered — by the heart's departure); the finding is that God's unsparing justice is not cold cruelty but the severity of a *betrayed lover* — his "eye will not spare" precisely because his heart has been "broken" by the "whoring heart that has departed from me"; the image is marital (whoring), so the judgment is the anguish of a spouse betrayed, not the indifference of a tyrant; and the goal even of the severity is *recognition and remembrance* — "then they will REMEMBER me" (6:9): the finding is that the wounded God's judgment aims to bring the straying heart back to *remember* the One it left)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the whole self conscripted into the message (the prophet's bound, suffering body made the sermon)* (thread — a gem: the calling reaching into physical existence, the body itself the word, w/ 4:1-8, 24:16-24, Hos 1-3, Isa 20:2-4); *un-pitying justice as the severity of wounded love ("my eye will not spare"… "I have been broken over their whoring heart" — the judging God himself heartbroken by the betrayal)* (thread — a gem: God's severity the anguish of a betrayed lover, not the indifference of a tyrant, w/ 5:11/6:9, Hos 11:8, Jer 3:20, Isa 63:9-10); *the aim of judgment is remembrance ("then they will remember me… how I have been broken" — the severity meant to bring the straying heart back to remember)* (new — a gem: judgment aiming at the return of the heart, w/ 6:9, Luke 15:17-18); *the whoring heart departed (the interior's unfaithfulness figured as adultery)* (thread — w/ 6:9, Jer 3, Hos 2).
