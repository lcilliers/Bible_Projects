## EZE-glory-vision · Ezekiel 1:1–28 · [D, multi] — the overwhelming vision of God's glory that prostrates the seer

**The reading.** By the Chebar canal among the exiles, "**the heavens were opened, and I saw visions of God**" (v.1). An immense, mobile, many-eyed apparatus of living creatures and wheels-within-wheels rises, and above it all "**the likeness of a throne… and seated above the likeness of a throne was a likeness with a human appearance**" (v.26), wrapped in rainbow brightness — "**Such was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD. And when I saw it, I fell on my face, and I heard the voice of one speaking**" (v.28). The finding is **the encounter with the *glory of the LORD* that *prostrates* the seer — "when I saw it, I fell on my face"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior *undone* by the vision of transcendent glory — the involuntary collapse ("fell on my face") that the sight of God's majesty produces, and the disclosure that God's glory reaches his people *even in exile* ("by the Chebar canal", in Babylon), not confined to the temple** *(inferred: the whole cascade of imagery (creatures, wheels, eyes, crystal, fire, rainbow) piles up to overwhelm — and its effect on the interior is a single involuntary act: "**I fell on my face**" (v.28); the finding is that the true sight of God's *glory* (kavod — weight, majesty) does not elate but *floors* the beholder — the appropriate reflex of the creature before the Holy is prostration (as Isa 6:5, Dan 8:17, Rev 1:17); the elaborate "likeness… appearance… likeness" language (Ezekiel never says he saw God directly, only "the *likeness* of the glory") registers a further inner-being note — the seer is careful not to overclaim, awed into *reticence* before the ungraspable; and the *location* matters — this glory appears "**among the exiles by the Chebar canal**" (vv.1,3), in unclean Babylon far from the temple: the finding is that God's glory is *not confined* to the holy place — it comes to his people in their displacement, so the exile is not beyond the reach of the God whose throne is *mobile* (the wheels), able to go where his people are)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the glory that prostrates ("when I saw it, I fell on my face" — the sight of God's majesty flooring the beholder, prostration the creature's reflex before the Holy)* (thread — a gem: true sight of glory undoes rather than elates, w/ 1:28, Isa 6:5, Dan 8:17, Rev 1:17); *awed reticence before the ungraspable (the careful "likeness… appearance" language — the seer not overclaiming)* (new — reverent restraint before what cannot be grasped, w/ 1:26-28, Exod 33:20); *God's glory not confined to the holy place ("visions of God… by the Chebar canal" in exile — the mobile throne reaching his people in displacement)* (new — a gem: God's presence coming to the exiled, not bound to the temple, w/ 1:1-3, 11:16, Ps 139:7-10).
