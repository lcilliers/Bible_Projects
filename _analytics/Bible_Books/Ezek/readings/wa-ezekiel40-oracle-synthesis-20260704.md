## EZE-glory-returns · Ezekiel 40:1–43:27 · [D, multi] — the glory returns to dwell forever; the vision meant to produce shame and repentance

**The reading.** After chapters measuring the new temple in exact detail, the vision reaches its true climax — the *return* of the glory that had departed (chs 10–11): "**the glory of the God of Israel was coming from the east. And the sound of his coming was like the sound of many waters, and the earth shone with his glory**" (43:2); "**the glory of the LORD filled the temple**" (43:5). God speaks: "**this is the place of my throne… where I will dwell in the midst of the people of Israel forever**" (43:7). And the *purpose* of showing Ezekiel the plan is disclosed: "**describe to the house of Israel the temple, that they may be ashamed of their iniquities… And if they are ashamed of all that they have done, make known to them the design**" (43:10–11). The finding is **the *glory returning* to the temple it had left — God's promise to *dwell forever* — and the vision *given to produce shame and repentance*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the reversal of the book's darkest moment: the glory that *departed* by grieving stages (10–11) now *returns* "from the east", fills the temple, and God pledges to "**dwell… forever**" — presence restored *permanently*; and the disclosure that even the vision of the *holy* is meant to *humble* — the glorious temple-plan shown "**that they may be ashamed of their iniquities**", so that beholding God's holiness produces repentant shame, not mere aesthetic wonder** *(inferred: 43:1–5 is the deliberate counterpart to 10–11 — the glory that "**went up from the midst of the city**" (11:23) now "**came from the east… and filled the temple**" (43:2,5): the finding is that the *departed presence returns* — the withdrawal was not final; the God who left the defiled house comes back to a cleansed one to "**DWELL… FOREVER**" (43:7), the permanence answering the whole book's anxiety about the hidden face; and the striking inner-being note is the *purpose* of the vision (43:10-11) — Ezekiel is to describe the glorious temple "**that they may be ASHAMED of their iniquities**": the finding is that the sight of God's *holiness and glory* is meant to work *repentant shame* in the beholder — not to gratify curiosity or aesthetic delight but to *humble* (as Isaiah's "woe is me" before the throne, 6:5); the beauty and holiness of the restored dwelling expose, by contrast, the ugliness of the sin that had defiled the old one, so the right response to beholding glory is *shame and turning*; the finding is that God ties the *revelation* of his design to the people's *shame* — "IF they are ashamed… make known to them the design" — as though the fuller disclosure is *for* the humbled heart)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the departed glory returns to dwell forever ("the glory of the LORD… came from the east and filled the temple… where I will dwell forever" — the withdrawn presence restored permanently)* (thread — a gem: the reversal of the departing glory; presence restored for good, answering the fear of the hidden face, w/ 43:2-7, 10-11 reversed, 37:26-28, Rev 21:3); *the vision of holiness meant to produce shame ("describe the temple, that they may be ashamed of their iniquities" — beholding God's glory working repentant humility, not aesthetic wonder)* (new — a gem: the sight of holiness humbling the beholder, the beauty of the holy exposing the ugliness of sin, w/ 43:10-11, Isa 6:5, Luke 5:8, 36:31-32); *the fuller disclosure for the humbled heart ("if they are ashamed… make known to them the design")* (new — revelation tied to repentance, w/ 43:11).
