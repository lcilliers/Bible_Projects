## EZE-despised-oath · Ezekiel 17:1–24 · [D, multi] — the sanctity of a sworn oath; faithlessness to a covenant

**The reading.** A riddle of two eagles and a vine tells how Zedekiah, "**one of the royal offspring**", was "**made a covenant with… putting him under oath**" (v.13), "**that the kingdom might be humble and not lift itself up, and keep his covenant that it might stand**" (v.14). But he "**rebelled… by sending his ambassadors to Egypt**" (v.15). God's verdict fixes on the *broken oath*: "**he despised the oath in breaking the covenant… As I live… surely it is my oath that he despised, and my covenant that he broke. I will return it upon his head**" (vv.18–19). The finding is **a *sworn oath treated as binding — and as God's own* — so that faithlessness to one's word ("he despised the oath") is a *sin against God*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure that a *sworn commitment* has a *sacred weight* — even Zedekiah's oath to a *pagan king* is called by God "**my oath… my covenant**", so that breaking one's word is not merely a political failure but a *betrayal of God*, who stands behind every solemn promise** *(inferred: the striking thing is that Zedekiah's oath was sworn to *Nebuchadnezzar*, a foreign, pagan king — yet God says "**it is MY OATH that he despised, and MY COVENANT that he broke**" (v.19): the finding is that God takes a *sworn word* — even one given to an unbeliever — as *invested with his own name*, so that *keeping faith* is a sacred obligation and *breaking it* is a sin against God himself; the inner-being weight is on the *integrity of one's word* — a promise made "under oath" binds the interior before God, and to "despise" it (treat it lightly, break it for advantage) is to despise the God who witnesses it; the finding is that faithfulness to commitments is not a mere social nicety but a matter of *covenant integrity* that God guards jealously — the trustworthy word is an image of God's own faithfulness, and the broken word a betrayal that "returns upon the head" of the breaker; and the reason the oath bound Zedekiah was "**that the kingdom might be HUMBLE and not lift itself up**" (v.14) — the oath was meant to teach *humility* (accepting one's subordinate place), and its breaking was an act of proud self-assertion seeking Egypt's help rather than accepting the humbling)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the sacred weight of a sworn word ("he despised the oath… it is my oath that he despised" — a promise, even to a pagan, invested with God's own name)* (thread — a gem: keeping faith a sacred obligation, breaking one's word a sin against God who witnesses it, w/ 17:18-19, Ps 15:4, Eccl 5:4-5, Matt 5:33-37); *integrity of the word as covenant faithfulness (the trustworthy word an image of God's own faithfulness; the broken word a betrayal that recoils)* (new — a gem: faithfulness to commitments a matter of covenant integrity God guards, w/ 17:19, Num 30:2, Jas 5:12); *the oath meant to teach humility ("that the kingdom might be humble and not lift itself up" — the broken oath an act of proud self-assertion)* (new — w/ 17:14, refusing the humbling).
