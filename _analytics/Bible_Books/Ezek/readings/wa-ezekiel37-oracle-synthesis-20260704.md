## EZE-dry-bones · Ezekiel 37:1–28 · [D, multi] — the resurrection of the hopeless, dead interior by God's Spirit; the joined nation; the everlasting covenant

**The reading.** Set down in a valley "**full of bones**… very dry" (vv.1–2), Ezekiel is asked, "**Son of man, can these bones live?**" and answers, "**O Lord GOD, you know**" (v.3). He prophesies; the bones join, take sinew, flesh, skin — but "**there was no breath in them**" (v.8). He prophesies again to the breath: "**Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe on these slain, that they may live**" (v.9), and "**they lived and stood on their feet, an exceedingly great army**" (v.10). The meaning is given: "**these bones are the whole house of Israel. Behold, they say, 'Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost; we are indeed cut off'**" (v.11); to which God answers, "**I will open your graves and raise you from your graves… And I will put my Spirit within you, and you shall live**" (vv.12–14). The finding is **the *hopeless, dead* interior — "**our bones are dried up, our hope is lost, we are cut off**" — *raised to life* by God's *Spirit* ("I will put my Spirit within you, and you shall live")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the deepest form of the book's promise: not the *mending* of a weak interior but the *resurrection* of a *dead* one — the self reduced to "very dry bones" (past all hope, "cut off"), *unable to give itself life*, made alive only by the *breath/Spirit* of God breathed into it from outside; so the interior's ultimate renewal is *resurrection*, God's work on the utterly dead** *(inferred: the vision addresses the *extremest* inner-being condition — not weakness or sickness but *death*: "**very dry bones**" (v.2), the self's own verdict "**our HOPE IS LOST; we are CUT OFF**" (v.11) — a despair beyond the guilt-despair of 33:10, the sense of being *finished*, dead past reviving; the finding is that God's power reaches even *there* — "**can these bones live?**": the honest answer is "**you know**" (only God can say), because to human eyes dry bones cannot live; and the resurrection comes in *two stages* that make the inner-being point — first the bones are re-assembled with sinew, flesh, skin (a *body*, but "**no breath in them**", v.8: outward reconstruction without life), and only when the *breath/Spirit* (ruach) is breathed in do they "**live and stand**" (v.10): the finding is that the interior's true life is not mere outward restoration (a reassembled but breathless form) but the *inbreathing of God's Spirit* — the same Spirit promised in 36:27; the self can be outwardly put back together and still be *dead* until God's breath enters; and "**I will put MY SPIRIT within you, and you shall LIVE**" (v.14) names the whole: the hopeless, dead interior is not coaxed or repaired but *raised*, given life by God's own Spirit — the ultimate answer to "how can we live?" (33:10) is *resurrection*, a life the dead cannot generate and can only receive)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the hopeless, dead interior ("our bones are dried up, our hope is lost, we are cut off" — despair beyond weakness, the sense of being finished)* (thread — a gem: the extremest inner-being condition, past reviving, w/ 37:11, Ps 88, Lam 3:18, 2 Cor 1:9); *resurrection, not mending ("can these bones live?… I will open your graves and raise you" — God's power raising the utterly dead, not repairing the weak)* (new — a gem: the interior's ultimate renewal is resurrection, a life the dead cannot generate, w/ 37:3-14, Eph 2:1,4-5, John 11:25, Rom 4:17); *outward form without life vs the inbreathed Spirit ("flesh… but no breath in them"… "breathe on these slain, that they may live" — reassembly is not life until God's Spirit enters)* (new — a gem: the interior can be outwardly restored yet dead until God breathes in his Spirit, w/ 37:8-10, 36:27, Gen 2:7, John 6:63); *God's Spirit as the source of life ("I will put my Spirit within you, and you shall live" — life given, not achieved)* (thread — a gem, the ultimate answer to "how can we live?", w/ 37:14, 36:27, 33:10).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the hopeless, dead interior; resurrection not mending; outward form without life vs the inbreathed Spirit; God's Spirit as the source of life. Chapter 37 gives the book's deepest promise — the dead, dried-up, hope-lost interior *raised* by God's Spirit, its ultimate life not repaired but *resurrected*.
- **Book-level note (Ezekiel batch 6, chs 33–37):** the inner-being peak of the book. Its findings: the guilt-crushed "how can we live?" answered by God's "*I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked… turn back, why will you die?*" (33); the self-feeding shepherds against the true Shepherd who "*I, I myself*" seeks the lost and weak (34); the complete divine remedy — cleansing, the *heart of stone replaced by flesh*, and *God's Spirit put within to enable obedience*, all for God's name's sake and producing shame (36); and the *resurrection* of the dead, hope-lost interior by God's Spirit (37). Master-findings: **God longs for the sinner's life and pleads 'turn and live'**; **the true Shepherd's care runs toward the neediest**; **the stony heart is transplanted, not reformed, and obedience is empowered by the indwelling Spirit**; and **the ultimate renewal of the interior is resurrection — a life God alone gives.**
- **Threads:** the new heart / Spirit (← 11:19, 36:26); turn and live (← 33:11); the one Shepherd David / covenant of peace (← 34:23-25; 37:24-26); God's Spirit as life (36:27; 37:14).
