## MIC-mountain-peace · Mic 4:1–5 · [D, multi] — the resolved walk in the name of the LORD

**The reading.** The vision of the mountain of the LORD draws the nations to learn his ways: "that he may **teach us his ways** and that we may **walk in his paths**" (v.2); swords become plowshares, and each sits unafraid "under his vine and under his fig tree" (vv.3–4). The inner-being hinge is the closing resolve: "for all the peoples **walk each in the name of its god, but we will walk in the name of the LORD our God forever and ever**" (v.5). The finding is **a deliberate, differentiated allegiance — whatever the nations do, *we* will walk in the LORD's name** *(stated)*. The operation is **the resolved interior that chooses its God against the surrounding plurality** *(inferred: amid the universal vision (all nations flowing to Zion), v.5 turns personal and resolute — "*we* will walk in the name of the LORD" — a settled, present-tense commitment held *while* the peoples walk each after its own god; the finding is fidelity as a *chosen walk*, a daily allegiance maintained against the pull of a plural world; and the peace of vv.3–4 (unafraid under one's vine) is the fruit of that walk — security as the by-product of the resolved allegiance)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the resolved, differentiated walk (we will walk in the name of the LORD, whatever the nations do)* (new — chosen allegiance against plurality); *learning and walking God's ways (teachability + the walked path)* (new — w/ 6:8's "walk humbly"); *security as the fruit of allegiance (unafraid under the vine)* (new).

## MIC-labor-remnant · Mic 4:6–13 · [D, multi] — hope through travail; God's hidden plan

**The reading.** The restoration comes through pain: "the **lame I will make the remnant**" (v.7); "**writhe and groan, O daughter of Zion, like a woman in labor**… you shall go to Babylon. **There you shall be rescued; there the LORD will redeem you**" (v.10). And the assurance about the enemies: "**they do not know the thoughts of the LORD; they do not understand his plan**, that he has gathered them as sheaves to the threshing floor" (v.12). The finding is **deliverance reached *through* travail, and a divine plan hidden behind apparent defeat** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior called to endure the labor-pains of exile, trusting a purpose it cannot yet see** *(inferred: the labor imagery (vv.9–10) frames exile not as the end but as *birth-pangs* — pain with an issue ("there you shall be redeemed"); and v.12 names the hidden-plan datum — the nations, and the sufferers, "do not understand his plan": the finding is that God's redemptive purpose often works *through* and *behind* what looks like catastrophe, invisible to those in it; the inner-being posture required is endurance of the travail while trusting the unseen "thoughts of the LORD" — the lame made a remnant, the labor issuing in deliverance)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *deliverance through travail (exile as birth-pangs, not the end)* (new — pain with an issue); *God's hidden plan behind apparent defeat* (thread — w/ Job 42:2, the un-thwartable purpose); *the weak made the remnant (the lame gathered)* (new).
