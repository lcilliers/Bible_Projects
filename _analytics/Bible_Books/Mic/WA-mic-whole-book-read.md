4. **The exodus recital, transformed**: Mic 6:4 (past fact, legal evidence in the LORD's own case) → Mic 7:15 (identical historical reference, now a FUTURE promise of a new exodus) — the same historical anchor serving two opposite rhetorical functions.
5. **The nations, from hostility to total reversal**: Mic 4:11 (hostile gathering, "let our eyes gaze upon Zion") → Mic 7:16-17 (total shame, silence, deafness, dread, "lick the dust like a serpent") — the book's most complete single-collective reversal.
6. **The shepherd-vocabulary thread, three modes**: Mic 2:12 (declared future gathering) → Mic 5:4 (the ruler's own declared shepherding) → Mic 7:14 (a direct petition, asking the LORD to shepherd) — one image, three grammatical postures (future declaration, third-person description, first-person prayer).
7. **"Steadfast love" (chesed, H2617A), the book's closing keyword**: Mic 6:8 (the human requirement, "love kindness") → Mic 7:18 (the LORD's own defining character) → Mic 7:20 (the covenant's own fulfillment, "steadfast love to Abraham") — one word, three positions, carrying the book's ethical argument from requirement to divine ground to promised fulfillment.
8. **"From of old"/"ancient days" bracketing the book's hope**: Mic 5:2 (the coming ruler's own ancient origin) → Mic 7:20 (the covenant sworn "from the days of old") — the same phrase grounding both the messianic hope and the closing covenant appeal.
9. **"Pleads my cause" (rib, H7378/H7379) reversed**: Mic 6:1-2 (the LORD's own legal case AGAINST the people) → Mic 7:9 (the LORD pleading Micah's OWN cause) — the identical legal vocabulary, its direction of advocacy completely reversed.

## Closing synthesis

**What recurred and closed within the book (confirmed by repetition, not left as a single-chapter guess).**
- **Micah's own first-person voice** is the book's structural spine: personal lament (1:8) → emphatic self-declaration ("as for me," 3:8, empowered by the Spirit) → dialogue with the LORD (6:3-8) → lament again (7:1) → the identical "as for me" construction now for personal trust (7:7) → genuine confession (7:9). The prophet moves from lamenting the people's condition to modelling, in his own person, the confession and trust Mic 6:8 requires of everyone.
- **"My people" is a deliberately unstable label**, never settling into one referent: the LORD's own possession (1:9), the judgment's victims (2:4, 9), the oppressors themselves (2:8), a third, scorning party (6:16), and finally the household turned against itself (7:5-6, "a man's enemies are the men of his own house"). The instability is the book's own technique for showing how deeply corruption has penetrated — it cannot even be cleanly sorted into "us" and "them."
- **Judgment-to-hope pivots are a confirmed structural device**, not an isolated seam: Mic 2:1-11→12-13, Mic 3:12→4:1 (the clearest, almost-verbatim reversal — the ruined temple mount rebuilt as the highest of the mountains), and Mic 6:16→7:7 all repeat the identical shape. The mechanism is never explained by the text; the pattern itself is confirmed by its threefold repetition.
- **Divine empowerment of a human figure is a confirmed, twice-repeated pattern**: Micah himself (3:8, "filled with... the Spirit of the LORD") and the coming Bethlehem ruler (5:4, "in the strength of the LORD... the majesty of the name of the LORD his God") — the same construction, two different human roles.
- **"Steadfast love" (chesed) carries the book's own ethical argument to completion**: the human requirement (6:8, "love kindness") is grounded in the LORD's own defining character (7:18, "he delights in steadfast love") and fulfilled in covenant promise (7:20, "steadfast love to Abraham") — one word, three positions, the cleanest fully-resolved thread in the whole book.
- **The "shepherd" metaphor stretches across four distinct registers** within one book: false/hireling leadership (3:11), the true ruler's own caring rule (5:4), violent military subjugation (5:6), and direct petitionary prayer (7:14) — confirming this is a deliberately flexible, not a fixed, image.
- **The three-office corruption charge (heads/judges/notables, bribery) closes at its most explicit** (7:3, "thus they weave it together"), after being stated three times (3:1/9/11, 6:11-12, 7:3).

**What never resolved — held open by the text itself, not smoothed over.**
- **The peace-vs-conquest tension** is the book's most significant genuinely unresolved feature, recurring in near-identical shape twice: universal disarmament (4:3-4) alongside Zion's own militant threshing (4:13); the remnant of Jacob as gentle dew (5:7) alongside the remnant as a devastating lion (5:8) — the SAME collective, the SAME location, two opposite postures, both times with no textual harmonisation supplied.
- **Two imperial threats are named without their relationship being explained**: Babylon as the place of exile and rescue (4:10), Assyria as the live, present threat the book expects deliverance from (5:5-6) — both sit inside a book whose own superscription (1:1) dates its kings to the Assyrian period.
- **Several figures are never disambiguated**: "she who is in labor" (5:3); the "Breaker" who leads the flock out (2:13), plausibly but not explicitly resonant with the Bethlehem ruler (5:2-4); the precise identity of "they" streaming to Zion (7:12, exiles or nations).
- **v3:12's own later scriptural resonance** and **the traditional "Micah"/"who is like Yah" name-pun with 7:18** are both flagged as real possibilities but explicitly NOT asserted, since neither is confirmed by this book's own base-extract lexicon data — a discipline held consistently from Mic 1's town-name puns through to the book's closing verse.

**What stays open past this book, for future cross-book/whole-book comparison.**
- The divine-possessive "my people/my X" pattern (tracked here against Joel and Obadiah).
- The "come down"/"tread" (H3381/H1869) action-types as possible stock vocabulary for divine and hostile approach.
- Whether the "same term, opposite postures" device (many nations; my people; dew/lion) recurs as a deliberate rhetorical strategy in other prophetic books.
- How chesed functions across the wider corpus, now that Micah's own three-position argument is complete.
