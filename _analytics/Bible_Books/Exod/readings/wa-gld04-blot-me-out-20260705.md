## GLD-04-blot-me-out · Exodus 32:30–33:6 · [the mediator's self-offering; the presence withdrawn; the first mourning] — "blot me out of your book", and "I will not go up among you"

**The passage.** "You have **sinned** (chata) a great **sin** (chataah); perhaps I can make **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) for your **sin** (chattat)" (32:30); "this people has **sinned** (chata) a great **sin** (chataah)" (32:31); "if you will forgive their **sin** (chattat) — but if not, **blot me out** of your book" (32:32); "whoever has **sinned** (chata), I will blot out" (32:33); "in the day I visit, I will visit their **sin** (chattat)" (32:34); a plague (32:35); "go up to the land I swore to **give** (natan)… but I will not go up among you… for you are **stiff-necked** (qasheh), lest I consume you" (33:1–3); the people **mourned** (aval) the **disastrous** (ra) word, stripped their ornaments (33:4); "you are **stiff-necked** (qasheh)… take off your ornaments, that I may know what to do" (33:5–6).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **chata / chataah / chattat (sin) + kipper / kaphar (atonement) — the *tentative atonement sought* (32:30).** "'you have **sinned** (chata) a great **sin** (chataah)… perhaps I can make **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) for your **sin** (chattat)'" *(stated)*. The *sin*-root sounds three times (the calf-sin *named and re-named* — the scene's dominating fact); **kipper/kaphar** — *atone/cover*: the gem is the *tentative "perhaps"* — even Moses is *unsure atonement is possible* for so great a sin; the interior of *the mediator going up not in confidence but in hope* — *"perhaps"* — to *stand between the sin and its penalty* *(inferred: chata/kipper = the tentative atonement sought — "perhaps I can atone", the mediator's uncertain hope)*.
- **chata / chataah (sin) — the *sin owned before God* (32:31).** "'alas, this people has **sinned** (chata) a great **sin** (chataah); they have made gods of gold'" *(stated)* — the mediator *names the sin plainly before God* (no minimizing — unlike Aaron, 32:24): the interior of *honest confession on the people's behalf* — *owning the gravity* he pleads against *(inferred: chata/chataah = the sin owned before God — honest confession, not minimized)*.
- **chattat (sin) — the *self-offering* (32:32).** "'if you will forgive their **sin** (chattat) — but if not, **blot me out** of your book that you have written'" *(stated)*. **chattat** — *sin*: the gem is *THE peak of intercession* — Moses offers *his own place in God's book* for the people: *"blot ME out"*. Where GLD-02 pleaded God's *name and oath*, here the mediator *offers himself* — *he would rather be erased than see the people destroyed*. And the *broken sentence* — *"if you will forgive their sin—"* trails off, *unable to finish* — the *anguish* of a love that cannot voice the alternative. The interior of *self-substituting love* — *the mediator laying down his own life/standing for the guilty* *(inferred: chattat = the self-offering — "blot me out", the mediator's self-substituting love)*.
- **chata (sinned) + chattat (sin) — *God's answer: each bears his own* (32:33–34).** "'whoever has **sinned** (chata) against me, I will blot out… in the day I visit, I will visit their **sin** (chattat)'" *(stated)*: the gem is *the answer that refuses the substitution* — *God will not blot Moses for the people* (*"whoever has sinned"* — individual responsibility), yet *relents from total destruction* (*"go, lead the people"*, the angel sent); *judgment deferred, not erased* (*"in the day I visit"*). The interior (of God) of *justice that holds the sinner accountable* — *the mediator's offer honored but not accepted*; each *bears his own sin* *(inferred: chata/chattat = each bears his own — the substitution refused, judgment deferred)*.
- **natan (give) + qasheh (stiff-necked) — the *terrible mercy: the presence withdrawn* (33:1–3).** "'go up to the land I swore to **give** (natan)… but I will **not go up among you**… for you are **stiff-necked** (qasheh), lest I consume you'" *(stated)*. **natan** — *give*; **qasheh** — *stiff-necked*: the gem is *the most terrible consequence* — *not destruction but ABSENCE*: God will *keep his promise* (the land *given*) and *send an angel*, but *will not go himself* — *because his holiness would consume a stiff-necked people*. The interior finding: *the withdrawal of the Presence is the deepest judgment* — worse than the plague; *the gift without the Giver*; and it is a *terrible mercy* (he withdraws *lest he consume*) *(inferred: natan/qasheh = the presence withdrawn — the gift kept but the Giver absent, a terrible mercy toward the stiff-necked)*.
- **aval (mourned) + ra (disastrous) — the *first mourning* (33:4).** "when the people heard this **disastrous** (ra) word, they **mourned** (aval), and no one put on his ornaments" *(stated)*. **aval** — *mourn*; **ra** — *evil/disastrous*: the gem is *the first sign of contrition* — the *stiff-necked* people, hearing *"I will not go with you"*, **mourn**; the interior of *grief that God's absence, not merely his wrath, would be unbearable* — *the loss of the Presence felt as the true disaster*; the beginning of a *turning* *(inferred: aval/ra = the first mourning — the loss of the Presence felt as the real disaster, the beginning of contrition)*.
- **qasheh (stiff-necked) — the *stripping as humbling* (33:5).** *qasheh distinct from 33:3:* "'you are **stiff-necked**… take off your ornaments, that I may know what to do with you'" *(stated)*: where 33:3 gave the *verdict*, 33:5 turns it to a *summons to humble* — the *ornaments stripped* as a sign; the interior-context of *the proud neck bent* — *the humbling that opens a space for mercy* ("that I may know what to do") *(inferred: qasheh = the stripping as humbling — the stiff neck bent, a space opened for mercy)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **the self-offering (chata/kipper 32:30 → chata/chataah 32:31 → chattat 32:32 → chata/chattat 32:33–34):** Moses goes up *"perhaps"* to atone, *owns the sin honestly*, and *offers himself* — *"blot me out"*; God *refuses the substitution* (each bears his own) yet *relents from destruction* and *defers judgment* — the mediator's *self-offering love* met by *justice that will not erase the guilty by erasing the innocent*.
2. **the presence withdrawn, the first mourning (natan/qasheh 33:1–3 → aval/ra 33:4 → qasheh 33:5):** God keeps the *promise* but withholds the *Presence* (*"I will not go up"*) — the *terrible mercy* toward the stiff-necked; and the people, hearing it, **mourn** and *strip their ornaments* — *the loss of God himself felt as the real disaster* — the *first contrition*, the *proud neck beginning to bend*.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** the *sin*-forms (chata/chataah/chattat — the *one great calf-sin* named and re-named through the scene: Moses' *tentative atonement* / his *honest owning* / his *plea for forgiveness* / God's *deferred visiting*); **kipper/kaphar (the *tentative* atonement sought — "perhaps")**; **chattat 32:32 (the *self-offering* — "blot me out")**; natan (the land *given* — promise kept); **qasheh ×2 (the *stiff-necked* verdict that withdraws the Presence / the *humbling* that strips the ornaments)**; **aval/ra (the *first mourning* — the loss of the Presence felt as the real disaster)**.
- **Whose interior (multi-interior):** **Moses** — *intercession deepened to self-offering* ("blot me out" — self-substituting love, the broken sentence of anguish); **God** — *refusing the substitution* (each bears his own sin), *relenting from destruction* but *withdrawing his Presence* (the terrible mercy toward the stiff-necked); **Israel** — the *first mourning*, the *stripping* — contrition beginning. The two gems: **the mediator's self-offering** and **the presence withdrawn**.
- **Threads:** **the self-substituting mediator** (32:32 → "I could wish myself accursed for my brothers", Rom 9:3; the Servant "poured out his soul to death… bore the sin of many", Isa 53:12; Christ the substitute — Moses' offer *refused here* because *no man can*, pointing beyond himself); **each bears his own sin** (32:33 → "the soul who sins shall die", Ezek 18:20 — individual responsibility, held with the later true substitution); **the presence as the true good** (33:3 "I will not go up" → Moses' whole plea "if your presence does not go, do not bring us up" 33:15 — the withdrawal *the* disaster; the gift without the Giver worthless); **stiff-necked** (qasheh 33:3,5 ← 32:9 → 34:9 — the obstinate interior, the reason for withdrawal); **contrition** (aval 33:4 — the first genuine mourning of the people, the turning that the whole wilderness-testing arc had not yet produced).
- **Guarded against:** reading Moses' "blot me out" as a bargaining ploy when it is *self-substituting love* (the broken sentence marks anguish, not tactic); collapsing the many *sin*-forms into nothing when each occurrence *does* something (sought-atonement / owned / pled / visited) — but not inventing distinct *meanings* for the same calf-sin; treating God's refusal (32:33) as mere severity when it holds *justice* (each bears his own) *with* mercy (relenting from destruction); missing that the *withdrawal of Presence* (33:3) is the *deepest* judgment (worse than the plague) — the finding the people themselves grasp by mourning; not over-reading the ornament-stripping (33:6) beyond the *humbling* the text gives.
