## ZECH-wickedness-removed · Zechariah 5:1–11 · [D, multi] — the curse on theft and false oaths; wickedness personified and carried away

**The reading.** Two visions of *evil dealt with*. First, a flying scroll — "**the curse that goes out over the face of the whole land**" against "**everyone who steals**" and "**everyone who swears falsely**", entering "**the house of the thief**" and consuming it (vv.3–4). Then a woman sitting in a basket, whom the angel names: "**This is Wickedness**" (v.8); she is thrust back, the lead cover sealed, and the basket carried off "**to the land of Shinar**" (v.11). The finding is **evil *named, contained, and removed from the land* — the "curse" pursuing theft and false-swearing into the very house, and "Wickedness" itself personified, sealed, and carried away** *(stated)*. The operation is **the vision of a purged interior/community: specific sins (theft, false oaths — offences against neighbour and against God's name) are actively *rooted out*, and evil as such ("Wickedness") is *personified, confined, and deported* — not merely forgiven but *taken away* from the place where God's people dwell** *(inferred: the two visions together portray God's *cleansing of the land* of evil — the finding is that dealing with sin is not only *pardon* (ch.3) but *removal*: the flying scroll targets specific interior-rooted sins ("steals", "swears falsely by my name" — the violation of the neighbour's goods and of God's name, vv.3–4), pursuing them relentlessly into "the house" until consumed; and the basket-vision personifies *evil itself* — "**this is Wickedness**" (v.8, ha-rish'ah) — as a thing to be *contained* (sealed under a lead weight) and *exiled* ("to the land of Shinar", i.e. Babylon, v.11), removed from the holy land; the finding is that God's purpose for his people includes not just the forgiveness of the sinner but the *expulsion of wickedness* from among them — evil is named for what it is, weighted down, and carried out, so that the community God dwells in is cleansed of it; the interior lesson is that grace does not merely cover sin but *works to remove* it, and that evil, however it may seem to sit enthroned (the woman *sitting* in the basket), is destined to be sealed and carried away)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *evil named for what it is ("this is Wickedness" — sin personified and identified, not excused)* (new — a gem: evil labelled plainly, w/ 5:8, Isa 5:20 inverted, Rom 12:9); *sin removed, not only forgiven (the curse rooting out theft/false-oaths; Wickedness contained and carried away)* (thread — a gem: grace works to expel evil, not merely cover it, w/ 5:3-4,8-11, Ps 101, 2 Cor 7:1); *the specific sins pursued (theft against neighbour, false swearing against God's name — offences relentlessly targeted)* (new — w/ 5:3-4, 8:16-17); *evil destined for confinement and exile ("sealed… carried to Shinar" — wickedness that seems enthroned is removed)* (thread — w/ 5:9-11).
