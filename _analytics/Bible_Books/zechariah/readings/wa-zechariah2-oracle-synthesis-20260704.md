## ZECH-apple-of-eye · Zechariah 2:1–13 · [D, multi] — God as wall of fire and glory within; the touched apple of his eye

**The reading.** A man goes to measure Jerusalem, but the word comes that the city will overflow its walls — "**Jerusalem shall be inhabited as villages without walls… And I will be to her a wall of fire all around, declares the LORD, and I will be the glory in her midst**" (vv.4–5). Then the tenderest image: "**he who touches you touches the apple of his eye**" (v.8). And the promise of nearness: "**Sing and rejoice, O daughter of Zion, for behold, I come and I will dwell in your midst**" (v.10). The finding is **God himself as the people's *protection* ("a wall of fire") and *glory* ("in her midst") — and his people as *"the apple of his eye"*, the tenderest, most-instinctively-guarded part of him** *(stated)*. The operation is **security relocated from *structures* (walls the self builds) to *God's own presence* ("I will be… a wall of fire… the glory in her midst") — and the depth of God's care for his people figured in the most protective reflex there is: to harm them is to jab *the eye*, which the whole body flinches to shield** *(inferred: the vision reframes *security* — the instinct to measure and wall the city (v.2) is answered by God saying the true defence is *himself* ("I will be… a wall of fire", v.5): the finding is that the interior's safety is not in the barriers it constructs but in God's encircling presence, and its glory not in its own grandeur but in God "in her midst"; and v.8's "**the apple of his eye**" (bavat eno — the pupil, the tenderest and most-guarded spot) is one of Scripture's most intimate disclosures of God's care: the finding is that God relates to his people with the *instinctive protectiveness* one has for one's own eye — the part of the body most reflexively shielded, most painfully touched; to "touch" God's people is to touch *him* at his most sensitive; the finding is that the interior is not merely defended by God but *cherished* with a tenderness as involuntary and total as the eye's flinch — its worth to God is that of his own pupil, and his response to its nearness is "sing and rejoice… I will dwell in your midst" (v.10))*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *God himself as wall and glory ("I will be to her a wall of fire… the glory in her midst" — security relocated from built structures to God's presence)* (thread — a gem: safety in God's encircling presence, not self-built barriers, w/ 2:5, Ps 46, Isa 26:1); *the apple of his eye ("he who touches you touches the apple of his eye" — God's instinctive, total protectiveness toward his people)* (new — a gem: the interior cherished with the reflexive tenderness one has for one's own eye, w/ 2:8, Deut 32:10, Ps 17:8); *God dwelling in the midst, rejoicing ("I will dwell in your midst… sing and rejoice")* (thread — nearness as the ground of joy, w/ 2:10, 8:3, Zeph 3:17).
