## ZECH-rejected-shepherd · Zechariah 11:1–17 · [D, multi] — the good shepherd rejected and priced at thirty pieces; the worthless shepherd

**The reading.** The prophet enacts a dark allegory. Commanded to "**become shepherd of the flock doomed to slaughter**" (v.4), he tends them with two staffs, "**Favour**" and "**Union**" (v.7). But "**they detested me**" (v.8), so he resigns the task and breaks the staff Favour. Then the wage: "**they weighed out as my wages thirty pieces of silver**" (v.12) — a contemptible price — which the LORD calls "**the lordly price at which I was priced by them**", telling him to "**throw it to the potter**" (v.13). In place of the good shepherd, God raises "**a foolish shepherd**" who "**does not care for those being destroyed… but devours the flesh of the fat ones**" (vv.15–16); "**Woe to my worthless shepherd, who deserts the flock!**" (v.17). The finding is **the *good shepherd rejected* ("they detested me") and *contemptibly priced* ("thirty pieces of silver") — and, in his place, the *worthless shepherd* who "does not care" and *devours* the flock** *(stated)*. The operation is **the tragedy of *spurned care*: a shepherd who tends "doomed" sheep with "favour" and "union" is *detested* and *valued at a slave's price* (thirty pieces of silver) — the wounding of genuine care by ingratitude — and the consequence is the loss of the true shepherd and the rise of a *self-serving* one who "devours" rather than tends** *(inferred: the allegory turns on the *rejection of genuine care* — the shepherd tends the "flock doomed to slaughter" (the most thankless charge) with staffs named "**Favour**" and "**Union**" (grace and fellowship, the very goods a shepherd gives), and is *"detested"* for it (v.8); the finding is that authentic care can be *spurned*, and the spurning wounds the carer ("I became impatient with them, and they also detested me"); the depth of the contempt is measured by the *wage* — "**thirty pieces of silver**" (v.12), the price of a gored slave (Exod 21:32), a "lordly price" said with bitter irony — the finding is that ingratitude can *value* the giver of grace at almost nothing, an insulting reckoning of what genuine care is worth; and the consequence (vv.15–17) is the *withdrawal of the good shepherd and the rise of the worthless one* — "a foolish shepherd who does not care… but **devours**": the finding is that when true care is rejected, the interior/community is left to *self-serving* leadership that "does not care" and "devours the flock" — the good shepherd spurned, the predatory one inherited; rejecting genuine care is not neutral but opens the door to exploitation)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *genuine care spurned ("I tended them… but they detested me" — authentic care wounded by ingratitude)* (thread — a gem: the spurning of the good shepherd's grace, w/ 11:7-8, John 1:11, 10:11-13); *care valued at nothing ("thirty pieces of silver — the lordly price at which I was priced" — ingratitude's insulting reckoning of the giver of grace)* (new — a gem: the contemptible pricing of genuine care, w/ 11:12-13, Exod 21:32, Matt 26:15); *the worthless shepherd who devours ("does not care for those being destroyed… but devours the flesh of the fat ones")* (thread — a gem: self-serving leadership that exploits rather than tends, w/ 11:16-17, Ezek 34:2-4, John 10:12-13); *rejecting the good shepherd inherits the predatory one (spurned care opening the door to exploitation)* (new — w/ 11:15-17).
