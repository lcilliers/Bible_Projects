## LAM-05-A · Lam 5:1–18 · [D, multi, thread] — the communal lament; the sick heart; joy ceased; guilt owned

**The reading.** The final poem drops the acrostic form and becomes a plain communal prayer: "**Remember, O LORD, what has befallen us; look, and see our disgrace!**" (v.1). It catalogues the loss — orphaned, weary ("we are given **no rest**", v.5), enslaved, hungry. The interior toll: "the **joy of our hearts has ceased**; our **dancing has been turned to mourning**" (v.15); "for this our **heart has become sick**, for these things our **eyes have grown dim**" (v.17); and the confession, "**woe to us, for we have sinned**!" (v.16). The finding is **a whole community's grief — joy ceased, heart sick, rest gone — owned with confession** *(stated)*. The operation is **the corporate self naming its collective sickness of heart and turning it Godward with "Remember"** *(inferred: the shift to "us/our" (the community) makes this a *shared* interior — "the joy of our hearts has ceased… our heart has become sick" (vv.15,17): the finding is that grief here is corporate, a whole people's joylessness and heart-sickness; and it opens (as ch.1 did) with the plea to be *seen* — "Remember, O LORD… look, and see" (v.1), the same recourse of the comfortless self; the confession "we have sinned" (v.16) keeps the honest posture of the whole book (owned guilt, not protested innocence); "we are given no rest" (v.5) echoes the lost *nuach* of 1:3 — the community, like the city, can find no place to lay itself down; the inner-being datum is the shared sick heart, joy ceased, turning its collective disgrace Godward)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *Remember* (H2142, zakar — "Remember, O LORD"), *disgrace* (H2781, cherpah), *rest* (H5117, nuach — "given no rest"), *sinned* (H2398, chata — twice), *iniquities* (H5771, avon), *raped* (H6031, anah), *joy* (H4885, masos — "the joy of our hearts has ceased"), *hearts/heart* (H3820, leb — twice), *become sick* (H1739, daveh — "our heart has become sick"), *desolate* (H8074, shamem) carried. *masos*/*leb*/*daveh* (joy ceased / heart / sick) carry the interior toll; *zakar* (Remember) the plea; *nuach* (no rest, echoing 1:3) the lost rest; correctly tagged. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The corporate sick heart (grief as shared)** — "the joy of our hearts has ceased… our heart has become sick" (vv.15,17): the finding that this grief is *collective* — a whole community's joylessness and heart-sickness, not one individual's (as ch.3 was); the shared interior naming its condition. (2) **"Remember, O LORD" as the comfortless community's recourse** — "Remember… look, and see our disgrace" (v.1): the finding that the corporate self, like the personified city (1:9,11), can still do one thing — ask God to *see and remember* — the minimal act of a faith that has lost everything but the address. And "no rest" (v.5, nuach) echoes 1:3 — the lost *nuach* is corporate too.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the corporate sick heart (grief as shared)* (new — the communal interior, joy ceased, heart sick); *"Remember, O LORD" as the comfortless community's recourse (the plea to be seen)* (thread — 1:9,11 → 5:1); *owned guilt in corporate grief (we have sinned)* (thread — the confessing lament); *no rest / lost nuach, corporate* (thread w/ 1:3, Job 3:13).

## LAM-05-B · Lam 5:19–22 · [D, multi, thread] — the final prayer: restore us — held against an unresolved dread

**The reading.** The book ends not in resolution but in a prayer suspended over a fear: "**but you, O LORD, reign forever**; your throne endures to all generations" (v.19); "why do you **forget us** forever, why do you **forsake us** for so many days?" (v.20); "**Restore us to yourself, O LORD, that we may be restored! Renew our days as of old** — **unless you have utterly rejected us**, and you remain exceedingly angry with us" (vv.21–22). The finding is **a plea for restoration grounded in God's eternal reign, held open against an unresolved dread of final rejection** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self reaching for restoration it cannot presume, ending on a plea rather than an assurance** *(inferred: the ending is remarkable for its *honesty* — after the turn of ch.3 (hope re-kindled), the book does *not* close with triumphant assurance but with "restore us… *unless* you have utterly rejected us" (vv.21–22); the finding is that faith can hold a real hope (grounded in "you, O LORD, reign forever", v.19, and the memory of God's chesed, 3:22) *together with* an unresolved fear (the "unless" of v.22) — it prays for restoration it cannot presume; and the theology of restoration is precise — "Restore us *to yourself*… that we may be restored" (v.21): the return is God's work (he must restore us before we can be restored), and its goal is *God himself* ("to yourself"), not merely improved circumstances (the same "God as portion" of 3:24); the inner-being datum: mature faith prays honestly into an open future — it hopes without presuming, holds the "unless", and rests its whole plea on God's eternal reign and its own longing to be restored *to him*)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *angry* (H7107, qatsaph — "you remain exceedingly angry") carried; the reign/forget/forsake/restore language sits in gate-2 content, read structurally. *qatsaph* (angry) carries the unresolved dread of v.22; the "restore us to yourself" rests on the verse structure (shuv — return, doubled), unambiguous. Sound (sparse substrate, structural reading of a bare prayer).

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **Hope holding an unresolved "unless"** — "restore us… unless you have utterly rejected us" (vv.21–22): the finding that mature faith can pray a real hope (grounded in God's eternal reign, v.19) *together with* an honest, unresolved dread — it hopes without presuming, ending on a plea, not an assurance. The book's refusal of a tidy resolution is itself a profound inner-being witness: faith prays truthfully into an open future. (2) **Restoration as God's work, toward God himself** — "restore us *to yourself*, that we may be restored" (v.21): the finding that the return is God's doing (he must restore before we can be restored) and its *goal* is God himself (the "God as portion" of 3:24), not merely better circumstances — the deepest longing is not relief but *re-union*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *hope holding an unresolved "unless" (faith praying into an open future)* (new — the book's honest ending; hope without presumption); *restoration as God's work toward God himself (restore us TO yourself)* (thread — God as the goal/portion, w/ 3:24); *the plea grounded in God's eternal reign* (new — the one fixed point).
