## ECC-01-A · Ecc 1:2–11 · [F, multi, thread] — the perception of futility; weariness; the insatiable eye/ear; oblivion

**The reading.** Before the book argues anything it establishes an *arena the inner being must live inside*: a world of ceaseless motion that never arrives. Generations go and come, the sun races back to where it rose, the wind wheels "around and around," the streams run to a sea "not full" (vv.4–7). The finding is not a doctrine of futility but a **perception seated in a watching self** — the person who sees the cycles and feels their weight: "all things are **full of weariness**; a man cannot **utter** it" (v.8) *(stated)*. Three inner-being operations are folded together here. (1) **Weariness** — the exhaustion of watching motion without progress. (2) The **insatiable senses** — "the **eye is not satisfied** with seeing, nor the **ear filled** with hearing" (v.8): the taking-in faculties that never reach *enough*, the first sounding of the never-satisfied thread that runs the whole book. (3) **Oblivion** — "there is **no remembrance** of former things, nor will there be any remembrance of later things" (v.11): the self faces not only a wearying world but a *forgetting* one, where its own trace will not hold *(inferred: the three together define the pressure the rest of the book's inner life answers to — a self that tires, cannot be filled, and will be forgotten)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** The substrate carries the cycle-verbs (*goes, comes, rises, returns, run, flow*) correctly as `standalone` actions of the world, not the self — right, since the finding is the *observer's* response, not the sun's motion. Two notes. (1) **Positional-target over-stamp:** the v1.1 unmarked-object rule fires repeatedly here (e.g. *says*→*Preacher*, *goes*→*generation*, *comes*→*earth*), each flagged `discovery=target positional (unverified)` — these are the poetry's adjacent nouns, not real objects; correctly self-flagged, and the reading ignores them. (2) **`seat=spirit` on H7307 (v.6, "wind")** — the lexical tags the physical *wind* with `seat=spirit` because H7307 = *ruach*. Here it is plainly the meteorological wind, not the human spirit; a lemma-level seat-stamp to discount (the ruach/wind ambiguity, flagged for the rule-adjustment list — *seat=spirit* should not fire where the surface is elemental wind). The genuinely inner terms — *weariness* (H3023, v.8), the *eye* and *ear* not satisfied/filled — sit in gate-2 content; the reading is read from the verse structure, correctly.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The watching self as a fixed datum** — the whole unit is *someone observing*; the observing-and-weighing self (not yet named) is the book's most persistent characteristic and begins here. (2) **The un-utterable** — "a man cannot utter it" (v.8): a distinct inner state, the surplus of weariness that overflows speech (kin to the Psalter's "too deep for words," but here negative).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the perception of futility / weariness* (new — Ecclesiastes' distinctive arena); *the insatiable senses (eye/ear never filled)* (new thread — never-satisfied); *oblivion / being-forgotten* (thread, shared w/ the book's later 2:16, 9:15); *the watching-and-weighing self* (new — the book's frame-characteristic).

## ECC-01-B · Ecc 1:12–15 · [D, multi, thread] — the searching self (heart applied to seek out); the unhappy business

**The reading.** The Preacher names his method, and the method is an *inner-being posture*: "I **applied my heart** to **seek** and to **search out** by wisdom all that is done under heaven" (v.13). The finding is the **directed, effortful heart** — the self that deliberately sets its interior to investigation *(stated)*; this "applied heart" recurs as the engine of the whole book (2:3, 7:25, 8:16). What it finds recoils on it: the seeking is "an **unhappy business** that God has given" (v.13), and its object resists — "what is **crooked cannot be made straight**, and what is **lacking cannot be counted**" (v.15). The operation is **the seeking heart meeting resistance** — investigation that returns not mastery but the discovery of an unfixable, unfillable reality *(inferred: the "crooked/lacking" pair frames the search's limit; the heart can seek exhaustively and still not straighten the world)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *applied* / *seek* / *search out* (vv.13) carry the directed-heart finding; the lexical tags the seeking-verbs as actions, sound. The phrase "my heart" (the *leb* seat) is the bearer of the searching — the reading rests here. *unhappy business* (H6045, v.13) sits as content. No systemic residual beyond the recurring positional-target flags; pairs sensible.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **The seeking as self-assigned burden** — it is "given by God to be busy with" (v.13): the searching heart is not idle curiosity but an *appointed labour*, a hidden note that the inner drive to understand is itself experienced as a weight, not a freedom.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the searching/seeking self (the applied heart)* (new thread — recurs 2:3, 7:25, 8:16); *the seeking heart meeting an unfixable reality* (new).

## ECC-01-C · Ecc 1:16–18 · [D, multi] — knowing as grief; the heart's great experience of wisdom

**The reading.** The self takes stock of what it has become: "I have **acquired great wisdom**… my **heart has had great experience** of wisdom and knowledge" (v.16) — a heart *enlarged* by learning. Then the gem, stated flatly as a law of the interior: "in **much wisdom is much vexation**, and he who **increases knowledge increases sorrow**" (v.18). The finding is **understanding that deepens grief** — the inner being does not find that knowledge soothes; the more it takes in, the more it aches *(stated)*. Set against the ordinary hope that wisdom brings peace, this is a counter-finding: the growing mind is a *grieving* mind, because it sees more of the crooked and the lacking it cannot mend *(inferred: v.18 completes v.15 — the searching heart of 1:13 not only fails to straighten the world but is *pained in proportion to its sight*)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *wisdom, knowledge, experience* correctly carried; the pivot terms *vexation* (H3708, v.18) and *sorrow* (H4341, v.18) sit as the grief-content the reading rests on. The "I said in my heart" of v.16 correctly places the utterance in the *leb* — the dialogical self speaking to itself, a form the book shares with the Psalter (Ps 4, 42). Sound; no material mis-tag.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The self-addressing heart** — "I said in my heart" (v.16): the interior conversation, the self reporting to itself, recurs through the book and is a genuine characteristic (the reflexive/dialogical self, shared w/ the Psalter's self-address). (2) **Wisdom as a *quantity* that scales with pain** — "in *much*… *increases*… *increases*": the finding is proportional, not binary; a hidden precision the segmentation's "knowing as grief" flattens.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *knowing as grief (wisdom deepening sorrow)* (new — a Qoheleth counter-finding); *the self-addressing / dialogical heart* (shared w/ Ps); *the enlarged heart (great experience of wisdom)* (new).
