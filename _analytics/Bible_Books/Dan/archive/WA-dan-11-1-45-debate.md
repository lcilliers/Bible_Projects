## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, not fact).** Dan 11 continues the speaker's first-person monologue opened in chapter 10 without a scene break (11:1 begins mid-speech: "And as for me..."). The chapter narrates a long sequence of political and military conflict between role-titled figures ("the king of the south," "the king of the north," "a contemptible person," etc.) — almost none of whom are named individually, a marked contrast with chapters 8-10. In scope: verses that state a ruler's or people's own interior condition directly — anger (11:11), self-exaltation (11:12, 11:14, 11:36-37), heart-orientation (11:12, 11:25, 11:27, 11:28), directed intent via "set/turned his face" (11:17-19), fear and rage (11:30, 11:44), knowledge of God and standing firm (11:32), imparted understanding (11:33), and redirected honor/devotion (11:38). The great majority of the chapter's 45 verses narrate external events (armies, sieges, treaties, deaths) with no interior content stated for any candidate; each is checked per note (f) rather than assumed, and recorded as an explicit silence where it resolves to nothing.

**Reading rule applied.** Standard STEP-live precedence per base extract; no [AMBIGUOUS] spans encountered in this range.

**Corpus-continuity check.** Read `WA-dan-10-1-21-debate.md` before drafting this chapter. Carried forward: the unnamed speaker of chapter 10 continues speaking without interruption into 11:1; the H2388 "strengthen" root (five occurrences in ch.10) continues here (11:1, 11:5 x2, 11:32); the H0995 "understand" root, consistently positive in ch.9-10 (Daniel's own understanding; Gabriel's granting of it), recurs here with a different moral valence (11:30, 11:37).
