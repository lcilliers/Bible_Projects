## Preliminaries

**Working scope (declared as assumption, not fact).** Dan 4:1-37 is taken as a single reading unit for this debate — larger than any prior range in this corpus (previous largest: 3:8-30, 22 verses) but textually one unit: the whole chapter is King Nebuchadnezzar's own first-person testimony, framed by his own voice at both ends (the decree/greeting of 4:1-3 and the closing doxology of 4:34-37), narrating a single connected episode (dream → interpretation → the boast that triggers it → the judgment → the restoration). Splitting it would have cut across that first-person frame more than the size alone would justify. The working assumption is that "bearing on the inner being" here includes: (a) Nebuchadnezzar's own stated and inferred interior throughout, since he is both narrator and subject; (b) Daniel/Belteshazzar's interior, stated explicitly at 4:19 and implied elsewhere; (c) the wise men, the watcher/holy one, and the king's counselors/lords, each treated per step 2 notes (b)-(f) rather than dismissed as scenery; (d) the dream's tree-and-watcher content itself, on the ground that it is explicitly identified in-passage (4:20-22) as figuring Nebuchadnezzar's own person — so per step 3 note (e), his own interior may be "hidden behind" the dream-images, and per step 2 note (d) the content is related to a human in the passage's context and therefore in scope, not set aside as non-human-only.

## Per-verse debate

### Dan 4:1

> King Nebuchadnezzar to all peoples, nations, and languages, that dwell in all the earth: Peace be multiplied to you!

**Observation.** The verse is the salutation-header of Nebuchadnezzar's decree: he names himself (H4430/H5020, "King Nebuchadnezzar"), addresses "all peoples, nations, and languages" (H5972/H0524/H3961, a three-part collective formula) "that dwell [H1753] in all the earth," and pronounces a peace-blessing: "Peace [H8001] be multiplied [H7680] to you."

**Operation 1 — the king's public proclamation of peace.**
- **Action-type:** proclaimed/blessed (a peace-greeting issued as royal decree).
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** a movement — an utterance going out from the king to a named collective; state/status: king in the posture of address, not yet interior content.
- **Source:** self (the king issuing the decree in his own voice and title).
- **Target:** "all peoples, nations, and languages... in all the earth" — a maximal collective (Q8).

**Interrogative — questions considered.** (`WA-interpretation-questions` Q1-Q11 — every human mentioned in this verse is a presumptive candidate, per read-guidance step 2 note (f); a candidate that resolves to nothing is recorded as an explicit silence, per Part B.4, not omitted)
- Q1/Q2: Nebuchadnezzar is a focused inner being throughout this chapter (he narrates it). At this opening line specifically, no interior disposition is yet stated — the verse is the address formula, not yet the content. An implied disposition (goodwill, or a felt need to testify, given what follows) is plausible but not textually stated here; held open, resolved by 4:2's "it has seemed good to me." The addressed collective ("all peoples...") is not individuated; per Q8 it is treated as a target-collective, not itself a focused inner being in this verse.
- Q3: Not applicable yet for Nebuchadnezzar's own interior (nothing stated this verse); the collective's interior is not in view.
- Q4: Source of the utterance = self (his own royal voice/authority); no other party sourced.
- Q5: Target = the addressed collective, named as three overlapping categories (peoples/nations/languages) — a rhetorical maximal-inclusion formula, not distinguishing sub-groups.
- Q6: A movement — the decree going out from the king to the collective.
- Q7: Forward linkage to 4:2-3 (the decree's stated motive follows immediately) and to the near-identical greeting formula elsewhere in Daniel's Aramaic chapters (e.g. Dan 3:4, 6:25) — same collective-address pattern recurring; noted, not resolved, per corpus-continuity discipline (B.9/B.8 — carried forward, not merged across passage debates).
- Q8: The collective is maximal and undifferentiated ("all... all the earth") — the broadest addressee formula in the corpus so far; its nature (a political/rhetorical universal claim, not an ethnographic one) reshapes the target as "everyone under the king's claimed reach," not any bounded group.
- Q9: Sufficient — the verse is short and self-contained; no missing data flagged.
- Q11: proclaimed.

**Decision.** Retain as a stated IB operation (the king's own act of address), with the interior content explicitly deferred to 4:2 — flagged as a forward-pointer per Part B.4, not a silence needing separate resolution.

**Observation.** "At the end of the days [H3118] I... lifted [H5191] my eyes [H5870] to heaven, and my reason [H4486] returned [H8421G] to me, and I blessed [H1289] the Most High, and praised [H7624] and honored [H1922] him who lives [H2417] forever, for his dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom endures from generation to generation."

**Operation 1 — the restoration of Nebuchadnezzar's reason.**
- **Action-type:** was restored (reason returned).
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** a movement — the reversal of 4:16's decree, his mind/reason (H4486, distinct root from H3825's "heart/mind" at 4:16, worth noting as a lexical shift — "reason/understanding" specifically, rather than the broader "heart") returning to him.
- **Source:** not explicitly stated within this specific clause ("my reason returned to me" is passive/自动, no agent named in this exact verse) — inferred, on the strength of the whole passage's theology and the "at the end of the days" temporal marker matching the "seven periods" bound (4:16/4:25/4:32), to be the Most High's own action, but this inference is named as such rather than imported as bare fact — a genuine case for Part B.5's caution (the outcome is stated; the text does not, in this exact clause, re-state the divine agency behind it, though it is the overwhelming contextual implication).
- **Target:** Nebuchadnezzar's own mind/understanding.

**Operation 2 — Nebuchadnezzar's resultant worship.**
- **Action-type:** blessed/praised/honored.
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** a movement — an immediate, triple-verbed response (blessed, praised, honored) directly upon the reason's return — a clear, stated cause-effect (Q7) within the verse itself.
- **Source:** self, in direct response to the restoration.
- **Target:** the Most High ("him who lives forever").

**Interrogative — questions considered.**
- Q1/Q2: Nebuchadnezzar — the passage's other major stated-interior turning point (with 4:30-31 the first). The restoration itself (Operation 1) and his response (Operation 2) are both explicit.
- Q3: The restoration is stated as fact; its direct agency within this clause is inferred (not restated as "the Most High restored me," though the passage's cumulative thesis makes this the only reasonable reading) — flagged precisely per Part B.5/B.3.
- Q4: Source of the restoration = inferred (Most High, on contextual grounds, not restated in this clause). Source of the worship = self, directly responsive.
- Q5: Target of the restoration = Nebuchadnezzar's own understanding; target of the worship = the Most High.
- Q6: Both movements.
- Q7: Direct reversal-linkage to 4:16/4:33 (the mind/reason taken, now restored); direct echo-linkage to 4:3 (near-identical language: "everlasting kingdom... generation to generation" repeated almost verbatim) — the beginning and the resumption of the doxology use the same formula, an inclusio within an inclusio, flagged prominently below.
- Q8: N/A.
- Q9: Sufficient for what is stated; the direct-agency question for the restoration itself is the one soft spot, named above.
- Q11: was restored; blessed/praised/honored.

**Decision.** Retain both operations; the restoration's direct agency is flagged as contextually overwhelming but not textually re-stated within this exact clause — preserved as a precise distinction rather than smoothed over.

### Dan 4:35

> all the inhabitants of the earth are accounted as nothing, and he does according to his will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay his hand or say to him, “ What have you done?”

**Observation.** "All the inhabitants [H1753] of the earth are accounted [H2804] as nothing [H3809/H9004]... he does [H5648] according to his will [H6634] among the host [H2429] of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay [H4223] his hand [H3028] or say to him, 'What have you done?'"

**Operation 1 — Nebuchadnezzar's confession of the Most High's unopposed sovereignty over all humanity.**
- **Action-type:** confessed/testified (God's supremacy).
- **Subject:** the Most High (non-human, in scope per note (b), stated in direct relation to humanity throughout this verse — "the inhabitants of the earth" named twice).
- **Operation:** a state/status — sovereignty asserted as total and unchallengeable ("none can stay his hand").
- **Source:** the Most High's own will, stated as self-sufficient (not derived from or answerable to anything else named).
- **Target:** "all the inhabitants of the earth" (maximal collective, Q8) — including, self-referentially though not named as such in this verse, Nebuchadnezzar himself, who has just personally experienced exactly this unchallengeable "hand."

**Interrogative — questions considered.**
- Q1/Q2: This verse's grammatical subject is the Most High, not a human — but per note (b) and (d) it is squarely in scope because its content is explicitly about God's dealings *with* "the inhabitants of the earth," a human collective, and because Nebuchadnezzar is the one voicing it, from his own recently-completed experience. The confession is itself Nebuchadnezzar's stated interior (a settled theological conviction, reached only after 4:33's ordeal) even though its grammatical content is about God and humanity generally rather than about Nebuchadnezzar's own feelings.
- Q3: Stated, in Nebuchadnezzar's own first-person testimony (continuing from 4:34).
- Q4: Source = the Most High's own will, self-sufficient.
- Q5: Target = "all the inhabitants of the earth" — and, self-referentially, Nebuchadnezzar (a linkage this debate names explicitly rather than leaving implicit, since the whole chapter's narrative logic makes him the paradigm case of the very truth he is now stating generally).
- Q6: A state (sovereignty asserted).
- Q7: Direct linkage — this verse generalizes the personal lesson of 4:25/4:32 ("till you know that the Most High rules the kingdom of men") into a universal theological claim; also echoes 4:1's "all peoples... in all the earth" and 4:12's "all flesh," continuing the passage's repeated universal-scope language, now turned from address-formula into confession.
- Q8: "All the inhabitants of the earth" — the passage's fourth or fifth use of maximal-collective language (4:1, 4:12, 4:17 "the living," now 4:35), a consistent rhetorical register across the whole chapter.
- Q9: Sufficient.
- Q11: confessed/testified.

**Decision.** Retain — Nebuchadnezzar's own stated theological conviction, self-referential though not grammatically self-focused; the self-reference is named explicitly as the debate's own observation, distinguished from what the verse literally states.

### Dan 4:36

> At the same time my reason returned to me, and for the glory of my kingdom, my majesty and splendor returned to me. My counselors and my lords sought me, and I was established in my kingdom, and still more greatness was added to me.

**Observation.** "My reason [H4486] returned [H8421G] to me... my majesty [H1923] and splendor [H2122] returned to me. My counselors [H1907] and my lords [H7261] sought [H1156] me, and I was established [H8627] in my kingdom, and still more [H3493] greatness [H7238] was added [H3255] to me."

**Operation 1 — the fuller restoration, now including Nebuchadnezzar's political standing.**
- **Action-type:** was restored (kingdom/majesty/splendor).
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** a state — a fuller, second statement of restoration (reason again named, as 4:34, now joined by majesty, splendor, and political re-establishment).
- **Source:** as 4:34, contextually the Most High (not re-stated as agent within this clause either).
- **Target:** Nebuchadnezzar's own standing and kingdom.

**Operation 2 — the counselors' and lords' act of seeking him out.**
- **Action-type:** sought/restored-to (an act of political recognition).
- **Subject:** "my counselors and my lords" — a human collective (Q8), named here for the first time in the passage.
- **Operation:** a movement — seeking Nebuchadnezzar out, implicitly restoring him to functioning kingship (the mechanism by which "I was established in my kingdom" occurs).
- **Source:** self (their own initiative — the text does not state what moved them: loyalty, expedience, or the recognition that the seven periods had ended and restoration was due; unstated).
- **Target:** Nebuchadnezzar.

**Interrogative — questions considered.** Per step 2 note (f), the counselors/lords are carried forward as a presumptive candidate rather than treated as a bare mechanical detail — precisely the kind of clause (a human collective acting, with unstated motive) the method-assessment flagged as previously skipped (its own closest parallel: the corpus's earlier miss on Ashpenaz's presentation-moment at Dan 1:18-19).
- Q1/Q2: Nebuchadnezzar — as 4:34, continuing restoration. The counselors/lords — a genuine candidate: what moved them to seek him out after his humiliation? Loyalty maintained through the ordeal? Political necessity (a kingdom needs a king)? Genuine belief in his restoration? None of this is stated.
- Q3: Nebuchadnezzar's restoration — stated. The counselors'/lords' motive — unstated, a recorded silence, not omitted.
- Q4: Source of Nebuchadnezzar's restoration = as 4:34 (contextually inferred, not re-stated). Source of the counselors'/lords' seeking = self (their own act), disposition unstated.
- Q5: Target = Nebuchadnezzar, in both operations.
- Q6: Both a state (restoration) and a movement (the seeking-act).
- Q7: Direct continuation of 4:34; also the passage's only mention of Nebuchadnezzar's court/administration as actors in their own right — worth noting as a a new (if brief) human collective introduced late in the passage, distinct from the wise men (4:6-7), the audience (4:1), or "the oppressed" (4:27).
- Q8: "My counselors and my lords" — a specific political-class collective (contrast the maximal "all peoples" collectives elsewhere); its nature (the ruling establishment, not the general population) reshapes the operation as a movement of institutional recognition, not popular acclaim.
- Q9: Insufficient to resolve the counselors'/lords' motive — named, not filled, per Part B.7/B.4.
- Q11: was restored; sought/restored-to.

**Decision.** Retain both operations. The counselors'/lords' motive is recorded as an explicit, named silence — carried into the record per the corrected step 2 discipline, rather than treated as inert scene-furniture.

### Dan 4:37

> Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and extol and honor the King of heaven, for all his works are right and his ways are just; and those who walk in pride he is able to humble.

**Observation.** "Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise [H7624] and extol [H7313] and honor [H1922] the King [H4430] of heaven, for all his works [H4567] are right [H7187] and his ways [H0735] are just [H1780]; and those who walk [H1981] in pride [H1467] he is able [H3202] to humble [H8214]."

**Operation 1 — Nebuchadnezzar's closing worship.**
- **Action-type:** praised/extolled/honored (triple-verbed, matching 4:34's triple response and 4:2-3's opening testimony).
- **Subject:** Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Operation:** a movement — the passage's closing act of worship, completing the frame opened at 4:1-3.
- **Source:** self.
- **Target:** "the King of heaven" — notably a new divine title, not used earlier in the passage (contrast "the Most High," used throughout) — worth flagging as a lexical shift at the very close.

**Operation 2 — the closing thesis, generalized to any human ("those who walk in pride").**
- **Action-type:** humbles (a general, ongoing capacity, not a single past event).
- **Subject:** the Most High/"the King of heaven."
- **Operation:** a state/capacity — "he is able to humble" — stated as a standing truth, not a one-time act.
- **Source:** the Most High, explicitly.
- **Target:** "those who walk in pride" — an unnamed, general human collective (Q8) that unmistakably includes Nebuchadnezzar himself (the direct referent of 4:30's boast — "I have built... by my mighty power... for the glory of my majesty" is precisely "walking in pride"), though the verse itself does not say "as I did" — the self-application is left for the reader/hearer to draw, not stated outright.

**Interrogative — questions considered.**
- Q1/Q2: Nebuchadnezzar — focused inner being, closing the chapter's frame with explicit worship (Operation 1). Operation 2's collective ("those who walk in pride") is general, but the whole chapter's narrative logic makes Nebuchadnezzar its paradigm instance — a self-referential closing generalization, structurally identical in kind to 4:35's self-referential confession, and worth naming as the passage's final instance of that same move (a person generalizing from their own experience to a universal claim, without stating the self-application outright).
- Q3: Both operations stated; the self-referential *application* of Operation 2 to Nebuchadnezzar himself is this debate's own inference from the passage's structure, named explicitly as such rather than asserted as textual fact — the verse itself speaks generally.
- Q4: Source of Nebuchadnezzar's worship = self. Source of the humbling-capacity = the Most High/King of heaven, explicitly.
- Q5: Target of the worship = the King of heaven. Target of the humbling-capacity = "those who walk in pride," generally.
- Q6: A movement (worship) and a state/standing-capacity (the humbling power).
- Q7: **The passage's final and most consequential linkage**: this verse is the direct, explicit answer to 4:30's boast — "those who walk in pride he is able to humble" is, in effect, Nebuchadnezzar's own retrospective verdict on his own earlier self. Also the fourth occurrence of the passage's repeated thesis (4:17, 4:25, 4:32, now 4:37, compressed into "he is able to humble"). Also closes the 4:3/4:34 inclusio (everlasting-kingdom language) with a final, distinct formula. Flagged as the anchor of the passage-level linkages section.
- Q8: "Those who walk in pride" — general, unbounded human collective; the passage's final and broadest such reference, generalizing from the single, named case (Nebuchadnezzar) the entire chapter has just narrated.
- Q9: Sufficient.
- Q11: praised/extolled/honored; humbles.

**Decision.** Retain both operations. The self-referential reading of Operation 2 (that Nebuchadnezzar is naming his own former self as an instance of "those who walk in pride") is recorded as this debate's own structural inference, explicitly distinguished from the verse's own general wording — a deliberate application of Part B.3's discipline at the passage's final verse.

## Passage-level linkages (Q7)

Per Q7/B.8, linkages are surfaced rather than passed over, and absence of linkage is itself to be named. The following recur across multiple verses and are gathered here rather than left scattered:

1. **The four-fold thesis repetition** — "the Most High rules the kingdom of men and gives it to whom he will [and sets over it the lowliest of men]": stated at 4:17 (in the watcher's decree, third-party), 4:25 (Daniel's forecast, second-person), 4:32 (the voice's pronouncement, second-person, present), and 4:37 (Nebuchadnezzar's own closing confession, compressed and generalized to "those who walk in pride"). The same claim moves from embedded-decree → predicted address → executed pronouncement → confessed personal conviction across the passage — a deliberate escalation the text itself constructs, not one this debate imports.
2. **The ease → disturbance pattern, doubled.** 4:4 ("at ease... prospering") → 4:5 (the dream, disturbance) mirrors 4:29 ("walking on the roof," unremarked ease) → 4:30-31 (the boast, then the interrupting voice) at larger scale. The second occurrence is worse in both trigger (self-generated pride, not an external dream) and consequence (total transformation, not merely fear).
3. **The 4:3 / 4:34 doxological inclusio.** "His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion endures from generation to generation" (4:3) is repeated almost verbatim at 4:34, framing the entire chapter between two statements of the same content — spoken once before the ordeal (as received teaching/observation) and once after it (as tested, personally-verified conviction). The repetition is exact enough to be a deliberate structural frame, not incidental phrasing.
4. **The stump (4:15) → restoration (4:26, 4:34-36) linkage**, explicitly named by the text itself at 4:26 ("as it was commanded to leave the stump... your kingdom shall be confirmed") — the clearest textually-self-declared linkage in the passage, requiring no inference from this debate.
5. **4:30's boast ↔ 4:37's closing verdict.** Directly addressed under 4:37's Q7 above; the single most consequential linkage in the passage, turning the chapter's ending into an explicit (if unstated-as-such) retrospective judgment on its own turning point.
6. **The universal-collective language recurring across the passage** ("all peoples, nations, and languages... in all the earth," 4:1; "food for all... all flesh," 4:12; "the living," 4:17; "all the inhabitants of the earth," 4:35) — a consistent rhetorical register from opening address to closing confession, tracked but not over-interpreted as more than a stylistic and thematic constant.
7. **Surfaced non-linkage**: Daniel's counsel at 4:27 is never explicitly answered, accepted, rejected, or referenced again anywhere in the passage — the twelve-month gap to 4:29 is the only textual trace of what happened in response, and it is silent. This absence is named per Q7's requirement that non-linkage be surfaced, not passed over: the text does not say Nebuchadnezzar ignored the counsel, only that judgment fell regardless, which is compatible with several readings (unheeded counsel; heeded-but-insufficient counsel; the ordeal being unavoidable regardless of response) — none of them settled by the text, so none is asserted here.
8. **Corpus-level (not merged into this passage's own log, per B.9, but noted per the corpus-continuity check):** the ch. 3 → ch. 4 escalation from third-party admiration of God's power (3:29's decree) to first-person subjection to it (this chapter) — recorded in the Preliminaries above.

## Insufficiencies register

Per Q9/Part B.7 — data the text or base extract does not carry, named rather than filled:

- The wise men's own interior in being summoned and in failing to interpret (4:6-7, restated 4:18) is nowhere stated — silence recorded at each occurrence, not resolved.
- Daniel's own interior on his initial arrival (4:8, "at last") — why the delay, what he felt approaching the king — is not stated; distinguished explicitly from his richly-stated interior three verses later (4:19).
- The watcher's ultimate commissioning source (4:13-17) is given only one layer back ("the decree of the watchers... the word of the holy ones") — an ultimate divine source is the passage's overwhelming theological implication but is not stated as the watcher's own direct commissioner in the text.
- Nebuchadnezzar's response, if any, to Daniel's counsel (4:27) during the twelve-month gap before 4:29 — wholly unstated (see passage-level linkage 7 above).
- Nebuchadnezzar's own subjective experience during the seven periods of judgment (4:33) — the text narrates physical/behavioral facts only, no interior content for the duration.
- The direct agency behind the restoration of Nebuchadnezzar's reason (4:34, 4:36) — contextually overwhelming (the Most High, per the whole passage's thesis) but not re-stated as agent within those specific clauses.
- The counselors' and lords' motive in seeking Nebuchadnezzar out (4:36) — unstated; loyalty, expedience, and genuine recognition are all compatible readings, none textually settled.
- Name-etymology data for "Belteshazzar" (its meaning as a Babylonian theophoric name) is not present in the base extract; the text's own gloss ("after the name of my god," 4:8) is recorded as given, without supplementing from outside knowledge (Part B.7).

## Emergent questions log (filed against this passage — not merged across passages; resolved, if at all, at the whole-book read)

- **EQ-1 (from 4:2).** Does the Most High's stated enablement (doing signs/wonders "for" Nebuchadnezzar) extend to sourcing his *disposition* to testify, or does the text keep outcome-given and response-chosen distinct? An interpretive fork (Part B.9), carried forward rather than settled here.
- **EQ-2 (from 4:10-22).** This passage's dream-content (the tree, the watcher) was read as in-scope because it is *later, explicitly, textually* identified as figuring a human (4:20-22) — a stronger and more textually-anchored case than a general "symbolic content might relate to a human" rule would license. Worth testing against future dream/vision passages (e.g., if the book returns to visionary material) to see whether this explicit-later-identification pattern recurs, or whether other visionary content in the corpus lacks it and needs a different scope-call.
- **EQ-3 (from 4:15).** The pronoun-shift from neuter (the tree) to personal ("him," "his portion") mid-decree was treated as textually-marked evidence for a referential identity, distinguished from a purely inferred referential reading. Worth carrying forward as a technique — checking base-extract morph tags for pronoun/gender shifts as a source of *stated*, not merely inferred, referential signals — when other passages carry similarly-disguised human referents (e.g., other symbolic/dream material, if any, elsewhere in Daniel).
- **EQ-4 (from 4:33, 4:36).** Two instances in this passage state an outcome ("the word was fulfilled"; "my reason returned") in a passive/agentless construction where the surrounding context makes the agent (the Most High) overwhelming but the specific clause does not re-name it. Is there a consistent authorial pattern in this book of using agentless passives specifically for divine action once it has already been named once nearby (as if avoiding repetition), rather than genuine ambiguity about agency? Worth testing against other passages with repeated divine-action language.
- **EQ-5 (from 4:36).** The counselors'/lords' seeking-out of Nebuchadnezzar is the passage's only appearance of his court/administration as actors. Given the corpus-review's specific finding about missed political-actor interiors (Ashpenaz at Dan 1:18-19), this is flagged as a case correctly caught this time — worth checking, at the whole-book read, whether administrative/courtier figures continue to receive this treatment consistently across the rest of Daniel, or whether the discipline lapses again in later chapters.
- **EQ-6 (from 4:35, 4:37).** Both verses generalize from Nebuchadnezzar's own experience to a universal claim without stating the self-application outright (the reader/hearer is left to draw it). Is this a deliberate rhetorical feature of first-person royal testimony as a genre (a mode Daniel uses elsewhere for other kings, e.g. potentially in later chapters), or specific to this chapter? Held open for the whole-book read.
