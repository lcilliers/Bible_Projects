## DAN-pride-humbled · Daniel 4:1–37 · [D, multi] — self-glorying pride humbled; the lifted eyes and returning reason

**The reading.** Nebuchadnezzar, "**at ease… and prospering**" (v.4), dreams of a great tree felled to a stump. Daniel warns him: "**break off your sins by practising righteousness… that there may perhaps be a lengthening of your prosperity**" (v.27). A year later, on his roof, the king speaks the sentence that triggers everything: "**Is not this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power as a royal residence and for the glory of my majesty?**" (v.30). He is driven mad, to live as a beast, "**till you know that the Most High rules the kingdom of men**" (v.32). The recovery is exact: "**At the end of the days I lifted my eyes to heaven, and my reason returned to me, and I blessed the Most High**" (v.34); "**those who walk in pride he is able to humble**" (v.37). The finding is **self-glorying *pride* ("*I* have built by *my* mighty power for the glory of *my* majesty") broken by humbling — and *sanity itself* returning only when the eyes are "lifted to heaven"** *(stated)*. The operation is **pride located precisely at the peak of success (the settled, prospering king surveying "*my* majesty") — a self so swollen it credits itself with everything and God with nothing — and the cure enacted as a *reduction to the beastly* until the interior *learns* what it would not confess; the restoration turns on a single reorientation: "**I lifted my eyes to heaven**", after which "my reason returned"** *(inferred: the placement of the pride is instructive — it comes not in crisis but in *ease* ("at ease… prospering", v.4) and at the *height* of achievement (the completed Babylon, v.30): the finding is that pride's danger-point is *success* — the self surveying what it has built and attributing it to "*my* mighty power… *my* majesty" (v.30, the triple "my" the whole disease); and the judgment is *fitted* — the man who exalted himself above his creatureliness is *reduced below* it (to a beast, eating grass), "till you **know** that the Most High rules" (v.32): the finding is that pride is a *failure to know one's place*, and its cure is an experiential un-knowing-then-knowing — humbling *teaches* what argument could not; the pivot of recovery is exquisitely simple — "**I lifted my eyes to heaven, and my reason returned**" (v.34): the finding is that *sanity itself* is bound up with looking *up* — the proud self, curved in on its own majesty, is literally out of its mind, and reason returns only when the gaze turns from self to God; and the restored king's confession names the law — "**those who walk in pride he is able to humble**" (v.37) — spoken now not against him but *by* him, the humbled self become the witness)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *pride at the peak of success (self-glorying in ease and achievement — "I have built by my mighty power for the glory of my majesty")* (thread — a gem: pride's danger-point is success, the self crediting itself with everything, w/ 4:30, Deut 8:12-17, 1 Cor 4:7); *pride as a failure to know one's place, cured by experiential humbling ("till you know that the Most High rules" — humbling teaches what argument could not)* (new — a gem: the self reduced below its creatureliness to learn its creatureliness, w/ 4:32,25); *sanity bound up with looking up ("I lifted my eyes to heaven, and my reason returned" — the proud self curved inward is out of its mind; reason returns with the Godward gaze)* (new — a gem: reorientation to God as the restoration of the self, w/ 4:34, Ps 121:1, 123:1); *the humbled self become the witness ("those who walk in pride he is able to humble" — spoken by the one it humbled)* (thread — w/ 4:37).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** pride at the peak of success; pride as a failure to know one's place, cured by experiential humbling; sanity bound up with looking up; the humbled self become the witness. Chapter 4 is the corpus's clearest case-study of pride and its cure — the self-glorying king reduced to a beast until he "lifts his eyes to heaven" and his reason (and worship) return.
- **Threads:** pride/self-credit (← 2:30 contrast; → 5:20-23, the son who would not learn); knowing one's place (w/ 4:25,32); the Godward gaze restoring the self (w/ Ps 121:1).
