## MIC-theophany-judgment · Mic 1:2–16 · [D, multi] — the prophet's grief; sin as an incurable wound

**The reading.** The LORD comes down in theophany to judge Samaria and Judah for idolatry (vv.2–7); then the prophet's own interior breaks in: "**for this I will lament and wail; I will go stripped and naked; I will make lamentation like the jackals**" (v.8); "for her **wound is incurable**, and it has come to Judah" (v.9). The finding is **the prophet's personal grief over his people's sin — a mourning that strips and wails, because the wound is past cure** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that *grieves* the judgment it must announce** — Micah does not proclaim doom coldly but laments it bodily, feeling the incurable wound as his own *(inferred: the shift from the LORD's descent (vv.3–4) to "I will lament and wail" (v.8) locates a real inner-being datum — the true prophet *grieves* over the sin he indicts; the finding is compassion, not detachment, in the one who must speak judgment (cf. Jeremiah's tears); "incurable wound" (v.9, anushah) names sin reached a point medicine cannot touch — the grief is for a people beyond self-repair; the bodily lament (stripped, naked, howling like jackals) makes the interior visible in the body)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the prophet's grief over his people's sin (lament, not detachment)* (new — compassion in the one who indicts, w/ Jer); *sin as an incurable wound* (new); *grief made bodily (stripped, wailing)* (new).
