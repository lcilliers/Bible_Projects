## EXO-03-moses-born-drawn-out · Exodus 2:1–10 · [compassion crossing the death-decree] — the fine child hidden, the pity at the weeping baby, and the deliverer drawn out

**The passage.** "She saw he was a **fine** (tov) child and hid him three months" (2:2); the basket in the reeds; "his sister stood at a distance to **know** (yada) what would happen" (2:4); Pharaoh's daughter opened it — "the baby was **crying** (bakhah); she took **pity** (chamal) on him: 'one of the Hebrews' children'" (2:6); "shall I **call** (qara) a nurse?" (2:7); "she **called** (qara) the child's mother" (2:8); "the child **grew older** (gadal)… she named him Moses" (2:10).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **tov (fine) — the mother's *seeing-good* that moves her to save (2:2).** "when she saw he was a **fine** (tov) child, she hid him" *(stated)* — the gem: **tov** — the mother *sees her child is good* and the seeing *moves her to act* against the death-decree; an echo of the divine *"saw that it was good"* (Gen 1) — but here a *mother's love-perception* that *hides and protects*. The interior: *love that sees worth and defies the law to guard it* *(inferred: tov = the mother's seeing-good that motivates the hiding)*.
- **yada (to know) — the sister's *vigilant watching* (2:4).** "his sister stood at a distance to **know** (yada) what would be done to him" *(stated)* — yada, *know*: the gem is the *caring knowing* — Miriam *watches to know* the child's fate; set against Pharaoh's *not-knowing* (1:8, the forgetting that fears), here a *knowing that loves and guards*. The interior of *watchful care* *(inferred: yada = the vigilant, caring knowing)*.
- **bakhah (crying) — the *wordless cry* that evokes pity (2:6a).** "the baby was **crying** (bakhah)" *(stated)* — bakhah, *weep*: the gem is that the *infant's tears* — a cry *without words* — are what *reach* the Egyptian princess; the *helpless weeping* that *moves an adversary's household*. (Israel's future *crying out*, 2:23, prefigured in this baby's tears.) *(inferred: bakhah = the wordless infant cry that evokes compassion)*.
- **chamal (took pity) — *compassion crossing the decree* (2:6b).** "she took **pity** (chamal) on him: 'this is one of the Hebrews' children'" *(stated)* — the gem: **chamal** — *spare / have compassion* — the *first pity* in the book, and it comes *from the adversary's own household*: Pharaoh's daughter *knows* the child is Hebrew (under her father's death-law) *and spares him anyway*. The interior of *compassion overriding the law* — as the midwives' *fear-of-God* overrode the king (1:17), here *pity* overrides him. The deliverer *preserved by an Egyptian's mercy* *(inferred: chamal = the compassion that crosses the death-decree)*.
- **qara (call a nurse) — the sister's *quick offer* (2:7).** "shall I go and **call** (qara) a nurse from the Hebrew women?" *(stated)* — qara, *call-to*: the gem is the *shrewd initiative* — Miriam *steps in at the moment of pity* to *arrange* what love needs; the interior of *ready wit serving the rescue* *(inferred: qara = the quick offer that turns pity into provision)*.
- **qara (called the mother) — the *reunion arranged* (2:8).** *qara distinct from 2:7:* "she **called** (qara) the child's mother" *(stated)* — where 2:7 was the *offer*, 2:8 is the *doing*: the very *mother* is *called* to nurse her own child — *for wages*, in Pharaoh's daughter's care. The gem: providence *returns the child to the mother* through the adversary's household — the interior's *love rewarded with her own son back* *(inferred: qara = the calling that restores the child to his mother)*.
- **gadal (grew older) — the child *grows* toward his role (2:10).** "when the child **grew older** (gadal), she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter… named him Moses" *(stated)* — gadal, *grow/become great*: the interior-adjacent note is *the deliverer maturing* in the adversary's house (→ 2:11 "when Moses had grown", the killing); the child *raised by Pharaoh's daughter*, drawn out of the water *(inferred: gadal = the growth of the preserved deliverer)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **love sees and hides (tov 2:2 → yada 2:4):** the mother *sees her child is good* and *hides* him; the sister *watches to know* — two women's interiors *guarding the child* against the decree.
2. **the cry that crosses the decree (bakhah 2:6a → chamal 2:6b):** the infant's *wordless weeping* reaches *Pharaoh's own daughter*, whose *pity overrides her father's law* — compassion from *inside the adversary's house* preserves the deliverer.
3. **wit turns pity into provision (qara 2:7 → qara 2:8):** the sister's *quick offer* and the *calling of the mother* return the child *to his own mother's arms* — providence weaving love, pity, and cunning together.
4. **the deliverer grows in the adversary's house (gadal 2:10):** the child *matures* under Pharaoh's daughter's care — the one who will confront Pharaoh *raised within Pharaoh's household*.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** **tov (the mother's *seeing-good* that moves her to save — the love-perception defying the decree)**; yada (the sister's *vigilant, caring* watching — vs Pharaoh's *not-knowing* 1:8); bakhah (the *wordless infant cry* that evokes pity); **chamal (the *first pity* — compassion *crossing the death-decree*, from the adversary's household)**; qara ×2 (2:7 the *offer* to call a nurse / 2:8 the *calling* of the mother); gadal (the deliverer *growing* in Pharaoh's house).
- **Whose interior (multi-interior):** the **mother** (love that sees good and hides), the **sister** (watchful care and quick wit), and **Pharaoh's daughter** (pity overriding her father's law) — a *chain of women's interiors* preserving the deliverer; the **adversary's** law *subverted from within his own house*; God *unnamed* but the providence *evident* — no divine speech, the deliverer saved by *mercy and cunning*.
- **Threads:** the **women who preserve** (the midwives 1:17 → the mother, sister, Pharaoh's daughter here → Zipporah 4:25 → Miriam 15:20 — the deliverance carried by women's interiors before Moses acts); *compassion/fear overriding the king* (chamal 2:6 ← yare 1:17; law crossed by mercy); the *wordless cry* (bakhah 2:6 → Israel's *crying out* 2:23, `zaaq`); *not-knowing vs caring-knowing* (yada 2:4 ← 1:8); the deliverer *raised in the adversary's house* (gadal 2:10 → the flight 2:11–15).
- **Guarded against:** over-reading tov (2:2) as mere physical beauty when the verse makes the *seeing-good* the spur to *save* (the love-perception is the finding); merging the three women's interiors (mother / sister / princess — distinct acts of love, care, pity); reading Pharaoh's daughter's pity as naïveté when the verse says she *knew* it was a Hebrew child *and spared him* (compassion *against* the law); importing a Moses-typology (the ark/basket → salvation) over the *women's* plain acts of rescue; treating gadal here as anything but *growth* (its weightier "become great" sense is not yet in play).
