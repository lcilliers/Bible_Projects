**seek** — Psa 9:10  ·  `H1875:seek#1`

_Story_

Here the psalm speaks of those who seek the Lord. It is something a person chooses and keeps doing, a steady turning of the whole self toward God rather than a feeling that sweeps over them. The verse does not say what first moved them to it, nor does it locate it in one part of a person; it is simply how they are set. Their reward is stated plainly: God has not forsaken those who seek him. Nothing is added about how hard or how fiercely they seek, and nothing is forbidden; the turning toward God, and the being held by him, is the whole of it.

_Reading_

Sense 101 reads the movement as 'seek the LORD', typed 102 as volition - an act of the will, not a passion undergone. Source 103 is present:false: the verse does not trace the seeking back to any prior interior cause; it stands as the self's own orientation. Seat 104 is 'none' - the operation is not lodged in an organ (no heart, no reins); it is a whole-person disposition, not a faculty. Bearer 105 is 'those who seek', target 107 is the seeking itself, and coupling 112 is 'seek-the-LORD': the operation 106 is the self and its kind sustaining a turn toward God and, on that ground, not being forsaken. Manner 108 is 'none', and intensity 109, specifier 110 and effect 111 are all present:false - the text does not colour how intensely or in what mode it is done, nor name a downstream effect beyond the not-forsaking already in the operation. Prohibition 113 present:false: nothing is forbidden here. Discovery 114 fixes it: v10, 'you have not forsaken those who SEEK you' - the interior orientation of seeking is the condition God honours, a directed reaching after him. Locus 116 is internal:ib-state and role 115 is characteristic: within the inner life this is a settled Godward set of the whole self, held up as the security of the one who knows God's name.

*Why this reading differs:* As the opening darash reading, this is seeking held up simply as the security of the faithful - those who seek God are not forsaken. It differs from Psa 10:4 (the proud who will not seek), Psa 14:2 (God searching for a seeker he scarcely finds), and Psa 34:10 (seeking answered with provision): here the finding is the bare covenant assurance that the seeker is never abandoned.

**seek** — Psa 10:4  ·  `H1875:seek#2`

_Story_

The same act appears here in reverse. The proud man does not seek God at all, and this is not a passing mood but the whole shape of his thinking: God simply has no place in it. His pride fills him so completely that, as he reckons things, there is no God to consider. Nothing outside him is blamed for this; it is who he is. No consequence is spelled out and nothing is forbidden - the point is the emptiness itself, a mind with no room left for God.

_Reading_

This is the dark counter-reading of the same item. Sense 101 is 'pride crowds God out' and type 102 shifts to cognition - where the God-seeker's turn was volitional, here the failure to seek is a settled state of mind. Source 103 present:false: the not-seeking is not derived from a named cause outside itself, yet the operation 106 supplies its interior ground - 'in the pride of his face the wicked will not seek God; there is no God is the whole content of his thoughts'. Seat 104 'none': again not organ-located; it is the cast of the whole self. Bearer 105 is 'the wicked', target 107 is practical-atheism, coupling 112 is 'no-room-for-God' - pride so fills the interior that God is absent from every calculation. Manner 108 'none'; intensity 109, specifier 110, effect 111 present:false - the descent is not intensified or given an outcome; the absence of seeking IS the condition. Prohibition 113 present:false. Discovery 114: v4 names it as a mind so full of self that God does not enter its thinking. Locus 116 internal:ib-state, role 115 characteristic: within the inner life this is the interior organised around its own pride to the exclusion of God - seeking read in its negative, given the same analytic weight as its positive.

*Why this reading differs:* Against the other H1875 seek readings, which are volitional turnings TOWARD God, this one is the operation in its absence: pride's cognition (type shifts from volition to cognition) that will not seek. The difference is the finding - the same characteristic read as descent rather than ascent, its bearer the wicked rather than the faithful.

**seek** — Psa 14:2  ·  `H1875:seek#3`

_Story_

God looks down from heaven over all people to see whether anyone understands and seeks him. Seeking here is a turning of the mind toward God, the very thing that separates a wise person from a fool. It is not traced to any cause; it is simply what a person is or is not doing. The verse grades nothing and forbids nothing - it only asks whether the searching heart is there, and the psalm's sober answer is that it is rare.

_Reading_

Sense 101 'any who seek after God', type 102 volition. Source 103 present:false - the seeking is not attributed to an antecedent cause; it is the self's own reaching. Seat 104 'none': not organ-bound. Bearer 105 'the seeker', target 107 God-seeking, coupling 112 'seek-after-God'. Operation 106: the LORD looks down to see if any understand and seek after God - the rare interior that reaches for him. Manner 108 'none'; intensity 109, specifier 110, effect 111 present:false - the text does not grade or resolve it; the finding is simply the presence or absence of the seeking mind. Prohibition 113 present:false. Discovery 114: v2 pairs understanding with seeking - the operation God searches the earth for is a mind that turns toward him; it is what marks a person as not-a-fool. Locus 116 internal:ib-state, role 115 characteristic: within the inner life, seeking here is the distinguishing interior God scans humanity to find, and (in this psalm's verdict) scarcely finds.

*Why this reading differs:* Distinct from the other seek readings by its vantage: God is the one searching for the seeker. Where Psa 9:10 rewards the seeker and Psa 10:4 shows the proud who will not seek, here the operation is held up as the scarce interior God hunts across all mankind - seeking as the mark of the non-fool.

_Story_

*Why this reading differs:* Here the item is typed as affect, not volition - seeking read as the hunger of the afflicted that is satisfied, its reward life and praise. **seek** — Psa 27:4  ·  `H1245:seek#1`

_Story_

The psalmist says there is one thing he asks and will seek after: to live in God's house and gaze on his beauty. Seeking here is desire narrowed to a single point - all his many wants pulled together into one, the longing to behold God. It is chosen, and it is the set of his whole self rather than any one part of him. The verse does not measure how strongly he wants it; the force is in the fact that everything else has given way to this one thing.

*Why this reading differs:* Within baqash's many readings, this is the item as consolidated inward longing for God - desire funnelled to one object.
