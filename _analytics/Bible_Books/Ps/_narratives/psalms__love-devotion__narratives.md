## Psa 4:1-5

> To the choirmaster : with stringed instruments . A Psalm of David . Psa 4:1 Answer me when I call , O God of my righteousness ! You have given me relief when I was in distress . Be gracious to me and hear my prayer ! Psa 4:2 O men , how long shall my honor be turned into shame ? How long will you love vain words and seek after lies ? Selah Psa 4:3 But know that the Lord has set apart the godly for himself; the Lord hears when I call to him . Psa 4:4 Be angry , and do not sin ; ponder in your own hearts on your beds , and be silent . Selah Psa 4:5 Offer right sacrifices , and put your trust in the Lord . Psa 4:6 There are many who say, “Who will show us some good? Lift up the light of your face upon us, O Lord!”

**love** — Psa 4:2  ·  `H0157:love#1`

_Story_

Here the psalmist turns on his opponents and names what is wrong inside them: they love empty, worthless things and run after lies, and in doing so they trade their own honour for shame. It is real love - a genuine pull of the heart - but pointed at nothing, at vanity. The psalm does not say where this craving came from or where in them it sits; it simply grips the whole person. It measures no heat and pronounces no verdict beyond the plain fact: a self can fasten itself to emptiness, and when it does, it quietly destroys its own dignity.

_Reading_

Aheb here (sense 7691307) is read as affect (type 7691308) but a DISORDERED one: the operation (7691311) is that the sons of men turn their own honour into shame precisely because they love empty things and chase after lies - misdirected desire. The bearer (7691310) is 'the men', the opponents; the target (7691312) is not a good object but 'misdirected-love' itself, and the coupling (7691314) welds love to vanity (love-vanity). Locus is internal:ib-state (7691315) with role characteristic (7691317): this is genuinely an inner-being movement, the interior fastening on emptiness (discovery 7691316). The silences carry weight. There is NO source (103 absent): the psalm does not trace where this love comes from, only that it grips. There is NO seat (7691309='none'): the disordered love is not organ-located, it is diffused through the whole self, not lodged in heart or kidneys. Manner is 'none' (7691313) - no qualifying mode is given; the love is bare, unmodulated appetite. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): the passage does not measure how hot this love burns, adds no further specification, records no downstream effect, and issues no explicit ban - the diagnosis stands as bare description of a self bent toward the vain. Read without valence-comfort, this is love operating as ruin: the same faculty that elsewhere clings to God here binds a person to lies and hollows out his own honour.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the 13 readings of aheb in this family, this is the clearest DESCENT: love as misdirected appetite (bearer = the opponents, target = vanity/lies, locus internal), where nearly every other reading has love pointed at God, his name, house, law or salvation. The difference is the whole finding - the same verb runs downward here.

**love** — Psa 5:11  ·  `H0157:love#2`

_Story_

The psalm ends by picturing those who love God's name breaking out in glad singing. Their joy is not just relief at being kept safe - it rises specifically from loving who God is. Love and rejoicing are bound together here: to love him this way is to overflow into song. The psalm does not explain how the love began or locate it in any one part of the person; the whole self simply loves and exults. Nothing more is measured or added - the picture is a people so taken with God's name that gladness pours out of them.

_Reading_

Aheb (sense 7691584) is affect (type 7691585): those who love God's NAME. The bearer (7691587) is 'those who love the name'; the operation (7691588) is love of the name overflowing into exultation, and the target (7691589) is 'name-love' - love aimed specifically at who God is as revealed in his name. The coupling (7691591, love-and-exult) welds the love to jubilation: this love does not stay quiet but breaks into song. Locus is internal:ib-state (7691592), role characteristic (7691594): the exultation springs from an inner attachment, not merely from being sheltered (discovery 7691593 marks this as distinct from refuge-joy). The silences: NO source (103 absent) - the psalm does not derive the love, it simply attaches; NO seat (7691586='none') - not organ-located, the whole self loves; manner 'none' (7691590) - the love is unqualified, direct. Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition all absent (109-113): the passage does not gauge the fervour, adds nothing further, names no separate consequence beyond the exultation already given in the coupling, and forbids nothing. The reading holds a real distinction: joy here is not the relief of the protected but the overflow of love for God's very identity.

*Why this reading differs:* Unlike #1 (disordered love of vanity) and the later law-love readings, this aheb targets God's NAME and its distinctive fruit is exultation (coupling love-and-exult); the finding is that love of God's identity, not mere shelter, is what breaks into joy.

**love** — Psa 18:1  ·  `H7355:love#1`

_Story_

The great song of rescue opens not with 'thank you' but with 'I love you.' The word used is unusually tender - the kind of love a mother has in her body for her child - and it is aimed straight at the LORD, whom the psalmist calls his strength. This affection comes first, before any account of being saved, as though the love is the root and the rescue the fruit. The psalm does not say where the love began or place it in any one part of him; his whole self simply leans toward God in warmth. It is the quietest and most intimate opening he could have chosen.

_Reading_

This is racham (sense 7693986), the rare and tender verb of womb-love, read as affect (type 7693987): 'I love you, O LORD, my strength.' The bearer (7693989) is the psalmist himself; the operation (7693990) is the self DECLARING a tender, intimate love for the LORD its strength - and strikingly this whole deliverance-song opens not with thanks but with affection (discovery 7693995). The target (7693991) is 'tender-love', and the coupling (7693993, love-you-LORD) binds the love directly to the LORD as its object. Locus internal:ib-state (7693994), role characteristic (7693996). The silences: NO source (103 absent) - the love is not derived from the deliverance that follows; it stands first, as if the rescue flows from the bond rather than the bond from the rescue; NO seat (7693988='none') - the intimate love is not lodged in a single organ but is the posture of the whole self; manner 'none' (7693992) - no qualifying mode, the love is bare and direct. Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109-113): the passage does not measure the warmth, add specification, name a separate effect, or prohibit. The finding is that the interior movement opening the great rescue psalm is warm attachment - love as the first word, softer and more intimate than gratitude.

**love** — Psa 26:8  ·  `H0157:love#3`

_Story_

In a psalm where the writer insists he has kept clear of liars and wrongdoers, he names what he is drawn TO: 'I love the house where you live, the place where your glory dwells.' His love here reaches not for God in the abstract but for the very place of God's presence. It is the warm opposite of his refusal to sit with the wicked. The psalm does not say how this love arose or fix it in any single part of him; the whole self simply longs for that place. It is affection for where God can be found.

_Reading_

Aheb (sense 7696187) is affect (type 7696188): 'O LORD, I love the habitation of your house and the place where your glory dwells.' The bearer (7696190) is the psalmist singular; the operation (7696191) is the self loving God's dwelling-place, the very site where his glory resides (discovery 7696196). The target (7696192) is 'love-of-the-house', and the coupling (7696194, love-your-house) fastens the affection to the sanctuary. Locus internal:ib-state (7696195), role characteristic (7696197). The distinction preserved here: the love is aimed not at God directly, nor at his name (5:11) or law, but at his HOUSE - the place of presence. The silences: NO source (103 absent) - the psalm, set amid protests of integrity, does not derive the love; NO seat (7696189='none') - not organ-located; manner 'none' (7696193) - unqualified. Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition all absent (109-113): no measure of fervour, no further specification, no named downstream effect, no prohibition. Set within a psalm defending the psalmist's separation from evildoers, this love of the sanctuary functions as the positive counterpart to his hatred of the assembly of the wicked - the interior drawn toward the place where glory dwells.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the aheb readings, this one's target is God's HOUSE/dwelling-place (not his name, law, salvation, or God himself), and it works as the positive mirror of the psalm's hatred of the wicked's assembly.

**love** — Psa 31:23  ·  `H0157:love#4`

_Story_

After being rescued, the psalmist does not simply feel grateful - he turns to everyone around him and commands, 'Love the LORD, all you his faithful ones!' His love here is not a private feeling being described but a summons being issued: he wants the whole community to love God as he does, because God guards the faithful and pays back the proud. The psalm does not say where this love comes from or locate it in any part of the person; it is a deliberate act of the will, reaching outward. Having been saved himself, he moves to draw others into the same devotion.

_Reading_

Uniquely among these aheb readings, the type is VOLITION (7697217), not affect: 'Love the LORD, all you his saints!' The sense (7697216) is love the LORD, but here it arrives as an imperative summons. The bearer (7697219) is 'the saints', the community being called; the operation (7697220) is the self turning its personal deliverance into a call - drawing the whole community into love and confidence, since the LORD preserves the faithful but repays the proud (discovery 7697225). The target (7697221) is 'summoning-love', and the coupling (7697223, love-the-LORD) binds the summons to its object. Locus internal:ib-state (7697224), role characteristic (7697226). The silences: NO source (103 absent) - though contextually the summons flows from the rescue just recounted, the love itself is not derived; NO seat (7697218='none') - not organ-located; manner 'none' (7697222) - the command is unqualified. Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109-113): no measured fervour, no further specification, no separate named effect, no prohibition. The finding is that love here is not reported as a feeling but WILLED and commanded outward - the interior, having been delivered, moves to enlist others' interiors in the same love.

*Why this reading differs:* This is the ONLY aheb reading typed as volition (a commanded summons) rather than affect/disposition; the bearer is the whole community being enlisted, not an individual reporting a feeling - love here is willed and broadcast outward.

**friends** — Psa 38:11  ·  `H0157:friend#1`

_Story_

_Reading_

**loved** — Psa 45:7  ·  `H0157:lov#1`

_Story_

_Reading_

The finding: love here is a moral orientation, not warmth toward a person - to love righteousness AND hate wickedness together is the interior that qualifies one to reign.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the three 'loved' (aheb participle) readings, this alone is a POSITIVE moral affection (love of righteousness, welded to hatred of wickedness) borne by the king; #2 is the enemy's love of cursing and #3 is love of God's promise.

**love** — Psa 52:3  ·  `H0157:love#5`

_Story_

The psalm exposes a powerful, boastful man by naming what he loves: he loves evil more than good, and lying more than honest speech. This is love genuinely at work, but flipped upside down - a heart that weighs good against evil and deliberately chooses the worse. The psalm does not say how his loves got twisted or fix the fault in any one part of him; the whole man is bent this way. It does not even stop to forbid it - it simply shows it. The same power to love that can cling to God is here running in reverse.

_Reading_

Aheb (sense 7652797) as action (type 7652798), read at full descent: 'You love evil more than good.'
