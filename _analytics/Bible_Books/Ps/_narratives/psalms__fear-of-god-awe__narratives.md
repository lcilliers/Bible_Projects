**afraid** — Psa 3:6  ·  `H3372:afraid#1`

_Story_

Surrounded by thousands who have turned against him, the psalmist makes a plain decision inside himself: he will not be afraid. This is not a passing flicker but a settled state of the whole person — you cannot point to one part of him where the courage sits; it fills all of him. Nothing in the verse says the fear rushes in and is beaten back; he simply names the enemy's overwhelming numbers and refuses to let them frighten him. The refusal has no particular degree, no bodily trigger, no spelled-out consequence — just a steady, chosen fearlessness resting on the trust that the God who let him sleep and wake will keep him.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'not afraid of many thousands' and the type (102) is affect: an emotional posture, not a mere event. No source (103 absent) is named for the fearlessness within the verse itself, and no seat (104 none) is given — the refusal of fear is not lodged in a single organ but held as a whole-self state (locus 116 internal:ib-state). The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the operation (106) is a deliberate act of the self: it declares it will not fear even ten thousands ranged against it, fear refused on the ground of God's sustaining. The target (107) is fearlessness itself and the coupling (112) is 'not-afraid' — the two describe one resolved interior. Manner (108) is marked none, intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are all absent, and no prohibition (113) attaches: the fearlessness is simply asserted, not commanded or forbidden. The discovery (114) fixes it at v6 — the numbers that should terrify are faced down by trust. Role (115) is characteristic: this is a stable trait of the inner being, courage generated not by the size of the threat but by confidence that God upholds the sleeper who wakes again.

*Why this reading differs:* Unlike the terror readings of this word elsewhere, and unlike the reverent 'fear of the LORD', #1 is deliberate, chosen fearlessness (locus internal:ib-state) grounded in God's personal sustaining of the psalmist — courage against human numbers, not dread and not worship.

**enter** — Psa 5:7  ·  `H0935:enter#1`

_Story_

Because God's love is so abundant, the psalmist dares to come into God's house and bow low toward the holy temple — and the mood in which he comes is fear. It is not dread that keeps him out but reverence that shapes how he goes in; entering and bowing are a single act of awe. He does not lean on his own goodness to justify the approach; the love shown to him is what lets him come at all. This reverent worship is not felt in one part of him but is the whole man's posture, a steady reverence rather than a momentary shudder.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'enter to bow in the fear of God' and the type (102) is affect. No source (103 absent) is separately tagged and no seat (104 none) is given — the reverent approach is a whole-self posture (locus 116 internal:ib-state), not an organ's act. The bearer (105) is the psalmist; the operation (106) is the decisive movement: borne by God's steadfast love, the self enters the house and bows toward the temple in reverent fear. The target (107) is reverent-worship and the coupling (112) is 'bow-in-fear' — approach and awe are one motion. Manner (108) is none; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) are absent; no prohibition (113) attaches. The discovery (114) at v7 makes the ground explicit: the interior does not approach on its own merit but on the abundance of God's love, and the posture in which it arrives is fear. Role (115) is characteristic. The process runs from God's love (the enabling ground) through the self's entering and bowing to a settled reverence — awe here is not paralysis but the very form worship takes.

*Why this reading differs:* This first reading of 'enter' is reverent approach to worship, awe as the shape of coming near (target reverent-worship); it contrasts with #2 (Psa 143:2), where the same word turns to a plea NOT to be entered into judgment — worship-approach here versus dread of a verdict there.

_Story_

_Reading_

The process moves outward: an inner deliverance becomes a will to gather other reverent hearts into shared praise; here 'fear' functions almost as a name for the worshipping people.

*Why this reading differs:* Almost alone among the twenty-two readings of this word, #1 is tagged volition, not affect/disposition: the God-fearers are the object of a summons the rescued self issues, so 'fear' names the worshipping community being recruited rather than the psalmist's own reverence or a promised blessing.
