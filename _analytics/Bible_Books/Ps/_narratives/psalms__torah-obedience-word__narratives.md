**law** — Psa 37:31  ·  `H8451:law#1`

_Story_

In this psalm the just man is set against the scheming wicked, and one line quietly explains why he stands where they fall: God's law lives inside him. It is not a rule he consults from outside but something he knows and carries in himself — held not in some single corner of him but by the whole person he is. The passage never says how it got there; it simply finds it already settled within him and shows it at work, keeping his footing steady so that he does not slip. There is no struggle in the telling and no reward tacked on at the end — the sure step is simply what a man is like when the law has become part of him. The law inside and the steady walk are one thing, not two: because it lives in him, he holds his ground.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is exact: 'The law of his God is in his heart; his steps do not slip' — law that has come inside, coupled to a walk that stays sure. Its type (102) is cognition, not affect or bare act: the law is held as something known and carried in the interior, not merely obeyed at the surface. On source (103) the row is absent — the passage does not trace where this interior law came from; it does not name the LORD, teaching, or hearing as its origin, so the reading leaves the intake unaccounted and reports only the settled state, not its acquisition. Seat (104) resolves to none: for all that 'heart' is named in the verse, the reading does not treat the operation as organ-located — 'heart' here reads as the whole inner person rather than a faculty-seat, so the law is not lodged in one interior organ but held by the person as such. The bearer (105) is the righteous — inferred (to_span 277754); this is the interior of the just man, the one already set over against the wicked through the whole psalm. The operation (106), the core event, is 'internalised law producing a steady walk': the law inside is not inert but working — it issues in conduct, the 'steps' that do not slip. The target (107) is internalised-law itself (inferred, to_span 277754): the object the interior bears and acts on is the law-made-inward, so the movement is reflexive — the inner being holds the very thing that then steadies it. Manner (108) is none: no mode, no marked how — the operation is stated flatly, without qualifier of ease, struggle, or style, which itself says the steadiness is presented as plain fact, not achievement wrung out under strain. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are all absent — no degree is measured, nothing narrows or particularises the law beyond 'his God's', and the consequence is not booked as a separate outcome even though 'his steps do not slip' names one; the reading leaves the effect fused into the operation rather than split out, so the not-slipping is the working of the law, not a downstream reward. Coupling (112) is 'law-in-heart' (inferred, to_span 277754): the one binding the reading does register is precisely the weld of law to interior — the two are not separable, the law's location IS its being held. Prohibition (113) is absent: this is a positive interior state, not a forbidding or a restraint. Discovery (114) states the finding plainly: because the law lives in the heart, the interior's walk stays sure-footed — location produces stability. Role (115) is characteristic: this is read as a standing trait of the inner being, not a passing reaction. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state — the whole thing sits inside, a condition of the inner life rather than an outward transaction. The full process, then: an unaccounted-for law is found already settled inside the righteous person (not in an organ but in the person), held cognitively, welded to that interior, and from there working reflexively to keep the walk from slipping — a steadiness reported without degree, without strain, and without a separately-booked reward, as simply what an inwardly-lawed interior is.

**come cringing** — Psa 66:3  ·  `H3584:comecring#1`

_Story_

The psalm calls the whole earth to praise a God so mighty that even his enemies come crawling to him. But their surrender is a sham. It is not love and it is not real yielding — it is the flinch of the beaten, a show of submission forced out of them by sheer overwhelming power. Nothing inside them has changed; there is no genuine turning, no inward bowing, only an outward cringe put on because they cannot stand against his might. It is dread wearing the face of obedience. This is the exact opposite of the man whose heart holds God's law: where his obedience lives deep inside him, theirs is only skin, a posture on the surface between them and the God they fear.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is kachash — cringe / feign submission: 'your enemies come cringing to you.' This is not obedience but its counterfeit — a false, cowering show of yielding. Type (102) is action: an outward doing, not a felt state — the enemies perform submission. Source (103) is absent: the reading does not trace what inside the enemies drives the cringe; no interior origin is named, which fits a submission that is surface and forced rather than welling up from within. Seat (104) is none: the cringing is not organ-located — it is not lodged in an interior faculty, consistent with its being feigned, a bodily/relational posture rather than a movement of the inner organs. Bearer (105) is the enemies of God (inferred, to_span 281341): this is the interior — or rather the outward comportment — of the hostile, the wicked's own posture read from the outside. The operation (106) is 'cringe / feign obedience': the event is the pretence itself, submission mimed. Target (107) is God (inferred, to_span 281341): the cringe is directed Godward, but as arena, not as love — the enemies bend toward the One whose power overwhelms them. Manner (108) carries a real value here and is a pair (from_span 281341 to_span 281338, span-resolved): 'before the greatness of God's power' — the mode is compelled, wrung out by overwhelming might; this is the one dimension that marks HOW, and it marks it as coercion. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: no degree of the cringe is measured, nothing further particularises it, and no downstream effect is booked — the pretence is reported as a fact of the enemies' posture, not scored or followed to a consequence. Coupling (112) is none: the cringing is not welded to another interior movement — it stands alone, a bare feigned yielding with nothing bound to it, underscoring its hollowness. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114) is decisive: this is 'a submission of dread, not love' — obedience-language emptied out, the cowering pretence of obedience wrung from enemies by God's great power (the qualifier). Role (115) is characteristic: a standing trait read off the wicked's stance toward God. Locus (116) is external:person — and this is the sharpest finding: unlike the inward law of the righteous, this 'obedience' sits OUTSIDE, on the surface between persons, not within the inner being at all. The full process: overwhelming power presses on the hostile; from no named interior source and lodged in no interior seat, they produce an outward action — a feigned, dread-driven cringe aimed at God, compelled by his might, welded to nothing, sitting entirely on the external surface. It is submission's shape without submission's heart. Read without valence-filtering, this is a genuine reading of 'obedience' in the family: the data shows a form of yielding that is pure exterior dread — and the study must report it as fully as the righteous man's inward law, precisely because it is its exact photographic negative (external vs internal, feigned vs held, dread vs delight).

**obey** — Psa 106:25  ·  `H8085:obey#1`

_Story_

Retelling Israel's wilderness story, the psalm reaches the people's great failure: they would not listen to God. In their tents they grumbled, and underneath the grumbling was the real thing — they simply refused to heed the voice that spoke to them. The passage does not dig into why; it just records that the hearing-and-obeying never came. It was God's own word they turned from, and the grumbling and the refusing were really one act seen from two sides — the complaint you could hear, and the obedience they withheld. The psalm goes on to tell the ruin that followed, but at this moment the point is plainly the failure itself: a whole generation, offered a word that could have held them steady, choosing not to listen.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is shama, obey — but negated: 'they did not obey the voice of the LORD.' The reading is of obedience by its failure, a hearing-and-heeding that did not happen. Type (102) is action: obeying is a doing, and here the doing is withheld. Source (103) is absent: the passage does not name what interior drove the refusal — no root cause inside the fathers is traced, though the surrounding verses ('despised the pleasant land,' 'no faith in his promise,' 'murmured') supply the atmosphere; the reading leaves the source unbooked. Seat (104) is none: the disobedience is not organ-located, not lodged in a named interior faculty — it is the person's failure to heed, not the malfunction of an inner organ. Bearer (105) is the fathers (inferred, to_span 269676): the wilderness generation, the interior of the covenant people in their rebellion. The operation (106) is 'fail to obey': the event is a negation — an absence where obedience should have been, the non-occurrence of heeding. Target (107) is the voice of the LORD (inferred, to_span 269676): what was not obeyed is God's own utterance — so the failure is aimed squarely at the covenant word. Manner (108) is none: no mode of the disobedience is marked — not defiant, not weak, just not done; the flat report withholds any 'how,' presenting the refusal as bare fact. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: the degree of the disobedience is not measured, nothing narrows it, and though the psalm's next verses book terrible consequences (sworn to fall in the wilderness), the reading does not attach an effect-row to this lexical — the judgement is context, not filed as this operation's booked outcome. Coupling (112) carries a value: external:god (inferred, to_span 269676) — the disobedience is bound to God as the one whose voice is spurned, the relation that gives the failure its weight; it is coupled outward to the LORD, not to another inner movement. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114) names the placement: v25, 'did not obey the voice of the LORD' — 'the disobedience behind the murmuring,' i.e. the refusal to heed is read as the interior fact sitting underneath the audible complaint in the tents. Role (115) is characteristic: a standing trait of that generation, not a one-off. Locus (116) is 'paired with murmuring': and this is the finding — the disobedience does not stand alone but is read as the inner counterpart of the murmuring, the two being one movement seen from two sides (the grumbling heard, the heeding refused). The full process: the covenant people, from an unnamed interior source and no organ-seat, produce a negation — they do not heed God's own voice; the failure is welded outward to God and paired inwardly with their murmuring, a standing refusal reported flatly, its dreadful consequences left as surrounding context rather than booked to the act. Read without valence-bias, this is the descent-form of the family: obedience present only as its absence, the inner being turning from the very word that would have steadied it.

**hasten** — Psa 119:60  ·  `H2363:hasten#1`

_Story_

Deep in the long psalm about loving God's law, the writer describes what his devotion looks like in practice: the moment he sees what God asks, he moves — he hastens and does not drag his feet. There is no gap between knowing and doing; the obedience comes quickly, eagerly, rushing toward the very commands others resist. The passage does not spell out what fuels the hurry, though the whole psalm is soaked in love and delight for the word; here it simply shows the speed itself, a settled habit of the man's heart rather than a one-time rush. And it belongs to a larger pattern running through the psalm — seeking, keeping, remembering, loving. Where God's enemies only cringed under force and the wilderness generation refused to listen, this man runs to obey, and gladly.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is chush, hasten: 'I hasten and do not delay to keep your commandments.' The reading is of speed in obeying — promptness, the closing of the gap between knowing the command and doing it. Type (102) is action: hastening is a doing, obedience carried out quickly. Source (103) is absent: the reading does not name what interior drives the haste — no motive-root is booked, though the surrounding psalm (delight, love, comfort in the word) supplies the warmth; the source-row itself is left empty, so the reading reports the speed, not its spring. Seat (104) is none: the hastening is not organ-located, not lodged in a named inner faculty — it is the person's promptness, not an organ's operation. Bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred, to_span 271962): the first-person interior of the devoted keeper of the law, the voice running through Psalm 119. The operation (106) is 'hasten to obey': the event is obedience done without delay — the movement itself is the swiftness. Target (107) is God's word (inferred, to_span 271962): what the haste rushes toward is the commandments, so the interior speeds Godward, to the very law the fathers of Psalm 106 refused. Manner (108) is none: no mode is separately marked — the 'how' beyond the speed itself is not booked; the not-delaying is stated as fact, the promptness left as its own plain quality. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: no degree of the haste is measured beyond 'do not delay,' nothing further particularises it, and no downstream effect is booked — the promptness is reported as a trait, not scored or followed to an outcome. Coupling (112) carries a value: 'paired within its char-arc across the psalm' (inferred, to_span 271962) — the hastening is not an isolated spike but bound into the larger movement of devotion that runs through Psalm 119 (seeking, keeping, loving, remembering); it is welded to that arc rather than to a single companion movement. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114) names it: v60, 'I hasten and do not delay to keep your commandments' — 'the promptness of obedience.' Role (115) is characteristic: a standing trait of the psalmist's inner being, not a single burst. Locus (116) is external:god: the hastening is oriented outward toward God — its reference-point sits with the LORD and his commands, the interior turning outward to obey. The full process: from an unnamed interior source and no organ-seat, the psalmist's inner being produces a prompt action — obedience without delay, rushing toward God's word, welded into the whole devotional arc of the psalm and oriented outward to God. Set against the family's other readings, this is the ascent-form of true obedience: where the enemies of Psalm 66 only feign submission under compulsion and the fathers of Psalm 106 refuse to heed, here the interior runs to obey of its own eagerness — the same word, met not with a forced cringe nor a withheld ear but with haste.
