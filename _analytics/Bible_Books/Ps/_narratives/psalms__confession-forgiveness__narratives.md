**pardon** — Psa 25:11  ·  `H5545:pardon#1`

_Story_

In this prayer the man asks God to pardon his guilt, and the striking thing is that he does not make his guilt smaller to make it easier to forgive - he admits plainly that it is great. This is the act of a clear-eyed, honest mind owning what he has done, not a person swept along by feeling. The prayer is his own; nothing is said to have forced it out of him, and it does not belong to any single part of him - it is his whole self turning honest. What he is really after is not just to feel better but to tell the truth about himself, and his plea for pardon and his confession that the guilt is great are bound so tightly they cannot be pulled apart. The passage never tells us how forcefully he prays, and - tellingly - it never says he was forgiven; the prayer simply ends with the appeal, leaving the outcome in God's hands rather than claiming it.

_Reading_

At Psa 25:11 the inner-being characteristic is pardon (H5545), read from the plea 'pardon my guilt, for it is great' (sense 101, 7695798). Its type is cognition (102, 7695799): the movement is an act of the knowing, appraising self, not a bodily impulse or a surge of affect. The bearer is the psalmist (105, 7695801) - the self that owns the guilt and voices the appeal. No source is recorded (103 present:false): the passage does not name a prior stimulus producing this act; it is not tagged as caused by anything, but stands as the self's own turn toward the LORD ('for your name's sake'). No seat is found (104 seat=none, 7695800): the pardon-seeking is not localised to any inner organ - not heart, not kidneys, not bones - so it reads as a whole-self act rather than an organ-bound one. The operation (106, 7695802) is 'the self asks pardon for its guilt, honestly owning that it is great - confession without minimising': the movement runs outward as appeal (to God, the arena it acts toward) while turning inward to name its own guilt truthfully. The target (107, 7695803) is honest-confession - what the operation aims at is not merely relief but truthful self-naming. Manner is none (108, 7695804): no qualifying mode is recorded (no 'secretly', 'boldly', or the like) - the act is bare, unmodified confession. The coupling is pardon-great-guilt (112, 7695805): the request for pardon is welded to the admission that the guilt is great; the two cannot be prised apart, and the interior refuses to shrink the sin to make it more pardonable. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are all present:false: the passage does not measure the force of the act, does not narrow it further, and - tellingly - reports no outcome. Unlike Psa 32:5, confession here draws no immediately-recorded forgiveness; the reading ends open, at the appeal, its result unstated. That silence at effect is itself evidence: the movement is the self's, and its outcome is left with God rather than claimed. Prohibition is absent (113 present:false): nothing is forbidden in this act. The discovery note (114, 7695807) confirms the reading: the operation is unflinching confession - the interior does not shrink the sin but names it great and appeals to God's name. Role is characteristic (115, 7695808): this is a genuine inner-being trait, not a passing qualifier. Locus is internal:ib-state (116, 7695806): the whole movement is an internal state of the self, not an outward observable performance.

**confess** — Psa 32:5  ·  `H3034:confes#1`

_Story_

Here the man describes the moment he stopped hiding. He had been keeping quiet about his wrong, and it was wearing him down; then he made a decision - he owned up to his sin and refused to cover it over, choosing to confess. This is an act of the will, a deliberate choice to come clean rather than just a change of mood. Admitting the sin inwardly and speaking it out are, for him, one and the same move; you cannot separate the private admission from the open confession. It is his whole self that turns, not merely one corner of him. And the moment he spoke, forgiveness came - the passage presents this as the turning point of the whole prayer, the instant hiding gave way to honesty and relief followed at once.

_Reading_

At Psa 32:5 the characteristic is confess (H3034), read from 'I confessed and did not cover it' (sense 101, 7697514). Its type is volition (102, 7697515) - and this sets it apart from the two cognition-typed readings in this family: here confession is an act of the will, a chosen resolve ('I will confess'), not primarily an appraisal. The bearer is the psalmist (105, 7697517). No source is recorded (103 present:false): although the wider psalm shows the self crushed under silence in vv3-4, that pressure is not tagged as the source within this lexical; the confession stands as the self's own decisive turn. Seat is none (104, 7697516): not organ-located - a whole-self resolve rather than a movement lodged in any inner organ. The operation (106, 7697518) is 'the self acknowledged its sin, did not cover its iniquity, resolved to confess - and God forgave - the decisive turn': the movement runs acknowledge to refuse-to-cover to resolve-to-confess, and is met by forgiveness. The target (107, 7697519) is confessing-turn - the aim is the turn itself, the shift from hiding to open speech. Manner is none (108, 7697520): no qualifying mode is recorded. The coupling is acknowledge-confess (112, 7697521): inward acknowledgement and outward confession are welded into one act, not two separable stages - inner recognition and spoken admission are the same move. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are all present:false. The absence at effect is notable: even though the verse says 'you forgave the iniquity of my sin', that forgiveness is folded into the operation event rather than tagged as a separate effect dimension - so at the dimension level the outcome is carried by the operation itself, not by 111. Prohibition is absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7697523) reads this as the hinge of the psalm: the interior stops hiding and speaks, and forgiveness follows at once. Role is characteristic (115, 7697524). Locus is internal:ib-state (116, 7697522): the whole movement is an internal state of the self.

**confess** — Psa 38:18  ·  `H5046:confes#1`

_Story_

In the middle of great suffering - his body aching, his wounds festering, his friends keeping their distance - the man turns and says plainly that he confesses his wrongdoing and is genuinely sorry for it. What stands out is that he grieves the sin itself, not just the misery it has brought him. This is a clear-eyed recognition of guilt, and it comes with real sorrow; the confessing and the grieving are one and the same movement, not two separate things. It is his whole self, not merely one part of him, that turns penitent. The passage does not measure how intensely he feels it, and it never says that forgiveness came in answer; it leaves him at the point of honest, sorrowful confession, with the outcome unspoken.

_Reading_

At Psa 38:18 the characteristic is confess (H5046), read from 'I confess my iniquity; I am sorry for my sin' (sense 101, 7699648). Type is cognition (102, 7699649) - like the pardon reading at Psa 25:11 and unlike the volition of Psa 32:5: here the accent falls on the self's appraising recognition of guilt rather than on a willed resolve. The bearer is the psalmist (105, 7699651). No source is recorded (103 present:false): though the psalm is soaked in bodily suffering (festering wounds, failing strength, throbbing heart, vv3-10), the confession is not tagged as produced by that suffering within this lexical; it stands on its own. Seat is none (104, 7699650): not organ-located, despite the surrounding heart-and-bones language of the psalm - the confessing act itself reads as whole-self, not lodged in an organ. The operation (106, 7699652) is 'the self confesses its iniquity and is sorry for its sin - penitence spoken and felt': the movement pairs speech (confess) with feeling (sorrow). The target (107, 7699653) is penitence - the aim is penitence itself, guilt named and grieved. Manner is none (108, 7699654): no qualifying mode recorded. The coupling is confess-and-sorry (112, 7699655): confession and sorrow are welded into one act - the naming of guilt and the grief over it are inseparable, so penitence is not merely spoken but felt. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are all present:false: no measured force, and - as at Psa 25:11 and unlike Psa 32:5 - no answering forgiveness is recorded; the reading ends at the penitent act, its result unstated. That silence at effect is evidence in its own right. Prohibition is absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7699657) draws the key distinction: the penitent turn names its guilt and grieves it, not merely its consequences - in a psalm heavy with consequences (pain, stinking wounds, abandonment by friends), the confession targets the sin itself, not just the suffering it caused. Role is characteristic (115, 7699658). Locus is internal:ib-state (116, 7699656): an internal state of the self throughout.
