**wrong** — Psa 7:3  ·  `H5766:wrong#1`

_Story_

The psalmist does something bold: he puts his own conscience on trial. He names a wrong - and then dares it to be true of him. If I have done this, he says, if there is any crookedness in what my hands have done, if I have paid back a friend with evil, then let my enemy hunt me down and trample me. It is not a feeling but a hard act of self-examination, the mind searching itself without flinching. He never claims the wrong came from anywhere or lives in any part of him; he simply holds the whole self up to the light and stakes everything on being clean. He does not say what the outcome will be - he only makes the daring wager, resting his entire appeal on an integrity he has looked at honestly and found intact.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is an oath of innocence: 'if there is wrong (evel) in my hands' - wrong named only to be disowned. Type (102) is cognition: this is an act of the mind, a searching self-examination, not a felt state or a deed. Source (103) goes unrecorded - the reading does not trace where the wrong would spring from; it is entertained hypothetically, not attributed. Seat (104) resolves to none: the wrong is not lodged in an organ but held against the whole self. Bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred): the interior that carries the operation is his own, turned on itself. Operation (106) is the core event - the self submits itself to a conditional self-curse: if I have done this wrong, let the enemy overtake me; a rigorous self-audit staked on integrity. Target (107) is self-imprecation (inferred): the operation aims the curse back at the bearer if guilt is found. Manner (108) is none - no mode is marked; the oath stands plainly. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) and prohibition (113) are all absent: no degree measured, nothing narrowed, no downstream outcome booked, no forbidding issued - the reading reports the daring examination, not its result. Coupling (112) is oath-if-guilty (inferred): the wrong is bound into a conditional, only real if the self is guilty. Discovery (114) locates it in v3-4, the searching 'if I have done this... if I have repaid my friend with evil'. Role (115) is characteristic; locus (116) is internal:ib-state - the whole movement sits inside as a condition of the moral self. The full process: from no named source, the psalmist's mind turns on itself, dares the worst upon itself should wrong be found in its hands, and rests its whole plea on the integrity that self-audit confirms.

*Why this reading differs:* This is one of two readings of 'wrong' (evel). Here at Psa 7:3 the wrong is entertained by the righteous self as a hypothesis it disowns - a cognition, self-directed, an oath staked on innocence. At Psa 58:2 (H5766:wrong#2) the same word names the actual product of the wicked rulers' hearts. The difference is decisive: the bearer shifts from the upright self examining itself to the corrupt powerful manufacturing injustice, and the movement flips from ascent (integrity confirmed) to descent (injustice devised). The same word marks both the thing the righteous refuses and the thing the wicked produces.

**integrity** — Psa 7:8  ·  `H8537:integrity#1`

_Story_

Most people fear being judged. This man asks for it. Judge me, he says to God - weigh me by my own uprightness, by the wholeness of purpose you will find inside me. He does not explain where that integrity came from or point to any single part of himself; it is simply there, in him, and he is willing to have the whole of it examined. What would terrify a guilty person he welcomes, because he expects the verdict to clear him. His integrity is not something he hides but something he offers up, confident that the closest possible inspection will only prove him right.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is an appeal to be judged on integrity: 'judge me... according to the integrity (tom) that is in me'. Type (102) is volition: an act of the will, integrity offered up to the divine court. Source (103) is unrecorded - the reading does not say whence the integrity comes; it is simply claimed as present. Seat (104) resolves to none: not organ-located but held by the whole person ('that is in me'). Bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred). Operation (106) is the core event: the self asks God to judge it by its own righteousness and integrity - a confidence so settled it invites, rather than dreads, the verdict. Target (107) is the appeal-to-be-judged (inferred). Manner (108) is none. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) and prohibition (113) are all absent - no degree, no narrowing, no outcome booked, no forbidding. Coupling (112) is judge-my-integrity (inferred): the integrity is welded to the plea for judgment. Discovery (114) sets it at v8. Role (115) is characteristic; locus (116) is internal:ib-state. The full process: from no named source, the will lifts the integrity it holds within and stakes it before God's tribunal, so sure of its innocence that judgment is sought as vindication.

*Why this reading differs:* First of four readings of 'integrity' (tom). Here at Psa 7:8 integrity is offered to God's court as grounds for a favourable verdict - a volition of confident self-exposure. It differs from Psa 25:21 (integrity pleaded as a preserver in dependence), from Psa 41:12 (integrity gratefully seen to have been honoured by God), and from Psa 101:2 (integrity resolved on as a way of life). Here the accent is on inviting judgment; the same character stands under four different pressures.

**upright** — Psa 11:7  ·  `H3477:upright#1`

_Story_

The psalm ends not with a prize but with a promise: the upright will see God's face. Everything the wicked aim at the honest heart - their arrows shot in the dark - comes to nothing, because God himself is righteous and loves what is right. And the reward he holds out to the straight-hearted is not treasure or safety but himself: they shall behold his face. The passage does not measure this hope or spell out what follows from it. It simply sets the whole direction of an upright life toward one thing - the sight of God at the end.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'the upright (yashar) shall behold his face'. Type (102) is affect: the reading reads uprightness by its emotional-relational reward, a longing fulfilled in sight of God. Target (107) is the hope-of-seeing-God (inferred). Manner (108) is none. *Why this reading differs:* First of ten readings of 'upright' (yashar). Here at Psa 11:7 the operation is the final vision of God - uprightness rewarded by seeing his face, read as affect. Across the ten the yashar-heart does many different things: rules over the doomed rich (49:14), exults in heart (64:10), pursues returning justice (94:15), has joy stored for it (97:11), rejoices at God's reversals (107:42), gathers to praise (111:1), sees its line blessed (112:2), receives light in darkness (112:4), and is the object of God's good (125:4). This first reading is distinctive for making the reward a face-to-face seeing rather than a benefit received.

**moved** — Psa 15:5  ·  `H4131:mov#1`

_Story_

Then it ends with a single verdict: a person like this will never be shaken. It does not explain where the steadiness comes from or what happens next. It simply says that a life built this way becomes unmovable, like bedrock. The integrity is the foundation, and the reward is a stability that nothing can knock down.

**interest** — Psa 15:5  ·  `H5392:interest#1`

_Story_

Integrity here shows itself in two refusals. The person who may live in God's presence is one who will not lend money at interest to squeeze the poor, and will not take a bribe to twist a verdict against an innocent person. It is character measured by what it will not do - not by any dramatic act, but by the quiet decision to keep clean hands where money and power meet. He is not obeying a rule imposed from outside; this is simply who he is. The passage does not weigh how hard it is or what he gains by it; it just names the two things a person of integrity refuses to touch.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'no usury, no bribe' - integrity read through what it refuses. Type (102) is volition: an act of the will, a deliberate abstention. Source (103) is unrecorded. Seat (104) resolves to none - not organ-located; the refusal belongs to the whole moral person. Bearer (105) is the righteous (inferred). Operation (106) is the core event: he does not lend money at interest or take a bribe against the innocent - integrity worked out in money and in justice. Target (107) is financial-integrity (inferred). Manner (108) is none. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) and prohibition (113) are all absent: though the content is a refusal, no formal prohibition is booked - the row reads it as the person's own conduct, not a law laid on him; no degree or outcome is scored. Coupling (112) is no-usury-no-bribe (inferred): the two refusals are welded together as one integrity. Discovery (114) sets it at v5. Role (115) is characteristic; locus (116) is internal:ib-state. The full process: from no named source, the will refuses to profit from the vulnerable or to sell a just verdict - integrity shown not in grand deeds but in what it will not do with money and power.

**righteousness** — Psa 18:20  ·  `H6664:righteousnes#1`

_Story_

Rescued from enemies too strong for him, the psalmist does not treat his deliverance as luck. He reasons it out: God dealt with me like this because of my righteousness, because my hands were clean. He can point to a record - ways kept, God not abandoned - and he reads his rescue as a fair response to it, not an accident. Inner rightness and clean conduct go together in his mind, and he is confident that God noticed and answered them. The passage does not say where that righteousness came from; it simply shows a man reading his own rescue as the just reward of an integrity he knows he kept.

**pure** — Psa 24:4  ·  `H1249:pure#1`

_Story_

Who is allowed to climb God's holy hill and stand in his presence? The psalm answers with two things held together: clean hands and a pure heart. It is not enough to act rightly on the outside if the inside is corrupt, and it is not enough to feel pure within if the hands are dirty. Both must be clean - the deed and the heart, matching each other. The passage does not say how a person gets this purity or what follows from it; it simply names it as the single condition for coming near to God. Purity, inside and out, is the door.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'clean hands and a pure (bar) heart'. Type (102) is state: a settled condition of purity, not an act. Source (103) is unrecorded. Seat (104) resolves to none for the term itself, though the verse pairs the pure heart with clean hands - the reading treats purity as a state of the whole person rather than an isolated organ.
