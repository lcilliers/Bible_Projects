## Psa 15:1-5

> A Psalm of David . Psa 15:1 O Lord , who shall sojourn in your tent ? Who shall dwell on your holy hill ? Psa 15:2 He who walks blamelessly and does what is right and speaks truth in his heart ; Psa 15:3 who does not slander with his tongue and does no evil to his neighbor , nor takes up a reproach against his friend ; Psa 15:4 in whose eyes a vile person is despised , but who honors those who fear the Lord ; who swears to his own hurt and does not change ; Psa 15:5 who does not put out his money at interest and does not take a bribe against the innocent . He who does these things shall never be moved .

**swears** — Psa 15:4  ·  `H7650:swear#1`

_Story_

This is about a person who gives their word and keeps it even when keeping it hurts. The psalm is asking who is fit to be God's guest, and one mark of that person is this: they make a promise, discover it will cost them, and still refuse to back out. It isn't a mood that comes over them; it's a settled choice they hold to. Notice what the text does not say — it never tells us the promise was kept gladly or grudgingly, only that it was kept. The promise and the loss are bound together as a single act, and the point is simply this: the inside of such a person will not break a word just because it became expensive.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is a costly oath kept, and the type (102) is volition — not a feeling that seizes the interior but a stance it takes and holds. No source (103, absent) is named: the passage does not trace where this fidelity comes from, so the reading rests on the act itself, not on its origin. No seat (104, value 'none') is given — this is telling: the operation is not lodged in a named organ (not heart, not tongue); it is a whole-person disposition rather than an interior faculty firing. The bearer (105) is the righteous, the one who may sojourn in God's tent (vv.1–2), inferred from the qualifying clauses that define him. The operation (106) is the event itself: 'he swears to his own hurt and does not change' — integrity that holds even when keeping the word costs him. The target (107) is costly-fidelity: what the interior aims at is not advantage but the standing of its own given word. Manner (108, value 'none') is not specified — no adverb of how (not reluctantly, not gladly); the text reports only that it holds, leaving the inner tone unrecorded. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are all absent: the passage does not grade the strength of the resolve, does not narrow it further, and does not name a downstream result within this interior — though the psalm's frame ('shall never be moved', v.5) supplies a corporate reward, no per-item effect is coded here. The coupling (112) is 'swear-to-own-hurt': the swearing is welded to loss; the promise and its cost are held together as one movement, not two. Prohibition (113) is absent — this is framed positively, as a thing done, not a thing forbidden. The discovery note (114) confirms the finding: the interior will not renege on a promise merely because it turned out expensive. The role (115) is characteristic — a genuine trait of the inner life, not a passing qualifier. The locus (116) is internal:ib-state: this is a condition of the inner being, God and neighbour standing as the arena the fidelity acts toward (the oath is sworn before them and to them), while the operation itself is interior.

**set** — Psa 16:8  ·  `H7737:set#1`

_Story_

Here a person deliberately keeps God in view all the time — not as a passing thought but as a steady, chosen focus. Because he keeps looking that way, he feels that God stands right beside him, and so he is not thrown off balance by whatever comes. What is striking is that his steadiness isn't a separate strength he works up; it is simply the result of where he keeps looking. The psalm names his glad heart elsewhere, but this particular act belongs to the whole person, not to one part of him. It is a habit of attention, freely chosen and held moment by moment.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'set the LORD always before me', and the type (102) is volition — a chosen act of the will, not an involuntary state. No source (103, absent) is coded: the psalm does not say what prompted the setting, so the reading stands on the practice itself. No seat (104, value 'none'): the setting is not assigned to a bodily organ — significant, because vv.7 and 9 do name the heart ('my heart instructs me', 'my heart is glad'), yet this particular operation is left un-seated, marking it as a whole-self orientation rather than a heart-event. The bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred), the 'I' of the poem. The operation (106) is a sustained practice of attention: the self keeps the LORD continually before it, so that with him at its right hand it will not be shaken. The target (107) is 'God-before-me' — the aim is God held constantly in view. Manner (108, value 'none') is unspecified: the text does not describe the style of the attending. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent — no grading, no narrowing, and no separately coded result, though the passage's own logic ('I shall not be shaken', v.8) shows the steadiness that follows. The coupling (112) is 'set-the-LORD': the act of setting is bound to its object; the steadiness is not an independent achievement but the fruit of the fixed gaze. Prohibition (113) is absent — this is a positive practice. The discovery note (114) states it exactly: this is a chosen, continual orientation, and the steadiness follows from the practice. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state — an interior posture, with God as the arena toward which the whole inner life is turned.

*Why this reading differs:* The item 'set' has two readings. Here (16:8) the target is God himself held before the inner eye, the locus is internal:ib-state, and the coupling is the resulting steadiness ('shall not be shaken'). At 119:30 the same verb sets God's rules (his word) before the self, with an external:god locus and a covenant-arc coupling. The difference is the object of the setting: a Person to be gazed on versus a rule to be lived by — the same volitional act aimed once at God and once at his law.

**keep** — Psa 25:10  ·  `H5341:keep#1`

_Story_

This describes the person who holds to God's agreement with them — who stays true to the terms. The psalm makes a quiet promise to such a person: everything God does toward them will turn out to be love and faithfulness. In other words, the one who keeps faith discovers that every path God leads them down is kindness. The keeping isn't tied to any single part of them; it is simply how they live. And the text does not measure how hard they keep or what reward follows — it only says that fidelity opens the eyes to see that all of God's ways are mercy.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'those who keep his covenant', and the type (102) is volition — covenant-keeping as a stance the will maintains. No source (103, absent): the passage does not name what generates the keeping. No seat (104, value 'none'): the keeping is not organ-located; it is the disposition of a whole person, not a faculty. The bearer (105) is the covenant-keepers (inferred), those of v.10. The operation (106) is the event of v.10: all God's paths are steadfast love and faithfulness for those who keep his covenant — fidelity that walks in love; the keeping is the condition under which the whole of God's dealing is experienced as mercy. The target (107) is covenant-fidelity — the aim is holding to God's terms. Manner (108, value 'none'): the style of the keeping is unstated. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) are absent — no grade, no narrowing, no separately-coded result, though the discovery draws out that such a one finds all God's ways to be love. The coupling (112) is 'keep-covenant': the keeping is welded to its object, the covenant and testimonies together. Prohibition (113) is absent — positively framed. The discovery note (114): the interior that holds to God's terms finds all his ways to be love. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state — an interior fidelity, with God as the arena; note the contrast with most other 'keep' readings whose locus is external:god, marking this one as read from the side of the keeper's inner condition rather than from the object kept.

*Why this reading differs:* 'keep' (natsar/shamar) has four distinct readings. This one (25:10) reads the keeping from inside the keeper (locus internal:ib-state) as the condition for experiencing God's paths as love; type volition. It differs from 34:13 (keeping the tongue from evil — a speech-restraint), from 78:7 (children keeping commandments as the fruit of hope), and from the 119:2 arc (guarding the testimonies as the mark of the blessed). The shift is in what is kept and to what end: a whole covenant embraced inwardly here, a mouth policed there, a rule obeyed as fruit elsewhere.

**keep** — Psa 34:13  ·  `H5341:keep#2`

_Story_

The teacher turns to those who want a good and long life and gives them the first step: guard your mouth. Keep your tongue away from evil and your lips from lies. The point is that the desire for a good life has to begin with controlling what you say. The mouth is what gets policed, but the power to police it belongs to the whole person, not to any one part. The psalm doesn't spell out how hard this is or exactly what reward follows — it simply sets a clean tongue as the doorway to the life you are hoping for.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'keep your tongue from evil', and the type (102) is volition — a commanded act of self-restraint the will must perform. No source (103, absent): no origin for the restraint is coded. No seat (104, value 'none'): although the operation concerns the tongue and lips, the keeping itself is not assigned an interior seat — the mouth is the thing governed, not the place from which the governing issues; the reading leaves the governing power un-located, a whole-self act. The bearer (105) is the hearer (inferred), the 'children' addressed in v.11 who desire life (v.12). The operation (106) is the teaching event of v.13: keep the tongue from evil and the lips from deceit — the guarded mouth as the path to life. The target (107) is tongue-guarding — the aim is a mouth kept clean of evil and deceit. Manner (108, value 'none'): the how is unstated. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) are absent — no grade, no narrowing, no separately coded result, though the frame promises 'life' and 'good days' (v.12) as the horizon. The coupling (112) is 'keep-tongue': the keeping is bound to its object, the tongue. Prohibition (113) is absent as a coded row, yet notably the operation is itself negatively shaped ('from evil', 'from deceit') — the guarding is a holding-back; the absence of a prohibition row alongside a restraint-operation shows the restraint is read as a positive discipline, not a rule imposed. The discovery note (114): the interior that wants good life must first police its speech. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state — the disposition is interior, its arena the neighbour who would be harmed by evil or deceitful speech.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the four 'keep' readings, this one (34:13) is the only one whose object is the tongue rather than the covenant or testimonies: keeping here is speech-restraint aimed at the neighbour, the guarded mouth as the path to life. It differs from 25:10 (covenant embraced inwardly), 78:7 (commandments kept as hope's fruit), and 119:2 (testimonies guarded as the mark of blessedness). The keeping turns outward to speech here, inward to fidelity elsewhere.

**guard** — Psa 39:1  ·  `H8104:guard#1`

_Story_

A man decides he will watch every step and put a muzzle on his mouth so that he won't sin with his words — especially while wicked people are watching him. It is a deliberate, hard clamp on his own speech. But the psalm is honest about what happens next: the forced silence doesn't calm him; it makes things worse. Holding it all in, his pain grows and something burns inside until he finally has to speak. So this is a real resolve to guard the tongue, chosen freely and aimed at avoiding sin — yet the poem shows that bottling everything up can bring its own kind of trouble.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'guard my ways, muzzle my mouth', and the type (102) is volition — a deliberate resolve of the will. No source (103, absent): the trigger is the presence of the wicked (v.1b), but this is not coded as a source row, so the reading rests on the resolve itself. No seat (104, value 'none'): the guarding is not lodged in an organ; the mouth is muzzled but the guarding power is un-seated, a whole-self act. The bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred). The operation (106) is the event of v.1: the self resolves to guard its ways so as not to sin with the tongue, keeping its mouth with a muzzle while the wicked are near — deliberate speech-restraint. The target (107) is tongue-guarding. Manner (108, value 'none'): the how is unstated, though the image of a 'muzzle' supplies the severity that the manner-row itself does not code. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) are absent as coded rows — yet the passage's own aftermath is stark and must not be filtered (directive 6): the enforced silence does not bring peace but makes his 'distress grew worse' and his 'heart became hot within' (vv.2–3), so that the muzzle finally breaks. No effect row captures this, but the reading should note that the resolve, coded here only up to v.1, is one the psalm goes on to show failing. The coupling (112) is 'muzzle-my-mouth': the guarding is welded to the muzzle-image, a self-imposed clamp. Prohibition (113) is absent as a row, though the operation is again negatively shaped ('that I may not sin'). The discovery note (114): a self-imposed silence, the interior clamping down on speech to avoid sin. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state — an interior resolve, its arena the wicked in whose presence he will not speak.

*Why this reading differs:* 'guard' has two readings. Here (39:1) guarding is self-imposed speech-restraint under pressure from the wicked — a muzzle on the mouth that the psalm later shows straining and breaking. At 119:9 guarding is keeping one's way pure 'according to your word', aimed at God's word rather than at silencing the tongue. The difference is direction and outcome: a defensive clamp against sin here, a positive purity kept by the word there.

**watch** — Psa 56:6  ·  `H8104:watch#1`

_Story_

The very same word for keeping watch shows up here on the wrong side: it is the psalmist's enemies who are watching. They track his every step, hiding and waiting like hunters in ambush, and their watching is tied to one aim — they are lying in wait for his life. This is vigilance turned into a hunt. The poem refuses to prettify it: the careful attention that can be a virtue is here the alert, patient malice of people who want to destroy him. It is a picture of what watchfulness becomes when a hostile heart puts it to use.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'watch / keep an eye on' (shamar — 'they watch my steps'), and the type (102) is action, not volition — an outward deed rather than an inner stance; this matters, because the same keeping-verb elsewhere in the family is a disposition, but here it is a hostile activity. No source (103, absent): the passage does not code why the enemies watch, though their malice is plain in context. No seat (104, value 'none'): the watching is not organ-located. The bearer (105) is the enemies (inferred) — and this is the point directive 5 forbids us to soften: the keeping-verb is here carried by the wicked, the descent read with the same weight as any ascent. The operation (106) is 'watch / spy on'. The target (107) is 'my steps', coded as a span-pair (from_span 280300 to_span 280301) — a real linkage in the text between the watchers and the psalmist's every movement. Manner (108) is 'lurking / in ambush' — unlike most readings this manner IS specified: the watching is predatory, done from concealment. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) are absent as rows. The coupling (112) is a span-pair, 'paired with waiting for his life' (from 280300 to 280303): the surveillance is welded to a design on his very life — they watch his steps as they have 'waited for my life'. Prohibition (113) is absent. The discovery note (114): predatory surveillance; shamar here is the enemies dogging his every move, lurking to catch him — vigilance turned to a hunt for his soul. Role (115) is characteristic — read as a genuine trait of the wicked interior, not a mere qualifier. Locus (116) is external:person — the operation is lodged in the hostile persons, the arena being the psalmist they hunt; this is the inner life of the wicked expressed as watchfulness bent to harm.

*Why this reading differs:* 'watch' has three readings and they split sharply. Here (56:6) and at 71:10 the watchers are the enemies and the locus is external:person — predatory surveillance bent on the psalmist's life (this reading adds the 'lurking/in ambush' manner and the span-pair binding the watching to a design on his life). At 59:9 the same verb is turned around: the psalmist watches FOR God (locus external:god), the one reading in this trio that even carries a source (God as fortress). This 56:6 reading is the descent — vigilance as a hunt.

## Psa 59:7-13

> Psa 59:7 There they are , bellowing with their mouths with swords in their lips — for “ Who ,” they think, “will hear us?” Psa 59:8 But you , O Lord , laugh at them; you hold all the nations in derision . Psa 59:9 O my Strength , I will watch for you, for you, O God , are my fortress . Psa 59:10 My God in his steadfast love will meet me; God will let me look in triumph on my enemies . For the cursing and lies that they utter , Psa 59:13 consume them in wrath ; consume them till they are no more , that they may know that God rules over Jacob to the ends of the earth . Selah

**watch** — Psa 59:9  ·  `H8104:watch#2`

_Story_

In a psalm surrounded by enemies who keep watch to do harm, the psalmist takes that same watching and turns it upward: 'I will keep watch for you.' And here, unusually, the poem tells us why he can — because God is his strong fortress who comes to meet him in love. So his watching is not anxious scanning of the horizon but expectant waiting on God, confident help is coming. Even better, the very words he watches with here come back later as a song of thanks, so his waiting ends in praise. It is the bright mirror-image of the enemies' watching: the same alertness, but aimed at God and grounded in trust.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'watch / keep watch' (shamar — 'I will watch for you'), and the type (102) is action. Uniquely in this whole family, the source (103) is PRESENT (not absent): 'because God is his fortress who meets him in steadfast love' (vv.9–10), coded as a span-pair (280648→280651). This is the standout finding — nearly every other reading has no source, but here the watching is explicitly grounded: the interior keeps watch because God is its fortress. No seat (104, value 'none'): the watching is not organ-located. The bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred). The operation (106) is 'watch / wait expectantly'. The target (107) is 'for God (my Strength, my fortress)', a span-pair (280648→280647) — the watching is aimed at God himself. Manner (108, value 'none'): the how is unstated. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) are absent. The coupling (112) is a span-pair, 'the refrain answered in singing' (280648→280573): the expectant watching is welded to the psalm's later song of the same words (vv.16–17), so the watching finds its resolution in praise. Prohibition (113) is absent. The discovery note (114): the turn from the watching enemies (superscription/v.1) to the watching believer — shamar here is the psalmist keeping watch FOR God as the enemies keep watch against him, expectant waiting on his Strength and fortress. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is external:god — the operation reaches out toward God as its arena and object.

*Why this reading differs:* This is the ascent among the three 'watch' readings and the only one that carries a source: the psalmist watches FOR God (locus external:god), grounded because God is his fortress, and the watching resolves into later song.
