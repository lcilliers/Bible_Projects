## Psa 2:1-2

> Psa 2:1 Why do the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain ? Psa 2:2 The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together , against the Lord and against his Anointed , saying,

**plot** — Psa 2:1  ·  `H1897:plot#1`

_Story_

When the nations rage against God, their fury does not stay raw — it turns into thinking. The heat cools into a plan, a scheme worked out in the mind, and yet the poem says from the first breath that the plan is empty, doomed before it starts. The text does not tell us what first lit the anger; it simply shows the plotting already in motion, and it does not pin the scheming to any single part of them — it is the whole crowd's mind bent to a purpose. Nothing comes of it: no force, no result, only the futility that clings to the plan itself. What we see is a real inward movement — a people's minds hardening a wish into a design — and that design is hollow.

_Reading_

At Psa 2:1 the sense is 'the peoples plot in vain', typed as cognition (101/102): the inner faculty in view is not raw feeling but the mind at work. The bearer is the peoples (105), and the operation traces a movement from heat to design — the raging cools into scheming, a plan devised though stamped empty from the outset (106, 114). The target is the scheming itself, 'futile-scheming' (107), and the coupling binds the plot to its vanity ('plot-vain', 112): the act of planning is inseparable, in this verse, from its foredoomed failure. No source is recorded (103, present:false) — the text does not name what first stirs the rage into thought; the reader is shown the plotting already underway. Seat is 'none' (104): the plotting is not lodged in a named organ; it is a movement of the collective mind, not an anatomised faculty. Manner is 'none' (108) and intensity, specifier, effect are all absent (109/110/111) — the passage gives no style, no degree, no downstream consequence within the inner life; the plotting simply is, and its emptiness is its whole outcome. Prohibition is absent (113): this is not framed as forbidden but as observed. Role is characteristic (115) and locus is internal:ib-state (116): the plotting is a genuine inner-being movement, seated within the peoples' collective interior even as it aims outward against the LORD and his Anointed.

**lurks** — Psa 10:9  ·  `H0693:lurk#1`

_Story_

The wicked man here is like a lion hidden in the brush, perfectly still, waiting for the weak to come close enough to seize. This is not a flash of temper but a settled act of will — his whole inner self is crouched and ready, tuned to the kill. The waiting is not passive; it is the first move of the pounce, bound up with the seizing that will follow. The poem does not explain where such a predatory bent comes from, and it does not locate it in any one part of him — it is the whole man, coiled. There is no display, no measure given, no aftermath described; just deliberate, patient readiness to spring on the helpless.

_Reading_

At Psa 10:9 the sense is 'lurks like a lion to seize', typed as volition (101/102): this is the will coiled and set, not a passing mood. The bearer is the wicked (105). The operation is patient predation — he lies in ambush like a lion in its thicket, waiting to seize and drag off the poor (106); the discovery names the whole inner posture as a hunter's patience, the interior 'coiled to spring on the helpless' (114). Target is 'predatory-ambush' (107) and the coupling ties the lurking to the seizing that completes it ('lurk-and-seize', 112): the waiting is not idle but is the first movement of the strike. No source is recorded (103, present:false) — the passage does not say what breeds this predatory will; it presents it fully formed. Seat is 'none' (104): the lurking is not assigned to a named organ but is a whole-self stance. Manner, intensity, specifier, effect are all absent or 'none' (108/109/110/111): no described style, degree, or consequence within the interior — the predation is given as bare, deliberate readiness. Prohibition is absent (113). Role is characteristic (115), locus internal:ib-state (116): this crouching, waiting malice is a real inner-being disposition, an interior tuned entirely to violence though its object stands outside.

## Psa 26:1-12

Psa 26:8 O Lord , I love the habitation of your house and the place where your glory dwells . **hate** — Psa 26:5  ·  `H8130:hate#1`

_Story_

The psalmist declares that he hates the gathering of evildoers and will not take his seat among them. His hatred is a feeling, but a chosen and aimed one — it is really a refusal to belong, a drawing of a line between himself and the corrupt. It is bound to their company: he hates being among them, not people in the abstract. The poem does not spell out here what grounds the aversion, though his claim to integrity stands nearby, and it does not lodge the feeling in any single organ — it is the whole man taking a stand. There is no heat or outburst described and no consequence traced; it reads as a steady boundary he keeps within himself.

_Reading_

At Psa 26:5 the sense is 'hate the assembly of evildoers', typed as affect (101/102): a movement of feeling, here directed and chosen. The bearer is the psalmist (105). The operation is a self-distancing: he will not sit with the false or with hypocrites, he hates the assembly of evildoers and refuses the company of the wicked — aversion to their fellowship (106, 114). Target is 'separation' (107); the coupling binds the hatred to their company ('hate-their-company', 112), so the affect is not free-floating malice but a refusal of association. No source is recorded (103, present:false) — the ground of the aversion (his integrity, vv.1–3) stands in the passage but is not captured as this dimension's antecedent. Seat is 'none' (104): the hatred is not lodged in a named organ but is a stance of the whole self. Manner, intensity, specifier, effect absent or 'none' (108/109/110/111): no described heat or degree, no recorded consequence — the passage gives the aversion as a settled boundary, not an outburst. Prohibition absent (113). Role characteristic (115), locus internal:ib-state (116): this is a genuine inner movement of the righteous interior, a chosen distancing seated within, though it acts toward people outside.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the many 'hate' (sane) readings, this is one of the righteous, first-person cases: the bearer is the psalmist and the movement is a chosen self-separation from evil company (contrast the enemies' hostile hate of the psalmist or of God, e.g. #6, #8, #13).

Psa 36:11 Let not the foot of arrogance come upon me, nor the hand of the wicked drive me away. Psa 36:12 There the evildoers lie fallen ; they are thrust down , unable to rise .

**plots** — Psa 36:4  ·  `H2803:plot#1`

_Story_

Even lying on his bed, where most people rest, the wicked man's mind is busy devising trouble. His scheming does not switch off with the day; it works on in the dark, and it is fixed — he has set himself on a bad road and simply will not turn away from evil. The mischief he plans is tied to that unlikely place, the bed becoming a workshop for wrongdoing. The poem shows this as a working of his thinking rather than an act of his hands, and it does not fasten it to one part of him; it is the whole restless mind at work. Nothing more is described — no manner, no measure, no outcome — only that his refusal of good runs on even in stillness.

_Reading_

At Psa 36:4 the sense is 'he plots trouble on his bed', typed as cognition (101/102): the mind devising, not the hand acting. The bearer is the wicked (105). The operation shows the interior at work even in the place of rest — he plots trouble while on his bed, sets himself in a way that is not good, and does not reject evil (106, 114): scheming that fills even the hours of stillness, with a settled refusal to turn from wrong. Target is 'nocturnal-scheming' (107); the coupling anchors the plotting to the bed, its unlikely workshop ('plot-on-bed', 112). No source is recorded (103, present:false) — the passage's own preface (transgression speaking deep in the heart, no fear of God, v1) supplies a ground but is not tagged here. Seat is 'none' (104): the plotting is not assigned to a named organ, though the surrounding verses speak of the heart; as this movement it is a whole-mind activity. Manner, intensity, specifier, effect absent or 'none' (108/109/110/111): no described style, degree, or traced consequence — the scheming is given plainly, its refusal-of-evil its own weight. Prohibition absent (113). Role characteristic (115), locus internal:ib-state (116): the plotting is a real inner-being movement, running on within the wicked even when the body lies down.

*Why this reading differs:* Both H2803:plot readings show the mind devising harm, but here (Psa 36:4) the plotting is nocturnal and self-directed inertia ('he plots on his bed', refusing to reject evil); at Psa 52:2 the same scheming is weaponised through the tongue toward another's destruction.

## Psa 44:1-10

> To the choirmaster . A Maskil of the Sons of Korah . Psa 44:4 You are my King , O God ; ordain salvation for Jacob ! Psa 44:5 Through you we push down our foes ; through your name we tread down those who rise up against us . Psa 44:6 For not in my bow do I trust , nor can my sword save me . Psa 44:7 But you have saved us from our foes and have put to shame those who hate us. Psa 44:8 In God we have boasted continually , and we will give thanks to your name forever . Selah Psa 44:9 But you have rejected us and disgraced us and have not gone out with our armies . Psa 44:10 You have made us turn back from the foe , and those who hate us have gotten spoil .

**hate** — Psa 44:10  ·  `H8130:hate#2`

_Story_

The nation's enemies, driven by hatred, have now come away with plunder. Their hatred is not just an inward feeling but something acted out, and at this point it has the upper hand — the same foes God had once put to shame are now the winners. The poem leaves the hatred standing on its own here, not linked to any further inner motive; its whole weight is in the spoil it has seized. Scripture does not say what feeds this hostility, and it does not probe the enemies' inner organs; it simply records that their enmity is directed at God's people and, for the moment, prevailing.

_Reading_

At Psa 44:10 the sense is 'those who hate us have gotten spoil', typed as action (101/102): hatred here is not merely felt but enacted and, in this moment, rewarded. The bearer is the foes (105); the target is the nation (107). The operation is simply 'hate' (106), but the discovery sets it in the reversal of v7: the very haters God once shamed now plunder — the enmity triumphant after God made the nation turn back (114). Notably the coupling is 'none' (112): the passage does not bind this hatred to any accompanying inner movement of the enemy — it stands bare, its whole force in the plundering it has won. No source is recorded (103, present:false): the text does not name what breeds the foes' hatred; it registers its ascendancy. Seat is 'none' (104): no organ is named. Manner, intensity, specifier, effect absent or 'none' (108/109/110/111): no described heat or measure, no consequence traced onto the enemy's interior — the reading is of hatred external in its arena and locus. Prohibition absent (113). Role characteristic (115), locus external:person (116): the hatred is treated as a real characteristic, but seated in and directed among persons — the enemies over against the nation.

*Why this reading differs:* Within Psa 44 this pairs with reading #3 (v7): #3 recalls the foes God once 'put to shame', while #2 (v10) is the bitter reversal — those same haters now plundering. The difference marks the lament's turn from remembered victory to present defeat.

**hated** — Psa 45:7  ·  `H8130:hat#1`

_Story_

The king is praised for hating wickedness — and this hatred is a firm act of the will, not mere dislike. What it aims at is evil itself, not any person. Its real meaning lies in its twin: he loves what is right and hates what is wrong, and the two belong together as one ordered inner posture, the very thing for which God honours him. The poem gives no backstory and does not lodge this in any single organ; it simply presents a settled moral bearing. There is no heat or measure described, only the balanced love-and-hatred that marks a rightly-tuned heart.

_Reading_

At Psa 45:7 the sense is 'hated wickedness', typed as action (101/102): a decisive movement of the will, not a passing distaste. The bearer is the king (105). The operation is 'hate' (106), and the target is wickedness itself, given as a span-linked pair (107, from/to) — the object is not a person but evil as such. The coupling is the load-bearing dimension: this hatred is paired with loving righteousness ('paired with loving righteousness', 112, span-resolved) — the two-sided moral affection the discovery names as the necessary counterpart of love, the ordered interior God rewards with anointing (114). No source is recorded (103, present:false): the passage does not trace an antecedent; the king's aligned loves and hatreds simply stand. Seat is 'none' (104): not organ-located. Manner, intensity, specifier, effect absent or 'none' (108/109/110/111): no described heat or degree, no consequence beyond the anointing named in the coupling's own verse — the affect is given as settled moral orientation. Prohibition absent (113). Role characteristic (115), locus internal:ib-state (116): a genuine inner-being characteristic, seated within, its whole meaning in the pairing of loving good with hating evil.

*Why this reading differs:* This righteous 'hate' (the king hating wickedness, paired with loving righteousness) stands opposite the wicked's hatred of discipline in the very next narrated reading (#4, Psa 50:17). The same verb, opposite object: aversion to evil vs aversion to correction.

To loathe correction is, at the same moment, to discard the instruction that could save him. The poem draws the sharp contrast with a good hatred of evil — this is its dark twin, aimed not at wrongdoing but at the very thing that would put him right. No cause is named and no single part of him singled out; it is simply a settled inner bent, hating what would help.

*Why this reading differs:* The mirror image of the king's righteous hatred (#1/#7 in this family, Psa 45:7 hating wickedness): same verb sane, but the wicked's object is God's discipline and word, so the aversion is self-destructive rather than ordered.

**plots** — Psa 52:2  ·  `H2803:plot#2`

_Story_

The tyrant's tongue devises destruction, and behind that tongue is a scheming mind: this is malice worked out in thought, not a careless outburst. Unusually, the poem does fasten the scheming to an organ — the tongue that speaks it — while the man himself is the boaster of verse 1. The plotting is sharp, likened to a razor's edge, and it is bound to the ruin it intends. Scripture does not say what gave him such a taste for harm, though his love of evil and lying is plain nearby. What stands out is the cold, deliberate calculation of the design.

_Reading_

At Psa 52:2 the sense is 'your tongue plots destruction' (chashab), typed as action (101/102): calculated devising, malice thought out rather than blurted. Uniquely in this family, a seat IS recorded — 'the tongue' (104, inferred) — so the scheming is here lodged in the instrument of speech; the bearer is the tyrant (105). The operation is 'plot / devise' (106); the target is destruction, a span-linked pair (107). The manner is vivid — 'like a sharpened razor' (108, inferred): the plotting has an edge, a honed cruelty. The coupling binds the devising to the ruin it aims at (112, span-resolved), and the discovery names it as the scheming mind behind the tongue, 'malice thought out, not blurted' (114). No source is recorded (103, present:false): the passage does not name what breeds the tyrant's appetite for ruin, though vv.3–4 show his love of evil. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (109/110/111): no degree marker or traced consequence beyond the razor-image. Prohibition absent (113). Role characteristic (115), locus internal:ib-state (116): a real inner-being characteristic — the calculating mind — expressed through the tongue but seated within.

*Why this reading differs:* Distinct from H2803:plot#1 (Psa 36:4): there the scheming is inward and nocturnal, self-directed; here it is externalised and weaponised — a seat (the tongue) is recorded and a razor-manner is added, so the devising becomes an edged instrument aimed at another's destruction.
