## Psa 2:3

> Psa 2:3 “Let us burst their bonds apart and cast away their cords from us.”

**cast** — Psa 2:3  ·  `H7993:cast#1`

_Story_

This single line is the voice of rebellion caught in the act. The kings and nations of the psalm have set themselves against God and his anointed, and here we hear what is going on inside them: a determination to be free of all control. Let us burst their bonds, they say, and throw off their cords. It is an act of the will, not a mood that came over them - they are choosing to be unbound. The passage never says what set them off; the craving simply rises out of them, self-made, as if freedom from any authority were reason enough. What they are reaching for is not a thing or a person but a condition: no yoke, no rule, no claim on them at all. And their will is bound up entirely with the restraints they hate - the whole of it is defined by what it refuses. It is stated plainly, without heat or fear, and it lives deep inside them as a settled posture of the heart. This is the shadow side of resolve in these psalms: not a promise faithfully kept, but a will bent on shaking off everything that would hold it.

*Why this reading differs:* The verb shalak ('cast') carries two opposite readings in this family, and the difference is the finding. Here at Psa 2:3 (H7993:cast#1) it is the rebels casting AWAY the cords of God's rule - a self-sprung volition aimed at throwing off all restraint, located internally as a rebel ib-state, with no source and no divine companion booked. At Psa 55:22 (H7993:cast#2) the same verb is the believer casting his BURDEN onto the LORD - an act of trust whose source is God's sustaining and whose locus is external, resting on God. One motion throws off; the other hands over. The identical word thus marks the two poles of the will: rebellion that refuses to be held, and trust that lets itself be carried.

**cast** — Psa 55:22  ·  `H7993:cast#2`

_Story_

In the middle of a psalm about betrayal by a close friend, the tone shifts to a piece of hard-won counsel: cast your burden on the Lord, and he will sustain you; he will never permit the righteous to be moved. To cast is to throw - a deliberate act of taking the weight you cannot carry and handing it over to God. It is trust turned into a decision, the believer telling himself to let go. What stands out here is what makes the casting possible in the first place: not his own strength but God's promise to hold him up. The strength that answers the throw is God's, and the act is aimed straight at him - the burden thrown onto the Lord himself. And it comes bound to a promise of steadiness: the one who casts his care on God will not be shaken. The passage does not analyse the impulse beyond that sustaining; it simply shows a person, worn down by treachery, choosing to unload what he cannot bear onto the God who can. This is the bright opposite of rebellion - not throwing off God's hold, but throwing yourself into it.
