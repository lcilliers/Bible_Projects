**languishing** — Psa 6:2  ·  `H0536:languish#1`

_Story_

The psalmist says he is wasting away, and the trouble has gone right down into his bones. It is not something he is doing but a state he has sunk into — a weakness that shakes his whole frame. We are not told what caused it, and it isn't pinned to any single part of him; it simply overtakes the whole person and is felt in the body. Nothing is said about how severe it grows or where it leads — the moment holds only the collapse itself, carried inward and laid open before God.

_Reading_

Sense (101): the psalmist is 'languishing', his bones troubled (Psa 6:2). Type (102) is a state, not an act the self performs but a condition it is in. Source (103) is absent — no cause is named at the word itself; the surrounding verses hint at God's rebuke, but the languishing is reported bare, as sheer weakness. Seat (104) is explicitly 'none': this collapse is not lodged in one organ but suffered by the whole person — though the discovery (114) notes it reaches the bones, so the interior distress registers as bodily give-way. Bearer (105) is the psalmist himself; operation (106) is that the self is languishing, its very frame shaken — weakness carried down into the skeleton. Target (107) is his own frailty: the process turns inward, the self undone by its own giving-out. Manner (108) is 'none' — no how is specified; intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are all absent, so the reading stops at the state and does not track a degree or an outcome. Coupling (112) binds the languishing to itself as the marking condition. Prohibition (113) is absent — nothing is forbidden or commanded. Role (115) is a characteristic; locus (116) is internal, an inner-being state. The whole picture is of an inner-being process of depletion so deep it is felt in the bones, laid before God without any named cause.

**weary** — Psa 6:6  ·  `H3021:weary#1`

_Story_

He is worn out by his own groaning. Night after night his weeping has drained him until there is no strength left — the very act of grieving has used him up. It is not fixed in any one part of him; the tiredness soaks through the whole person. We aren't told how far it goes or what comes of it; the point is simply that sorrow, poured out without end, has emptied him.

_Reading_

Sense (101): 'weary with my moaning' (Psa 6:6). Type (102) a state. Source (103) is absent as a formal slot, yet the discovery (114) is precise: the weariness is depletion — the sustained groaning has drained the interior of strength; the moaning is the wearing agent named within the operation, not as an outside cause. Seat (104) is 'none': the exhaustion is not organ-located but wears down the whole self. Bearer (105) is the psalmist; operation (106) is that the self is worn out by its own groaning — grief exhausting the whole person, a process where the very expression of sorrow consumes the one expressing it. Target (107) is exhaustion itself. Manner (108) is 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) all absent — no degree, no qualifier, no tracked outcome; the reading rests on the depletion. Coupling (112) is weary-to-moaning: the fatigue is welded to the night-long weeping the passage describes. Prohibition (113) absent. Role (115) characteristic; locus (116) internal ib-state. An inner-being process in which sorrow, endlessly voiced, empties the self of strength.

*Why this reading differs:* This weariness differs from the same word at Psa 69:3. Here at 6:6 the wearing agent is the psalmist's own inward moaning — grief that exhausts from within — whereas at 69:3 the same weariness comes specifically from crying out to God and waiting unanswered. Both lack a bodily seat, but the source differs: self-consuming sorrow here, unrewarded petition there.

**affliction** — Psa 9:13  ·  `H6040:affliction#1`

_Story_

The psalmist asks God to look at his own suffering — the pain that comes from people who hate him. He is not speaking of others' troubles here but bringing his own case, caught between death's door and rescue. We aren't told exactly who or what is behind it, and it isn't tied to any part of his body; it is simply his whole self in distress. He holds this misery up to God and makes it the reason God should be gracious to him.

_Reading_

Sense (101): 'see my affliction' (Psa 9:13). Type (102) a state. Source (103) is absent — no external agent of the affliction is fixed at the word, though the verse frames it as coming 'from those who hate me'. Seat (104) 'none': not organ-located, borne by the whole self. Bearer (105) is the psalmist; operation (106) is that the self pleads its own affliction from those who hate it, asking to be lifted from the gates of death. Target (107) is personal-suffering — the particular, individual misery, distinguished (discovery 114) from the general 'afflicted' class the psalm elsewhere names: here the self brings its own case, poised between death's gates and rescue. Manner (108) 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) absent — no degree or outcome tracked; the reading holds the plea itself. Coupling (112) is 'my-affliction' — the suffering owned as the speaker's own. Prohibition (113) absent. Role (115) characteristic; locus (116) internal ib-state. The inner-being movement is a suffering self turning its particular anguish Godward as the ground of its appeal for grace.

*Why this reading differs:* This affliction differs from the word's other readings. At 9:13 it is an individual's own suffering, personally owned and pleaded to God (bearer = the psalmist), unlike 44:24 where a whole nation's affliction lies unrelieved while God hides his face, 107:10 where prisoners sit in it as a plight, and 119:50 where it becomes the arena in which God's promise sustains. Here the distinctive is the single self, poised between death and deliverance.

**dismayed** — Psa 30:7  ·  `H0926:dismay#1`

_Story_

The psalmist had felt secure — sure he would never be shaken. Then God hid his face, and in an instant that confidence collapsed into terror. The dread isn't lodged in one part of him; it grips his whole self. What the moment reveals is how thin his security had been: the very second the felt ground of God's favour was taken away, everything inside him fell apart.

_Reading_

Sense (101): 'you hid your face; I was dismayed' (Psa 30:7). Type (102) a state. Source (103) absent as a slot, but the operation (106) makes the trigger plain: when God hid his face, the self was dismayed — the collapse of a false security once favour was withdrawn. Seat (104) 'none': not organ-located; the terror seizes the whole self. Bearer (105) the psalmist; target (107) is dismay itself — the reading turns on the interior falling apart. The discovery (114) sharpens it: this is the terror that exposed his complacency ('I shall never be moved') — the moment God withdrew, the interior fell apart. Manner (108) 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) absent — no degree or outcome tracked. Coupling (112) 'hidden-face-dismay' binds the dismay to God's hidden face as its hinge. Prohibition (113) absent. Role (115) characteristic; locus (116) internal ib-state. The inner-being movement: presumed stability shatters into terror the instant its felt ground (God's favour) is removed.

*Why this reading differs:* This dismay differs from the item's others by its bearer and trigger. At 30:7 it strikes the psalmist himself — his complacency shattering when God hides his face — whereas at 83:17 the same dread is invoked on the enemies under judgment, and at 90:7 it falls on all mortals before God's wrath. The bearer shifts from the believing self, to foes, to humankind at large — the terror is the same, its subject is not.

_Story_

_Reading_

*Why this reading differs:* This reading is the item's outlier by agent and type: here affliction is self-imposed (type 'affect') — the psalmist afflicting himself in compassion for others — whereas at 94:5 the wicked inflict affliction on God's people, and at 116:10, 119:67 and 119:107 the psalmist receives/bears affliction. Same root, but the doer shifts from the self-in-love, to the wicked-as-aggressor, to the self-as-sufferer — the difference is the finding.

**affliction** — Psa 44:24  ·  `H6040:affliction#2`

_Story_

A whole people cries out over their affliction and asks why God forgets it and hides his face. Here the suffering is not described as moving anywhere or doing anything — it simply sits, unrelieved. What keeps it going, the psalm says, is that God seems to look away. It isn't the pain of one person but of a nation, and it isn't fixed in any part of the body; it is a standing misery held up in the anguished question, 'Why do you forget us?'

_Reading_

Sense (101): affliction (oni, 'our affliction', Psa 44:24). Type (102) is 'status' — a standing condition. This reading is distinctive because its process-slots are deliberately empty: operation (106) is 'none', target (107) is 'none', coupling (112) is 'none' — the affliction is presented not as a movement with a direction but as a bare, unrelieved status. Crucially, source (103) IS present here (unlike most in this family): the affliction is 'unrelieved while God hides his face and forgets' (v24) — the cause of its persistence is God's seeming inattention. Seat (104) 'none': not organ-located. Bearer (105) is the nation, not an individual — a corporate sufferer. Manner (108) 'none'; intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) absent. Prohibition (113) absent. Role (115) characteristic; locus (116) internal ib-state. Discovery (114): the anguished 'why?' of the faithful sufferer — misery God seems to ignore. The inner-being reading is a whole people's affliction held as an open, unanswered condition, its persistence pinned on God's hidden face.

*Why this reading differs:* This affliction stands apart from the item's others in two ways. Its bearer is the nation (corporate), not an individual as at 9:13, 107:10 and 119:50; and its process-slots are empty — no operation, target or coupling — so it reads as a bare, static status rather than a movement. It is also the one reading where a source is named: the affliction stays unrelieved because God hides his face and forgets.
