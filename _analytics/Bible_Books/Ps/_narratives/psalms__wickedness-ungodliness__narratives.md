**conceives** — Psa 7:14  ·  `H2254:conceiv#1`

_Story_

The psalm pictures the inner life of a wicked man as a kind of pregnancy. He conceives evil the way a mother conceives a child - it starts as something carried inside, in the mind, long before it is seen. Then he is pregnant with mischief, and at last he gives birth to lies. The harm is not something done to him or planted in him by anyone else; the passage never says where it comes from - it simply grows out of the man himself. It is his whole self that does this, not just one part of him. And once evil is conceived, it will be born: carrying it and delivering it are one unbroken thing. There is no measuring of how much or how fierce, and no note here of where it ends up, though the verses that follow show his mischief falling back on his own head. What the passage fixes on is the generation itself - a settled bent of the inner life that keeps producing evil from within, as naturally and inevitably as a body brings a child to term.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'the wicked man conceives evil... and gives birth to lies' - conceiving read not as a bare thought but as a gestation, harm carried and delivered. Type (102) is cognition: this is an interior mental act, evil formed in the mind before it ever shows in deed. Source (103) is absent: the reading does not trace where the conceiving springs from - no prior wound, no tempter, no drive is booked; the wicked interior is simply shown generating evil out of itself, its origin left unaccounted and the whole weight resting on the man's own generative interior. Seat (104) resolves to none: for all the womb-imagery, the operation is not lodged in a named organ - it is the whole wicked person that conceives, not a faculty switched on. Bearer (105) is 'the wicked' (inferred, to_span 283602): the interior read is the wicked man's, the one the psalm has just set opposite the righteous God who tests minds and hearts. Operation (106), the core event, is 'the wicked conceives evil, is pregnant with mischief, and gives birth to lies - harm gestated from within': a grim pregnancy, evil conceived, carried, and delivered. Target (107) is gestation-of-evil (inferred, to_span 283602): what the operation aims at is the bringing-forth itself - the object is the production of harm, evil made to term. Manner (108) is none: no mode is marked - not sudden, not laboured; the flat report presents the gestation as bare fact. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are all absent: no degree is scored, nothing narrows the evil beyond 'mischief' and 'lies', and though the next verses show the mischief returning on his own head, the reading does not book that as this operation's effect - it holds the conceiving as generation, not yet consequence. Coupling (112) is 'conceive-and-birth' (inferred, to_span 283602): the one binding the reading registers is the weld of conception to delivery - the two are inseparable, evil conceived is evil born; there is no interior that carries evil without in the end producing it. Prohibition (113) is absent: no forbidding is attached; the verse indicts by picture, not by command. Discovery (114) names the placement: 'he conceives evil and is pregnant with mischief and gives birth to lies' - the operation a grim generation, the wicked interior gestating and delivering its harm like a pregnancy. Role (115) is characteristic: a standing trait of the wicked's inner being, not a passing impulse. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - the whole movement sits inside, a condition of the inner life. The full process: from no named source and no organ-seat, the wicked man's interior conceives evil, carries it as a pregnancy, and delivers it as lies - a cognitive generation welded from conception to birth, held wholly within as a settled trait of the wicked self, evil produced out of the man's own inner ground.

**evil** — Psa 37:27  ·  `H7451:evil#1`

_Story_

Here evil appears from a different angle than usual - not as something a wicked person is busy doing, but as the very thing a person is told to walk away from. The command is simple and double: turn away from evil, and do good. It is an act of the will, a decision to change direction. And the two halves belong together - you cannot really leave evil behind without at the same time moving toward good; they are one turn, not two. The passage does not say where the evil came from or how hard the turning is; it just issues the charge and holds out its promise, that the one who turns will dwell secure for good. What it fixes on is the reorientation itself, a settled inward direction the person is called to take and keep.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'Turn away from evil and do good' (ra) - evil named here not as a deed being done but as the thing the will is told to turn FROM. Type (102) is volition: unusually for this family, this is an act of the will, evil as the object of a commanded reorientation, not a settled wicked bent. Source (103) is absent: the reading books no origin for the evil - it is not traced to a drive or a tempter; it stands simply as the direction the interior is charged to abandon. Seat (104) is none: not organ-located - it is the whole self told to turn, not a faculty. Bearer (105) is 'the hearer' (inferred, to_span 277716): the interior addressed is the one who receives the charge, the reader summoned to reorient. Operation (106), the event, is 'the self is told to turn away from evil and do good, so as to dwell forever - the twofold moral turn': a double movement, away from evil and toward good. Target (107) is moral-turn (inferred, to_span 277716): what the operation aims at is the reorientation itself. Manner (108) is none: no mode is marked - the command stands flatly. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: no degree scored, nothing narrows it, and 'so shall you dwell forever' is read as the charge's horizon rather than booked as this operation's effect. Coupling (112) is 'turn-and-do-good' (inferred, to_span 277716): the binding is the weld of the turn-from-evil to the doing-of-good - the two are one act, the negative and positive halves of a single reorientation; one cannot leave evil without moving toward good. Prohibition (113) is absent as a marked flag, yet the sense itself is quasi-prohibitive - 'turn away from evil.' Discovery (114): 'Turn away from evil and do good, so shall you dwell forever' - the double movement of the will, away from evil and toward good, the interior reorienting its whole direction. Role (115) is characteristic: a standing trait the inner being is charged to hold. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - the turn is a condition of the inner life. The full process: from no named source and no organ-seat, the hearer's will is commanded to turn away from evil and toward good in one welded movement - evil here read from the outside, as the thing the interior is to leave, a volitional reorientation held within as a standing posture.

*Why this reading differs:* This is one of thirteen distinct readings of evil (ra) in the family, and it is the outlier. Where the other twelve read evil as a settled wickedness borne by the wicked or the enemies (a status or disposition of theirs), this reading alone reads evil as VOLITION - not evil possessed but evil renounced, the object of a commanded turn. The bearer is not a wicked man but 'the hearer' summoned to reorient; the direction is away from evil, not toward it. The difference is the finding: the same word names both the thing the wicked cling to and the thing the righteous will is charged to abandon.

**evil** — Psa 40:12  ·  `H7451:evil#2`

_Story_

This is evil felt from the inside as being drowned. The man is not doing wrong here - he is buried under it. Troubles have surrounded him past counting, and his own sins have caught up with him until he cannot even see; there are more of them, he says, than the hairs on his head, and his heart gives out. The passage does not sort out where it all comes from or separate his own guilt from the troubles pressing in; it simply reports the flood. The sheer, unmeasured weight of it is the point - too many to number, enough to make his courage fail. And the guilt and the drowning are one thing: it is exactly the mass of his iniquity that swamps him. This is evil as an overwhelming condition of the inner life, a man going under.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'evils have encompassed me... my iniquities have overtaken me... my heart fails' (ra) - evil here read as an engulfing mass that overwhelms the self. Type (102) is state: not a deed and not a settled wickedness, but a condition suffered, the interior swamped. Source (103) is absent: the reading does not trace where the flood of evils originates - notably it does not separate the psalmist's own iniquities from outward troubles as their spring; both are named but their origin is left unbooked, the report holding the inundation itself rather than its cause. Seat (104) is none: not organ-located, though 'heart' is named - the failing heart reads as the whole inner person giving out, not a faculty malfunctioning. Bearer (105) is 'the psalmist' (inferred, to_span 278245): the interior read is the sufferer's own, buried under his guilt. Operation (106), the event, is 'evils have encompassed the self beyond number; its iniquities have overtaken it till it cannot see, more than the hairs of its head, so its heart fails - being swamped by guilt': inundation, the self buried. Target (107) is swamped-by-guilt (inferred, to_span 278245): what the operation lands on is the self overwhelmed. Manner (108) is none: no mode is marked. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent as marked flags - yet the sense itself carries an unmeasured extremity ('beyond number,' 'more than the hairs of my head,' 'my heart fails'); the reading reports that extremity in the sense-line without booking a separate degree or downstream outcome. Coupling (112) is 'iniquities-overwhelm' (inferred, to_span 278245): the binding welds the evils to the overwhelming - the guilt and the drowning are inseparable, the mass of iniquity being precisely what buries the courage. Prohibition (113) is absent. The full process: from no booked source and no organ-seat, the psalmist's own iniquities and encompassing evils overtake him beyond counting until he cannot see and his heart fails - evil read here not as something done but as an engulfing state, guilt welded to the drowning of the self, held wholly within as the collapse of inner courage.

*Why this reading differs:* Against the family's twelve other readings of evil (ra), this one is distinctive in type and direction. It is not the wicked's settled bent nor the enemies' malice aimed outward, but a STATE the psalmist suffers - his own iniquities turned back on him as an engulfing flood. Where most readings locate evil in a hostile 'them,' here it overtakes the 'I,' and where most read it as status or disposition, here it is a condition of collapse. The difference marks how the same word can name both the evil the wicked wield and the evil that buries the penitent under his own guilt.

**wickedness** — Psa 45:7  ·  `H7562:wickednes#1`

_Story_

This is wickedness seen from the opposite side - not as something a bad man is doing, but as the thing a good king cannot stand. The line says he has loved righteousness and hated wickedness, and that hatred is the real subject here. Wickedness itself does nothing in this scene: it has no plan, no victim, no force of its own. It shows up only as the object the king's heart rejects, the exact opposite of the goodness he loves. The two are set against each other like poles, and what the passage actually shows us is the inner recoil of a righteous person - the settled turning-away that is the flip side of loving what is right. Wickedness is present only as the thing being refused.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'you have loved righteousness and hated wickedness' (resha) - wickedness named not as something the bearer does but as the thing the righteous king abhors. Type (102) is status: wickedness as a moral condition, an abstract evil, here appearing as an object rather than an act. Source (103) is absent: no origin for the wickedness is booked; it stands simply as the moral opposite the king rejects. Seat (104) is none: not organ-located. Bearer (105) is booked oddly as 'wickedness (the king's hatred)' (inferred, to_span 278988): the reading reads the interior through the king's HATRED of wickedness - the operation of the inner being caught here is the righteous recoil, wickedness present only as its object. Operation (106) is none (resolution none): no wicked act is performed - fittingly, for in this passage wickedness is not being done but being hated; the reading marks the absence of any operation by wickedness itself. Target (107) is none: no target, again fitting - the wickedness here acts on nothing; it is acted upon, the object of hatred. Manner (108) is none. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: no degree, no narrowing, no outcome - the reading holds wickedness as a static moral object, not a moving force. Coupling (112) carries a value and is a span-resolved pair (from_span 278988 to_span 278987): it is 'the object of the king's hatred' - wickedness welded to the king's hating of it, paired with the righteousness he loves; the two are set as poles, and wickedness registers only in that opposition. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state. The full process: with no source, no operation, and no target of its own, wickedness appears in this reading not as a force but as an object - the thing a righteous heart hates, welded to the righteousness it loves, registered wholly through the king's inward recoil from it. *Why this reading differs:* This item has two distinct readings. The difference is stark: in one the interior caught is a righteous heart recoiling from wickedness; in the other it is wickedness itself enthroned and pressing down. One word, seen once as hated object and once as ruling force.

_Story_

God turns and speaks directly to a certain kind of person: the wicked one who keeps reciting God's laws and taking his covenant on his lips - and God asks what right he has to do it. The passage does not point to one particular crime here. He is not caught doing a single deed; he is exposed as a whole - a hypocrite, religion without obedience. And he stands here as an outsider, another person God addresses across the room, not an inner life described from within. The silence about any specific act is the point: it is his condition, not his conduct in this moment, that is on trial.

_Story_

This is evil that comes out of the mouth. God charges the wicked man with giving his mouth free rein - letting his speech run loose with no brake on it - and with a tongue that frames deceit. The evil is not traced to any cause here; it is simply a settled condition of the man that spills out in words. What the passage catches is the loosing itself - a mouth let off the leash for harm, an inner corruption that shows itself in what he says.
