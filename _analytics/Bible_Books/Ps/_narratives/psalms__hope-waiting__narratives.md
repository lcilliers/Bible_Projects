**prepare** — Psa 5:3  ·  `H6186:prepar#1`

_Story_

At first light the psalmist deliberately sets his prayer before God, laying it out as carefully as an offering, and then stays awake and alert for a reply. This is a chosen discipline, not a mood that washes over him: he readies himself and then watches, and the two acts are really one steady posture of expectation. The psalm does not say what first moved him to it, nor does it locate the waiting in any one part of him - it is his whole self leaning forward. It also does not tell us how intensely he waits or what finally comes of it; the watching is simply left open, its answer still ahead. What we see is a person schooling himself each morning to wait on God.

_Reading_

The verse reads 'prepare' (sense 101) as a volition (type 102): an act the will orders, not a feeling that simply arrives. No prior source is recorded (103 absent) - the passage does not say what stirs the impulse; it shows the posture already resolved. The operation (106) is that at dawn the self lays out its prayer like a sacrifice and then keeps watch - disciplined expectancy - and its target (107) is expectant-waiting: the whole movement bends toward an answer not yet given. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the two beats are welded by a coupling (112) of prepare-and-watch, so the ordering and the watching are one motion, not two. No organ seats this (104 none): waiting here is a whole-self stance, not lodged in heart or eyes. Manner is unmarked (108 none), and intensity, specifier, and effect are all unrecorded (109/110/111 absent) - the verse grades no degree, adds no qualifier, and names no result inside itself; the watching is left open-ended, its outcome deferred. Nothing is forbidden (113 absent). The discovery note (114) confirms the reading: prayer set out, then alert waiting. It stands as a characteristic of the inner being (role 115) whose locus is the interior's own state (116) - the self disciplining itself toward God, who is the one watched for, not the actor here.

**hope** — Psa 9:18  ·  `H8615:hope#1`

_Story_

The psalm makes a quiet but stubborn claim: the hope carried by the poor and forgotten will not be crushed out of them. However long they are overlooked, that inner expectation survives. The psalm does not explain where such hope comes from or pin it to any single feeling; it simply insists the hope lasts. It does not measure how strong it is or spell out how it is finally rewarded within the verse - only that it will not perish. The focus stays on the durable inner life of people the world ignores.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is that the poor's hope shall not perish, read as affect (type 102) - a felt orientation, not a deliberate act. Its bearer (105) is the poor and needy, and the operation (106) is that the needy are not forever forgotten nor their hope finally destroyed: hope that outlasts neglect, targeted (107) at enduring-hope. The coupling (112) hope-not-perish binds the interior movement to its own durability - the point of the reading is that this hope survives. No source is recorded (103 absent): the psalm does not trace where the poor's hope comes from, only that it holds. No seat locates it (104 none) - it is not an organ's act but the whole condition of the lowly. Manner is unmarked (108 none); intensity, specifier, and effect are unrecorded (109/110/111 absent) - no degree, no qualifier, and, tellingly, no in-verse outcome beyond the promise that it will not perish. Nothing is prohibited (113 absent). The discovery (114) underlines the finding: the interior of the poor holds a hope the psalm insists is durable, not finally disappointed. Role is characteristic (115); locus is the interior's own state (116). The lens stays on the inner life of the powerless - God is the arena their hope reaches toward, but the claim is about what endures inside them.

*Why this reading differs:* This reading of tiqvah is the enduring hope of the poor as a durable communal condition, whereas the same word at Psa 62:5 is the psalmist commanding his own soul and at Psa 71:5 is God himself named as a lifelong personal hope; here the bearer is a class (the needy) and the accent falls on hope surviving neglect rather than on its object.

**wait** — Psa 25:3  ·  `H6960:wait#1`

_Story_

The psalm draws a line between two kinds of people: those who wait on God, and those who betray without cause. The ones who wait, it promises, will never be humiliated for it. This is trust stretched out into patience - a settled inner leaning on God that is bound to be honoured rather than let down. The psalm does not say what first produces such waiting or how strong it must be; it simply guarantees the waiter will not be put to shame. The whole weight rests on the quiet confidence of the one who waits.

_Reading_

Here 'wait' (qavah, sense 101) is affect (type 102) - a felt trust generalised into patience. Its bearer (105) is those who wait, and the operation (106) is that none who wait for God are put to shame while the wantonly treacherous are: waiting as the vindicated posture, targeted (107) simply at waiting itself. The coupling (112) wait-not-shamed ties the inner posture to its promised outcome of non-disappointment. No source is recorded (103 absent) - the psalm does not say what generates the waiting, only that it will be honoured. No seat locates it (104 none): it is a whole-self stance, not an organ's act. Manner is unmarked (108 none); intensity, specifier, and effect are unrecorded (109/110/111 absent) - no degree, no qualifier, and the outcome, though promised (not shamed), is not spelled out as a separate in-verse effect. Nothing is prohibited (113 absent). The discovery (114) reads it as trust widened into patient waiting, promised it will not be disappointed. Role is characteristic (115); locus is the interior's own state (116) - the waiting self, with God as the one waited on.

*Why this reading differs:* This first qavah reading is general - a class of 'those who wait' promised vindication - and impersonal, distinct from Psa 25:5 immediately after, where the same verb becomes the psalmist's own unremitting, all-day waiting. Here waiting is a category promised safety; there it is a personal, sustained act.

**wait** — Psa 27:14  ·  `H6960:wait#3`

_Story_

This time waiting takes the form of an order the psalmist gives himself: wait for the LORD, be strong, let your heart be brave, and wait again. Waiting here is anything but limp - it takes courage, and he has to rally himself to it. The psalm does not say what prompted the self-talk or how hard the struggle is; it simply shows a person bracing his own inner life to keep waiting. It is the sound of someone stiffening his resolve to stay hopeful when it would be easier to give up.

_Reading_

Here 'wait' (qavah, sense 101) shifts to volition (type 102): the self commands itself to wait. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, the operation (106) is the interior exhorting itself (and others) to wait for the LORD, be strong, and let the heart take courage - self-command to patient courage - with the target (107) being self-exhortation. The coupling (112) wait-and-courage welds waiting to the summoning of strength: waiting is not passive here but demands mustered courage. No source is recorded (103 absent); no seat locates the act (104 none), even though the verse names the heart as what must take courage - the waiting itself is not organ-seated. Manner is unmarked (108 none); intensity, specifier, and effect are unrecorded (109/110/111 absent) - no degree graded, no result named. Nothing is prohibited (113 absent). The discovery (114) reads it as the interior addressing itself, commanding the patience and courage it needs to keep waiting. Role is characteristic (115); locus is the interior's own state (116) - the self turning on itself to hold its ground toward God.

*Why this reading differs:* Unlike the affect-readings at Psa 25, this qavah is volitional - the self commanding itself. Its distinctive is that waiting is coupled to courage and self-exhortation: not a promised outcome or a sustained mood but an act of will the interior musters against its own faltering.

**hope** — Psa 147:11  ·  `H3176:hope#10`

_Story_

The psalm says something surprising about what pleases God: not the strength of a warhorse or the speed of a runner, but people who fear him and put their hope in his faithful love. Hope here is a leaning-forward of the heart onto God's steady, covenant love, and it stands side by side with reverent awe. Where awe looks up, hope leans in - and God takes delight in exactly this. It is the picture of an inner life that trusts God's love, and finds that this very trust is what gladdens him.
