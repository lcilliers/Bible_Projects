**thank** — Psa 7:17  ·  `H3034:thank#1`

_Story_

This psalm is the cry of a man being hunted, protesting his innocence and begging a righteous God to judge his case. It ends not in relief but in gratitude and song - he thanks the LORD for his righteousness and sings to his name. The thankfulness is a felt thing, welling up from the whole person rather than from any one part of him, and it needs no separate cause: trusting that his judge is just, the man is already grateful before the rescue is even complete. The gratitude does not lead anywhere further; it is where his inner life comes to rest, blending seamlessly into praise.

_Reading_

Psa 7:17 closes a psalm of pursued innocence with thanks. The sense is 'give thanks and sing' (101), typed as affect (102): felt gratitude, not merely a duty performed. No interior source is traced (103 absent) and no bodily organ is named as its seat (104=none) - thanksgiving here is not lodged in an organ but is a posture of the whole inner self. The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is the whole movement of the psalm landing: vindication sought throughout is answered with praise, thanks to the LORD 'due to his righteousness' and song to his name. Its target is thanksgiving itself (107) - the interior's own grateful discharge - and it is coupled to song, 'thanks-and-sing' (112). Manner is none (108), and intensity, specifier, and effect are all unrecorded (109-111): the thanks is logged as an endpoint, ungraded and without a further consequence - it terminates in itself. Nothing is prohibited (113). The discovery note (114) shows the interior already praising before rescue is visibly complete: the righteous judge is trusted, so the self rests in gratitude. Typed as a characteristic (115) and located internally as an inner-being state (116), this is thanksgiving as the resting-place the inner life reaches when it entrusts its cause to a just God.

*Why this reading differs:* Distinct among the 'thank' readings as an inner landing-point: typed as affect with an internal locus (an ib-state), where the whole psalm's movement resolves into felt gratitude twinned with song - unlike the public-vow (35:18) or the summons-refrains of Ps 107.

**thank** — Psa 35:18  ·  `H3034:thank#2`

_Story_

The thanks here is a decision of the will more than a feeling yet realised - a vow made in advance. Even before the rescue arrives, his inner life has committed itself to grateful praise in front of everyone.

_Reading_

Psa 35:18 turns an appeal for rescue into a pledge of public praise: 'I will thank you in the great congregation.' Its sense carries the whole clause (101), and it is typed as volition (102) - a vow of the will, not yet a discharged act. No interior source is traced (103 absent) and no organ seats it (104=none): the thanks is a resolve of the whole self, not organ-lodged. The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is the self vowing to thank God in the great congregation, to praise him among the mighty throng - gratitude aimed at public discharge. Its target is public-thanks (107), coupled as 'thank-in-congregation' (112). Manner is none (108); intensity, specifier, effect unrecorded (109-111) - the vow is registered without gradation or downstream result. Nothing is prohibited (113). The discovery (114) shows the operation converting an anticipated rescue into a pledge of public thanksgiving. Typed as a characteristic (115), located internally as an ib-state (116): the inner life, still awaiting rescue, already binds itself by a vow of open, communal gratitude.

*Why this reading differs:* Unusual among 'thank' readings as a willed vow (typed volition, not affect or action) with an internal locus: gratitude pledged toward a future public congregation while still awaiting rescue - distinct from the already-landed affect of 7:17 and the summons-refrains of Ps 107.

**give thanks** — Psa 44:8  ·  `H3034:givethank#2`

_Story_

Here a whole nation speaks, remembering how God won them the land and declaring that in him they have always boasted and will thank his name forever. The thanks is a corporate act, an enduring devotion with no end-date, tightly bound to their proud confidence in God. It is aimed squarely outward at God himself. What makes the moment sharp is its placement: this settled, forever gratitude stands right before the psalm turns to protest defeat and abandonment - the confident thanks is about to be tested against bitter experience.

_Reading_

Psa 44:8 sets a settled national gratitude before the psalm's bitter turn: 'in God we have boasted... and we will give thanks to your name forever.' The sense is yadah, give thanks (101), typed as action (102). No interior source is traced (103 absent) and no organ seats it (104=none). The bearer is not an individual but the nation (105) - corporate gratitude. The operation (106) is simply to give thanks; its target is 'your name' (107), and its manner is 'forever' (108), an unbounded duration. Intensity, specifier, effect are unrecorded (109-111): the thanks is an endpoint without logged gradation or consequence. The coupling (112) is a pair with boasting (278852->278850) - thanksgiving welded to the nation's glorying in God. Nothing is prohibited (113). The discovery (114) names it the vow of perpetual praise joined to boasting, the people's settled devotion standing just before the lament breaks. Typed as a characteristic (115), its locus is God-ward (116 external:god): the movement is oriented outside the self, toward God's name. Here the inner life of a whole community expresses gratitude as its lasting boast - and the psalm will let that confident thanks collide with unexplained defeat.

*Why this reading differs:* Distinct within 'give thanks' by its bearer - the nation, not the psalmist - and its coupling to boasting: corporate, God-directed, 'forever' gratitude set just before a lament's turn, unlike the individual wholehearted thanks of 9:1 or 111:1.

**thanksgiving** — Psa 50:14  ·  `H8426:thanksgiv#1`

_Story_

God speaks and overturns the assumption that he needs the animals brought to his altar - he owns every beast of the forest and would not tell us if he were hungry. What he asks instead is a sacrifice of thanksgiving. The point rests entirely on who God is: since he lacks nothing, the offering that counts is a grateful heart, not a killed bull. This thanksgiving is a standing posture of worship aimed at God, and here it stands on its own as the real thing God wants from a person's inner life.

_Reading_

Psa 50:14 reframes worship itself: 'Offer to God a sacrifice of thanksgiving.' The sense is todah, thanksgiving offered as sacrifice (101), typed as status (102) - a standing offering, a state of grateful worship rather than a momentary act. Uniquely here a source is recorded (103): the God who owns all and needs nothing (v10-12, pair 279514->279499) - the ground of the demand is that God is not hungry for bulls. No organ seats it (104=none); the bearer is the worshipper (105); the operation is to offer (106), targeted to God (107). Manner is none (108) and coupling is none (112) - this thanksgiving stands alone, not welded to another act; intensity, specifier, effect unrecorded (109-111). Nothing is prohibited (113), though the passage sets todah over against empty ritual. The discovery (114) makes the point: because God owns all and is not fed by sacrifice, it is the grateful heart's offering, not slaughtered animals, that honours him. Typed as a characteristic (115), locus God-ward (116 external:god): the inner life's gratitude is here elevated to the true sacrifice - what God actually desires from the human interior.

*Why this reading differs:* Distinct within 'thanksgiving' as the one reading with a stated source (God who owns all and needs nothing) and no coupling: todah defined against empty ritual as the sacrifice God actually desires - unlike the paired 'magnify him with thanksgiving' (69:30) or the entry-liturgy of 95:2 / 100:4.

Together, the inner life of God's people becomes grateful, taking up the praise that all God's works already offer.

_Reading_

Psa 145:10 turns thanks into the whole creation's and community's response: 'All your works shall give thanks to you, O LORD, and your saints shall bless you.' The sense is 'the saints give thanks' (101), typed as affect (102) - felt, willing gratitude. No interior source is traced (103 absent) and no organ seats it (104=none). The bearer is 'the saints' (105) - the godly community (alongside all God's works). The operation (106) is that all God's works give thanks and his saints bless him - the community's gratitude gathered into a chorus. Its target is 'corporate-thanks' (107) and it is coupled as 'saints-give-thanks' (112). Manner is none (108); intensity, specifier, effect unrecorded (109-111). Nothing is prohibited (113). The discovery (114) reads it as the interior of the whole godly community turning grateful. Typed as a characteristic (115), located internally as an ib-state (116): here the inner life is not one person's but a shared, corporate state - the collective interior of the faithful turned toward thanks, echoing the praise of all created things.

*Why this reading differs:* Within 'give thanks', distinctive for its corporate, internal locus (an ib-state, like 9:1, 140:13): the shared gratitude of the whole godly community and all creation - felt corporate thanks, not the individual act or the God-directed public summons that mark this item elsewhere.
