# The Psalter's inner being — a cross-chapter synthesis, per characteristic (v1)

> **Scope:** all 150 Psalms, read Phase-2 (verse-analysis chapter-readings, ids 549–579 for Ps 1–31 v2 + 402–548 for Ps 32–150). This is the **cross-chapter synthesis** the per-chapter run was built for — a **summary per inner-being characteristic/movement** (researcher's framing, 2026-07-02). Produced *after* the re-alignment sweep (aheb Phase-1 re-run + Ps 1–31 process-lens re-frame), so it draws on a fully-aligned corpus.
>
> **Method:** characteristics were let to **emerge** from the recurring findings (not sorted into a pre-set grid — [[project_RESET_characteristics_to_movements_changeover]]). A read-only **harvest** (`scripts/_harvest_characteristic_evidence_v1_20260703.py`) located candidate attesting psalms across the 150 readings; each summary below is then **grounded in the specific strong exemplars**, not in the raw counts. The harvest grid is filed at [`wa-characteristic-harvest-20260703.md`](wa-characteristic-harvest-20260703.md).
>
> **Honesty on the numbers:** the harvest counts are a keyword **discovery aid, not a census** ([[feedback_no_stats_trends_review_fabricated_data]]) — broad-net buckets (trust, rest, joy, grief) over-match on common words. The "spread" lines below name the psalms where the movement is a genuine **finding**, not an incidental mention. The **lens** throughout: the finding is the inner-being **operation**; God-relation is the arena ([[feedback_lens_is_inner_being_process_not_god_relation]]).
>
> **Structure per characteristic:** *definition-in-operation · spread & strongest exemplars · variations/poles · open questions.*

## Family A — How the self relates to itself (reflexive operations)

### A1. Self-address — the dialogical, self-governing self
- **Operation:** the self *speaks to its own soul* to move it — rousing it to praise or stilling it to rest; the inner being is not a single voice but a self that *governs itself*.
- **Spread & exemplars:** the paradigm is **Ps 42/43** ("Why are you cast down, O my soul?… hope in God" — self-interrogation, thrice). Two poles: the **rousing** register — **103:1** ("Bless the LORD, O my soul… all that is within me"), 104:1, 108:1, 57:8 ("Awake, my glory"); and the **stilling** register — **62:5** ("For God alone, O my soul, wait in silence"), **116:7** ("Return, O my soul, to your rest"), and its summit **131:2** (the *weaned child* — the self quieted past craving). **27:14 / 31:24** turn the self-address outward ("let your heart take courage"). 
- **Poles:** rouse ↔ still; inward (to my own soul) ↔ outward (to others' hearts).
- **Open question:** is the "soul" addressed a *faculty* distinct from the addressing "I", or the same self under strain viewing itself? The Psalter never resolves the grammar — it simply *does* it.

### A2. Being known / searched / tested before God
- **Operation:** the self *offers its interior to God's examination* — and, at the summit, *asks* to be searched; transparency embraced rather than resented.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **139** is the master text — exhaustively known (vv.1–6), the arc from "you have searched me" (v.1) to "**search me**" (v.23). Earlier: **26:2** ("test my heart and my mind" — three assaying verbs), **7:9** ("you who test the hearts"), **17:3** (the night-tested heart), **11:4–5** (his eyes test). **31:7 / 142:3** — the comfort of being *known* in distress ("you have known the distress of my soul").
- **Variation:** God tests the *righteous* to prove (assay, not condemn — 11:5, 7:9) vs. the self *inviting* the test (26, 139:23).
- **Open question:** the move from being-known-as-threat to being-known-as-comfort — what makes the difference is *trust in the searcher* (139); worth tracing whether that trust is prior or produced.

### A3. Entrustment — committing the self to God's hand
- **Operation:** the self *deposits itself* — its spirit, cause, or whole life-span — into God's keeping rather than holding or avenging it.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **31:5,15** ("into your hand I commit my spirit… my times are in your hand" — the signature act). **22** (trust *constituted from the womb*). **55:22** ("cast your burden on the LORD"). **37:5** ("commit your way"). **109:4** (the wronged self *becomes prayer* rather than retaliating). **142:5** (God as refuge/portion when abandoned).
- **Poles:** entrusting the *self* (spirit) ↔ entrusting the *cause/grievance* (handing vengeance to God).
- **Open question:** entrustment and refuge (B1) overlap — entrustment is the *active deposit*, refuge the *sheltering*; worth keeping distinct in a finer taxonomy.

## Family B — How the self orients toward God (Godward postures)

### B1. Trust / refuge — and what trust *does* to the self
- **Operation:** the self *leans its security on God* — and that leaning **re-forms** the self: it stabilises, and it *conforms the self to its object*.
- **Spread & exemplars:** pervasive; the load-bearing findings: **the object of trust forms the truster** — *"you become what you trust"* (**115:8** = **135:18**, dead idols deaden their trusters); **trust confers stability** — the *nakon* (fixed) heart of **112:7** and the immovable-as-Zion self of **125:1**, against the *masas* (melting) heart of the un-anchored (112:10); **trust as a deliberate valuation** — **118:8** ("better to take refuge in the LORD than to trust in man"), **146:3–5** (argued from mortality); trust that *stays put* (**11:1**), *is declared at the lowest point* (**31:14**, **13:5**), and *cancels the fear of man* (**56:11**, **118:6**). Refuge→joy is a recurring resolution (2, 5, 34, 64).
- **Poles:** trust as *shelter* (refuge, hiding) ↔ trust as *formation* (you become what you trust) ↔ trust as *reasoned choice* (better-than).
- **Open question:** the formation-law (115:8) is the deepest datum in the family — if trust *makes* the self, then every other characteristic is downstream of *what* the self trusts. Candidate organising principle for the whole study.

### B2. The fear of the LORD — reverent awe as root
- **Operation:** a *reverent awe* that is not dread-of but the *foundation* of the wise, glad inner life.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **111:10** ("the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom" — the hinge datum linking M01 fear + M15 wisdom); **112:1** (fear *matures into delight* in the commandments); **128:1** (root of the blessed life); **147:11** (God's *pleasure* is in the fearing/hoping heart, not in strength); **2:11** ("serve with fear… rejoice with trembling" — the weld of *glad awe*); **130:4** (forgiveness is what *makes God feared* — see D3). 
- **Variation — two fears, kept distinct:** *reverent* fear (root of wisdom, worshipful) vs. the *dread of God's anger/silence* (6:1, 28:1, 88 — see C-note). The Psalter treats these as different operations sharing a word.
- **Open question:** how reverent fear *becomes* delight (112:1) — the mechanism by which awe warms into love is asserted, not explained.

### B3. Waiting / hope on the LORD
- **Operation:** the self *holds itself expectantly toward God under delay* — taut, watchful, grounded, not passive.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **130:5–6** (the summit — "my soul waits… more than watchmen for the morning," doubled; grounded "in his word"); **27:14** ("wait for the LORD… let your heart take courage"); **40:1** ("I waited patiently… he inclined to me"); **62:5** (wait in silence); **25** (qavah brackets the psalm, vv.3,5,21); **33:20**; **123:2** (the servant's eyes on the master's hand "till he has mercy"); **9:18 / 62** (hope as the poor's inner posture).
- **Poles:** waiting as *silence/stillness* (62) ↔ waiting as *straining watchfulness* (130); certainty *and* ache fused (130:6).
- **Open question:** waiting is where hope (M18), trust (M19), and stillness (M33) meet — is it a distinct movement or their intersection?

### B4. The seeking of God's face / the one thing / presence
- **Operation:** the self *consolidates its desire onto God's presence* and *directs its gaze/aim* to seek his face.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **27:4** ("*one thing* I have asked… to gaze on the beauty of the LORD") + 27:8 (the heart's dialogue: "Seek my face" / "Your face I seek"); **24:6** (the qualified are "seekers of your face"); **42:1–2** (thirst to *appear before God*); **63:1–2** (seeking in the sanctuary); **84** (longing for the courts); **25:15 / 121:1 / 123:1** (the *lifted/fixed gaze* as directed attention — where the eyes go, the trust goes).
- **Poles:** seeking as *desire consolidated* (the "one thing") ↔ seeking as *directed gaze* (the eyes lifted/fixed).
- **Open question:** the face longed-for (27:4) is the face dreaded-to-lose (27:9) — longing and dread of the same object; a tension the Psalter holds without dissolving.

### B5. Desire / longing / appetite — met in God
- **Operation:** the inner being *hungers and thirsts*, and finds its appetite *answered* in God rather than shamed or left gnawing.
- **Spread & exemplars:** the thirst image — **42:1–2 / 63:1 / 143:6** ("my soul thirsts… like a parched land"); the *fainting/longing* soul satisfied — **107:9** ("he satisfies the longing soul"); the word craved — **119** ("consumed with longing," "I pant," vv.20,131); the universal resolution — **145:16,19** ("you open your hand, satisfy the desire of every living thing… fulfils the desire of those who fear him"). Disordered appetite is the foil — **106:14–15** (the craving that tests God and is answered to its ruin). The *weaned child* (**131:2**) is the appetite *transcended* — content past craving.
- **Poles:** honest longing *met* (107, 145) ↔ disordered craving *self-destructive* (106) ↔ appetite *weaned/stilled* (131).
- **Open question:** is contentment (131) the *satisfaction* of desire or its *renunciation*? The Psalter shows both as ends — God as the desire's object, and the soul weaned past demanding.

## Family C — How the self processes experience (dynamic operations)

### C1. Grief / lament handled with integrity
- **Operation:** the self *voices sorrow honestly* — often unresolved for most of a psalm — and handles grief without faking, denying, or being destroyed by it.
- **Spread & exemplars:** the whole self *undone* (**6**, **22:14** "my heart melts like wax," **102:3–11**, **31:9–10**); grief that **won't fake joy** (**137:1–4**, the hung-up harps); **sorrow *sown* reaps joy** (**126:5–6**); **deliberate remembering** as a discipline *against* despair (**143:5**, **77:11**); the *pouring out* of complaint (**142:2**, **62:8**); and the darkest — grief with **no resolution given** (**88**, which ends in darkness). 
- **Poles:** grief *voiced and turned* (most laments pivot) ↔ grief *held open* (88, no turn); grief *invested* (126, sown) ↔ grief *guarded* (137, won't counterfeit joy).
- **Open question:** 88 is the test case — the Psalter *permits* unresolved lament; the inner being's integrity does not require resolution. How that coexists with the pervasive "he has heard" turn (C2) is unresolved by design.

### C2. Being heard / the cry answered — the pivot
- **Operation:** the self *cries from the bottom*, and the whole inner state *inverts on the single fact* that it is heard — often with no change in circumstances.
- **Spread & exemplars:** the recurring **pivot** — **6:8–9**, **22:24** ("he has *not* hidden his face… has heard"), **28:6** ("he has heard"), **31:22** ("I am cut off" / "but you heard"); the **cry from the depths** (**130:1–2**); the **fourfold refrain** of the cry-and-deliverance (**107**:6,13,19,28); the answered longing (**116:1–2**, love *born of being heard*). 
- **Poles:** the cry from *extremity* (107, 130) ↔ the *panic-verdict overturned* (31:22, 30:6 — the self's alarmed misjudgement of God, corrected).
- **Open question:** the pivot turns on being-heard *grasped as certain* before evidence changes — this is the same structure as "trust declared at the lowest point" (B1); the two may be one operation seen from complaint- vs. trust-side.

### C3. Restoration / revival of the self
- **Operation:** the flagging or dead-feeling self is *brought back / made alive again* by God.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **23:3** ("he restores my soul" — *shub nephesh*); **30** ("you brought up my soul from Sheol… turned my mourning into dancing"); **51:10,12** ("create in me a clean heart… restore the joy"); **71:20** ("you will revive me again"); **103:5** (youth *renewed* like the eagle); **119** (the "give me life / revive me" refrain, vv.25,50,…). 
- **Poles:** restoration as *revival* (life to the fainting) ↔ restoration as *re-creation* (51, a new/clean heart) ↔ restoration as *reversal* (30, mourning→dancing).
- **Open question:** is restoration a *return* to a prior state or a *making-new*? 23 suggests return, 51 suggests re-creation — both are called restoration.

### C4. Joy / gladness — its varieties
- **Operation:** the self *rejoices* — and the Psalter distinguishes several *kinds* of joy by their source and location.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **joy above plenty** (**4:7** "more joy than… grain and wine"); **fullness of joy in presence** (**16:11**); the **new song** for fresh divine work (**33:3 / 40:3 / 96:1 / 98:1**); **dreamlike, disbelieving** joy of restoration (**126:1–3**); **private** joy that sings "on their beds" (**149:5**, cf. 42:8, 63:6); joy *grounded in being heard/known* (**31:7**); joy as the fruit of *refuge* (2, 5, 34). 
- **Poles:** joy *derived* (from presence, from being heard) vs. joy *expressed* (new song, dancing, private singing); public ↔ private (149:5).
- **Open question:** joy is downstream of nearly every other movement (trust, being-heard, restoration, presence) — is it a characteristic in its own right or the *affective signature* of the others?

### C5. Rest / stillness / peace / sleep
- **Operation:** the self is *brought to rest* — a settledness that shows itself bodily as *sleep* and inwardly as *stillness/silence*.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **sleep amid danger** as the proof of trust (**3:5**, **4:8** "in peace I will lie down and sleep"); rest that is *made*, not achieved (**23:2**, "he *makes* me lie down"); anxious toil vs. **God-given sleep** (**127:2**); the **stilled/quieted** soul (**131:2**, the weaned child; **62** "wait in silence"; **4:4** "be silent"); the "waters of *menuchah*" (23). 
- **Poles:** rest as *sleep* (bodily trust) ↔ rest as *stillness/silence* (the quieted soul); rest *given* (23, 127) ↔ rest *self-worked* (131:2, "I have calmed and quieted my soul").
- **Open question:** overlaps with waiting (B3) and self-address-stilling (A1) — stillness may be the *state*, waiting the *posture*, self-quieting the *act* that produces it.

## Family D — How the self is sized, shaped, and cleansed

### D1. Humility / self-sizing / the un-lifted heart
- **Operation:** the self *rightly measures itself* — small and fleeting before God — and *refuses over-reach*.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **the wonder that God regards transient man** (**8:4**, re-attested **144:3** — "what is man… but a breath"); the **un-lifted heart** that will not over-reach (**131:1**, "my heart is not lifted up… things too great"); **God draws near the lowly** (**138:6**, **113:7**, **147:6**); the humble *adorned* (**149:4**); right *self-sizing* as the corrective to pride (**9:20** "know they are but men"). Pride is the foil — the self-glorifying heart (10:3, 49:11).
- **Poles:** humility as *self-measurement* (8, 144 — small before God) ↔ humility as *refused ambition* (131:1 — chosen proportion).
- **Open question:** 131 links humility (v.1) to inner rest (v.2) causally — you cannot still a soul still grasping "things too great." Humility as the *precondition* of rest is a strong thread.

### D2. Memory — the self remembers; God remembers
- **Operation:** the self *governs its own remembering* (a discipline), and rests on *God's* remembering of it.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **willed remembering** against despair (**77:11**, **42:6**, **143:5**); memory guarded by **vow** (**137:5–6**, "if I forget you, O Jerusalem"); **forgetting as the seedbed of rebellion** (**106:7,13**, **78:11**); praise as *recounting* (9:1); and the counter-pole — **God does not forget the afflicted** (9:12,18), *remembers his covenant* (105, 106:45), and the self pleads "remember *me*" (25:6–7). **103:2** ("forget not all his benefits") joins self-command and memory.
- **Poles:** the self's memory as *duty/discipline* (77, 137) ↔ God's memory as *security* (9, 105); remembering ↔ the danger of forgetting (106).
- **Open question:** memory is the hinge between grief-handling (C1, deliberate remembering) and trust (B1, remembering God's proven character) — a connective tissue more than a standalone.

### D3. Forgiveness — and the awe it generates
- **Operation:** the self is *pardoned*, and (the deep datum) the pardon *produces reverence* rather than reducing it.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **130:4** — the searching inversion: "with you there is forgiveness, **that you may be feared**" (grace generates awe — the fear of the *forgiven*); **32** (the blessedness and bodily relief of confessed/forgiven sin); **51** (the penitent's re-creation); **103:3,12** ("forgives all your iniquity… as far as the east from the west"); **19:12–13** (hidden faults, presumptuous sins). 
- **Poles:** forgiveness as *relief* (32, the lifted weight) ↔ forgiveness as *awe-generating* (130:4) ↔ forgiveness as *re-creation* (51).
- **Open question:** 130:4 is the study's most counter-intuitive datum — that mercy *deepens* fear. Worth its own investigation: how grace shapes reverence.

### D4. Integrity — the inner condition, and its legibility in speech
- **Operation:** the self is *true all the way through* — and the inner condition is *read off the words* it produces.
- **Spread & exemplars:** **integrity as wholeness** (**15**, **26:1**, **101** — integrity *in the private sphere*, "within my house"); **the inner condition legible in speech** — the *double heart* that produces lies (**12:2**), the deceiver's throat/tongue as the outlet of inward *destruction* (**5:9**), the wicked's heart *quoted* (**10:6,11**); **truth in the inward being** (**51:6**, **15:2**). Governing the gates — the eyes (101:3, 119:37), the mouth (141:3), the heart's *inclination* (141:4).
- **Poles:** integrity as *inner truth* (15, 51:6) ↔ integrity as *its outward legibility* (5, 10, 12 — speech as the window); integrity *claimed* (26) vs. integrity *not self-trusted* (26:11, resolves into grace).
- **Open question:** 26 shows the integrous self does *not* plead its integrity as merit but casts itself on grace — integrity and humility (D1) held together; the line between confidence and self-righteousness runs through *chesed*, not the record.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Two master-data stand out** as candidate organising principles: **B1's formation-law** ("you become what you trust," 115:8/135:18) and **A2/139's** "the self invites its own searching." If trust *forms* the self and the formed self *opens* itself to God, the whole inner life is a function of *what it trusts and whether it will be seen*.
- **Poles recur** across families: rouse ↔ still (A1, C5), given ↔ self-worked (C5), voiced-and-turned ↔ held-open (C1), met ↔ weaned (B5). The inner being is consistently drawn as *held between two legitimate poles*, not resolved to one.
- **The arena is overwhelmingly God-interaction** — which is *right* for the Psalter (prayer/praise), but the lens held to the *operation*: what the self *does* (trusts, waits, cries, remembers, quiets itself), with God as the One before whom it does it.

## Honest notes & limits
- **Emergent, not exhaustive:** ~18 movements across 4 families emerged; they **overlap** (waiting/trust/stillness; memory/trust; joy downstream of all) — flagged rather than forced apart. A finer taxonomy is a next pass.
- **Grounded, not census-driven:** exemplars are the psalms where the movement is a genuine *finding*; the harvest counts are a discovery aid only ([[feedback_no_stats_trends_review_fabricated_data]]).
- **Arena-only psalms** (no distinct inner-being finding): from the harvest, **117, 148** (confirmed arena-only in their readings), plus **53, 54, 58, 66, 75, 129, 133** matched no characteristic net — a mix of genuinely thin/arena psalms and harvest-regex gaps; **not** asserted as findings. Worth a targeted check that none hides a missed finding.
- **Not yet split per-characteristic in the DB:** this v1 is one unified synthesis (filed as a single `lexical_synthesis_psalter` prose_section). If useful, each section can later be promoted to its own `lexical_synthesis_characteristic` record.
- **Open loops for the next phase:** (1) investigate the two master-data (formation-law; invited-searching); (2) resolve the pole-pairs into a finer taxonomy; (3) sweep the 9 unmatched psalms for missed findings; (4) extend the same synthesis method to Proverbs when the poetic-books run reaches it.
