## The reading

**I. The consuming vow that refuses its own rest (vv.2–5).**
"how he **swore** to the LORD and **vowed** to the Mighty One of Jacob, 'I will **not enter my house** or get into my bed, I will **not give sleep to my eyes** or slumber to my eyelids, **until I find a place for the LORD**'" (vv.2–5). The gem-finding: David binds himself by oath to **deny himself house, bed, and sleep** *until* God has a dwelling *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being is **wholly bent on a single God-ward purpose**, so that it *subordinates its own comfort and rest* to it — no house, no bed, no sleep "until"; a consuming, single-minded devotion that will not let the self rest while God is unhoused *(inferred: "I will not… I will not… until I find a place for the LORD" is the inner being *organised around one priority* — the self's own needs (rest, home) held in abeyance beneath the desire to honour God; devotion as *refusal of self-comfort* until the God-ward aim is met; cf. Ps 69:9 "zeal for your house has consumed me", 27:4)*.

## What Psalm 132 says about the inner being
- **The self can be *wholly bent on one God-ward purpose*, subordinating its own rest (vv.3–5).** David's vow denies house, bed, and sleep "until I find a place for the LORD" — a consuming, single-minded devotion that will not let the self rest while God is unhonoured; the inner being's needs held beneath the God-ward aim.
