# Psalm 105 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 105:1–45 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked; aheb-fix build). Complete chapter (13 vv. backfilled with the fixed full-text logic). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **Lens:** the finding is the inner-being **operation**, God-relation as arena (`feedback_lens_is_inner_being_process_not_god_relation`).
- **Scope honesty up front:** Psalm 105 is a **historical recital** — God's covenant faithfulness through Abraham, Joseph, Egypt, and the Exodus (vv.7–45). Most of it narrates *God's dealings*, not the inner being. The inner-being findings are three, concentrated in the opening call and two narrative touch-points: the **rejoicing of the *seeking heart*** and the disposition to seek/remember (vv.3–5); **Joseph *tested by the word* until it came to pass** (vv.18–19); and a **heart *turned* to hate** (v.25).

## The reading

**I. The rejoicing of the seeking heart; the disposition to seek and remember (vv.3–5).**
"Glory in his holy name; **let the hearts** (H3820, the seat — M47) **of those who seek the LORD rejoice** (H8055, M04)! **Seek** (H1875, M41) the LORD and his strength; seek his presence continually! **Remember** (H2142, M41) the wondrous works that he has done" (vv.3–5). The gem-finding: the inner being that **seeks the LORD** is the one whose **heart *rejoices*** — seeking and gladness are joined *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being is summoned to a **posture of *continual seeking and remembering*** — "seek his presence *continually*," "*remember* the wondrous works" — and in that seeking the heart finds its joy *(inferred: "let the hearts of those who seek… rejoice / seek continually / remember" makes seeking-and-remembering the disposition, and gladness its fruit — the heart is glad *in the seeking*, not only in the finding; cf. Ps 27:8 "your face, LORD, do I seek")*.

**II. Joseph tested by the word until it came to pass (vv.18–19).**
"His feet were hurt with fetters; his **neck** (H5315 — *nephesh*, the self/soul — M47) was put in a collar of iron; until what he had said came to pass, **the word of the LORD tested him**" (vv.18–19). The gem-finding: in the fettered, imprisoned Joseph, "the word of the LORD **tested him**" — the *self* held under trial until the promise was fulfilled *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being is **proved / refined by *the word* over a long delay** — the interval between promise and fulfilment is itself the testing, the self held in iron until the word "came to pass" *(inferred: "until what he had said came to pass, the word… tested him" makes the *waiting on the word* the crucible — the self is tested precisely in the gap; cf. the purposed/tested heart of Ps 17:3, here stretched across years)*.

**III. A heart *turned* to hate (v.25).**
"He **turned their hearts** (H3820, M47) **to hate** (H8130, M06) his people, to deal craftily with his servants" (v.25). The finding: the Egyptians' **hearts were *turned* to hate** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's disposition can be **turned** — the heart is here *moved into hate* within God's sovereign ordering of the history *(inferred: "he turned their hearts to hate" presents the heart's hostility as something into which it was *turned*, a datum on the heart's turnability; noted, not developed — the arena is the Exodus narrative, cf. Ex 1's oppression)*.

## What Psalm 105 says about the inner being
- **The *seeking heart rejoices* (vv.3–5).** Seeking and gladness are joined — the inner being summoned to continual seeking and remembering finds its joy *in the seeking*, not only the finding.
- **The self is *tested by the word* across a long delay (vv.18–19).** Joseph, fettered, is proved in the gap between promise and fulfilment — the waiting on the word is itself the crucible.
- **The heart can be *turned* (v.25).** The disposition is turnable — here turned to hate — a datum on the heart's malleability within the ordering of events.

## Honest notes
- **Scope honesty:** this is overwhelmingly a historical recital; ~35 verses narrate God's covenant dealings (Abraham, Joseph, plagues, Exodus, wilderness). The recurring "he **remembered** his covenant/promise" (vv.8, 42) is *God's* faithful memory driving the history — the arena, not the human inner being. The inner-being weight is the seeking-heart call (vv.3–5), Joseph's testing (vv.18–19), and the turned heart (v.25).
- **v.19 "the word of the LORD tested him"** is the psalm's richest inner-being touch-point — the long testing of Joseph in the gap; the "neck/soul" of v.18 (H5315) anchors the self under trial.
