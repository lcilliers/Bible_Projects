## The reading

**I. The wonder that God regards transient man (vv.3–4).**
"O LORD, **what is man that you regard him**, or the son of man that you **think of him**? Man is like a **breath**; his days are like a **passing shadow**" (vv.3–4). The gem-finding: the self marvels that God should *regard* and *think of* man — and in the same breath owns man's *transience* ("like a breath… a passing shadow") *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being holds together **its own *smallness/fleetingness* and God's astonishing *attention*** — the self sees itself as breath and shadow, *and* is amazed that such a creature is *noticed* by God; humility (I am nothing lasting) and wonder (yet you think of me) fused *(inferred: "what is man that you regard him… man is like a breath" fuses the self's honest sense of its own transience with astonishment at being *regarded* — the same wonder as Ps 8:4; the inner being humbled by its brevity yet lifted by God's notice; cf. Ps 39:5, Job 7:17)*.

## What Psalm 144 says about the inner being
- **The self holds together its own *transience* and God's *astonishing regard* (vv.3–4).** Seeing itself as breath and passing shadow, the inner being is amazed that God *notices* it at all — humility (I am nothing lasting) and wonder (yet you think of me) fused in one recognition.
