## The reading

**I. The servant's eyes on the master's hand — attentive waiting dependence (vv.1–2).**
"To you **I lift up my eyes**, O you who are enthroned in the heavens! Behold, as the **eyes of servants look to the hand of their master**… so our eyes look to the LORD our God, **till he has mercy** upon us" (vv.1–2). The gem-finding: the self's posture is a **fixed, waiting gaze** — the servant's eyes on the master's *hand*, watching for the least signal, *until* mercy comes *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **attends on God in *patient dependence*** — not glancing but *fixed* on the hand that provides and directs, holding the look "*till* he has mercy"; a waiting that is both submission and expectant watchfulness *(inferred: "as the eyes of servants… to the hand of their master, so our eyes… till he has mercy" figures dependence as a *sustained watchful gaze* — the self reads the master's hand for its next provision, and does not look away until answered; attentive, patient, submissive expectancy; cf. Ps 25:15, 141:8, 130:6 "more than watchmen for the morning")*.

**II. The soul *sated with contempt* (vv.3–4).**
"Have mercy upon us… for we have had **more than enough of contempt** (H0937, M06). Our **soul** (H5315, M47) has had **more than enough** of the **scorn** of those who are at ease, of the **contempt of the proud**" (vv.3–4). The gem-finding: the **soul is *filled to satiety* with scorn** — "more than enough" (Hebrew *rab*, sated/glutted) *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being is **saturated past its capacity by contempt** — the scorn of the comfortable and proud has *filled the soul to overflowing*, and it is this *fullness of humiliation* that presses the cry for mercy *(inferred: "our soul has had more than enough of… contempt" makes the scorn a thing the soul has been *force-fed* until sated — the self glutted with humiliation, its endurance at its limit; the satiety is the datum, and the ground of the appeal; cf. Ps 69:20 "reproach has broken my heart", 44:13)*.

## What Psalm 123 says about the inner being
- **Dependence is an *attentive, patient watching* (vv.1–2).** The servant's eyes fixed on the master's hand, reading it for provision, holding the gaze "till he has mercy" — submission and expectant watchfulness together.
- **The soul can be *sated past capacity with contempt* (vv.3–4).** "More than enough" — the self glutted with scorn, filled to overflowing with humiliation; the fullness itself presses the cry for mercy.
