## The reading

**I. The anchoring confession amid danger — "You are my God" (v.6).**
"I say to the LORD, **You are my God**; give ear to the voice of my **pleas for mercy**, O LORD!" (v.6). The gem-finding: encircled by violent men, the self makes a **declaration of belonging** — "You are my God" — before it even presses its plea *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **anchors itself in an act of *allegiance*** — in danger it *names* whose it is ("you are my God"), and that confession is the ground from which it then asks for mercy; the self steadies itself by re-affirming its bond to God *(inferred: "I say to the LORD, You are my God… give ear" makes the confession of allegiance the *anchor* preceding the petition — the self locates its security in *belonging* to God before it asks anything; cf. Ps 31:14 "I trust in you… you are my God", 16:2)*.

**II. The settled *knowing* that God vindicates the afflicted (vv.12–13).**
"I **know** that the LORD will **maintain the cause of the afflicted** (H6041, M24), and will **execute justice for the needy**. Surely the **righteous shall give thanks**… the **upright shall dwell in your presence**" (vv.12–13). The gem-finding: the self declares a **settled *knowledge*** — "I know" — that God will uphold the afflicted's cause and give justice to the needy *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being rests on a **firm conviction about God's *character toward the oppressed*** — not a wish but a *known* certainty ("I know") that God takes up the afflicted's case; and it looks forward to *dwelling in God's presence*, the hope beyond the conflict *(inferred: "I know that the LORD will maintain the cause of the afflicted" is the self's *settled assurance* — the conviction that steadies it under assault is knowledge of God's bias toward the wronged; the confidence rests on God's nature, not the odds; cf. Ps 9:18, 12:5, 34:6)*.

## What Psalm 140 says about the inner being
- **The self *anchors in allegiance* amid danger (v.6).** Before pressing its plea, the inner being *names whose it is* — "You are my God" — steadying itself by re-affirming its bond; the confession of belonging is the ground of the petition.
- **The self rests on a *settled knowing* of God's justice (vv.12–13).** "I *know* the LORD will maintain the cause of the afflicted" — not a wish but a certainty about God's character toward the oppressed; the conviction that steadies the self under assault, with the hope of dwelling in God's presence beyond it.
