## The reading

**I. Whole-being longing — ache and joy together (vv.1–2).**
"How **lovely** is your dwelling place… My **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) **longs** (H3700, M18, *kasaph* — yearns), yes, **faints** for the courts of the LORD; my **heart** (H3820, M47) and **flesh** (H1320, seat=flesh — M47) **sing for joy** (H7442, M42) to the living God" (vv.1–2). The finding: the longing for God's presence-place engages the **whole person** — *soul* and *flesh* — and it holds **ache and joy at once** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's desire is **total and consuming** ("faints" — spent with yearning) *and simultaneously* breaks into **song** — the soul *aches* while the heart and flesh *sing* *(inferred: "longs… faints… sing for joy" combines the consuming yearning and the joy in one movement; cf. Ps 63:1's soul-thirsts/flesh-faints, here with the added *joy*)*.

**II. The pilgrim-roads *within the heart* (v.5).**
"Blessed are those whose **strength** (H5797, M23) is in you, in whose **heart** (H3824, the seat — M47) are the **highways** (H4546, *mesillah* — the pilgrim-roads) to Zion" (v.5). The gem-finding: the inner being can carry the **roads to God's dwelling *inside* the heart** — "in whose *heart* are the highways to Zion" *(stated)*. The operation: the pilgrimage is **interiorised** — the *way* to God is set *within* the inner being, the heart already **oriented toward and travelling** the road to the dwelling *(inferred: "the highways… in the heart" locates the journey in the interior — the heart is on the way before the feet are)*. The self whose *strength* is in God has the *road* in its heart.

**III. A journey that *accumulates* strength (vv.6–7).**
"As they go through the **Valley of Baca** (weeping) they make it a place of **springs**… They go **from strength to strength** (H2428, M23); each one appears before God in Zion" (vv.6–7). Two findings. The inner being **turns the valley of tears into springs** as it passes — it *transforms* the place of weeping in the going *(stated)*. And it goes **"from strength to strength"** — the God-ward journey **accumulates rather than depletes** strength *(stated)*. The operation: unlike ordinary travel that tires, the pilgrim inner being **grows stronger as it goes** — the journey toward God is *strengthening*, and even its valleys become sources *(inferred: "Valley of Baca… springs… strength to strength" makes the road a place of increasing, not diminishing, power; cf. Ps 71:14's hope "more and more")*.

**IV. The re-valuation — a day, a threshold, outweighs a thousand (v.10).**
"For a **day** in your courts is better than a **thousand** elsewhere. I would rather be a **doorkeeper** in the house of my God than dwell in the tents of wickedness" (v.10). The gem-finding: the inner being's value-hierarchy is **re-ordered** — *proximity* to God, even *briefly* (a day) and at the *lowest place* (a doorkeeper, the threshold), **outweighs abundance and status elsewhere** *(stated)*. The operation: the self prizes **nearness over quantity and position** — a day at the threshold near God is worth more than a thousand days, a doorkeeper's place worth more than dwelling among the wicked *(inferred: "a day… better than a thousand… doorkeeper rather than dwell in wickedness" re-values nearness above duration and rank; cf. Ps 63:3 chesed>life, 73:28 nearness is my good)*.

## What Psalm 84 says about the inner being
- **The longing for God's dwelling is *whole-being* — ache and joy at once (vv.1–2).** Soul and flesh together; the soul *faints* with yearning while the heart and flesh *sing* — the consuming desire and the joy held in one movement.
- **The pilgrim-roads can be carried *within the heart* (v.5).** The way to God's dwelling is interiorised — the heart is on the road before the feet are; the self whose strength is in God has the journey set within it.
- **The God-ward journey *accumulates* strength (vv.6–7).** Unlike ordinary travel, it goes "from strength to strength" — the inner being grows stronger as it goes, and even the valley of tears is turned to springs in the passing.
- **Nearness outweighs quantity and rank (v.10).** A day at the threshold near God is worth more than a thousand elsewhere; the doorkeeper's lowest place outweighs dwelling among the wicked. The value-hierarchy is re-ordered around proximity.
