- **Scope honesty up front:** Psalm 108 is a **composite** — its two halves are lifted from Ps 57:7–11 and Ps 60:5–12. ## The reading

**I. The steadfast/fixed heart, ready to praise (v.1).**
"My **heart** (H3820, the seat — M47) is **steadfast**, O God! I will sing and make melody with **all my being** (H3519 — *kabod*, glory/the honoured self)!" (v.1). The gem-finding (continuous with Ps 57:7): the inner being is **settled, fixed** — a *steadfast heart* — and *out of that settledness* it praises with its *whole self* *(stated)*. The operation: the heart's **fixed/anchored condition is the ground of wholehearted praise** — not a scattered or divided heart but a *settled* one, from which the song rises with "all my being" *(inferred: "my heart is steadfast… I will sing with all my being" makes the steadiness the precondition — the fixed heart can give itself *entire* to praise; cf. Ps 57:7, 112:7 "his heart is firm")*.

**II. Rousing the whole being to praise — waking the dawn (vv.1–2).**
"…I will sing and make melody with **all my being**! **Awake**, O harp and lyre! **I will awake** (H5782) **the dawn**!" (vv.1–2). The gem-finding: the self **musters its "glory" (its whole inner being) for praise** and is so eager it will **wake the dawn** — rouse the very morning to worship *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **stirs *itself* (and even the day) into praise** — a self-rousing so ardent it precedes and summons the dawn, the whole self and its world called up to worship *(inferred: "with all my being… I will awake the dawn" makes the praise an act of self-arousal that overflows onto creation — the eager self will not wait for the morning but *wakes* it; cf. Ps 57:8, 103:1's "all that is within me")*.

## What Psalm 108 says about the inner being
- **The *steadfast/fixed heart* is the ground of wholehearted praise (v.1).** A settled, anchored heart — not scattered or divided — can give itself *entire* to worship, singing "with all my being."
- **The self *rouses its whole being (and the dawn) to praise* (vv.1–2).** A self-arousal so ardent it summons the very morning — the inner being stirring itself, and its world, up to worship.
