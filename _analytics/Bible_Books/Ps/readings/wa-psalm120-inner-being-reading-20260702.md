## The reading

**I. The cry from distress, answered (v.1).**
"In my **distress** (H6869, M03) I **called** to the LORD, and he **answered** me" (v.1). The finding: the pilgrim song begins where so many do — distress voiced, and *answered* *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being under pressure **turns its distress into a *call*** and finds it met — the settled testimony ("he answered me") that grounds the pilgrimage *(inferred: "in my distress I called… he answered" restates the cry-and-answer move as the pilgrim's starting confidence; cf. Ps 118:5, 107)*.

**II. The peaceable self worn by dwelling among peace-haters (vv.5–7).**
"Woe to me… **Too long** have I had my dwelling among those who **hate peace** (H7965, M33). **I am for peace**, but when I speak, they are for **war**!" (vv.5–7). The gem-finding: the self is **weary of a *prolonged* dwelling among the hostile** — "too long" — and names its own inmost alignment: "**I am for peace**" (Hebrew *"I — peace"*), set against a surrounding *war* *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being suffers the **friction of being *peaceable in a hostile environment*** — its own bent is toward peace, but every word meets war; the sustained exposure *wearies* the self, which longs to be elsewhere *(inferred: "too long… among those who hate peace; I am for peace, but… they are for war" makes the affliction *environmental and chronic* — the self's peaceable nature is ground down by prolonged company with the contentious; the weariness is the datum, cf. 2 Pet 2:8 Lot "worn out", Ps 55:6 "oh that I had wings")*.

## What Psalm 120 says about the inner being
- **Distress becomes a *call* that is answered (v.1).** The pilgrim's starting confidence — pressure voiced Godward and met.
- **The peaceable self is *worn down by hostile company* (vv.5–7).** "I am for peace… they are for war" — the inner being's own bent toward peace is ground down by *prolonged* dwelling among those who hate it; a chronic, environmental affliction, and a longing to be elsewhere.

## Honest notes
- **A distinctive datum:** the self *identifying itself with peace* ("I — peace") against a warlike environment, and the *weariness* of chronic exposure to hostility — an inner-being affliction that is relational/environmental rather than internal (with Ps 55, and the peace-thread of 34:14, 37:37).
- **Scope honesty:** the "deceitful tongue" and its judgment (vv.2–4) are the *arena* of the distress (the hostile speech that surrounds the self); the inner-being weight is the answered cry (v.1) and the worn peaceable self (vv.5–7).
