## The reading

**I. The delight in unity as good and pleasant (v.1).**
"Behold, how **good** (H2896, M04) and **pleasant** (H5273) it is when **brothers dwell in unity**!" (v.1). The finding: the self exclaims over unity as *good* and *pleasant* — a warm, appreciative valuation *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **savours *relational harmony* as a delight** — not a duty observed but a *sweetness relished*; the "behold… how good and pleasant" is the self's *pleasure* in seeing brothers dwell together, an aesthetic-moral delight in concord *(inferred: "how good and pleasant… when brothers dwell in unity" registers unity as something the inner being *enjoys and prizes* — the self responds to harmony with warmth (good = morally-approved, pleasant = felt-as-sweet); the delight is the datum, cf. Ps 16:3 "the saints… in whom is all my delight", the love-of-community of 122)*.

## What Psalm 133 says about the inner being
- **The self *delights in and prizes unity* as good and pleasant (v.1).** Relational harmony is savoured, not merely approved — the inner being responds to concord with warmth: *good* (morally approved) and *pleasant* (felt as sweet); a delight in harmonious community.
