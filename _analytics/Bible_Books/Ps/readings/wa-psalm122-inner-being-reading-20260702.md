- **Gladness at the summons to worship; love that *seeks the good* of the place of worship.** Psalm 122 is the pilgrim's joy at arriving at Jerusalem. Its inner-being findings: the **gladness at being called to God's house** (v.1); and the **love that moves the self to *seek the good* and peace of the city — for others' sake and God's** (vv.6–9).

## The reading

**I. Gladness at the summons to worship (v.1).**
"I was **glad** (H8055, M04) when they said to me, 'Let us go to the **house of the LORD**!'" (v.1). The gem-finding: the invitation to worship *itself* produces **gladness** — the self rejoices not (yet) at arriving but at the *summons* to go *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **delights in the *call* to God's presence** — the prospect of worship gladdens the self before the journey even begins; the house of the LORD is desired, so the word "let us go" lands as joy *(inferred: "I was glad when they said… let us go" locates the gladness in the *invitation* — the self's affection for God's house is such that being *called* to it is already joy; cf. Ps 27:4, 84:1–2,10)*.

**II. Love that *seeks the good* and peace of the place of worship (vv.6–9).**
"**Pray for the peace** (H7965, M33) of Jerusalem! May they be **secure who love** (H0157, *aheb*, M05) you!… For my brothers and companions' sake I will say, 'Peace be within you!' For the sake of the **house of the LORD** our God, **I will seek your good**" (vv.6–9). The gem-finding: the self's **love** for the city moves it to *pray for its peace* and *actively seek its good* — and it does so **for the sake of others** ("my brothers and companions") **and of God's house** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's love issues in ***other-directed seeking*** — love become the will to *pursue the welfare* of the beloved place, motivated not by self-interest but by concern for the community and for God's dwelling; affection expressed as *active good-seeking* *(inferred: "for my brothers' sake… for the house of the LORD… I will seek your good" makes the love outward and self-giving — the self labours for the peace of what it loves, on others' behalf; love as pursuit-of-the-other's-good, cf. Ps 137:6, Neh 2:10)*.

## What Psalm 122 says about the inner being
- **The *summons* to worship gladdens the self (v.1).** The inner being's affection for God's house is such that being *called* to it is already joy — gladness at the invitation, before the arrival.
- **Love issues in *seeking the good* of what it loves (vv.6–9).** The self prays for and actively pursues the peace and welfare of the city — for its brothers' sake and God's house; affection become other-directed, self-giving labour.
