## The reading

**I. "Bless the LORD, O my soul" — the self rousing its whole inner being (vv.1–2, 22).**
"**Bless the LORD, O my soul** (H5315, the seat — M47), and **all that is within me**, bless his holy name! Bless the LORD, O my soul, and **forget not** all his benefits" (vv.1–2; refrain at v.22). The gem-finding: the inner being **commands *itself* to bless God** — an "**I**" rousing "**my soul**" (and "all that is *within me*") to worship *(stated)*. The operation: self-address in the **mode of *stirring to praise*** — the self **enlists its *whole* inner being**, "all that is within me," every faculty gathered into the blessing *(inferred: "O my soul… all that is within me" musters the entire interior, cf. Ps 57:8 "awake, my glory", 42's self-interrogation — here the positive rousing)*. And it commands its own **memory**: "**forget not** all his benefits" — the self governs its *remembering*, guarding against the ingratitude of forgetting *(stated; the self directs its own memory, cf. Ps 77's willed remembering)*.

**II. The renewal of the self (vv.3–5).**
"who **forgives** (H5545, M11) all your iniquity, who **heals** all your diseases, who **redeems** your life from the pit… who **satisfies** (H7646, *saba*) you with good so that your **youth** is **renewed** (H2318, *chadash*) like the eagle's" (vv.3–5). The finding: the inner being, satisfied with good, is **re-youthed** — its *youth renewed* like the eagle (which molts and renews) *(stated)*. The operation: the self is **renewed/rejuvenated** by God's benefits — not merely maintained but *made young again*, its vitality restored *(inferred: "satisfies you with good so that your youth is renewed" makes the satisfaction issue in a renewal of the self's very youth; cf. the revived-again of Ps 71:20)*.

**III. God's fatherly compassion grounded in knowing our dust-frame (vv.11–14).**
"As high as the heavens are above the earth, so great is his **steadfast love**… as far as the east is from the west, so far does he **remove our transgressions**… As a **father** shows **compassion** (H7355, M05, *racham*) to his children, so the LORD shows compassion to those who **fear** (H3373, M01) him. **For he knows our frame; he remembers that we are dust**" (vv.11–14). Two gem-findings. The inner being's **transgressions are removed to an infinite distance** — "as far as the east is from the west" — the completeness of forgiveness *(stated)*. And, the psalm's tender centre: God's compassion is **fatherly** and **grounded in his *knowing our frailty*** — "he knows our *frame*; he remembers that we are *dust*" *(stated)*. The operation: God's tender regard **factors in the self's weakness** — his compassion turns on an *accounting of how frail the inner being is* (dust), like a father's toward a child *(inferred: "he knows our frame… we are dust" makes the divine compassion rest on a reckoning of human frailty; the same finding as Ps 78:39's "he remembered they were but flesh", here as a father's tenderness)*.

## What Psalm 103 says about the inner being
- **The self can *command its whole inner being to bless* (vv.1–2, 22).** "O my soul… all that is within me" — self-address in the mode of rousing to praise, mustering every faculty; and it governs its own *memory* ("forget not"), guarding against the ingratitude of forgetting.
- **The self can be *renewed / re-youthed* (v.5).** Satisfied with good, the inner being's youth is renewed like the eagle's — vitality restored, not merely maintained.
- **God's compassion is grounded in *knowing our frailty* (vv.13–14).** Fatherly, and turning on his accounting that "we are dust" — the tender regard for weakness (with Ps 78:39). And transgressions are removed to an infinite distance — forgiveness complete.
