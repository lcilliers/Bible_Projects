## The reading

**I. The "how long" — and the anguish of *not knowing the duration* (vv.9–10).**
"We do not see our signs; there is **no longer any prophet**, and there is **none among us who knows how long**. **How long**, O God, is the foe to scoff?" (vv.9–10). The finding: the inner being's anguish is sharpened by **not knowing *how long* the affliction will last** — and by the **absence of any word** (no prophet) to tell it *(stated)*. The operation: the self can bear affliction, but the **unbounded, un-datable duration** — "none knows how long" — is its own torment; the inner being reaches for an *end-point* and finds none *(inferred: "none knows how long… how long, O God?" makes the *not-knowing-the-end* the specific weight, compounding the affliction; cf. Ps 13's fourfold "how long")*.

**II. The pivot to remembered, ancient faith (v.12).**
"**Yet** God my King is **from of old**, working salvation in the midst of the earth" (v.12). The finding: against the present desolation, the inner being **anchors on the *ancient*, remembered God** — "from of old" *(stated)*. The operation: the "**yet**" turns the self from the *present ruin* to the *remembered, long-established faithfulness* of God — memory of God's ancient works becomes the ground the inner being stands on when the present gives nothing *(inferred: the "yet… from of old" pivots from desolation to remembered antiquity, the self reaching back past the present)*.

**III. The self as a defenceless dove (v.19).**
"Do not deliver the **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) of your **dove** to the wild beasts; do not forget the **life** of your poor forever" (v.19). The finding: the inner being is figured as a **defenceless dove** — the fragile, vulnerable *soul* exposed to beasts *(stated)*. The operation: the self names its own **helplessness** — a dove among predators — as the ground of its plea *(inferred: "the soul of your dove" casts the inner being as small and defenceless, the vulnerability itself the appeal; cf. Ps 55:6's dove-longing)*.

## What Psalm 74 says about the inner being
- **Not knowing *how long* is its own torment (vv.9–10).** The inner being can bear affliction, but the un-datable, unbounded duration — "none knows how long," no word to tell it — is a distinct weight; the self reaches for an end-point and finds none.
- **The self anchors on *remembered, ancient* faith amid ruin (v.12).** The "yet" turns the inner being from the present desolation to God's long-established works — memory of the ancient becomes the ground when the present gives nothing.
- **The self names its own defencelessness (v.19).** Figured as a *dove* among beasts, the vulnerable soul makes its very helplessness the ground of its plea.
