## The reading

**I. Gratitude born of reckoning what one was spared (vv.1–5).**
"**If it had not been the LORD** who was on our side… then they would have **swallowed us up alive**… then the **flood** would have swept us away… the raging waters" (vv.1–5). The gem-finding: the self's thanksgiving is generated by **imagining the catastrophe it *narrowly missed*** — the swallowing, the flood, the raging waters that *would have* overwhelmed *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **reckons its deliverance by contemplating the *counterfactual ruin*** — it looks back and *pictures what would have been* without God, and that vivid sense of near-destruction *fuels* the gratitude; thanksgiving deepened by *imagining the alternative* *(inferred: the repeated "if not for the LORD… then… then… then" is the self deliberately rehearsing the disaster it escaped — gratitude intensified by counterfactual imagination; the deeper the imagined ruin, the fuller the thanks; cf. Ps 124:6, 129:1–2)*.

**II. The exhilaration of the self set free (vv.6–7).**
"**Blessed be the LORD**, who has not given us as **prey to their teeth**! We have **escaped like a bird** from the snare of the fowlers; **the snare is broken, and we have escaped!**" (vv.6–7). The gem-finding: the deliverance breaks out in **exhilarated relief** — the doubled "we have escaped!" and the image of a bird flying free from a shattered snare *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being, once trapped and now free, is **flooded with the joy of *escape*** — the repetition ("escaped… escaped!") enacts the breathless relief; the self that was *prey* is suddenly *airborne*, and the freedom is *felt* as elation *(inferred: "escaped like a bird… the snare is broken… we have escaped!" is the affect of sudden liberation — the doubling and the flight-image carry the exhilaration; the self's relief at being sprung from the trap; cf. Ps 18:19, the wide place)*.

## What Psalm 124 says about the inner being
- **Gratitude is *fuelled by reckoning what one was spared* (vv.1–5).** The self rehearses the ruin it narrowly missed — "if not for the LORD, then…" — and the vivid counterfactual *deepens* the thanks; imagining the alternative intensifies gratitude.
- **Escape breaks out in *exhilarated relief* (vv.6–7).** The self once *prey* is suddenly *airborne* — "the snare is broken, and we have escaped!"; the doubled cry carries the breathless joy of sudden liberation.
