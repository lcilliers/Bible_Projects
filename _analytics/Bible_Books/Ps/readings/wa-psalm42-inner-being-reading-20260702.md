- **The dialogical inner being.** Psalm 42's central discovery is *structural*: the inner being is **not monolithic — it can stand apart from itself and address itself.** An "**I**" interrogates and commands "**my soul**": "Why are you cast down, O my soul?… Hope in God" (vv.5, 11). Around this run the modes of the soul's desire — **thirst** (appetitive craving, vv.1–2) and **self-outpouring** (vv.4) — and the sense of being **flooded** ("deep calls to deep," v.7), held by a **song in the night** (v.8).

## The reading

**I. Thirst — the appetitive structure of the soul's desire (vv.1–2).**
"As a deer **pants** (H6165, *arag*) for flowing streams, so pants my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) for you… My soul **thirsts** (H6770, *tsame*) for God, for the **living** (H2416) God" (vv.1–2). The operation: the inner being's desire for God is **appetitive, not intellectual** — it operates as *thirst*, a felt bodily lack driving toward a source *(stated)*. The finding: the soul's longing has the **structure of a life-or-death craving** — panting, thirsting — an urgent deficit that only its object can slake *(inferred: the deer-panting image makes the desire a survival-appetite)*.

**II. Pouring out the soul — self-emptying fuelled by memory (vv.3–4).**
"My **tears** (H1832, M03) have been my food day and night… These things I **remember** (H2142, M41), as I **pour out my soul** (*shaphak nephesh*): how I would go with the throng… to the house of God with glad shouts" (vv.3–4). The operation: the inner being **pours itself out** — an active *self-emptying* in lament *(stated)*. And its fuel is **memory**: recalling the former festival-gladness *intensifies* the present ache *(inferred: "these things I remember, as I pour out my soul" joins the remembering to the outpouring — the remembered joy sharpens the grief)*.

**III. The self-dialogue — the inner being addressing itself (vv.5, 11).**
The refrain, twice: "**Why are you cast down** (*shachach* — bowed low), O my **soul** (H5315, M47), and why are you in **turmoil** (H1993, *hamah* — disquiet/roar) within me? **Hope** (H3176, M18, *yachal*) in God; for I shall again **praise** (H3034, M22) him" (vv.5, 11). The gem-finding: the inner being **splits and speaks to itself** — an "**I**" that stands apart from "**my soul**," **interrogating** its state ("why are you cast down?") and **commanding** it ("hope in God") *(stated)*. The structure disclosed: the inner being is **dialogical** — it contains a *reflective/governing self* that can counsel the *affective/downcast self* *(inferred: the "I…my soul…within me" grammar makes one part address another)*. And the operation is **honest**: the downcast state is *named*, not denied — the governing "I" does not pretend the soul is fine but **addresses the reality and directs it toward hope** *(inferred: the self-command follows an admission, not a suppression)*. The refrain **enacts self-government twice**, so it is a *practice*, not a one-off *(inferred)*.

**IV. Two directions of "why" (vv.5, 9).**
The same interrogative runs **inward and upward**: "Why are you cast down, O my soul?" (self-directed, vv.5, 11) and "I say to God, my rock, 'Why have you **forgotten** (H7911) me? Why do I go **mourning**?'" (God-directed, v.9). The finding: the inner being poses "why" **both to itself and to God** — self-interrogation and God-interrogation run together, neither cancelling the other *(inferred: the two "why"s coexist across the psalm)*.

**V. Flooded — deep calling to deep (v.7).**
"**Deep** (H8415, *tehom*) calls to deep at the roar of your waterfalls; all your breakers and your waves have gone over me" (v.7). The operation: the inner being **submerged** — one depth summoning another, wave upon wave *(stated)*. The finding: grief **compounds** — depths answer depths; the affective flood is not a single blow but a self-amplifying surge *(inferred: "deep calls to deep" makes the overwhelming recursive)*.

**VI. The song in the night amid the downcast state (v.8).**
"By day the LORD commands his **steadfast love** (H2617, M05), and at night his **song** (H7892) is with me, a prayer to the God of my life" (v.8). The finding: the downcast soul (v.6) and a **sustaining night-song** (v.8) **coexist** — the inner being can hold a music in the dark *while still* in turmoil; the two are not mutually exclusive *(inferred: v.6's cast-down and v.8's night-song stand together without either erasing the other)*.

## What Psalm 42 says about the inner being
- **The soul's desire for God is *appetitive* (vv.1–2).** It operates as thirst — a felt, life-or-death craving driving toward a source, not a considered preference.
- **The inner being can *pour itself out*, fuelled by memory (vv.3–4).** Self-emptying in lament, sharpened by remembering former joy — the recalled gladness intensifies the present ache.
- **The inner being is *dialogical* — it can address itself (vv.5, 11).** An "I" stands apart from "my soul," interrogating its state and commanding it toward hope. The self is not monolithic; it can counsel and govern itself — honestly, naming the downcast state rather than denying it. The refrain makes this a *practice*.
- **"Why" runs both inward and upward (vv.5, 9).** The inner being questions itself *and* questions God — self-interrogation and God-interrogation together.
- **Grief compounds — deep calls to deep (v.7).** The overwhelming is recursive; depths summon depths, wave upon wave.
- **A song in the night can coexist with the downcast state (v.8).** The inner being holds a sustaining music in the dark *while still* in turmoil — the two are not exclusive.
