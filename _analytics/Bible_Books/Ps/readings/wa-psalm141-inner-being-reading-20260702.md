## The reading

**I. Praying God to guard the heart's inclination (vv.3–4).**
"Set a **guard**, O LORD, over my **mouth**; keep watch over the door of my lips! **Do not let my heart** (H3820, M47) **incline to any evil**, to busy myself with wicked deeds… and let me **not eat of their delicacies**!" (vv.3–4). The gem-finding: the self asks God to guard not only its *speech* but the **inclination of its *heart*** — "do not let my heart incline to any evil" — and to keep it from the *appetite* for the wicked's "delicacies" *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being, **distrusting its own capacity to stay upright, asks God to *restrain its interior*** — it recognises that the heart can *incline / lean* toward evil and the appetite be drawn to forbidden pleasures, and so it seeks *divine* help to guard the very *bent* of the self; self-suspicion turned into a prayer for keeping *(inferred: "do not let my heart incline… let me not eat of their delicacies" is the self acknowledging its own *pull* toward evil and enlisting God to guard against it — the inner being does not trust itself to hold the line but asks God to keep its *heart* and *appetite*; a mature humility about the self's own reliability, cf. Ps 19:13 "keep back your servant from presumptuous sins", 119:36 "incline my heart… and not to selfish gain")*.

**II. The eyes turned to God for refuge (v.8).**
"But my **eyes are toward you**, O God, my Lord; in you I **seek refuge** (H2620, M19); leave me not **defenseless**!" (v.8). The finding: amid the snares of the wicked, the self keeps its **eyes fixed on God** and takes refuge in him *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **directs its attention and dependence Godward** — "my eyes are toward you" enacts the turn from the surrounding threat to God as refuge; where the eyes go, the trust goes *(inferred: "my eyes are toward you… in you I seek refuge" re-attests the eye/refuge posture — the self's gaze deliberately held on God as its safety; cf. Ps 25:15, 123:1–2, 121:1)*.

## What Psalm 141 says about the inner being
- **The self asks God to *guard its heart's inclination* (vv.3–4).** Distrusting its own reliability, it prays God to restrain not only its speech but the *bent* of its heart toward evil and its appetite for forbidden pleasures — self-suspicion turned into a plea for keeping. A mature humility about the self's own capacity to stay upright.
- **The self *fixes its eyes on God for refuge* (v.8).** Amid snares, it directs its attention and dependence Godward — "my eyes are toward you"; where the gaze goes, the trust goes.
