## The reading

**I. The shining face as the means of restoration (vv.3, 7, 19).**
The refrain, three times: "**Restore us**, O God; let your **face shine** (H0215, *or*), that we may be **saved**!" (vv.3, 7, 19). The finding: the inner being's **restoration and salvation are tied to God's *face shining* upon it** *(stated)*. The operation: the self is not restored by its own effort but by the **turning of God's lit, favourable face toward it** — "let your face shine… *that we may be saved*" makes the face-shine the *means* of the saving *(inferred: the shining face is the cause, the salvation the effect; the light-of-the-face thread, Ps 4:6, 31:16, 67:1)*. The refrain's repetition makes it the psalm's one persistent request — everything hinges on the face *(inferred)*.

**II. The bread of tears — grief as the self's sustenance (v.5).**
"You have fed them with the **bread of tears** (H1832, M03, *dimah*) and given them tears to **drink in full measure**" (v.5). The gem-finding: the inner being's **grief becomes its food and drink** — fed *with tears*, drinking tears *in full measure* *(stated)*. The operation: sorrow so pervasive it is the self's **entire intake** — grief is not an interruption of nourishment but *is* the nourishment; the inner being *lives on* its tears *(inferred: "bread of tears… tears to drink in full measure" makes grief the whole diet, filling both bread and cup; cf. Ps 42:3 "my tears have been my food")*.

## What Psalm 80 says about the inner being
- **Restoration comes through God's *shining face* (vv.3, 7, 19).** The inner being is saved not by its own effort but by God turning his lit, favourable face toward it — the face-shine is the *means* of the restoration, and the psalm's one persistent plea.
- **Grief can become the self's *food and drink* (v.5).** Fed with the bread of tears, drinking tears in full measure — sorrow so pervasive it *is* the inner being's sustenance, its whole intake.
