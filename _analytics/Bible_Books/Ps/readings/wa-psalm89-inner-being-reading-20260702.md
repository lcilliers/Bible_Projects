## The reading

**I. Walking in the light of God's face (vv.15–16).**
"Blessed are the people who **know the festal shout** (*teruah* — the joyful acclamation), who **walk**, O LORD, in the **light** (H0216) of your **face** (H6440); who **exult** in your name all the day, and in your righteousness are **exalted**" (vv.15–16). The finding: the blessed inner being is one that **knows the festal shout** and **walks in the light of God's *face***, its whole day shaped by exultation in the name *(stated)*. The operation: the self's life is **lived *in* God's lit face** — the *light of the face* (the Aaronic-blessing thread, Ps 4:6, 31:16, 80:3) is not a momentary favour but the **medium the blessed inner being walks in**, "all the day" *(inferred: "walk… in the light of your face… exult all the day" makes the lit face a continuous atmosphere of life, not a passing gift)*.

**II. The short time, amid the unanswered "how long" (vv.46–48).**
"**How long**, O LORD? Will you hide yourself forever?… **Remember how short my time is!**… What man can live and never see death? Who can deliver his **soul** (H5315, M47) from the power of Sheol?" (vv.46–48). The finding: against God's seeming absence ("how long… hide yourself"), the inner being pleads **its own brevity** — "remember how short my time is" *(stated)*. The operation: the self **presses its *finitude* as an argument** — it cannot afford an unbounded wait, for its time is short and no one delivers his own soul from death *(inferred: "how long… remember how short my time is… who can deliver his soul from Sheol" makes the self's mortality the ground of the urgency; cf. Ps 39:4, 90:12)*.

## What Psalm 89 says about the inner being
- **The blessed self *walks in the light of God's face* (vv.15–16).** Knowing the festal shout, exulting in the name "all the day" — the lit face is the continuous atmosphere the blessed inner being lives in, not a passing favour.
- **The self presses its own *finitude* as an argument (vv.46–48).** Amid God's seeming absence, it pleads "how short my time is" — its mortality the ground of its urgency; it cannot afford an unbounded wait, and cannot deliver its own soul from death.
