# Psalm 104 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 104:1–35 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked; aheb-fix build). Complete chapter (14 vv. backfilled with the fixed full-text logic). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **Lens:** the finding is the inner-being **operation**, God-relation as arena (`feedback_lens_is_inner_being_process_not_god_relation`).
- **Scope honesty up front:** Psalm 104 is the great **creation hymn** — its body (light, waters, mountains, springs, birds, beasts, moon, sun, sea, Leviathan, vv.2–30) is *God's works*, not the inner being. The inner-being findings are three, and they **bookend and punctuate** the contemplation: the self **rousing itself to bless upon beholding creation** (vv.1, 35); creational **provision aimed at the heart** (v.15); and the **contemplation itself offered as worship** (v.34).

## The reading

**I. The self rousing itself to bless — upon beholding creation (vv.1, 35).**
"**Bless the LORD, O my soul** (H5315, the seat — M47)!… You are clothed with splendor and majesty" (v.1); and at the close, "**Bless the LORD, O my soul**! Praise the LORD!" (v.35). The finding — continuous with Ps 103 — the inner being **commands *itself* to bless God**, and here that self-address **frames a long contemplation of creation** *(stated)*. The operation: the self rouses itself to bless **as the response to *beholding God's works*** — the blessing is *evoked by contemplation*, the whole survey of creation gathered up into the self's "Bless the LORD, O my soul" *(inferred: the same self-address as 103 opens and closes 104, but here it brackets the creation-survey — the beholding of the works is what the self blesses God *for*; contemplation → blessing)*.

**II. Provision aimed at the heart (v.15).**
"…and **wine to gladden** (H8055, M04) the **heart** (H3824, M47) of man, oil to make his face shine and bread to **strengthen** man's heart" (v.15). The finding: God's material provision is **aimed at the inner being** — the wine *gladdens the heart*, the bread *strengthens the heart* *(stated)*. The operation: bodily provision **reaches through to the heart** — creation's gifts are not merely for the body but *gladden and sustain the inner being* *(inferred: "wine to gladden the heart… bread to strengthen the heart" makes the creational provision terminate in the heart's gladness and strength — the outer gift touches the inner life)*.

**III. The contemplation itself offered as worship (v.34).**
"May my **meditation** (H7879, M03) be pleasing to him, for I **rejoice** (H8055, M04) in the LORD" (v.34). The finding: the self **offers its *meditation*** to God and **rejoices** in him *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's *contemplation itself becomes an offering* — the very act of meditating on the works is presented *to God as pleasing*, and issues in rejoicing *(inferred: "may my meditation be pleasing… I rejoice in the LORD" turns the psalm's own act of beholding-creation into worship — the meditation is offered up, and the beholding gladdens the self; cf. Ps 19:14's "meditation of my heart… acceptable")*.

## What Psalm 104 says about the inner being
- **The self rouses itself to bless *upon beholding creation* (vv.1, 35).** The 103-style self-address here brackets a long contemplation — the survey of God's works is gathered into the self's "Bless the LORD, O my soul"; contemplation issues in blessing.
- **Creational provision *reaches the heart* (v.15).** Wine gladdens the heart, bread strengthens it — the outer gift terminates in the inner being's gladness and sustenance, not merely the body.
- **The *contemplation itself* is offered as worship (v.34).** The self presents its meditation to God as pleasing and rejoices in him — the act of beholding-creation becomes an inner offering.
