- **The chapter is multi-characteristic** (as expected for a poem): it works by **two ways** set in antithesis, and reads the inner being from several perspectives at once — **desire, cognition, moral condition, rootedness, and destiny**.

## The reading
Psalm 1 does not describe a deed so much as an **orientation of the inner being** — which way the whole person is turned — and it contrasts two.

**I. The blessed man — a desire that becomes meditation (vv.1–2).**
The blessed man is first defined by a **refusal**: he does *not* walk in the counsel of the wicked, nor stand in the way of sinners, nor sit in the seat of scoffers (v.1). The three verbs *walk → stand → sit*, paired with *counsel → way → seat*, read as a **deepening of association** *(inferred from the progression)* — from taking advice, to taking the road, to taking a seat among mockers. What he refuses is a settling-in with evil. Positively, his inner life is a **desire**: his *delight* (H2656, characteristic — M04) is in the law of the LORD (v.2). *Delight* is affection, not duty *(stated: the term is pleasure/delight)* — the law is what he *wants*. And that desire does not stay still: it becomes **meditation** (H1897, characteristic — M42), a low continual muttering *day and night* (v.2). So the inner sequence is **desire → rumination**: what the inner being loves, it dwells on *(inferred)*.

**II. The impact — rootedness, fruit, permanence (v.3).**
The effect of that inner orientation is given as a simile: he is *like a tree planted by streams of water.* Its lexical operations — it **yields** its fruit in season, its leaf does **not wither**, and *in all he does he prospers* — read as the **stability and fruitfulness** the delighting-meditating inner life produces *(inferred: the tree is the vehicle; the tenor is the man of vv.1–2)*. The inner being that is rooted in what it loves does not run dry; it bears in season and endures out of season.

**III. The other way — the wicked, rootless and driven (vv.4–5).**
*Not so the wicked* (v.4). Where the righteous is planted, the wicked (H7563, characteristic — M10) is *like chaff that the wind drives away* — the exact negation of rootedness: no soil, no water, no permanence, carried off by a breath *(stated: chaff/wind/drives)*. The consequence is inability to **stand**: the wicked will not stand in the judgment, nor sinners (H2400, characteristic — M10) in the congregation of the righteous (v.5). The inner condition that took its seat among scoffers has no standing when it counts *(inferred: the v.1 progression closes here)*.

**IV. The two destinies — knowing and perishing (v.6).**
The psalm resolves the two ways into two ends held by the LORD: he *knows* the way of the righteous (H6662, characteristic — M26), but the way of the wicked will *perish* (v.6). The righteous's way is **relationally known** — held, acknowledged *(stated: "the LORD knows")*; the wicked's way simply ends.

## What Psalm 1 says about the inner being
- The inner being is fundamentally **oriented** — turned one of two ways — and the psalm reads that orientation from several angles at once: **desire** (delight), **cognition** (meditation), **moral condition** (righteous / wicked–sinner–scoffer), **rootedness** (planted tree vs driven chaff), and **destiny** (endure/known vs perish).
- The generative core is **desire that becomes meditation**: the blessed inner life *loves* the law and therefore *dwells* on it continually — and that inner orientation, not any single act, is what roots the person into stability and fruitfulness.
- The wicked is drawn as the **privation** of this: not a rival root but *no* root — rootless, transient, unable to stand.
- **Blessedness** is thus presented as an inner state (a rooted, God-delighting, meditating orientation), not a reward for deeds.
