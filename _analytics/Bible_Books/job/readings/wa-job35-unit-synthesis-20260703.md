## JOB-35-A · Job 35:1–8 · [D, multi] — God above human merit; sin and righteousness touch only man

**The reading.** Elihu argues that human conduct neither enriches nor harms God: "if you are **righteous**, what do you **give to him**? Or what does he receive from your hand?" (v.7); "your **wickedness concerns a man like yourself**, and your **righteousness a son of man**" (v.8). The finding is **the transcendence of God above human merit — our good and evil affect *us* and our neighbours, not God's being** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self relocated: its righteousness is not a favour to God but a good to itself and others** *(inferred: Elihu corrects a subtle self-importance in Job's protest (that his righteousness *should* have obligated God) — "what do you give to him?" (v.7): God is not in Job's debt, because human virtue adds nothing to God's fullness; the finding is that righteousness is not a *transaction* with God (the very error of the friends' retribution, inverted) but a good whose effects land on *man* ("a son of man", v.8); this both humbles (you cannot put God in your debt) and clarifies (do good for its own sake and your neighbour's, not to obligate God); it prepares Job for the whirlwind, where God will be shown to owe him no explanation)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *think* (H2803, chashab), *right* (H6664, tsedeq), *sinned* (H2403/H2398), *higher* (H1361), *transgressions* (H6588), *righteous* (H6663, tsadaq), *give* (H5414), *wickedness* (H7562, resha), *righteousness* (H6666, tsedaqah) carried. *tsedaqah* (righteousness that concerns man, not God) is the reading's spine; correctly tagged. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Righteousness as a good to self and neighbour, not a claim on God** — "your righteousness concerns a son of man" (v.8): the finding that virtue is not a *transaction* that obligates God (the retribution error, from the other side) but a good whose effects land on people; the self cannot put God in its debt. The inner-being lesson: do good for its own sake and the neighbour's, not to obligate heaven — a correction of the subtle self-importance in expecting God to *owe* a return for one's righteousness. Prepares Job for a God who owes him no explanation (38).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *God above human merit (righteousness cannot obligate God)* (new — corrects the transaction-assumption); *righteousness as a good to self and neighbour* (new); *the humbling of expecting God to owe a return* (thread → the whirlwind).

## JOB-35-B · Job 35:9–16 · [D, multi, thread] — the empty cry; God who gives songs in the night

**The reading.** Elihu explains why some cries go unanswered: "because of the multitude of **oppressions people cry out**… but **none says, 'Where is God my Maker, who gives songs in the night**'" (vv.9–10). The problem is not God's deafness but the *kind* of cry: "surely God does **not hear an empty cry**" (v.13) — a cry of mere pain, not of *seeking God*. The finding is **the distinction between an empty cry of pain and a true cry that seeks God — and the beautiful image of God who gives "songs in the night"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self's cry qualified by whether it turns toward God or merely voices distress** *(inferred: Elihu's point is pointed (and partly aimed at Job) — people "cry out" under oppression (v.9) but do not *seek God* ("none says, Where is God my Maker", v.10), so the cry is "empty" (v.13), a reflex of pain rather than a turning to God; the finding distinguishes two interiors — the cry that merely *vents* and the cry that *seeks* — and only the latter is heard; and the luminous phrase "God my Maker, who gives **songs in the night**" (v.10) names a distinct inner-being gift: joy *in* darkness, a song God places in the sufferer's night (cf. Ps 42:8, 77:6); the finding is double — a rebuke (seek God, don't just vent) and a promise (God gives songs in the night to those who turn to him))*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *multitude* (H7230), *cry out* (H2199, za'aq), *gives* (H5414), *songs* (H2158, zemirah — "songs in the night"), *pride* (H1347, ga'on), *hear* (H8085), *case* (H1779, din), *note/knowledge* (H3045/H1847) carried. *za'aq* (cry out) and *zemirah* (songs in the night) are the reading's spine — the empty cry vs the God who gives night-songs; correctly tagged. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The empty cry vs the seeking cry** — "none says, 'Where is God my Maker'… God does not hear an empty cry" (vv.10,13): the finding that a cry which merely *vents* pain differs from one that *turns to God*, and only the latter is heard; a challenge to the interior to *seek* God in suffering, not just voice distress (partly aimed at Job, though Job *does* cry to God). (2) **Songs in the night** — "God my Maker, who gives songs in the night" (v.10): a distinct inner-being gift — joy placed *in* darkness, a song for the sufferer's night (cf. Ps 42:8, 77:6); the promise that God gives gladness within the darkness itself, not only after it.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the empty cry vs the seeking cry (venting vs turning to God)* (new — the qualified cry); *songs in the night (joy given within darkness)* (new — a gem, w/ Ps 42:8, 77:6); *seeking God in suffering, not merely voicing pain* (thread — the God-directed cry).
