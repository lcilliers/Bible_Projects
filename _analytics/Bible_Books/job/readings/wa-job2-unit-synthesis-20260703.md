## JOB-02-A · Job 2:1–10 · [D, multi] — integrity under bodily affliction; the tested self refusing to curse

**The reading.** The test escalates from possessions to the body: Satan strikes Job "with loathsome **sores** from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head" (v.7). God's own testimony is the key inner-being datum: "he still **holds fast his integrity**" (v.3) — integrity described as something *gripped*, held onto under force. The wife voices the tempting exit: "Do you still **hold fast your integrity**? **Curse God and die**" (v.9). Job's answer: "Shall we **receive good** from God, and shall we **not receive evil**?" (v.10); "in all this Job did **not sin with his lips**" (v.10). The finding is **integrity as an active grip, not a passive state** — twice "hold fast" (chazaq): the self *clenches* its integrity against pressure to release it *(stated)*. The operation is **the tested self receiving good and evil from one hand without cursing** — the reception of 1:21 extended to the body's own suffering, and against the intimate pressure of the wife *(inferred: "shall we receive good… and not evil?" is the reasoned core of Job's integrity — a refusal to make his relationship with God conditional on outcomes; the "for no reason" question (1:9) answered again, now at the extremity of physical torment; the note "with his lips" quietly leaves room for the coming inner storm — his lips do not sin, though his heart will soon cry out)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *hold fast/fast* (H2388, chazaq — twice, correctly `characteristic`, the gripping operation), *blameless/upright/fears/away* (the integrity cluster, God's testimony repeated), *life* (H5315, nephesh — "spare his life/soul"), *curse* (H1288, the euphemism again), *sin* (H2398), *considered* (v.3, tagged H3820, leb — "set your heart upon / considered") carried. The doubled *chazaq* (hold fast integrity) is the reading's spine and cleanly tagged. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **Integrity as a grip that can be pried at** — "hold fast" (chazaq) makes integrity an *active clenching* under force, not a static quality; the wife's counsel is precisely an invitation to *let go*. (2) **"With his lips"** — the careful qualifier (v.10): Job's *speech* does not sin here, which subtly distinguishes the outward from the inner and prepares for chapter 3, where the same self, still not "cursing God," will pour out anguish; integrity is not the absence of anguish but the refusal to let anguish become a curse.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *integrity as an active grip (chazaq — held fast under force)* (new — the tested self clenching); *receiving good and evil from one hand* (thread w/ 1:21); *the intimate pressure to release integrity (the wife)* (new); *the lips holding while the heart will cry* (new — outer/inner distinction).

## JOB-02-B · Job 2:11–13 · [D] — the ministry of silent presence; shared grief

**The reading.** The three friends come "to show him **sympathy** and **comfort** him" (v.11). Seeing him from a distance, unrecognisable, "they **raised their voices and wept**, and they tore their robes and sprinkled dust on their heads" (v.12); then "they **sat with him** on the ground **seven days and seven nights**, and **no one spoke a word** to him, for they saw that his suffering was very great" (v.13). The finding is **grief honoured by silent, shared presence** — before the friends open their mouths (and ruin it), they do the one thing that truly comforts: they *come, weep, and sit in silence* *(stated)*. The operation is **comfort as presence rather than speech** — the seven-day silence is the book's picture of true consolation, against which all the coming words will be measured and found wanting *(inferred: the friends are at their best here, wordless; the tragedy the book unfolds is that they cannot *stay* silent — the moment they explain his suffering they become "miserable comforters" (16:2); the finding is that shared grief needs presence, not answers, and that the impulse to explain suffering is where comfort goes wrong)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *heard* (H8085), *evil* (H7451 — "all this evil"), *comfort* (H5162, nacham — correctly `characteristic`), *wept* (H1058), *great* (H1431 — "very great" suffering) carried. *comfort* (H5162) is the reading's key term, correctly tagged; its later *failure* (the friends as false comforters) is set up here where it is briefly real. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Silence as the form of true comfort** — "no one spoke a word… for they saw his suffering was very great" (v.13): the finding that the magnitude of grief calls for *presence without explanation*; the friends' error is not coming but *speaking*. This frames the entire dialogue: the words that follow are the *undoing* of the comfort the silence achieved.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *comfort as silent shared presence* (new — the book's picture of true consolation); *the failure-in-waiting of explanatory comfort* (thread — sets up 16:2, "miserable comforters"); *grief that calls for presence, not answers* (new).
