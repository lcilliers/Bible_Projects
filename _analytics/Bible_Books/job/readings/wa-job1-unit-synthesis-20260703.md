## JOB-01-A · Job 1:1–5 · [D, multi, thread] — reverent integrity; protective, anxious piety

**The reading.** The book stakes its whole question on a portrait of an interior: Job was "**blameless and upright**, one who **feared God** and **turned away from evil**" (v.1). The finding is a **fourfold integrated inner posture** — wholeness (tam), straightness (yashar), reverent fear, and an active turning-from-evil; not four traits but one settled orientation of the self toward God *(stated)*. Then a quieter, telling detail: after his children's feasts Job "would rise early… and **offer burnt offerings**… For Job said, 'It may be that my children have **sinned**, and **cursed God in their hearts**'" (v.5). The operation is **piety that reaches into the unseen interior of others** — Job's reverence is not self-enclosed; it worries about the *hidden hearts* of his children and acts pre-emptively for them *(inferred: "cursed God in their hearts" shows Job takes the interior seriously as the real site of sin — a curse need not be spoken to be real; his intercession is anxious care for a region he cannot see; the same seriousness about the heart that the whole book will probe)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *blameless* (H8535, tam), *upright* (H3477, yashar), *feared* (H3373, yare), *turned away* (H5493, from evil), *sinned* (H2398), *cursed* (H1288 — here the euphemistic "bless"=curse) all correctly `characteristic`. The four integrity-terms are the reading's spine and cleanly tagged. Note *cursed* (H1288, barak) is the well-known euphemism ("cursed God in their hearts"); the lexical carries the surface "bless" lemma — a genuine sense-inversion to read from context, correctly handled. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **Integrity as a single fused posture, not a list** — tam/yashar/yare/turn-from-evil operate together; the finding is the *wholeness* itself (tam = complete), the opposite of a divided self. (2) **The heart as the true site of sin** — "cursed God *in their hearts*" (v.5): Job's operating assumption that the interior, not just the deed, is where reverence or its failure lives; the book's foundational anthropology, stated in passing.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *reverent integrity (the fused whole self: blameless/upright/God-fearing/evil-shunning)* (new — Job's defining interior; shared cornerstone *fear of God* w/ Pro/Ecc); *protective, anxious intercessory piety* (new); *the heart as the site of sin* (new — the book's anthropology).

## JOB-01-B · Job 1:6–12 · [D, multi, thread] — the question of disinterested piety

**The reading.** The scene shifts to the heavenly council, and the accuser poses the question that drives the entire book: "**Does Job fear God for no reason?**" (v.9) — is his reverence *free*, or bought by the "hedge" of blessing around him (v.10)? The finding is not about Satan but about the **motive-structure of the inner life** — the accusation is that all piety is self-interested, that no one fears God *for God* rather than for the goods God gives *(stated)*. The operation the book will test is **whether reverence can survive the removal of its rewards** — whether the fear of God is a *response to God himself* or merely a strategy for security *(inferred: "for no reason" (chinnam — for nothing, gratis) frames the whole trial as an experiment on the interior's authenticity; the question is whether Job's integrity (1:1) is a genuine orientation or a transaction; everything that follows is the answer being worked out in a real self under real loss)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** The integrity terms repeat (*blameless, upright, fears, away*) — God himself now speaks Job's character back (v.8), correctly tagged. *fear* (H3372/H3373, yare) and the *bless/curse* (H1288) pair carry the wager. The substrate is thin here (much is narrative dialogue) but the key terms are clean; the reading rests on the "for no reason" question, taken from the verse. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Piety's authenticity as the book's true subject** — the finding that the deepest question about an inner posture is *why* it is held: reverence held for reward is a different thing from reverence held for God, even if the outward acts are identical. This is the inner-being counterpart to Ecclesiastes' "fear God" conclusion — Job asks whether that fear can be *disinterested*, and stakes a life on the answer.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *disinterested piety (reverence held for God, not for reward)* (new — the book's engine-question); *the motive-structure of the inner life* (new — that the *why* of a posture is its truth).

## JOB-01-C · Job 1:13–22 · [D, multi] — worship in catastrophic loss; integrity holding

**The reading.** In one day four messengers strip Job of oxen, sheep, camels, servants, and all ten children (vv.13–19). His response is the chapter's gem: "Job **arose and tore his robe** and shaved his head and **fell on the ground and worshiped**. And he said, 'Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked shall I return. The **LORD gave**, and the **LORD has taken away**; **blessed be the name of the LORD**'" (vv.20–21). The finding is **grief and worship in a single act** — the torn robe (real mourning) and the falling-down worship are *not* alternatives; Job laments *and* blesses at once *(stated)*. The verdict: "in all this Job did **not sin** or charge God with wrong" (v.22). The operation is **worship that does not deny grief nor let grief become accusation** — the self holds sorrow and reverence together, refusing the two false exits (stoic suppression / bitter charge) *(inferred: "the LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away" receives both the gift and the loss from the same hand — a posture of radical reception (cf. Ecc's joy-as-gift, here its dark mirror: loss-as-from-God); the first answer to the "for no reason" question — Job blesses God stripped of every reward, so far without a transaction in view)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *gave* (H5414), *blessed* (H1288 — here the *genuine* "bless," not the euphemism), *sin* (H2398), *charge* (H5414 — "charge God with wrong") carried. The bless/curse verb (H1288) appears here in its true sense ("blessed be the name") — the same lemma that meant "curse" in v.5, now meaning bless; the context-sensitivity is the whole point (Job *blesses* where the accuser bet he would *curse*). Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **Mourning and worship as compatible, not sequential** — the torn robe and the worship are one movement (v.20); the finding that true reverence *includes* grief rather than requiring its suppression (against the friends' later demand that Job stop complaining). (2) **Radical reception** — "naked I came… naked shall I return": the self that owns nothing as its due can receive loss without accusation; a posture of held-openness that the book will stretch to breaking.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *worship in loss (grief and reverence fused)* (new — the prologue's climactic gem); *radical reception (naked I came)* (new); *reverence that refuses to become accusation* (new — sets up the book's tension).

The **bless/curse euphemism (H1288, barak)** is the key lexical subtlety — meaning "curse" at 1:5,11 and "bless" at 1:21 — read from context, not the lemma; a flag for the sense-inversion. Prose narrative, so fewer inner-being tags per verse than the poetic chapters.
