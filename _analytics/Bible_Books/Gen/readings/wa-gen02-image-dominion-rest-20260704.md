## GEN-02-image-dominion-rest · Genesis 1:26–2:3 · [divine interior → human vocation] — the image, the dominion that flows from it, the two blessings that are opposites, and the first holy thing

**The passage.** "Let us make man in our **image**… and let them have **dominion**" (1:26); "God created man in his own **image**… male and female" (1:27); "God **blessed** them… Be fruitful and **multiply**… and have **dominion**" (1:28); "I have **given** you every plant… for food" (1:29–30); "God saw everything… and behold, it was **very good**" (1:31); and on the seventh day God "**blessed**" it and "made it **holy**" (2:3).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **tselem (image) — the constituting operation.** The finding is the human made "in the **image** of God" (1:26–27, tselem ×2) *(stated)*. The operation is the **constitution of the human as image-bearer** — the interior's ground-fact: it exists as a *representation* of God *(inferred: the study weights tselem (non-T2) over demut/likeness (T2) — the operative note is *image* = the human set up to *stand for* God, the root from which everything the inner being later does derives its dignity and its accountability)*.
- **radah (dominion) — the delegated-rule operation, distinct from the lights' rule.** The finding is the human given "**dominion**" over the creatures (1:26,28) *(stated)*. The operation is rule — **but not the same verb as the lights' rule** (mashal, GEN-01 1:16–18): here it is *radah* (tread/subdue). The distinction is the finding — the lights *govern* (mashal) impersonally; the human *subdues/rules* (radah) as the image-bearer; so human dominion is a *representative* rule, exercised *because* it is image *(inferred: image → dominion is the interactive link — see the web; do not merge radah with mashal, they are different rules)*.
- **barak (bless) #1 — the creature-blessing of *increase*.** The finding is God **blessing** the humans "be fruitful and **multiply**" (1:28) *(stated)*. The operation is blessing-as-**empowerment-to-increase** — echoing the animal blessing (1:22) but now on the human; a *productive* blessing *(inferred)*.
- **ravah (multiply) — welded to the blessing.** The finding is "**multiply**, fill… subdue" (1:28) *(stated)*. The operation is the *content* of the increase-blessing — fruitfulness and filling *(inferred)*.
- **natan (give) — the provision operation, distinct from every later natan.** The finding is "I have **given** you every plant… for food" (1:29), and the same to the beasts (1:30) *(stated)*. The operation is **provision — food *given*, not seized**; the first diet is *granted* (plants), the creature receives its life-support as gift *(inferred: hold this natan apart — the *giving* here is pure provision; in Gen 3:6/3:12 the same verb becomes transmission-of-sin and blame-give; the contrast (God's food-gift vs the fall's poisoned gifts) is a thread the later passages will close)*.
- **nephesh (breath of life) — the life-vocabulary, a *different* sub-sense.** The finding is "everything that has the **breath of life** (nephesh)" (1:30) *(stated)*. The operation: the animating-life word again (as GEN-01) — **but a different sub-sense** ("soul: life", H5315H) than GEN-01's "soul: animal" (H5315K); the distinction is that here nephesh names *the breath-of-life itself* shared by man and beast, not the creature-kind *(inferred: keep the sub-senses apart)*.
- **tov + me'od (very good) — the appraisal *intensified and totalised*.** The finding is "God saw *everything*… it was **very good**" (1:31) *(stated)*. The operation is the evaluative act (GEN-01) now at its **only** superlative — "**very** good", and over the **whole** ("everything he had made"), not a single day's work; so the appraisal reaches a comprehensive climax distinct from the six partial verdicts *(inferred: this is *not* a seventh "good" — it is a different act, totalising and intensified; and it *restores* the rhythm broken on day 2 by summing all as very good)*.
- **barak (bless) #2 — the day-blessing of *cessation* (the same verb, opposite content).** The finding is "God **blessed** the seventh day" (2:3) *(stated)*. The operation is blessing — **but its object is a *day*, not a creature, and its content is the reverse of blessing #1**: the creature-blessing empowered *increase/production* (1:28); the day-blessing hallows *rest/cessation* (2:2–3). The same operation (barak) carries *opposite* content — increase vs stopping — and here it falls on **time** *(inferred: this is the passage's sharpest anti-lumping datum — two barak spans, and merging them would erase that God blesses *stopping* as much as he blessed *multiplying*)*.
- **qadash (holy) — the first sanctifying operation, on *time*.** The finding is God "made it **holy**" (2:3, qadash) *(stated)*. The operation is **sanctification — and it is the first "holy" in Scripture, applied not to a place or an object or a person but to a *day*** *(inferred: the inaugural holiness is *temporal* — a hallowed span of time; the sacred enters the world first as *rest*, not as a shrine — a seed for every later holiness)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **image → dominion.** The human's rule (radah) is issued *in the same breath* as the image (1:26): "let us make man in our image… *and let them have* dominion." Dominion is not a bare grant of power but the *exercise of image* — the creature rules *as* God's representative. Sever the two and dominion becomes mere domination; held together, it is representation.
2. **image → blessing → multiply → subdue → food-gift** (1:28–30): one continuous mandate-web — the image-bearer is *blessed*, and the blessing's content is *fill, subdue, rule, and be fed* — vocation and provision issued together.
3. **the sixfold "good" → "very good".** The appraisal-operation of GEN-01, broken on day 2, is here *resumed and totalised* — the whole pronounced "very good". The interactive datum: the withheld day-2 verdict is retroactively gathered into the summary "everything… very good".
4. **bless-increase (1:28) vs bless-cessation (2:3).** The two barak operations frame the passage: it opens blessing *productivity* (multiply, subdue) and closes blessing *rest* (the hallowed seventh) — the divine interior blesses both the working and the stopping, and makes only the *stopping* holy.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** image (tselem) as the human's constituting ground; dominion (radah) as *representative* rule, distinct from the lights' mashal; the increase-blessing (barak #1) vs the **cessation-blessing** (barak #2, opposite content, on a *day*); provision (natan) as pure gift (held apart from the fall's later natan); nephesh as "breath of life" (a distinct sub-sense); "very good" as the *only* superlative and totalising appraisal (not a seventh "good"); qadash — the **first holiness, applied to time/rest**.
- **Whose interior:** the **divine** interior still acts (blessing, appraising, sanctifying) — but it now *constitutes* the human interior (image) and issues its vocation (dominion, fruitfulness), and provides for it (food).
- **Threads opened/closed:** image (→ the image re-grounds the murder-prohibition, GEN-12 9:6); dominion (radah, distinct from mashal, GEN-01); the food-gift natan (→ contrasts the fall's natan, GEN-04 3:6,12); "very good" (← the sixfold good, GEN-01, day-2 gap resolved); holiness-of-time (→ the first sacred, seeding all later qadash).
- **Guarded against:** merging the two barak (increase vs cessation); merging radah with mashal; reading tselem and demut as one (demut is T2); treating "very good" as a seventh "good".
