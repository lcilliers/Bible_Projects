## JAC-19-esau-line · Genesis 36:1–43 · [the brother's separate nationhood] — Esau's generations, and the parting of the two too-great households

**The passage.** "These are the generations of Esau (Edom)" — his Canaanite wives, sons, and the chiefs and kings of Edom (36:1–43). The two non-T2 spans fall in the *parting*: "Esau took his wives, sons, daughters, and all the **members** (nephesh) of his household… and went to a land away from Jacob" (36:6); "their possessions were too great to dwell together; the land **could not** (yakhol) support them" (36:7).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **nephesh (members/persons) — the household *counted*, not its inner life (36:6).** "all the **members** (nephesh) of his household" *(stated)* — nephesh here in the *census/person* sense (*souls* = persons): the gem is the *thinness* — nephesh, elsewhere the *inner self / life* (2:7; 27:4 the soul-blessing; 35:18 the soul departing), is here a *head-count* of Esau's people; the *genealogical, external* use, not an interior operation. Noted *distinctly for its register* — a bounding of the term to *persons*, not soul-depth *(inferred: nephesh = the household reckoned as persons — the external census-sense)*.
- **yakhol (could not) — the land *unable* to hold both, and the *amicable parting* (36:7).** "their possessions were too great… the land **could not** (yakhol) support them because of their livestock" *(stated)* — yakhol, *be able*: the gem is the *echo* — the *same* "the land could not support them dwelling together" that parted *Abraham and Lot* (13:6); here it parts *Esau and Jacob* — the *reconciled* brothers (JAC-16) *settle apart*, Esau to Seir/Edom, *not in enmity but by the land's limit*; the interior-context is *separation without strife* — the peace of JAC-16 *holding* in a quiet, prosperous parting *(inferred: yakhol = the land's inability that parts the two great households peaceably)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **the peaceful separation (nephesh 36:6 → yakhol 36:7):** Esau *gathers his whole household* and *moves away from Jacob* because *the land cannot hold both* — the two brothers, *reconciled* (JAC-16) and *both prospered*, *part without conflict*; the genealogy that follows records Esau's *separate nationhood* (Edom, its chiefs and kings) — the outcast-brother's promise of a *nation* (25:23; 27:39–40) *kept*, as Ishmael's was (ABR-18).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** nephesh (the *census/person* sense — the household counted, *not* soul-depth — bounded distinctly against the soul-uses of 27:4; 35:18); yakhol (the land's *inability* that parts the brothers *peaceably* — the Abraham/Lot echo).
- **Whose interior:** essentially *none in depth* — this is a **genealogical** unit; the only inner-being-adjacent note is the *peaceful parting* (yakhol) of the reconciled brothers. Esau/Edom's *nationhood* is *recorded*, not *analysed* — thin yield by design.
- **Threads:** the land *too small for both* (yakhol 36:7 ← Abraham & Lot 13:6 — the recurring *separation-by-prosperity*, now *without strife*); the reconciled brothers *part* (← JAC-16, the wary distance after the embrace); the outcast-brother's *nation* kept (Esau/Edom ← the oracle 25:23; the lesser blessing 27:39–40 — parallels *Ishmael's twelve princes*, ABR-18); nephesh as *persons* (a genealogical bounding of the soul-word).
- **Guarded against:** *manufacturing* inner-being operations out of a genealogy (Gen 36 is proper-noun lists — chiefs, kings, clans — with *no* flagged inner-being spans beyond the two parting-verses; I resisted padding false distinctions, per the method's "never over-read genealogies"); reading soul-depth into the *census* nephesh (36:6 is a head-count, distinct from the soul-uses); importing Edom's *later* enmity with Israel (the prophets) over the *peaceable parting* Gen 36 actually narrates; treating the thinness as a gap to fill rather than *the datum itself* (a nation *recorded*, an interior *not in view*).
