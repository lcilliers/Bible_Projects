## JOS-16-jacob-death-burial · Genesis 49:29–50:14 · [the pilgrim gathered; the body returned to the land] — Joseph's weeping, Egypt's mourning, and the burial with the fathers

**The passage.** Jacob — "I am to be **gathered** to my people; bury me with my fathers" (49:29); he drew up his feet, breathed his last, "**gathered** to his people" (49:33); Joseph fell on his father's face and **wept** (bakhah) (50:1); the embalming; the Egyptians **wept** (bakhah) seventy days (50:3); "if I have found **favor** (chen)…" when the days of **weeping** (bekhit) were past (50:4); the oath to bury in Canaan (50:5); the great procession; at Atad a **mourning** (evel), a **lamentation** (misped), they **lamented** (saphad) seven days (50:10); the Canaanites saw the **mourning** (evel) (50:11); buried in Machpelah (50:13); Joseph returned to Egypt (50:14).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **(49:29,33 — "gathered to my people"; no non-T2 span.)** Flagged: Jacob's death carries no flagged span, but it is the *close of the patriarch's life*: *"gathered to his people"* — the **Abraham-death formula** (← 25:8; 35:29) — the *pilgrim's completed life*, dying *at peace* having blessed all his sons. Read as context *(no-span context; the peaceful, completed death)*.
- **bakhah (wept) — Joseph's *personal grief* (50:1).** "Joseph fell on his father's face and **wept** (bakhah) over him and kissed him" *(stated)* — bakhah, *weep*: the gem is the *son's raw grief* — *falling on the dead father's face*, weeping and kissing; the interior's *love and loss* poured out (the *most intimate* of Joseph's many weepings) *(inferred: bakhah = Joseph's personal grief over his father)*.
- **bakhah (wept) — Egypt's *formal mourning* (50:3).** *bakhah distinct from 50:1:* "the Egyptians **wept** (bakhah) for him seventy days" *(stated)* — where 50:1 was the *son's* grief, 50:3 is the *nation's formal mourning* — *seventy days* (near-royal honour); the interior-context of *Egypt honouring Jacob for Joseph's sake* — a whole nation's grief for a shepherd *(inferred: bakhah = Egypt's formal mourning — the nation's honour)*.
- **chen (favor) + bekhit (weeping) — the *deferential request* (50:4).** "when the days of **weeping** (bekhit) were past: 'if I have found **favor** (chen)…'" *(stated)*. **bekhit** — the *mourning-period*; **chen** — *favour*: the gem is that *even as ruler*, Joseph *asks Pharaoh's leave deferentially* to bury his father — the interior's *humility and filial duty* (he will not presume, though he governs Egypt) *(inferred: chen/bekhit = the deferential request after the mourning — humility in filial duty)*.
- **evel (mourning) + misped (lamentation) + saphad (lamented) — the *great lamentation* (50:10).** "at Atad they **lamented** (saphad) with a great **lamentation** (misped)… he made a **mourning** (evel) seven days" *(stated)*. **evel** — *mourning*; **misped** — *wailing/lament*; **saphad** — *mourn/wail*: the gem is the *fullness of the grief-rite* — a *great and grievous lamentation* — the register of *deep, formal mourning* (the son honouring the father with a full rite) *(inferred: evel/misped/saphad = the great lamentation — the full grief-rite for the father)*.
- **evel (mourning) — the *mourning observed* (50:11).** *evel distinct from 50:10:* "the Canaanites saw the **mourning** (evel): 'a grievous mourning by the Egyptians'" *(stated)* — the gem is the *witnessed grief* — even the *Canaanites mark it* (naming the place Abel-mizraim); the interior-context of *a grief so great it names a place* *(inferred: evel = the mourning observed and memorialised by the land)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **the pilgrim gathered (49:29,33):** Jacob, having blessed all his sons, *breathes his last and is gathered to his people* — the *Abraham-death formula*; the *completed pilgrim-life* at peace.
2. **the son's grief and the nation's honour (bakhah 50:1 → bakhah 50:3 → chen/bekhit 50:4):** Joseph *weeps on his father's face*; *Egypt mourns seventy days*; and Joseph, *even as ruler*, *deferentially asks leave* to keep the burial-oath.
3. **the great lamentation and the burial in the land (evel/misped/saphad 50:10 → evel 50:11 → 50:13):** a *great grief-rite* at Atad (the Canaanites marking it), and Jacob *buried in Machpelah with the fathers* — the *body returned to the promised land* (the faith-thread: Egypt is not home, the land is).
4. **the return (50:14):** Joseph and the brothers *return to Egypt* — the oath *kept*, the father *at rest with his fathers*.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** the *peaceful death* — "gathered to his people" (no-span; the Abraham-formula, the completed pilgrim); **bakhah ×2 (Joseph's *personal grief* / Egypt's *formal mourning* — the intimate vs the national)**; chen/bekhit (the *deferential request* — humility in filial duty even as ruler); evel/misped/saphad (the *great lamentation* — the full grief-rite); evel (the mourning *observed and memorialised* by the Canaanites).
- **Whose interior (multi-interior):** **Jacob** — the *completed, peaceful death* (gathered, having blessed all); **Joseph** — *raw personal grief* (weeping on his father's face) and *humble filial devotion* (keeping the oath, asking leave); **Egypt** — *national honour* (seventy days' mourning, the great procession); the **Canaanites** — *marking* the grief. The body *returned to the land* is the faith-note.
- **Threads:** the **Abraham-death formula** ("gathered to his people" 49:33 ← 25:8 Abraham; 35:29 Isaac — the pilgrim-life completed); **Joseph's weeping** (bakhah 50:1 — the *most intimate*, ← the arc of his many weepings 42:24; 43:30; 45:14; 46:29 — over the *father* now); the **burial-oath kept** (50:5,13 ← 47:29 — the *body returned to Canaan*, Egypt not home); the **pull to the land** (Machpelah ← Abraham's purchase 23; the faith that the *land* is home though the family dwells in Egypt → the Exodus); Egypt's *honour* (the great procession — Joseph's standing).
- **Guarded against:** over-reading the Machpelah genealogy-detail (49:30–32) into operations (scene-context — the burial-place); lumping the two bakhah (personal / national) or two evel (the rite / the observed); missing the *faith-thread* (the body returns to the *land* — Egypt is temporary); reading Egypt's mourning as mere protocol when it *honours Jacob for Joseph's sake* (a nation grieving a shepherd); importing resurrection-theology over the *"gathered to his people"* the text gives (the peaceful, completed death).
