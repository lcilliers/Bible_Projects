## ABR-11-abimelech-integrity · Genesis 20:1–18 · [integrity of heart, and the fear wrongly assumed absent] — the pagan's clean heart vindicated, God restraining sin, and the patriarch's exposed excuse

**The passage.** "Will you slay an **innocent** (tsaddiq) people?… in the **integrity** (tom) of my **heart** (levav) and the **innocence** (niqqavon) of my hands" (20:4–5); "I **know** (yada) that you did this in the **integrity** (tom) of your **heart** (levav), and I kept you from **sinning** (chata)" (20:6); "he will pray… you shall **live** (chayah); but if not… **know** (yada) that you shall die" (20:7); "Abimelech **called** (qara) his servants… very **afraid** (yare)" (20:8); "how have I **sinned** (chata)… you have brought great **sin** (chataah)… he **called** (qara) Abraham" (20:9); "there is no **fear** (yirah) of God in this place" (20:11); "this is the **kindness** (chesed) you must do me" (20:13); "he **gave** (natan) sheep and oxen" (20:14); "dwell where it **pleases** (tov) you" (20:15); "I have **given** (natan) a thousand pieces of silver… a vindication" (20:16); "the LORD had **closed** (atsar) all the wombs" (20:18).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **tsaddiq (innocent) — the pagan claims *righteous innocence* (20:4).** The finding is "will you slay an **innocent** (tsaddiq) people?" *(stated)*. The gem is that *tsaddiq* — the *righteous* (← 6:9 Noah; 18:23 the intercession) — is here claimed by a **pagan king**, *rightly* (he took Sarah unknowingly): the interior's *genuine innocence*, about to be *vindicated by God* *(inferred)*.
- **tom + levav + niqqavon — the *integrity of heart* (20:5).** The finding is "in the **integrity** (tom) of my **heart** (levav) and the **innocence** (niqqavon) of my hands" *(stated)*. The gem is the **first "integrity" (tom)** — *wholeness/blamelessness* (kin to tamim, 6:9/17:1) — of the **heart** (levav, the fuller form of lev): Abimelech acted in *sincerity of heart* and *clean hands* (niqqavon). The operation is *integrity as the heart's moral wholeness* — sincerity of intention *(inferred: tom = integrity; levav = the heart that holds it)*.
- **yada (know) #1 + tom + levav + chata — God *vindicates* the heart and *restrains* sin (20:6).** The finding is "I **know** (yada) that you did this in the **integrity** (tom) of your **heart** (levav), and I kept you from **sinning** (chata) against me" *(stated)*. Two gems: **yada** — God *knows/confirms* the heart's integrity (as he read Sarah's laugh, 18:13; the electing/investigating yada, 18:19,21) — God *vindicates* the pagan's inner sincerity; and **chata** — God "**kept** you from **sinning**": the divine interior *actively restrains* a man from sin — **preventing grace**, guarding even a pagan from wrong *(inferred: God's yada vindicates; God's restraint keeps from chata)*.
- **yada (know) #2 + chayah (live) — the warning (20:7).** The finding is "he will pray for you, and you shall **live** (chayah); but if you do not restore her, **know** (yada) that you shall surely die" *(stated)*. The operations: **yada** — *warning-knowledge* (know the consequence); **chayah** — *life contingent* on restoring Sarah and Abraham's prayer *(inferred: distinct yada — the sobering certainty, vs 20:6 the vindicating knowledge)*.
- **qara (called) + yare (afraid) — the court's *right fear* (20:8).** The finding is "Abimelech **called** (qara) all his servants… and the men were very **afraid** (yare)" *(stated)*. The gem is that the *pagan court* **fears** (yare) rightly on hearing God's warning — a fear *more responsive than Abraham assumed* (see yirah, 20:11) *(inferred: qara = summons; yare = the court's genuine dread of God)*.
- **chata + chataah + qara — the *pagan rebukes the patriarch* (20:9).** The finding is Abimelech's "how have I **sinned** (chata) against you, that you have brought a great **sin** (chataah) on me and my kingdom?" and he "**called** (qara) Abraham" *(stated)*. The gem is the *reversal*: the **pagan king rebukes the patriarch** for *nearly making him sin* — the moral roles inverted, Abimelech *more concerned about sin* than Abraham; chata (the verb) / chataah (the noun) both name the guilt Abraham's deception nearly incurred *(inferred: the wronged pagan's rebuke; sin-verb and sin-noun distinguished)*.
- **yirah (fear) — the fear-of-God *wrongly assumed absent* (20:11).** The finding is Abraham's "I thought, there is no **fear** (yirah) of God at all in this place, and they will kill me" *(stated)*. The gem is that Abraham *justifies his deception* by assuming **no *yirah* (fear of God)** in the place — **and he was *wrong*** (Abimelech had integrity, the court feared God, 20:8): the interior's *misjudgment of others' godliness*; the fear-lie of ABR-02 repeated, and here *exposed as groundless* *(inferred: yirah = the fear-of-God Abraham falsely presumed absent)*.
- **chesed (kindness) — the *compromised* loyalty-arrangement (20:13).** The finding is "this is the **kindness** (chesed) you must do me: at every place, say of me, He is my brother" *(stated)*. The gem is *chesed* — the *steadfast-love* word (← 19:19, first, God's saving chesed) — here in a *lesser, compromised* use: the *loyalty* Abraham asked of Sarah to *maintain the deception*; the great word *bent* to a fearful arrangement *(inferred: distinct chesed — God's saving loyalty vs the deception-pact)*.
- **natan (gave) + tov (pleases) + natan (given) — the wronged pagan's *generosity* (20:14–16).** The finding is Abimelech "**gave** (natan) sheep and oxen… restored Sarah" (20:14); "dwell where it **pleases** (tov) you" (20:15); "I have **given** (natan) your brother a thousand pieces of silver… a **vindication**" (20:16) *(stated)*. The operations: **natan #1** — *restitution*; **tov** — the generous "dwell where it is good in your eyes" (freedom of the land); **natan #2** — the silver *vindicating* Sarah's honour. The *wronged party* responds with *generosity* *(inferred: two natan — restitution and vindication)*.
- **atsar (closed) — the wombs *restrained*, the barrenness-word again (20:18).** The finding is "the LORD had **closed** (atsar) all the wombs of the house of Abimelech, because of Sarah" *(stated)*. The gem is *atsar* — the *same word* as **Sarai's own closed womb** (16:2, ABR-06, "the LORD has *restrained* me") — now the *protective judgment* on Abimelech's house (healed when Abraham prays, 20:17): God's *restraint of wombs*, there a trial, here a guard *(inferred: atsar — barrenness as trial (16:2) vs as protective judgment (20:18))*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **the pagan's integrity vindicated, sin restrained (tsaddiq/tom/levav/yada/chata 20:4–6):** Abimelech claims *innocence* and *integrity of heart*; God *confirms* it (yada) and reveals he *kept him from sinning* (chata) — the pagan's genuine sincerity *vindicated*, and God's *restraining grace* disclosed; set against Abraham's deception.
2. **the roles reversed (chata/chataah 20:9, yirah 20:11):** the *pagan rebukes the patriarch* for nearly bringing *sin* on him; and Abraham's excuse — "*no fear of God* in this place" — is *exposed as false* (the court *feared*, yare 20:8; the king had *integrity*): the interior *misjudged* others' godliness, and the fear-lie (ABR-02) is shown *groundless*.
3. **the great word compromised (chesed 20:13):** the *steadfast-love* word (God's saving chesed, 19:19) is *bent* to the deception-pact — faith's vocabulary pressed into a fearful arrangement.
4. **generosity and restraint (natan/tov 20:14–16; atsar 20:18):** the *wronged* pagan responds with *gifts, land, and vindication*; and God's *closing of the wombs* (atsar, the barrenness-word of 16:2) is *protective*, healed by Abraham's *prayer* — the patriarch, though at fault, still the *prophet* whose prayer restores.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** tsaddiq — the *pagan's* claimed innocence, *vindicated*; **tom — the *first* "integrity"**, of the *heart* (levav); **yada — God *vindicating* the heart (20:6)** vs the *warning*-knowledge (20:7); **chata — God *restraining* sin (20:6)** vs the *sin* Abraham nearly caused (20:9); yare — the court's *right fear* vs **yirah — the fear-of-God Abraham *wrongly assumed absent*** (the fear-lie exposed); **chesed — the great word *compromised*** into the deception-pact; natan (restitution / vindication); tov (the generous "as it pleases you"); **atsar — the barrenness-word (← 16:2), here *protective judgment***.
- **Whose interior:** the **pagan** interior *upright and God-fearing* (Abimelech, his court) — *more* so than the deceiving patriarch; the **divine** interior *vindicating integrity and restraining sin*; and the **human** interior *misjudging others' godliness* (Abraham's exposed excuse).
- **Threads:** the fear-lie (← ABR-02 Egypt; here *exposed* as a false assumption); tom/tamim (← 6:9/17:1 wholeness); yada (God knowing the heart, ← 18:13); chesed (← 19:19, now compromised); atsar (← 16:2 Sarai's womb, now Abimelech's house); Abraham as *prophet* whose prayer heals (20:7,17).
- **Guarded against:** merging the two yada (vindicating vs warning), two chata (restrained vs caused), two natan, or the two chesed (God's saving love vs the deception-pact); flattening tom into generic "integrity" without seeing it is the *heart's* wholeness; missing that the *pagan* is the upright one here; softening that Abraham's yirah-assumption was *wrong*; importing a harsh verdict on Abraham the text withholds (it *shows* the exposure and still calls him *prophet*).
