## GEN-04-fall-temptation · Genesis 3:1–13 · [human interior, undone] — the crafty bait, the stacked desire, the knowing that inverts, the birth of hiding, and the gift turned alibi

**The passage.** The serpent, "more **crafty** than any **beast**" (3:1), reframes God's word: "God **knows** that when you eat… you will be like God, **knowing** good and evil" (3:5). The woman sees the tree "a **delight** to the eyes… to be **desired** to make one **wise**"; she takes, and **gives** to her husband (3:6). "The eyes of both were opened, and they **knew** they were naked" (3:7). They **heard** God walking and hid (3:8); God **called** to the man (3:9); "I **heard**… and I was **afraid**… and I hid" (3:10); "the woman whom you **gave** to be with me, she **gave** me" (3:12).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **chayyah (beast) — the serpent placed among the *made* living things (3:1).** The finding is the serpent "more crafty than any **beast** (chayyah) of the field" *(stated)*. The operation is categorical: the tempter is *one of the living creatures God made* (the chayyah of 1:24–25), not an outside power — the subversion arises *from within* the good creation *(inferred)*.
- **arum (crafty) — prudence bent to subvert (3:1).** The finding is the serpent **crafty** (arum) *(stated)*. The gloss is neutral "prudent", but the verse-bounded operation is **intelligence operating as cunning** *(inferred: hold the lexical resonance for the web — the *arum* one will market *sakal*, "to make wise", 3:6: the prudent creature dangles prudence)*.
- **yada (know) #1 — God's knowing, weaponised as withholding (3:5).** The finding is "God **knows**… you will be like God, knowing good and evil" *(stated)*. The operation is **suspicion-seeding**: God's knowledge reframed as *self-interested withholding* — the first move against the interior is to make God's knowing look like God's grudging *(inferred)*.
- **tov (good) — the moral good as the promised content (3:5).** The finding is "knowing **good** and evil" *(stated)*. The operation names the *content* of the god-like knowing dangled — moral knowledge as the lure *(inferred)*.
- **the desire-stack: taavah / chamad / chamudah / sakal (3:6) — three faculties firing on one object.** The finding is the tree "a **delight** (taavah) to the eyes… **desired** (chamad) to make one **wise** (sakal)", a **desirable thing** (chamudah) *(stated)*. The operations, kept apart: **taavah (delight/desire)** = *appetite* (delight to the eyes); **chamad (desire/covet)** = *coveting* — and this is the **same chamad that at 2:9 named the trees' God-given desirability**; here it tips into *grasping* (design-desirability → disordered coveting, the thread from GEN-03 now closing); **sakal (make wise)** = *aspiration* — the reach to be wise; **chamudah** = the object seen *as* desirable. The gem is that the transgression is not one "desire" but a *stack* — appetite feeding coveting feeding aspiration *(inferred: do not lump into "desire"; the interior act is a sequence of distinct faculties handing off)*.
- **natan (give) #1 — giving as transmission across persons (3:6).** The finding is "she **gave** (natan) some to her husband" *(stated)*. The operation is **giving that *spreads* the act** — the transgression transmitted to another; giving as drawing-in *(inferred: hold apart from natan #2 at 3:12, and from the *provision*-natan of GEN-02 2:29)*.
- **yada (know) #2 — the knowing that *inverts* (3:7).** The finding is "the eyes of both were opened, and they **knew** (yada) they were naked" *(stated)*. The operation is knowing predicated now of *the couple*, and its content is **shame, not deity** — the *same verb* as 3:5, *antithetical* operation: the serpent promised knowing that makes one *like God* (3:5); what arrives is knowing one is *naked* (3:7). The gem is **self-defeating fulfilment** — the promise comes true and ruins; the god-like knowing turns out to be shame-knowing. *This distinction lives only in refusing to merge the two yada* *(inferred)*.
- **shama (hear) #1 — hearing God that triggers flight (3:8).** The finding is "they **heard** (shama) the sound of the LORD… and hid" *(stated)*. The operation is **perception of God's presence now producing concealment** — hearing that sends the self *away* *(inferred)*.
- **qara (call) — God's *pursuing* call (3:9).** The finding is "the LORD God **called** (qara) to the man, 'Where are you?'" *(stated)*. The operation is naming's cousin turned *pursuit* — God *moves toward* the hidden self; the call is not to inform but to *seek* *(inferred: distinct from the naming-qara of GEN-01/03 — this is qara "call *to*", H7121G, a summons across the hiding)*.
- **shama (hear) #2 — hearing *confessed*, welded to fear (3:10).** The finding is "I **heard** (shama) the sound of you… and I was afraid" *(stated)*. The operation is the *same* hearing as 3:8, now *avowed* and *fused with dread* — the distinction from 3:8 is that here hearing is confessed and named as the trigger of fear *(inferred)*.
- **yare (afraid) — dread, not reverence (3:10).** The finding is "I was **afraid** (yare)" *(stated)*. The gloss is "to fear: revere", but the verse-bounded operation is **terror-and-concealment**, not reverence — the affect that *produces hiding*. The gem is that the *gloss would mislead*: the operation is read off the verse, and the verse builds fright *(inferred: the first human fear is dread of God born of guilt, not worship)*.
- **natan (give) #2 — the gift turned alibi, aimed at the Giver (3:12).** The finding is "the woman whom you **gave** (natan) to be with me, she **gave** (natan) me" *(stated)*. The operation is giving **repurposed as blame** — and aimed *past* the woman at **God** ("the woman *you gave*"). The *same verb* as 3:6 runs first as transmission, then as blame-deflection that *indicts the Giver* *(inferred: God's good gift (the woman, GEN-03 2:22) becomes the sinner's excuse; merge the two natan and the whole reversal vanishes)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **arum → sakal: the bait matches the baiter.** The *crafty* (arum) serpent offers *wisdom* (sakal, "to make wise") — one prudence weaponising another. Lumping the two as "prudence" would erase that the lure is keyed to the very faculty the tempter embodies.
2. **yada(3:5) → yada(3:7): promise inverts into ruin.** God-like knowing promised (3:5) → shame-knowing delivered (3:7). The passage's central gem, alive only if the two yada are kept apart.
3. **perception → desire-stack → hand → transmission (3:6):** seeing-good → taavah → chamad → sakal → *took* → *gave* (natan): a single interior sequence, faculties handing off, ending in the act *crossing to another person*.
4. **shama(3:8) → qara(3:9) → shama(3:10) + yare: the birth of hiding-from-God.** Hearing God now triggers flight (3:8); God's call *pursues* (3:9); the self's hearing is confessed and *fused with fear* (3:10) — a new operation-compound that did not exist before the act: *God-presence → dread → concealment*.
5. **natan(3:6) → natan(3:12): gift becomes alibi.** Giving runs first as transmission (drawing the man in), then as blame that indicts God — the good gift turned excuse.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** the tempter as an insider *beast* (chayyah); arum as cunning; God's knowing weaponised (yada 3:5) vs the shame-knowing that inverts it (yada 3:7); the *desire-stack* (taavah/chamad/sakal/chamudah, not one "desire"); natan as transmission (3:6) vs natan as blame-indicting-the-Giver (3:12); shama that flees (3:8) vs shama confessed-and-feared (3:10); qara as *pursuing* call; yare as *dread* (the gloss "revere" refused).
- **Whose interior:** the **human** interior *undone* — its desire disordered, its knowing inverted to shame, its hearing turned to hiding, its speech turned to blame.
- **Threads closed/opened:** chamad (← 2:9 design-desirability, now grasping); the food-gift natan (← 2:29 provision) contrasted by the blame-natan (3:12); no-shame (← 2:25) destroyed by the shame-knowing (3:7); one-flesh (← 2:24) strained by the blame that turns on the woman *and* God (3:12); → the sentence and expulsion (GEN-05, 3:14–24).
- **Guarded against:** lumping the two yada (would bury the promise-inversion gem); lumping the two natan (would bury gift→alibi); reading yare as "reverence" (the verse builds dread); collapsing the desire-stack into one "desire"; importing later doctrine of the fall — every operation here is read off the Hebrew spans in these verses.
