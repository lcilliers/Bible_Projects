## GEN-06-cain-abel · Genesis 4:1–16 · [the interior that will not rule itself] — the knowing that begets, the anger that falls, the crouching sin to be mastered, the keeper refused, and the man himself cursed

**The passage.** "Adam **knew** (yada) Eve… and she bore Cain" (4:1); Cain a **worker** (avad) of the ground (4:2); the LORD had regard for Abel's offering but not Cain's, "so Cain was very **angry** (charah), and his face **fell** (naphal)" (4:5); God asks "why are you **angry** (charah)…? If you **do well** (yatav)… **sin** (chattat) is crouching… its **desire** (teshuqah) is for you, but you must **rule** (mashal) over it" (4:6–7); Cain kills Abel (4:8); "I do not **know** (yada); am I my brother's **keeper** (shamar)?" (4:9); "you are **cursed** (arar) from the ground" (4:11), which "shall no longer **yield** (natan) its **strength** (koach)" (4:12); "my **punishment** (avon) is greater than I can bear" (4:13).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **yada (know) #1 here — the conjugal knowing that begets life (4:1).** The finding is "Adam **knew** (yada) Eve… and she conceived and bore Cain" *(stated)*. The operation is knowing as **intimate union that begets** — and it is the *same verb* as the fall's shame-knowing (3:7) and God's confirmation (3:22): the first yada *outside* Eden is not shame but the knowing that *produces life* *(inferred: the knowing-word runs from shame (3:7) to begetting (4:1) to evasion (4:9) — keep the three apart)*.
- **avad (worker) #4 in the arc — Cain works the ground (4:2).** The finding is "Cain a **worker** (avad) of the ground" *(stated)*. The operation is the ground-vocation now Cain's — the cursed-ground toil (3:23) carried by the first son; against Abel the shepherd *(inferred: the avad arc — absent 2:5 → good 2:15 → cursed 3:23 → Cain's 4:2)*.
- **charah (angry) #1 — the interior incensed (4:5).** The finding is "Cain was very **angry** (charah)" at the unregarded offering *(stated)*. The operation is anger *kindling within* — the interior heating at not being preferred *(inferred)*.
- **naphal (fell) — the anger made visible in the face (4:5).** The finding is "his face **fell** (naphal)" *(stated)*. The operation is the *outward manifestation* of the inward anger — the countenance collapses; the interior is legible on the face *(inferred: charah is the inner heat, naphal its visible sign — two operations, inner and outer)*.
- **charah (angry) #2 — God *names* the anger (4:6).** The finding is God asking "why are you **angry** (charah), and why has your face fallen?" *(stated)*. The operation is the anger now *surfaced and questioned by God* — an invitation to self-examination before the anger acts; distinct from 4:5 (the anger itself) *(inferred)*.
- **yatav (do well) — the still-open choice (4:7).** The finding is "if you **do well** (yatav), will you not be accepted?" *(stated)*. The operation is the interior's choice held *open* — acceptance is still available; Cain is *not* yet determined *(inferred)*.
- **chattat (sin) — sin personified as a crouching predator (4:7).** The finding is "**sin** (chattat) is crouching at the door" *(stated)*. The operation is the **first "sin" in Scripture, personified** as a beast crouching to spring — sin as an *external-feeling power* lying in wait at the interior's threshold *(inferred)*.
- **teshuqah + mashal — the desire-toward / rule-over summons (4:7), paying off 3:16.** The finding is "its **desire** (teshuqah) is for you, but you must **rule** (mashal) over it" *(stated)*. The operations: **teshuqah** — sin's *desire to possess* Cain; **mashal** — Cain's *summons to master* it. The gem is that this is the **exact pairing of 3:16** (the woman's desire, the husband's rule) — now transferred to *sin toward Cain*: sin desires to have him as the wife desires the husband, and he is called to *govern* it. But where 3:16 was a *sentence*, 4:7 is a *summons Cain can heed or refuse* — and he refuses (the murder follows): the interior *called* to rule its craving *fails* to *(inferred: mashal here is the same govern-verb as 3:16 and the lights' rule — the moral question is whether the self will exercise it; do not merge this teshuqah/mashal with 3:16's — there it is imposed, here it is a live choice)*.
- **yada (know) #2 here — knowing turned evasive lie (4:9).** The finding is "I do not **know** (yada); am I my brother's keeper?" *(stated)*. The operation is knowing *weaponised as evasion* — Cain, who knows exactly, feigns not-knowing; the knowing-operation used to *conceal* (echoing the hiding of 3:8–10, now as spoken denial) *(inferred)*.
- **shamar (keeper) — the keeping-vocation refused toward a *person* (4:9).** The finding is "am I my brother's **keeper** (shamar)?" *(stated)*. The operation is the *same guard/keep word* as the garden-vocation (2:15) and the cherubim (3:24) — now **refused toward a brother**: Cain denies he is his brother's *shamar*. The gem is that the keeping man was made for (2:15) is disowned at the human level — one is *not* the keeper of one's brother, Cain claims *(inferred)*.
- **arar (curse) — the *man himself* cursed (4:11).** The finding is "you are **cursed** (arar) from the ground" *(stated)*. The operation is the curse now falling **on the person** — where the fall cursed the *ground* not the man (3:17), the first murder curses *Cain himself*; an escalation from cursed-domain to cursed-man *(inferred: distinct from 3:17 — the human is now the direct object of arar)*.
- **natan (yield) + koach (strength) — the ground's produce withheld (4:12).** The finding is "it shall no longer **yield** (natan) to you its **strength** (koach)" *(stated)*. The operations: **natan** — the *earth's giving* now *withdrawn* (a fourth natan-sense: provision 2:29 → transmission 3:6 → blame 3:12 → **withheld yield** 4:12); **koach** — the ground's *productive strength*, denied *(inferred)*.
- **avon (punishment/iniquity) — the felt weight, self-centred (4:13).** The finding is "my **punishment** (avon) is greater than I can bear" *(stated)*. The operation is the interior feeling the *weight* of guilt-punishment (avon carries both) — but bent on its *unbearableness to Cain*, not on the brother or on repentance; the self registers the penalty, not the wrong *(inferred: avon = guilt-and-its-penalty fused; Cain feels the penalty pole)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **charah → naphal → God's naming (4:5–6):** the anger kindles inward (charah), shows in the fallen face (naphal), and is *surfaced by God* (charah #2) *before* it acts — the interior is given a diagnostic pause.
2. **the summons and its refusal (4:7 → 4:8):** God personifies sin (chattat) crouching, holds the choice open (yatav), and issues the teshuqah/mashal summons — *rule the craving that would possess you*. Cain does **not** rule; the murder is the interior *failing the very mastery it was called to*. The 3:16 pairing, imposed on the woman, becomes here a *moral test Cain fails*.
3. **yada from begetting to lie (4:1 → 4:9):** the knowing that *made life* (4:1) becomes the knowing *denied to conceal death* (4:9) — the same faculty at opposite moral poles within one passage.
4. **shamar refused → arar on the man (4:9 → 4:11):** disowning the keeper-role toward his brother, Cain becomes the first *cursed man* — the refusal to *keep* his brother and the curse *on his own person* are joined.
5. **the curse deepens through the ground (4:11–12):** cursed *from* the ground, its *yield* (natan) and *strength* (koach) withheld — the avad-vocation (4:2) rendered barren; Cain a fugitive.
6. **avon closes on the self (4:13):** the interior registers the punishment's weight but curved inward — the movement of the whole passage is a self that will not *rule* itself, will not *keep* its brother, and finally feels only its *own* unbearable penalty.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** yada as begetting (4:1) vs evasive lie (4:9); charah (inner anger) vs naphal (the fallen face) vs charah God-named (4:6); yatav as the *open* choice; chattat — the first, *personified* sin; teshuqah+mashal as a *moral summons* Cain fails (vs the *imposed* pairing of 3:16); shamar *refused toward a brother*; arar now on the *man* (vs the ground, 3:17); natan as the ground's *withheld yield* (a fourth natan-sense); koach withdrawn; avon as the self-centred felt penalty.
- **Whose interior:** the **human** interior *that will not master itself* — anger unruled, sin unmastered, the brother unkept, guilt felt only as personal cost.
- **Threads:** teshuqah+mashal (← 3:16, GEN-05, now a failed summons); shamar (← 2:15/3:24, now refused at the human level); arar (← 3:17 ground → 4:11 man); the avad arc; yada (← 3:7/3:22 → 4:1/4:9); natan (← 2:29/3:6/3:12 → 4:12 withheld).
- **Guarded against:** merging the two yada (begetting vs lie); merging charah with naphal (inner vs outer); merging 4:7's teshuqah/mashal with 3:16's (summons vs sentence); reading the man-curse of 4:11 back onto 3:17 (there the ground); flattening avon to "regret" when the verse bends it to *self*-cost; importing later doctrines of sin onto chattat beyond the crouching-predator the verse gives.
