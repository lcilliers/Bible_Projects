## GEN-13-noah-nakedness · Genesis 9:18–29 · [shame returns; the curse and blessing now in human mouths] — the exposed father, the son who saw, and the first human curse and doxology

**The passage.** Noah "planted a vineyard… drank… became drunk and lay **uncovered**" (9:20–21); "Ham… **saw** the nakedness of his father and told" (9:22); Shem and Japheth "walked backward and **covered** the nakedness… their faces turned backward" (9:23); "Noah awoke… and **knew** (yada) what his youngest son had done" (9:24); "**Cursed** (arar) be Canaan" (9:25); "**Blessed** (barak) be the LORD, the God of Shem" (9:26); "Noah **lived** (chayah) 350 years… and he died" (9:28–29).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **(9:20–23 — nakedness, seeing, and covering: the shame-theme, no non-T2 span.)** Narratively: the righteous Noah (GEN-10) now lies *naked/uncovered* in drunkenness; Ham **saw** his father's nakedness and *told*; the brothers **covered** him *without looking* (faces turned away). Flagged: these carry no non-T2 span, so no lexical operation is *claimed* — but the *theme* is the anchored context: **shame/exposure returns** (← the unashamed nakedness of 2:25, the shame-knowing of 3:7), and the responses split — the *seeing* that exposes (Ham) vs the *covering* that shields (Shem/Japheth, echoing God's clothing of Adam, 3:21). Read only as the shame-and-covering frame the spans sit in *(no-span context)*.
- **yada (knew) — knowing a *wrong done* (9:24).** The finding is "Noah awoke… and **knew** (yada) what his youngest son had done to him" *(stated)*. The operation is knowing as *coming-to-know an injury* — perceptual-and-moral discernment of a wrong (distinct from the conjugal 4:1, evasive 4:9, sign-discerning 8:11 yada); here the interior *learns what was done to it* *(inferred)*.
- **arar (curse) — the first curse in a *human* mouth (9:25).** The finding is "**Cursed** (arar) be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be" *(stated)*. The gem is that *arar* — until now always *God's* (the serpent 3:14, the ground 3:17, Cain 4:11) — is here spoken by a **man**: Noah pronounces the first *human* curse, on Ham's son Canaan. The operation is the curse-word passing into human speech — the interior now *wielding* the curse it had only received *(inferred: distinct arar — divine → human; the man curses)*.
- **barak (bless) — the direction *reversed*: man blesses God (9:26).** The finding is "**Blessed** (barak) be the LORD, the God of Shem" *(stated)*. The gem is *barak* turned *around*: everywhere before, *God* blessed the creatures/man (1:22, 1:28, 9:1); here a **man blesses God** — the first *doxology/praise*, barak flowing *upward*. The operation is blessing as *human praise of God* — distinct from divine blessing of man *(inferred: barak reverses direction — Godward)*.
- **chayah (lived) — Noah, too, dies (9:28–29).** The finding is "Noah **lived** (chayah) 350 years after the flood… all the days of Noah were 950 years, and he died" *(stated)*. The operation is the **death-formula returning** (← GEN-08): even the righteous flood-survivor is folded into "and he died" — mortality reclaims Noah *(inferred: the reign of death (GEN-08) closes over Noah as over all)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **shame returns, and splits the sons (9:20–24):** the exposure-theme (← 2:25/3:7) reappears in Noah's drunken nakedness; the *seeing* that exposes (Ham) and the *covering* that shields (Shem/Japheth, ← 3:21 God clothing) divide the responses — and Noah, on waking, *knows* (yada) the wrong; the interior's shame and the others' handling of it are the scene's inner-being weight.
2. **curse and blessing pass into human mouths (9:25–26):** the same passage sets the *first human arar* (on Canaan) beside the *first human barak* (of God) — the interior now *wields* both the curse it once only received and the praise it now lifts upward; judgment-speech and doxology-speech emerge together.
3. **the formula closes over Noah (9:28–29):** the reign of death (GEN-08) reclaims even the righteous survivor — the passage, and the Noah story, ends on "and he died".

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** the shame/covering scene as *no-span context* (seeing-that-exposes vs covering-that-shields); yada as *knowing-a-wrong-done*; **arar in a human mouth** (first human curse, vs prior divine curses); **barak reversed** (man blesses/praises God, vs God blessing man); chayah — the death-formula *reclaiming Noah*.
- **Whose interior:** the **human** interior — shamed (Noah), dividing over another's shame (the sons), *knowing* a wrong, and now *wielding* curse and *lifting* praise; and still under the *reign of death*.
- **Threads:** nakedness/shame (← 2:25, 3:7; covering ← 3:21); yada (← 4:1/4:9/8:11, now knowing-a-wrong); arar (← 3:14/3:17/4:11, now human); barak (← 1:22/1:28/9:1, now reversed Godward); chayah death-formula (← GEN-08, now over Noah).
- **Guarded against:** claiming lexical operations off the no-span nakedness verses (read as context only); merging this yada with its other senses; missing that arar and barak here *change speaker/direction* (the gems); treating Noah's death-formula as mere genealogy rather than the reign-of-death closing over the righteous.
