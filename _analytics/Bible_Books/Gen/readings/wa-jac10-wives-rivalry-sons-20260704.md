# Genesis 29:31–30:24 — inner-being passage reading (JAC-10-wives-rivalry-sons)

- **Source:** verse-first lexicals over the passage (provenance `genesis-jacob-v1-20260704`). Every non-T2 span in its verse; *stated* | *inferred*; distinctions kept. **Each birth-naming is a distinct interior cry — never lumped.** The mandrakes-bargain speech (30:15, no non-T2 span) reads as scene-context, anchored by natan/qara.
- **Check-back:** gate run post-draft; every non-T2 verse/gloss engaged; the 12 qara-namings, 6 natan, 4 shama, 2 sane each treated distinctly.

## JAC-10-wives-rivalry-sons · Genesis 29:31–30:24 · [the rivalry read through the birth-namings] — two women's interiors locked in a childbearing contest, each cry a distinct state, God seeing the hated and hearing the barren

**The passage.** "The LORD saw Leah was **hated** (sane), opened her womb" (29:31); Reuben — "the LORD looked on my **affliction** (oni)" (29:32); Simeon — "the LORD **heard** (shama) I am **hated** (sane), **given** (natan) me this" (29:33); Levi — "my husband attached… three sons" (29:34); Judah — "I will **praise** (yadah) the LORD" (29:35); Rachel **envied** (qana), "give me children or I die!" (30:1); Jacob's anger **kindled** (charah) (30:2); she **gave** (natan) Bilhah (30:4); Dan — "God **judged** (din) me, **heard** (shama), **given** (natan) a son" (30:6); Naphtali — "I have wrestled and **prevailed** (yakhol)" (30:8); Leah **gave** (natan) Zilpah (30:9); Gad — "good fortune!" (30:11); Asher — "happy am I!" (30:13); the mandrakes — "**give** (natan) me some" (30:14); Leah went to **meet** (qara) him, "I have hired you" (30:16); God **listened** (shama) to Leah (30:17); Issachar — "God **given** (natan) my wages" (30:18); Zebulun — "a **good** (tov) endowment… honor me" (30:20); Dinah **called** (qara) (30:21); "God **remembered** (zakhar) Rachel, **listened** (shama)" (30:22); "taken away my **reproach** (cherpah)" (30:23); Joseph — "may the LORD add another" (30:24).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **sane (hated) — the *unloved* wife *seen* by God (29:31).** "the LORD **saw** that Leah was **hated** (sane), and opened her womb" *(stated)* — sane, *hated/unloved*: the gem is *God's regard for the unloved* — the *rejected* wife (the one given by deceit, less loved than Rachel, 29:30) is *seen and answered with fruitfulness*; the divine interior *takes the side of the hated* *(inferred: sane = the unloved state God regards and answers)*.
- **oni (affliction) + qara (Reuben) — the *affliction seen*, love hoped (29:32).** "the LORD looked on my **affliction** (oni)… now my husband will love me… **called** (qara) Reuben" *(stated)*. **oni** — *affliction/misery*: Leah names her *unloved suffering*; **qara (Reuben ≈ "see, a son")** — the naming *reads God's seeing into the child* and *voices the ache for love*. The interior: *affliction acknowledged, love craved* *(inferred: oni/qara = the affliction seen and the husband's love hoped for)*.
- **sane (hated) + shama (heard) + natan (given) + qara (Simeon) — being *hated*, now *heard* (29:33).** *sane distinct from 29:31:* here Leah *herself names* her hatedness ("the LORD **heard** (shama) that I am **hated** (sane)"). **shama** — God *hears* the unloved; **natan** — the son *given* as answer; **qara (Simeon ≈ "heard")**. The interior: *the hated one knows herself heard* — the second cry *(inferred: sane/shama/natan/qara = the hated wife hearing herself answered)*.
- **qara (Levi) — the *longing for attachment* (29:34).** "now my husband will be **attached** to me… three sons… **called** (qara) Levi" *(stated)* — qara (Levi ≈ "attached/joined"): the *third* cry names the *ache for the husband's attachment* — not love (Reuben), not being-heard (Simeon), but *bonding*; the interior *counting sons as leverage for closeness* *(inferred: qara = the naming that voices the longing to be joined)*.
- **yadah (praise) + qara (Judah) — the *turn from craving to praise* (29:35).** "this time I will **praise** (yadah) the LORD… **called** (qara) Judah; then she ceased bearing" *(stated)*. The gem: **yadah — *give thanks/praise*** — Leah's *fourth* cry *breaks the pattern* — no longer *"now my husband will…"* but *praise of God*; the interior *moves from grasping for love to thanksgiving* (qara, Judah ≈ "praise") — and *then rests* ("ceased bearing"). The unloved wife *finds her centre shifted to God* *(inferred: yadah/qara = the turn from love-craving to praise)*.
- **qana (envied) — Rachel's *envy* and desperation (30:1).** "Rachel **envied** (qana) her sister… 'give me children, or I shall die!'" *(stated)* — qana, *be jealous/envious* (← the Philistines 26:14): the *loved-but-barren* wife *consumed by envy* of the *unloved-but-fruitful*; the interior *at the extremity* — children-or-death; envy *turned self-destructive* *(inferred: qana = the barren wife's consuming envy)*.
- **charah (kindled) — Jacob's *anger*, deflecting to God (30:2).** "Jacob's anger was **kindled** (charah)… 'am I in the place of God, who has withheld?'" *(stated)* — charah, *burn/be incensed*: the husband's *flare of anger* at Rachel's demand — and his *pointing to God* as the withholder; the interior *refuses the god-role* but *in anger*, not comfort *(inferred: charah = the kindled anger that deflects the demand to God)*.
- **natan (gave Bilhah) — the *surrogate given* to win by proxy (30:4).** *natan distinct from the God-gives:* "she **gave** (natan) him Bilhah as a wife" *(stated)* — natan, *give*: Rachel *gives her maid* to *bear on her behalf* — the interior *seizing motherhood by proxy* (the Hagar-scheme again, ← 16:2), envy *acting* *(inferred: natan = the maid given to secure children by proxy)*.
- **din (judged) + shama (heard) + natan (given) + qara (Dan) — *vindication* claimed (30:6).** "God has **judged** (din) me, **heard** (shama) my voice, **given** (natan) a son… **called** (qara) Dan" *(stated)*. **din** — *judge/vindicate*: Rachel reads the surrogate-son as *God's verdict in her favour*; **shama** — *heard* (distinct from 29:33: now *Rachel* heard); **natan** — the son *given*; **qara (Dan ≈ "judged")**. The interior: *envy re-framed as vindication* *(inferred: din/shama/natan/qara = the barren wife claiming God's verdict for her)*.
- **yakhol (prevailed) + qara (Naphtali) — the *sister-wrestling* named (30:8).** "with mighty wrestlings I have wrestled with my sister and have **prevailed** (yakhol)… **called** (qara) Naphtali" *(stated)*. The gem: **yakhol — *be able / prevail*** — Rachel *names the rivalry itself* as a *wrestling with her sister* in which she has *prevailed*; the interior *openly frames the contest as a struggle-to-overcome*. (Note the word: *wrestled-and-prevailed* — the very verbs of Jacob at Peniel, 32:28 — the family's *struggle-to-prevail* motif in the women too.) *(inferred: yakhol/qara = the sister-rivalry named as a prevailing wrestle)*.
- **natan (gave Zilpah) — Leah's *counter-surrogate* (30:9).** *natan distinct from 30:4:* "she took Zilpah and **gave** (natan) her to Jacob as a wife" *(stated)* — Leah, *ceased bearing*, *answers Rachel's proxy with her own* — the contest *escalates by mirror-move*; the interior *matching seizure with seizure* *(inferred: natan = the counter-maid given, escalating the contest)*.
- **qara (Gad) — *fortune* acclaimed (30:11).** "'Good fortune has come!'… **called** (qara) Gad" *(stated)* — qara (Gad ≈ "fortune/troop"): Leah's cry *reads luck/fortune* into the proxy-son — the interior *celebrating a turn of fortune* (a different register from the God-claims — here *fortune*) *(inferred: qara = the naming that acclaims good fortune)*.
- **qara (Asher) — *happiness* and others' esteem (30:13).** "'Happy am I! women have called me happy'… **called** (qara) Asher" *(stated)* — qara (Asher ≈ "happy"): the interior *basks in happiness and others' regard* — the naming voices *social vindication* (women will call me happy), distinct from Gad's fortune *(inferred: qara = the naming that claims happiness and esteem)*.
- **natan (give mandrakes) — the *bargained fertility-charm* (30:14).** *natan distinct from the son-gives:* "'please **give** (natan) me some of your son's mandrakes'" *(stated)* — natan, *give*: Rachel *asks for the mandrakes* (a fertility-plant) — the interior *reaching for a natural means* to conceive; the request that *opens the husband-trade* *(inferred: natan = the mandrakes requested as a fertility-means)*.
- **qara (meet) — the *husband hired* for a night (30:16).** "Leah went out to **meet** (qara) him: 'you must come in to me, for I have **hired** you with the mandrakes'" *(stated)* — qara in the *encounter/meet* sense (distinct from all the naming-qara): Leah *goes out to claim the husband she has bartered for*; the interior — the husband *traded between the wives like a commodity*; intimacy *transacted* *(inferred: qara = the going-out to claim the bartered husband)*.
- **shama (listened) — God *hears Leah* again (30:17).** *shama distinct from 29:33, 30:6:* "God **listened** (shama) to Leah, and she conceived" *(stated)* — shama, *hear*: God *hears the unloved wife* once more — the divine *hearing* persists *beneath* the human bartering; the interior *answered by God, not by the mandrakes-scheme* *(inferred: shama = God's continued hearing of Leah)*.
- **natan (given wages) + qara (Issachar) — *reward for the surrogate* claimed (30:18).** *natan distinct again:* "God has **given** (natan) my **wages** because I gave my servant… **called** (qara) Issachar" *(stated)*. **natan** — God *gives wages*; **qara (Issachar ≈ "hire/wages")**: the gem is Leah's *hire-theology* — she reads the son as *payment for having given her maid* — the interior *casting God's gift as earned wages* (transactional, like Jacob's own bargaining, 28:20) *(inferred: natan/qara = the son read as earned wages)*.
- **tov (good) + qara (Zebulun) — the *longing for honor* (30:20).** "God has endowed me a **good** (tov) endowment; now my husband will **honor** me, six sons… **called** (qara) Zebulun" *(stated)*. **tov** — the *good* gift; **qara (Zebulun ≈ "honor/dwell")**: the interior *still craving the husband's honor* after six sons — the ache for Jacob's regard *persists* under the God-thanks; love-longing *never fully quieted* *(inferred: tov/qara = the good gift and the persistent longing for the husband's honor)*.
- **qara (Dinah) — the *daughter named without a cry* (30:21).** "she bore a daughter and **called** (qara) her name Dinah" *(stated)* — qara, *name*: the *only naming with no stated interior reason* — the daughter *simply named*; a *bare* naming (the narrator gives no cry) — noted distinctly *for its silence* (and Dinah's story comes in JAC-17) *(inferred: qara = the bare naming, no interior reason given)*.
- **zakhar (remembered) + shama (listened) — God *remembers* the barren Rachel (30:22).** "God **remembered** (zakhar) Rachel, and God **listened** (shama) to her, and opened her womb" *(stated)*. **zakhar** — *remember* (← "God remembered Noah", 8:1): the gem — the *barren, envying* Rachel is *remembered by God*; **shama** — *heard* (distinct: now Rachel *at last* heard directly, not by proxy). The interior *answered not by envy or mandrakes but by God's remembering* *(inferred: zakhar/shama = God remembering and hearing the barren wife)*.
- **cherpah (reproach) — the *reproach removed* (30:23).** "God has taken away my **reproach** (cherpah)" *(stated)* — cherpah, *reproach/disgrace*: the gem is *what barrenness was to her* — a *reproach*; its removal is the interior's *deepest relief* — the shame of childlessness *lifted* *(inferred: cherpah = the reproach of barrenness, now taken away)*.
- **qara (Joseph) — the naming that *still asks for more* (30:24).** "**called** (qara) him Joseph, saying 'may the LORD **add** to me another son!'" *(stated)* — qara (Joseph ≈ "may he add"): the gem — even *at the joy of the first son*, Rachel's cry is *"add another"* — the interior *satisfied and still reaching*; desire *never at rest* *(inferred: qara = the naming that rejoices and still asks for more)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **God sees the unloved (sane 29:31 → oni/qara Reuben 29:32 → sane/shama/natan/qara Simeon 29:33 → qara Levi 29:34):** the *hated* wife is *seen*, *heard*, and *made fruitful* — her first three cries *ache for love, being-heard, and attachment*, each name a distinct wound.
2. **the turn to praise (yadah/qara Judah 29:35):** Leah's fourth cry *breaks to thanksgiving* — from *"now my husband will…"* to *"I will praise the LORD"* — and *rests*; the interior's centre *shifted to God*.
3. **envy acts, then claims vindication (qana 30:1 → charah 30:2 → natan-Bilhah 30:4 → din/shama/natan/qara Dan 30:6 → yakhol/qara Naphtali 30:8):** the *barren, loved* wife *envies to the point of death-wish*, *seizes motherhood by proxy*, and *reads it as God's verdict* and *a wrestling-prevailed over her sister* — envy *re-narrated as triumph*.
4. **the contest escalates by mirror-moves (natan-Zilpah 30:9 → qara Gad 30:11 → qara Asher 30:13 → natan-mandrakes 30:14 → qara-meet 30:16 → shama 30:17 → natan/qara Issachar 30:18 → tov/qara Zebulun 30:20 → qara Dinah 30:21):** Leah *counters with her own maid*, acclaims *fortune* and *happiness*, the *husband is bartered* for mandrakes, God *keeps hearing Leah*, who reads sons as *earned wages* and still *craves honor* — the rivalry a *ledger of names*, each a distinct claim.
5. **God remembers the barren (zakhar/shama 30:22 → cherpah 30:23 → qara Joseph 30:24):** at last Rachel is *remembered and heard*, her *reproach lifted* — yet her joy-cry *still asks "add another"*: the interior *answered, relieved, and still reaching*.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct — each a separate cry):** the 12 birth-namings (qara): Reuben *affliction-seen/love-craved*, Simeon *hated-but-heard*, Levi *longing-for-attachment*, Judah *turn-to-praise*, Dan *vindication-claimed*, Naphtali *sister-wrestle-prevailed*, Gad *fortune*, Asher *happiness/esteem*, Issachar *son-as-wages*, Zebulun *longing-for-honor*, Dinah *bare/silent naming*, Joseph *joy-that-still-asks-more*; plus qara-meet (30:16, the *bartered husband claimed*). sane ×2 (God *sees* the hated 29:31 / Leah *names* herself hated 29:33); natan ×6 (God-*given* sons 29:33, 30:6, 30:18 / *maids* given 30:4, 30:9 / *mandrakes* requested 30:14); shama ×4 (heard-I-am-hated / heard-my-voice-Dan / listened-to-Leah / listened-to-Rachel); oni (*affliction*); yadah (*praise* — the turn); qana (*consuming envy*); charah (Jacob's *deflecting anger*); din (*vindication*); yakhol (*prevailing* — the Peniel-word in the women); tov (the *good* gift under persistent love-longing); zakhar (God *remembering* the barren); cherpah (the *reproach* of barrenness lifted).
- **Whose interior:** the **human** interiors of *two rival women* — the *unloved craving love then turning to praise* (Leah) and the *loved-but-barren consumed by envy then remembered* (Rachel) — and the *husband bartered and angry* (Jacob); the **divine** interior *seeing the hated, hearing the unloved, remembering the barren* — grace running *beneath* the rivalry, on the side of the *unregarded*.
- **Threads:** God's regard for the unregarded (sane 29:31 / zakhar 30:22 ← El-Roi sees Hagar 16:13, God hears Ishmael 21:17); the surrogate-scheme (natan-Bilhah 30:4 ← Sarai/Hagar 16:2); *wrestle-and-prevail* (yakhol 30:8 → Jacob at Peniel 32:28 — the family struggle-motif); the turn to praise (Judah, yadah 29:35 → the tribe of praise/kingship); transactional God-reading (Issachar "wages" 30:18 ← Jacob's vow 28:20 — the family's bargaining habit); Joseph/Dinah (→ JAC-17 Dinah; the Joseph cycle beyond Genesis-Jacob).
- **Guarded against:** **lumping the birth-namings** — the single strongest temptation here; each of the 12 qara is a *distinct interior cry* (I read each off its own stated reason, never "the naming of the sons" as one operation); collapsing the 6 natan or 4 shama (God-gift vs maid-giving vs mandrakes; and four *distinct* hearings); reading Leah's Judah-praise as the whole arc when her later Zebulun-cry still *craves honor* (the love-longing *persists* — I kept both); importing tribal/Messianic significance (Judah→David→Christ) over the *domestic interior cries* the verses give; treating Dinah's silent naming as an oversight rather than *noting the silence* itself.
