## The spine: creation-words invert under the fall, and revert after it

The single most insistent thing the primeval spans do — surfacing on its own across passage after passage — is this: **the vocabulary God uses to bless and order creation is later turned to curse and un-creation, and then, past the flood, turned back.** The same *word* carries the whole moral history of the interior.

| Word | Creation / blessing | Inverted (fall / flood) | Reverted / renounced |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ravah` multiply | be fruitful, 1:28 | multiply *pain*, 3:16 → the flood *swells*, 7:17 | home to blessing, 8:17; 9:1,7 |
| `shamar` keep | keep the garden, 2:15 | cherubim *guard it against man*, 3:24; Cain refuses to *keep* his brother, 4:9 | — |
| `shachat` ruin | — | man *corrupts* → God *destroys* (one verb pivoting), 6:11–17 | "never again… destroy", renounced 9:11,15 |
| `neshamah` breath | God breathes life *in*, 2:7 | the breath *undone* in the flood, 7:22 | — |
| `barak` bless | God→creatures/man, 1:22,28; 9:1 | — | *man* blesses/praises *God*, 9:26 |
| `qara` call | God names the domains, 1:5–10 | name a city *for self*, 4:17; Babel's self-*name*, 11:4 | *call upon* God, 4:26 |
| `arar` curse | — | God curses serpent/ground/Cain, 3:14,17; 4:11 | passes into a *human* mouth, 9:25 |
| `natan` give | food-*gift*, 1:29; 2:29 | *transmission* of sin 3:6 → *blame* indicting the Giver 3:12 → the ground's *withheld* yield 4:12 | *delivered* provision again, 9:2–3 |
| `tov` good | God's verdict, 1:4–31 | the covetous "*saw…good…took*", 3:6; 6:2 | — |

This is not a theme I brought; it is what the *repeated spans* do when they are refused the lump. The interior's fall is legible *in the words themselves* — the same operation, re-pointed. And crucially, the reversion after the flood (`ravah` re-blessed, `shachat` renounced) shows the pattern is *not* one-way: grace re-points the words back.

## The movements that emerged

### 1. The constitution of the interior — dust, breath, image, and (late) the heart
The primeval spans build up *what the interior is* before they ever show it acting. Man is **dust + God's own in-breathing** (`naphach`/`neshamah`, 2:7) becoming a **living `nephesh`** — the *same* soul-word first given to the animals (1:20,24): the interior's soul-life is *shared creatureliness*, received by a *more intimate process*. Man is the **`tselem` (image)** — conferred (1:26), then *transmitted* father-to-son (5:3), then made the *legal ground* of human life's sanctity (9:6). And strikingly, the **`lev` (heart)** — the seat the rest of Scripture will build on — does not appear until **6:5**, and its first appearance is *"only evil continually."* The interior is constituted good (image, God-breath) yet its named "heart" enters the story already corrupt.

### 2. The appraisal operation, godly then grasping
God's defining interior act is **evaluation** — the repeated "it was `tov` (good)", climaxing in the only "very good" (1:31), with the *withheld* verdict on day 2 as the proof it is a real judgment, not a refrain. The one "**not good**" in all creation is *human solitude* (2:18) — the interior is appraised as *made for relation*. Then the same appraisal-faculty **inverts**: the covetous "**saw** … was **good** … **took**" (3:6) — and replays at cosmic scale (the sons of God "saw the daughters were `tov`… took", 6:2). Evaluation is the interior's godliest and most dangerous operation.

### 3. Desire, and its disordering
Desire enters *good*: the trees are `chamad` (desirable) *by God's design* (2:9). It disorders into the **desire-stack** of the fall — appetite (`taavah`) + coveting (`chamad`) + aspiration (`sakal`), not one "desire" but a sequence of faculties handing off (3:6). And it becomes a *relational-mastery* dynamic: **`teshuqah` + `mashal`** (desire-toward / rule-over) — *imposed* between wife and husband (3:16), then held out to Cain as a *moral summons* to master the sin whose "desire is for you" (4:7) — a summons he **fails**.

### 4. Knowing (`yada`) across every moral pole
No operation is more protean. `yada` is God-like knowing *promised* (3:5), *arriving as shame* (3:7), *confirmed by God* (3:22); it is *conjugal begetting* (4:1), an *evasive lie* (4:9), *sign-discernment* (8:11), and *knowing-a-wrong-done* (9:24). The interior's knowing-faculty is morally *neutral in itself and decisive in its direction* — the same verb makes life and conceals murder.

### 5. The directions of "calling" (`qara`) — toward self or toward God
Naming/calling is the interior's reach outward, and it *splits by direction*. God names the domains (ordering); man names the animals (authority — and *discovers his own solitude* in the act, 2:20) and names the woman (recognition, 2:23). God **calls *to*** the hidden man (pursuit, 3:9); man **calls *on*** God (the first worship, 4:26) — the reciprocal. Against this, the self-ward calls: Cain **names a city for himself** (4:17), Lamech **conscripts an audience** for his boast (4:23), Babel resolves to **"make a name for ourselves"** (11:4) — and God answers by *giving* them the name **Babel/confusion** (11:9). The interior's calling is either self-securing or God-invoking.

### 6. Shame, hiding, and covering
A clean arc: the **unashamed baseline** (`bosh` negated, 2:25) → the **shame-knowing** (eyes opened, they knew they were naked, 3:7) → the **birth of hiding-from-God** (hearing God now welds to *dread* and concealment, 3:8–10) → **God covers** the shame he found (3:21) → and shame *returns* with Noah's nakedness, where the sons divide into *seeing-that-exposes* and *covering-that-shields* (9:22–23). Shame is the interior's response to exposure, and *covering* is the mercy that meets it.

### 7. The two hearts — and the divine interior disclosed
Uniquely, the primeval spans open *God's* interior. The **human `lev`** "forms only evil" (6:5); one verse later the **divine `lev`** is *"grieved"* (6:6) — two hearts, one word. God **`nacham`** (regrets/is-sorry, 6:6–7) — the same word man used for the *comfort* he hoped Noah would bring (5:29). God **`zakhar`** (remembers Noah, and the flood turns, 8:1; binds the rainbow to his own remembering, 9:15). And the keystone: the **same evil-heart diagnosis** (`yetser`/`lev`, "evil from his youth") grounds the *flood* at 6:5 and God's resolve *never again to curse* at 8:21 — one fact about the human interior, moving God first to judge, then to spare. The divine interior grieves the judgment it passes and sets its mercy on the very corruption that condemned.

### 8. The self that will not master, keep, or rule itself
Cain is the case study: **anger** kindles (`charah`) and shows in the **fallen face** (`naphal`, 4:5); God holds the choice *open* (`yatav`) and issues the summons to *rule* (`mashal`) the crouching **`chattat` (sin)** — the interior *called* to master its craving and *refusing*; then disowning his brother's *keeping* (`shamar`, 4:9) and feeling only his *own* penalty (`avon`, 4:13). The unmastered interior will not rule its desire, keep its neighbour, or repent its wrong.

### 9. The reign of death, and a line that bends toward promise
Mortality becomes the interior's *world*: the Sethite formula tolls "**and he died**" with relentless uniformity (GEN-08), broken only by Enoch (*taken*, not died) and by Noah's naming for *relief* — and it closes even over the righteous Noah (9:29). Yet the Shemite line (GEN-15) *compresses* the lifespans (500→119) while **dropping** the death-refrain and **running to Abram** — the same mortality, now *un-tolled*, the genealogy bending from dirge to *bridge*.

### 10. Self-securing grasp vs God-ward dependence
The two postures the whole block sets against each other: the self *builds and names* to secure itself (Cain's city, Babel's tower-and-name) and *boasts* (Lamech) — and is *scattered*; over against the interior that *calls on the Name* (4:26), *names its hope in God's appointing* (Seth, 4:25), *waits with hope* (`yachal`, 8:12), and is *remembered* and *kept in covenant*. Self-security produces the dispersion it feared; God-ward dependence is met by God's remembering.

### 11. The recovered vocabulary (Gate-1) — the Spirit that animates, the vengeance that enters

A Gate-1 span-orphan scan (run after the fact, the reading having drawn only on the curated term-seed) recovered two inner-being words the first pass missed, both now onboarded and coded:

- **`ruach` — SPIRIT (1:2; 6:3; 7:15,22; 8:1).** Movement 1 built the interior's constitution on `neshamah` (breath) and `nephesh` (soul) but never named **`ruach`** — though the block opens on it: *"the **Spirit** (`ruach`) of God was hovering over the face of the waters"* (1:2), the divine animating presence before a word is spoken. It returns as the life-breath *in all flesh* (`ruach chayyim`, 7:15,22), as the **wind** God sends to abate the flood (8:1, welded to his *remembering*), and — most pointedly for the interior — as the limit God sets on human life: *"My **Spirit** shall not abide in man forever… his days shall be 120 years"* (6:3). So the human interior is not only *dust + in-breathing*; it is *held in being by God's `ruach`*, and its mortality is that Spirit's withdrawal. This deepens the "reign of death" (movement 9): death is `ruach` no longer abiding.
- **`naqam` — VENGEANCE (4:15,24).** Movement 5 read Lamech's boast as *self-conscription of an audience*, but the boast's engine is **vengeance**: God first sets a protective **`naqam`** on Cain — *"whoever kills Cain, **vengeance** shall be taken sevenfold"* (4:15), a *restraint* on blood-feud — which Lamech then *seizes and inflates* into a *charter for retaliation* — *"if Cain is **avenged** sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy-sevenfold"* (4:24). Vengeance thus *enters the human interior* in the primeval history as a divine limit *perverted into human boast* — the first appearance of the retributive impulse the later corpus will everywhere regulate. (Jesus' "seventy times seven," Matt 18:22, is the deliberate reversal of Lamech's 4:24.)

Neither word changes the reading's shape; each names a datum the first pass carried only implicitly — the Spirit that constitutes and limits the interior, and the vengeance that enters it.

## The shape of the primeval interior

Read together, Genesis 1–11 gives the interior's *origin and fault-line*: **constituted** good (image, God-breath, made-for-relation), it **appraises, desires, knows, and calls** — and at the fall these very operations *invert* (the good seen-and-taken, desire grasping, knowing turned to shame and lie, calling turned self-ward), its **shame** born and its **heart** named "only evil." The block's God is no distant judge but a disclosed **interior** — grieved, regretful, remembering, resolving mercy on the ground that bore judgment — who **covers** shame, **renounces** destruction, and **bends the line of death toward Abram**. The primeval history ends not with the interior mastered but with the interior *narrowed to a single name* through whom the next movement — call, promise, faith — will begin.
