## PRO-10-speech · Prov 10:11,13–14,18–21,31–32 · [T, multi] — the inner condition legible in speech

**The reading.** Proverbs' dominant inner-being thread, gathered from across the chapter: **the mouth reveals the heart**. "The **mouth of the righteous** is a **fountain of life**, but the mouth of the wicked **conceals violence**" (v.11); "the one who **conceals hatred** has **lying lips**" (v.18); "when **words are many**, transgression is not lacking, but whoever **restrains his lips** is prudent" (v.19); "the **tongue of the righteous** is choice silver; the **heart of the wicked** is of little worth" (v.20). The finding: **speech is the outflow and the exposure of the inner being** — what is in the heart *comes out* the mouth, so that the lips *diagnose* the heart *(stated: the mouth conceals violence / reveals life)*. Two operations run under it: **concealment** (the wicked's mouth *hides* what is within — v.11, v.18) and **restraint** (the prudent *governs* the outflow — v.19); the un-governed tongue *leaks* transgression, the governed tongue *guards* the self *(inferred: v.19–20 make restraint an inner-being act, not mere silence — the self mastering its own outflow; cf. the Psalter's "inner condition legible in speech", Ps 5:9, 12, 141:3)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** The thread is well-supported: *righteous, wicked, life, hatred, lying, prudent* all tagged **characteristics**; the organs (*mouth, lips, tongue* — H6310/H8193) sit `standalone` gate-2, correctly (the reading reads the inner condition *off* them). Two notes. (1) **Under-tag:** v.19 "whoever **restrains** his lips" (H2820) is tagged `standalone`, but restraint-of-speech is a genuine **inner-being characteristic** (self-governance) — the reading promotes it (a correction candidate). (2) **Prohibition over-stamp** at v.19 ("transgression is *not* lacking") bleeds `prohibition` across the whole clause — a known residual. **Pair note:** v.13 *back* (H1460) is welded `manner-of found` to "wisdom is found"; it is really the body-part target of "a rod for the *back*" — a recipient-as-manner slippage, harmless to the thread.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **Restraint of speech as self-governance** (v.19) — under-tagged, but a real operation that ties this thread to the self-mastery thread (16:32, 29:11, 25:28). (2) **Concealment** — the wicked mouth that *hides* the heart (vv.11,18) is a distinct operation (the masking self), which surfaces fully at 26:23–26; a cross-chapter thread starts here.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the inner condition legible in speech* (shared w/ Ps); *restraint of speech / self-governance of the tongue* (new — under-tagged in the substrate); *concealment / the masking self* (new thread).

## PRO-10-desire · Prov 10:3,24,28 · [T, multi] — desire, dread, and the hope that brings joy

**The reading.** A scattered thread on the **appetite and expectation of the inner being**: "the LORD… **thwarts the craving of the wicked**" (v.3); "what the wicked **dreads** will come upon him, but the **desire of the righteous** will be granted" (v.24); "the **hope of the righteous** brings **joy**, but the expectation of the wicked will perish" (v.28). The finding: the inner being is a **creature of desire, dread, and hope** — and the *object/direction* of that desire decides its end *(stated: the righteous's desire granted, the wicked's craving thwarted)*. The operation: **hope issues in joy** (v.28) — expectation rightly placed is already a species of gladness, while the wicked's expectation is a *dread* that materialises *(inferred: v.24 pairs the wicked's dread with the righteous's desire as two futures the inner posture is already living toward; cf. Ps 112:7–8 the fearless vs the dread-filled, and the desire-met-in-God thread, Ps 145:19)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *desire, dread, hope, joy* carry the thread; sound. The wicked's *craving* (v.3) and the righteous's *desire* (v.24) are correctly distinguished — the same faculty (desire), opposite direction, exactly the Psalter's finding (honest longing met vs disordered craving thwarted, Ps 107:9 vs 106:14). Pairs sensible.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Dread as a lived future** (v.24) — "what the wicked dreads will come" — the inner being's fear is not merely felt but *anticipatory*, already inhabiting the feared outcome; a subtle characteristic (the self living toward what it dreads) distinct from present fear.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *desire / craving (directional)* (shared w/ Ps); *hope issuing in joy* (shared); *anticipatory dread (living toward the feared)* (new/less-obvious).

## PRO-10-diligence · Prov 10:4–5,26 · [T] — diligence vs sloth

**The reading.** "A **slack hand** causes poverty, but the hand of the **diligent** makes rich" (v.4); "he who **gathers in summer** is prudent, but he who **sleeps in harvest** brings shame" (v.5). The finding: diligence and sloth are **inner dispositions** (not merely behaviours) — a *slackness* vs an *industry* of the self that shows in timing (gather in summer / sleep in harvest) *(inferred: the contrast is of inner orientation toward one's work, the self that applies itself vs the self that lets the moment pass; a Proverbs-distinctive characteristic, lightly present in Ps 127:2's anxious-toil-vs-rest but here about industriousness itself)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *diligent* (H2742) tagged characteristic; *slack/sleeps/gathers* largely gate-2, read from structure. Sound; a thin but clear thread.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Timing/seasonality of the diligent self** (v.5) — not just "works hard" but *acts at the right season*; a discernment-of-the-moment folded into diligence.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *diligence vs sloth* (new — Proverbs-distinctive); *discernment of the season* (new/less-obvious).

## PRO-10-teachable · Prov 10:8,17 · [T] — receptivity / heeding instruction

**The reading.** "The **wise of heart** will **receive** commandments, but a babbling fool comes to ruin" (v.8); "whoever **heeds instruction** is on the **path to life**, but he who **rejects reproof** leads others astray" (v.17). The finding: **receptivity is a condition of the heart** — the "wise of heart" *receives*, the fool cannot; teachability is an inner posture, not an intellectual capacity *(stated: wise of heart receives)*. The operation: **heeding vs rejecting reproof** sets the very direction of the life ("path to life") — the self's openness to correction is life-or-death *(inferred; cf. the Psalter's teachable/asking-to-be-searched thread, Ps 139:23, 25:4–5)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *wise* (H2450), *heart* (H3820, seat) tagged; the "wise of heart" pair (wisdom seated in the heart) is sensible. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Receptivity as a *heart-condition*** (v.8, "wise of *heart* receives") — the substrate seats it in the heart; teachability is located *in the heart*, not the ears — a Proverbs sharpening.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *receptivity / teachability (heart-seated)* (shared w/ Ps, sharpened here).

## PRO-10-v12 · Prov 10:12 · [S, multi] — love vs hate as relational drivers

**The reading.** A self-contained gem: "**Hatred** stirs up strife, but **love** covers all offenses." The finding: **hatred and love are the two engines of relational life** — hatred *generates* conflict, love *absorbs* it *(stated)*. The operation: **love as a covering** — not merely a feeling but an act that *takes up and hides another's offense* rather than exposing it *(inferred: "covers" makes love active and self-giving — the opposite of the concealing/masking self of the speech thread, which hides its *own* evil; love hides *another's* fault; cf. Ps 32:1 "sin covered", 1 Pet 4:8)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *hatred* (H8135) and *love* (H0157, *aheb*) both tagged **characteristics** — the aheb-fix correctly makes "love" a characteristic here (relational love, not misdirected). Clean.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** The **covering** operation contrasts pointedly with the speech thread's *concealment*: the same motion (hiding) is love when it covers *another's* offense and wickedness when it hides *one's own* — a subtle inner-being distinction worth carrying.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *love vs hate as relational drivers* (shared w/ Ps); *love-as-covering* (new/less-obvious).

## PRO-10-fear · Prov 10:27 · [S] — the fear of the LORD lengthens life

**The reading.** "The **fear of the LORD** prolongs life, but the years of the wicked will be short." The finding: reverent fear is **life-extending** — the same root-characteristic as 1:7, here tied to the *duration* of the life it grounds *(stated)*. A brief re-attestation of the book's spine (fear-of-the-LORD thread).

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *fear* (H3374) tagged characteristic; sound (bearer bleed on "LORD" is cosmetic here).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the fear of the LORD (root)* (shared cornerstone — re-attested).

## Catch-up unit (2026-07-04) — loose study-term verse bound to a unit

**PRO-10-parents · Prov 10:1 · [S] — the child's wisdom or folly felt in the parent's heart.** "A **wise son makes a glad father**, but a **foolish son is a sorrow to his mother**" (v.1). Finding: a child's inner character registers as **joy or grief in the parent's interior** — wisdom gladdens, folly sorrows *(stated)*. Operation: **the interior bound across the generational tie** — one person's wisdom/folly is *felt* as another's gladness/sorrow; the inner life is not sealed but shared through love *(inferred; the relational transmission of inner states, recurs 17:21,25, 23:24–25; the heading proverb of the collection)*. *Surfaced:* the child's character felt in the parent's heart (new — inner states shared through the bond).
