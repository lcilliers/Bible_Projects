### Notable reads
The standout is **19:3 — the blame-shifting heart**: a man wrecks his own way by folly, then *rages against the LORD* for it (*zaaph* — a sharp psychological insight into self-deception, blaming God for self-caused ruin). The **wealth-and-friendship** motif read across 19:4/6/7 as a bleak, clear-eyed social observation (riches gather fair-weather friends; poverty is abandoned even by kin) — clear-eyed but, in light of 14:21/31 and 19:17, implicitly rebuked. And **19:8's good self-love** (getting wisdom *is* loving one's own soul — virtue as self-benefit). The **fittingness/order** theme (19:10, cf 17:7, 26:1 — goods misplaced when given to folly or authority to the unfit).
