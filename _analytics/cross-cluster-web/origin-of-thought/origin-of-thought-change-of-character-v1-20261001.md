## 2. What the thought words show (first inventory)

The verses come from `strong-thought.csv` and the open-thread pull. The *faces* are stated by the verses. The *circumstances that differ* are partly stated and partly my reading (marked R).

### 2.1 *mᵊzimmāh* (H4209): discretion · evil device · God's purpose

| Face | Verses | Who thinks | Joined with / aimed at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discretion** | Pro 1:4; 2:11; 3:21; 5:2; 8:12 | the young, the son, wisdom herself | knowledge, prudence, sound wisdom. It "watch[es] over you" (Pro 2:11) |
| **Evil device** | Pro 12:2; 14:17; 24:8; Ps 10:2, 4; 21:11; 37:7; 139:20; Job 21:27; Jer 11:15 | the wicked, the schemer, enemies | arrogance (Ps 10:2), pride (10:4), quick temper (Pro 14:17); aimed *against* the poor, against God, against "you" |
| **God's purpose** | Jer 23:20; 30:24; 51:11; Job 42:2 | God | "the intents of his heart"; cannot be thwarted |

**What changes it:** who is thinking, what the thinking is joined with (wisdom or arrogance), and whether it is aimed *for* someone (it "watch[es] over you") or *against* someone. **(R)** The faculty is the same; the company it keeps and its direction decide its character. The cluster tag (Malice) records only the second face. That is #1919's first data note, and it is not just a tagging question: **a cluster tag shows one face of a word and hides the others.**

### 2.2 *maḥăšāvāh* (H4284): design · plot · God's plan · a plan that needs counsel

| Face | Verses |
|---|---|
| **Craft and design** | Exo 31:4; 35:32–35; 2Ch 2:14; 26:15 ("invented by skillful men") |
| **Plot** | Pro 6:18; Est 8:3, 5; 9:25; Jer 11:19; 18:18; Lam 3:60–61; Ps 56:5; Job 21:27 |
| **God's plans** | Jer 29:11; Ps 33:11; 40:5; 92:5; Isa 55:8–9; Mic 4:12; 2Sa 14:14 ("devises means") |
| **God's plans *against*** | Jer 18:11; 49:20; 50:45; 51:29 |
| **Ordinary plans, in need of counsel or commitment** | Pro 15:22; 16:3; 20:18; 21:5 |
| **The righteous' thoughts** | Pro 12:5 ("the thoughts of the righteous are just") |

**One passage holds three faces together (Jeremiah 18).** God says, "Behold, I am shaping disaster against you and devising a plan against you" (18:11). The people answer, "We will follow our own plans, and will every one act according to the stubbornness of his evil heart" (18:12). Then they turn on the prophet: "Come, let us make plots against Jeremiah" (18:18).
- **(R)** God's plan *against* is a warning meant to bring them back ("Return, every one from his evil way", 18:11).
- Their own plans are stubbornness.
- Their plot *against* the messenger is the refusal turned outward.

The word is the same, and the *what for* differs completely.

**Wisdom literature gives plans a fourth character: unfinished.** They fail without counsel (Pro 15:22), are established by counsel (20:18) and by committing the work to the Lord (16:3), and reward diligence against haste (21:5). Thinking is completed with others and before God, not alone.

### 2.3 *ḥāšav* (H2803): to design · to count · to devise · to think

The sense variants in the data are design (19 rows), count/reckon (34), devise (44) and think (26). One verb covers making a pattern, assigning value, plotting and simply thinking. **(R)** The Hebrew holds these together as one capacity, and the English splits them. The change of character between them follows what is being *formed*: an object (design), a valuation (reckoning), a scheme (devising).

## 3. What seems to govern the change (to be tested, not imposed)

These factors keep recurring across the faces above. They are **observations to test against more verses, not a grid to fill**. They sit beside the existing statement in Ch 11 §4: "What decides is not the word but what the inner being is yielding to, or holding firm against."

1. **Who is thinking:** God · the wise · the wicked · a crowd (*mᵊzimmāh*; *maḥăšāvāh*).
2. **Toward or against whom:** "watch over you" vs "against me"; God's plan *for* welfare (Jer 29:11) vs *against* Babylon.
3. **What it is joined with:** wisdom and knowledge vs arrogance, pride and quick temper.
4. **The state of the heart it rises from:** troubled but honest (Luk 1:29) vs hardened (Mar 8:17).
5. **Alone or together, and whether counsel is taken** (Pro 15:22; Luk 20:14).
6. **Where and when:** the bed and the night (Ps 36:4 vs 63:6).
7. **What it leads to, and what comes back** (flow 5: consequences return to the one who devised).
8. **The narrative before and after** (Jer 18:11–18: God's warning, the refusal, the plot).

## 4. Proposal

### 4.1 Method: add change of character to the weaving method

For every strand, the ledger gains a required section **"Change of character"**. For each word or concept with more than one face, it records:
- the faces and their verses
- the circumstances that differ (who, toward whom, joined with, heart state, setting, what follows, narrative context)
- the *why*, stated or reading

A face is never dropped because the narrative already used another face of the word. **Data rule:** a strand pulls its words *by word across all clusters*, never by cluster, because a cluster tag shows one face only. The thought datasets already did this. #1919's notes show why it matters.

### 4.2 Narrative: where change of character lives

- **In each operation section:** a passage on *when this activity changes character, and why*. In 10.3 this would gather what is now spread across it (design and plot; reckoning wrongly and truly; troubled and hardened reasoning; the bed), and add *mᵊzimmāh*'s three faces and Jeremiah 18.
- **In Ch 11 §4 ("One word, opposite values"):** this grows from a list of examples into the cross-cutting account. It covers what governs change across all the activities, with §3 above tested against the verses, and it stays open as each strand adds to it.
- **Flows 3 and 5:** "thoughts that answer back" and "what is devised comes back on the deviser" are woven into 10.3 and 10.0, so OT-H2 is reopened.
