## 1. The original question

> **Leroux:** This is a philosophical question: what is the origin of a thought.

### Preliminary overview (Claude)

This is still an open question. There isn't one agreed answer, only several serious positions, and each one mostly answers a slightly different version of the question.

**1. Neuroscience: thoughts come from brain activity**
- **The claim:** A thought is what networks of neurons do. It's shaped by what you perceive, what you remember, your body's state and your prior patterns.
- **Evidence:** Brain imaging, lesion studies, and Libet-type experiments. Those experiments showed brain activity starting before people reported deciding, though what they prove is still debated.
- **Limit:** This explains how thoughts are carried out physically. It doesn't explain why there is something it feels like to think. That gap is David Chalmers' "hard problem" of consciousness.

**2. Phenomenology and contemplative traditions: thoughts arrive**
- **The claim:** Watch your mind closely and you'll notice you don't author most thoughts. They show up. Nietzsche said "a thought comes when *it* wants, not when *I* want." Buddhist practice treats thoughts as passing events, not as a self producing them.
- **Evidence:** First-person observation, which anyone can test for themselves.
- **Implication:** The "I" may be the witness of thoughts more than their origin.

**3. Philosophy of mind: thoughts come from the mind, whatever the mind is**
- **Dualism (Descartes):** Thought comes from a non-physical mind that interacts with the body.
- **Physicalism:** Thought is entirely physical. This is the view most neuroscientists hold.
- **Emergentism:** Thought arises from physical processes but can't be reduced to them. The whole is more than its parts.
- **Idealism and panpsychism:** Mind is basic, and matter is secondary or itself has something mind-like in it.

**4. Social and linguistic views: thoughts come from outside the individual**
- **The claim:** Vygotsky argued we internalise language and conversation, so much of thinking is inner speech learned from others. Wittgenstein argued there can be no truly private language.
- **Implication:** Your thoughts have a social and cultural ancestry. They aren't purely yours.

**5. Theological views: thoughts come from the inner being**
- **The Hebrew Bible:** The source is the *lēv* (heart), not the brain. Examples are Proverbs 4:23 ("from it flow the springs of life") and Genesis 6:5 ("every intention of the thoughts of his heart").
- **The New Testament:** Mark 7:21 says "from within, out of the heart, come evil thoughts." The heart here is the moral and volitional centre of the person.
- **Classical theology:** Some thoughts are also said to come from outside influences, such as divine prompting or temptation. Paul, for example, speaks of "taking every thought captive" (2 Corinthians 10:5).
- **Implication:** This view is less about how thoughts are produced and more about which part of the person they belong to and who is responsible for them.

**Claude's assessment (its view, not settled fact):** These answers can be layered rather than treated as rivals:
- The *mechanism* is neural.
- The *material* is mostly inherited: language, culture and memory.
- The *experience* is that thoughts arrive rather than being made.
- The *ownership* question, which part of me a thought belongs to, is where philosophy and theology do their real work.

The real disagreement is whether anything exists beyond the physical process. That's a metaphysical commitment, not something the evidence currently settles.

## 2. The neuroscience presumption

> **Leroux:** From a neuroscience perspective, if it is neural activity, then it means that the constituent parts of thought must exist in the brain. Is that the presumption?

### Claude

Yes, mostly, though the presumption needs some careful wording.

**What neuroscience does presume.** The working assumption is that thought depends entirely on physical processes, mainly in the brain. If you change the brain, you change the thought. There is strong evidence for this dependence:
- **Lesions and injury.** Damage to particular regions removes particular abilities, such as language (Broca's and Wernicke's areas) or recognising faces (prosopagnosia).
- **Direct stimulation.** Penfield's electrical stimulation of the cortex during surgery triggered memories, sensations and urges.
- **Drugs, anaesthesia and sleep.** These change or switch off thought in predictable ways.
- **Decoding.** Brain-imaging studies can partly reconstruct what someone is seeing or intending from their brain activity.

**Where "constituent parts" needs correcting.** Neuroscience does not presume that thoughts are stored in the brain as finished items, waiting to be retrieved. The current picture is different:
- **Distributed patterns.** A thought is a pattern of activity across many neurons at a given moment. It isn't located in one place. Your concept of "dog" is spread across networks for sight, sound, words and emotion.
- **Reconstruction.** Memories are rebuilt each time you recall them, which is why they change and can be falsely confident. Key research here is Bartlett (1932), Schacter's work on memory and Loftus on false memories.
- **Capacities, not contents.** What is stored is the *disposition* to produce a thought: synaptic connections shaped by experience. The thought itself exists only when that disposition is activated.

*Analogy:* a piano doesn't contain the music. It contains the capacity to produce it when played. The neuroscience claim is that the brain holds the "tuned instrument", and a thought is one performance of it.

**Where the presumption is contested.**
1. **Correlation vs identity.** Neuroscience has established that brain activity *correlates with* thought and that thought *depends on* the brain. That thought simply *is* brain activity is a philosophical step beyond the data. The field of "neural correlates of consciousness" (Crick and Koch) was deliberately named to avoid claiming identity.
2. **Methodological vs metaphysical.** Science has to assume physical causes to do its work (methodological naturalism). That doesn't prove nothing else exists (metaphysical naturalism). Many scientists don't separate the two, but philosophers do.
3. **Brain only, or brain plus more?** The "embodied" and "extended mind" views (Clark and Chalmers, 1998) argue that thought also involves the body, emotions, gut signals and even external tools like notebooks. So its parts may not be confined to the skull.
4. **Meaning.** Even a complete map of neural activity doesn't obviously explain why a pattern is *about* something, like your mother or justice. Philosophers call this the problem of *intentionality*, and it is still unresolved.

**Summary.**
- **Established:** Thought depends on the brain, and its raw material is laid down there as patterns and dispositions shaped by experience.
- **Not established:** That the brain alone contains everything thought is made of, or that brain activity fully explains what a thought *is* and what it *means*.

## 3. Three lenses

> **Leroux:** I am exploring it as part of the process of understanding thought better. And science, philosophy and the Bible all explore it in their own way.

### Claude

That's a good way to approach it. Each of the three asks a different question about thought, and a lot of confusion comes from using one to answer another's question.

| Lens | Its question | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Science | *How* does thought happen? | Mechanism: what thought depends on and how it is produced |
| Philosophy | *What* is a thought? | Its nature, what it means, how it relates to the self and to reality |
| Bible | *Where in the person* does it come from, and what is it oriented toward? | Its source in the inner being, its moral direction, and its relation to God |

**Where they seem to agree (Claude's reading, worth testing).**
1. **Thought isn't simply self-authored.**
   - Science: most processing is automatic and unconscious. Predictive-processing models describe the brain as constantly generating expectations before we are aware of them.
   - Philosophy: phenomenology observes that thoughts arrive rather than being made.
   - Bible: the heart runs deeper than we can see into. Jeremiah 17:9–10 says the heart is beyond knowing except by God, and in Psalm 139:23 the psalmist asks God to "know my thoughts."
2. **Thought is shaped by what it takes in and dwells on.**
   - Science: neuroplasticity. Repeated patterns strengthen connections.
   - Philosophy: Vygotsky's view that language and culture are internalised.
   - Bible: Proverbs 4:23 (guard the heart), Romans 12:2 (be transformed by the renewing of the mind) and Philippians 4:8 (think on these things).

**Where they differ.**
- **Location.** Hebrew anthropology places thinking in the *lēv* (heart), which covers reasoning, will and emotion together. That is a description of the inner person, not a claim about anatomy. Reading it as either "brain" or "feelings" imposes a modern category on it.
- **External sources.** Scripture allows that thoughts can be prompted from outside the person, by God, spirit or temptation. Science excludes such causes by method (it can't test them), not by proof.
- **What counts as knowing.** Science relies on repeatable observation, philosophy on reasoned argument, Scripture on revelation. Comparing conclusions needs you to keep those grounds of evidence distinct.

**Two method cautions.**
- **Concordism.** Don't force matches, for example "renewing of the mind = neuroplasticity." The parallel is suggestive, but the two claims are made on different grounds and mean different things.
- **Reductionism in either direction.** Saying "thought is *only* neurons" or "the heart *isn't* physical at all" goes beyond what its own discipline can show.

## 4. First dataset: `span-thought.csv` (English surface "thought")

> **Leroux:** Attached is a CSV with verses in the text column. These verses all have "thought" in common. Can you synthesise these verses in relation to the origin of thought, and reflect on what thoughts are.

**The dataset.** 84 rows (82 unique verse entries). ESV text. Every row was selected because the English word "thought" appears.

### 4.1 Where thoughts come from

**1. From within: the heart is where they come from.**
- Mark 7:21 and Matthew 15:19 say it directly: "from within, out of the heart … come evil thoughts."
- Genesis 6:5 speaks of "the intention of the thoughts of his heart," and Luke 1:51 of "the thoughts of their hearts."
- Also Hebrews 4:12, Luke 2:35, 1 Chronicles 29:18, Matthew 9:4 and Luke 5:22.

In these verses thoughts are never located in the head. They come out of the heart, the centre of the person.

**2. Thoughts arise before they are chosen.** Several verses describe thoughts that happen *to* the person:
- Ezekiel 38:10 has two stages: "thoughts will **come into** your mind, **and** you will devise an evil scheme." First something rises, then the person works on it. **[lexical]** The Hebrew there is *dᵉvārîm* (H1697, "words" or "things"), literally "things will come up on your heart."
- In Job 20:2, "my thoughts answer me, because of my haste within me." The thoughts *respond to* an inner state.
- In Daniel 7:28, "my thoughts greatly alarmed me." The thoughts act on the person.
- In Romans 2:15, "conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse them." They are like inner voices arguing with each other.

So in these verses the person is both where thoughts come from and on the receiving end of them.

**3. Outside influence acts on the heart.**
- 2 Corinthians 11:3: thoughts can be "led astray," as the serpent deceived Eve.
- 1 Chronicles 29:18: God is asked to "keep … such thoughts in the hearts of your people."
- 2 Corinthians 10:5: thoughts can be "taken captive."
- Proverbs 16:20: giving thought to "the word" leads to good.

**4. Knowing a thought is separate from producing it.** 1 Corinthians 2:11 says only "the spirit of that person" knows a person's thoughts, and only God's Spirit knows God's. Separately, God searches and knows human thoughts (Psalm 139:23; 1 Chronicles 28:9; Psalm 94:11; 1 Corinthians 3:20), and so does Jesus (Luke 5:22, 6:8, 11:17; Matthew 9:4, 12:25).
*Caveat:* in 1 Corinthians 2:11 the Strong's number is G3588, the Greek article. The word "thoughts" is supplied by the translation; the Greek says "the things of a person."

### 4.2 What thoughts are

1. **Inner speech.** 21 of the rows (H0559, *ʾāmar*, "to say") are places where the Hebrew says "he *said*" and the ESV renders it "he thought," as in 1 Samuel 18:11: "Saul thought, 'I will pin David…'" The Greek *dialogismos* (G1261) and *logismos* (G3053) are also built on *logos*, "word" **[lexical]**. In these texts, to think is to speak inwardly. *Caveat:* how many rows show this depends on the ESV's choices.
2. **Devising or planning.** H4284 *maḥăšāvāh* (from H2803 *ḥāšav*, "to devise"). These thoughts are designs with a direction. They can be just (Proverbs 12:5), evil (Isaiah 59:7), or God's own plan (Micah 4:12). Because they point somewhere, they can be judged, and they can be abandoned (Isaiah 55:7).
3. **Likening, or interpreting.** H1819 *dāmāh* ("to resemble") is used in Psalm 50:21: "you thought that I was one like yourself." Many rows record thoughts that were **wrong**: Genesis 38:15, Mark 6:49, Luke 24:37, Matthew 20:10, Acts 12:9, Judges 3:24 and 2 Kings 5:11. So a thought is a reading of reality, not reality itself.
4. **Something that grows and matures.** 1 Corinthians 13:11: "I thought like a child … I gave up childish ways."
5. **Transient, yet weighty.** Human thoughts are "but a breath" (Psalm 94:11) and "futile" (1 Corinthians 3:20). Yet God weighs them (Proverbs 15:26), and before the flood they were the measure of human wickedness (Genesis 6:5). God's thoughts, by contrast, are "very deep" (Psalm 92:5), "vast" (Psalm 139:17) and "higher" (Isaiah 55:8–9).

### 4.3 Pulling it together (Claude's reading)

Taken together, the verses give this picture:
- A thought comes up out of the heart as inner speech.
- It is shaped both by the person's inner state and by outside influence.
- It interprets reality by likening one thing to another, and can be wrong.
- It develops into intent, and that intent has a moral direction.
- It is fully known only to the person's own spirit and to God.

The verses don't explain *how* a thought is produced; they have no interest in the mechanism. What they do insist on is *where* it comes from (the heart), *where it is heading* (its moral direction), and *who can see it* (the self's spirit and God). That means they answer a different question from neuroscience, rather than contradicting it.

### 4.4 Limits of this set
- **The English word "thought" picks up rows that aren't about the inner life.** 1 Timothy 3:7 ("well thought of"), Acts 15:38, 2 Corinthians 9:5 and Philippians 2:25.
- **Duplicate rows.** Job 20:2 and Acts 26:8 each appear twice, under different clusters.
- **Key verses are missing because the ESV uses other English words there.** For example, Proverbs 4:23, Jeremiah 17:9 and Romans 12:2.

### 6.1 What the full pull adds

- **Craft:** Exodus 31:4 and 35:32–35 ("to devise artistic designs", "skilled designer"), 2 Chronicles 2:14 ("execute any design"), 2 Chronicles 26:15 ("invented by skillful men").

## 7. Debugging the extraction query

> **Leroux:** Can you debug this query? It seems to not pick up all the verses. (SQLite)

**Causes found (Claude):**
1. The query started from `cluster INNER JOIN cluster_strong`, so any Strong's number not assigned to a cluster was excluded. The `WHERE strong.strongNumber IN (...)` clause also cancelled the `LEFT JOIN`s.
2. It used exact matches (`=` / `IN`), but the data stores variant codes with suffix letters (`H2803I`, `H5587B`) and combined tags (`H4284 H9022`, where the extra codes mark attached parts of the word). Any word with a suffix letter or an attached part was missed.
3. Strong's numbers assigned to more than one cluster produced duplicate rows.

**Fix.** Start from `span`, match on the base number with `LIKE '%' || base || '%'`, `LEFT JOIN` the cluster tables, `GROUP_CONCAT(DISTINCT cluster_code)`, and order by `verse.id`.

**Result.** The list grew from 85 rows to 235. Every previously flagged gap was recovered: Ps 139:2, Dan 2:29 / 2:30 / 4:19 / 5:6 / 5:10, Jer 4:14, Prov 16:3, Rom 1:21, Luke 9:46, Job 4:13. Leroux then added H4209 and H6250, bringing it to **255 rows**.

**Remaining data notes.**
- Combined-tag rows have no gloss or cluster, because the joins to `strong` and `cluster_strong` still match exactly. A suggested join picks the number out of the tag instead: `' ' || sp.strong_variant || ' ' LIKE '% ' || s.strongNumber || ' %'`.
- Duplicate rows are mostly genuine repeated words in the same verse (Isa 55:8–9, 59:7; Gen 50:20; Exod 35:35; Jer 29:11).
- The H4209 rows that do have a cluster are all tagged M06 (Malice & Enmity), including the "discretion" rows (Prov 2:11, 5:2, 8:12). Worth reviewing.
- H2803H ("count", 34 rows) is mostly bookkeeping and could be tagged T3. A few rows are theologically weighty: Gen 15:6, Ps 32:2, Isa 53:3–4.

## 9. Updated synthesis (from the 255-row list)

### 9.1 Where thought comes from

**1. The heart or mind is the place thoughts come from.** Mark 7:21 and Matt 15:19 ("out of the heart come evil thoughts"). Also:
- "a heart that devises wicked plans" (Prov 6:18)
- "plan evil things in their heart" (Ps 140:2)
- "devise evil … in your heart" (Zech 7:10; 8:17)
- "The heart of man plans his way" (Prov 16:9)
- "his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy" (Isa 10:7)
- "the reasoning of their hearts" (Luke 9:47)

In Jer 4:14, thoughts *lodge* within: "How long shall your wicked thoughts lodge within you?" This follows the call to "wash your heart."

**2. Thoughts arrive, often at night.**
- "as you lay in bed came thoughts of what would be" (Dan 2:29)
- "thoughts from visions of the night, when deep sleep falls on men" (Job 4:13)
- "thoughts will come into your mind" (Ezek 38:10)
- "doubts arise in your hearts" (Luke 24:38)
- "an argument arose among them" (Luke 9:46)

In Dan 2:29–30, the thoughts that come in the night are tied to revelation: "he who reveals mysteries made known to you."

**3. The bed is also where people devise deliberately.**
- "He plots trouble while on his bed" (Ps 36:4)
- "devise wickedness … on their beds! When the morning dawns, they perform it, because it is in the power of their hand" (Mic 2:1)

The same place hosts thoughts that come unbidden (Dan 2:29) and thoughts that are worked on on purpose (Ps 36:4).

**4. The source is shaped from outside.**
- **By God:** he writes his laws on minds and hearts (Heb 8:10; 10:16) and fills craftsmen with skill "to devise artistic designs" (Exod 35:32–35).
- **By instruction:** Proverbs is written "to give … discretion (*mᵊzimmāh*) to the youth" (Prov 1:4), and Wisdom "find[s] … discretion" (Prov 8:12).
- **By counsel:** "Plans are established by counsel" (Prov 20:18; 15:22).
- **Against God:** minds blinded by "the god of this world" (2 Cor 4:4), "his designs" (2 Cor 2:11), and "hearts were darkened" alongside futile thinking (Rom 1:21).

**5. God is a thinker too, and his thinking has the last word.** The same vocabulary is used of God:
- *maḥăšāvāh*: Jer 29:11; Ps 33:11; 40:5; 92:5; Isa 55:8–9
- *ḥāšav*: Jer 18:11; 2 Sam 14:14; Lam 2:8; "the Lord takes thought for me" (Ps 40:17); "that you think of him" (Ps 144:3)
- *mᵊzimmāh*: "the intents of his heart" (Jer 23:20); "no purpose of yours can be thwarted" (Job 42:2)

In Gen 50:20 there are two opposite intentions about one event, expressed with the same verb: "you meant evil … God meant it for good." Human plans are real, but "it is the purpose of the Lord that will stand" (Prov 19:21; also Prov 16:9; Ps 33:10–11; Job 5:12).

### 9.2 What thoughts are

1. **Design: forming something that does not yet exist.** H2803G (19 rows) describes craftsmanship: cherubim "skillfully worked" (Exod 26:1, 31) and "skilled design" (Exod 39:3). The faculty itself is neutral. *Maḥăšāvāh* covers both artistry and plots, and *mᵊzimmāh* covers both "discretion" (Prov 2:11) and "evil devices" (Prov 12:2). What makes a thought good or evil is **what it is designed toward**.
2. **Reckoning: giving something its value or category.** H2803H/J (60 rows):
   - Human misreckoning: Eli "took her to be a drunken woman" (1 Sam 1:13), "we esteemed him stricken" and "we esteemed him not" (Isa 53:3–4), Judah "thought she was a prostitute" (Gen 38:15).
   - God's reckoning: "he counted it to him as righteousness" (Gen 15:6), "the Lord counts no iniquity" (Ps 32:2), and the nations "accounted as the dust on the scales" (Isa 40:15, 17).

   To think is to judge the worth of something, and people can judge wrongly.
3. **Inner speech.**
   - "all his thoughts are, 'There is no God'" (Ps 10:4). The thought is quoted as a sentence.
   - Hannah "was speaking in her heart; only her lips moved" (1 Sam 1:13).
   - *Dialogismos* covers both inner reasoning and outward "argument", "quarreling" and "disputing".
   - "conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse" (Rom 2:15).
4. **Something that affects the body and emotions.**
   - "his thoughts alarmed him; his limbs gave way, and his knees knocked together" (Dan 5:6; also 4:19; 5:10; 7:28).
   - "the cares of my heart" (Ps 94:19), and "haste within me" (Job 20:2).
   - A divided mind stalls a person: "limping between two different opinions" (1 Kgs 18:21, H5587A).
5. **Creaturely and passing.**
   - "but a breath" (Ps 94:11).
   - "When his breath departs … on that very day his plans perish" (Ps 146:4).
   - "futile" (1 Cor 3:20; Rom 1:21).
   - "striving of heart … striving after wind" (Eccl 2:22; 4:16).
6. **Fully open to God.**
   - "you discern my thoughts from afar" (Ps 139:2); also Ps 139:23; 1 Chr 28:9; "I know their works and their thoughts" (Isa 66:18); Heb 4:12.
   - Jesus knows people's thoughts (Luke 5:22; 6:8; 9:47; 11:17; Matt 9:4; 12:25).

### 9.3 From thought to action (Claude's reading)

The verses trace a path from thought to deed:

1. **Arising.** Thoughts come, often in the night (Dan 2:29; Job 4:13; Ezek 38:10; Luke 24:38).
2. **Lodging.** They stay and are dwelt on (Jer 4:14).
3. **Reckoning.** They weigh up and value things (Isa 53:4; 1 Sam 1:13).
4. **Devising.** They take the form of a plan (Ezek 38:10b; Ps 36:4).
5. **Expression.** Tongue, lips and eyes: "your tongue plots" (Ps 52:2), "devise words of deceit" (Ps 35:20), "winks his eyes plans" (Prov 16:30), "whisper … imagine the worst" (Ps 41:7).
6. **Action.** Hand and feet: "when the morning dawns, they perform it" (Mic 2:1); "a heart that devises … feet that make haste" (Prov 6:18); "their feet run to evil … their thoughts are thoughts of iniquity" (Isa 59:7).

The same path also runs toward good: "When I think on my ways, I turn my feet to your testimonies" (Ps 119:59).

**Where responsibility sits.** The commands address the stages from lodging onward:
- "forsake … his thoughts" (Isa 55:7)
- "let none of you devise evil" (Zech 7:10)
- "take every thought captive" (2 Cor 10:5)
- "wash your heart" (Jer 4:14)

The *arising* is not commanded, but it is not neutral either. What comes up shows the condition of the heart (Gen 6:5; Mark 7:21). So thoughts that arise reveal the heart, and devising engages the will. The heart itself can be changed from outside: written on, given discretion, guarded, or darkened.

**Relation to the earlier discussion.** These verses give no mechanism. They answer where in the person thoughts come from, what they are directed toward, who sees them, and where responsibility lies. Their stress on thoughts *arising* before they are chosen sits alongside the phenomenological observation (§1), without either depending on the other.

### 9.4 Limits
- The texts are ESV. Some English renderings, such as "took her" and "itself", obscure the Hebrew verb.
- The H2803H accounting rows (Lev 25, 27; 2 Kgs 12, 22) add little beyond the idea of reckoning.
- Cluster assignments are incomplete for combined-tag rows, and questionable for H4209 in M06.
- These inner-life words are not yet pulled: H3336 *yēṣer*; H0559 *ʾāmar* in "say in (the) heart"; H1897 *hāgāh*; H1819 *dāmāh*; G3049 *logizomai*; G1260 *dialogizomai*; and *lēv / kardia* themselves.

## 11. Thought as a system

> **Leroux:** It seems that thought in operation is a network of interrelated phenomena that all flow together in different directions, like a system of thought.

### Claude's response

The data supports this, and it corrects the straight-line path set out in §9.3. That path from arising to action is one route through the system, not the whole of it. The verses show flows in at least six directions.

**1. Outward: heart → speech → body → action.**
- "a heart that devises wicked plans, feet that make haste" (Prov 6:18)
- "your tongue plots" (Ps 52:2); "winks his eyes plans" (Prov 16:30)
- "when the morning dawns, they perform it, because it is in the power of their hand" (Mic 2:1)

**2. Inward: outside influences → heart and mind.**
- God writes on minds and hearts (Heb 8:10; 10:16) and fills people with skill (Exod 35:35).
- Instruction gives discretion (Prov 1:4).
- Counsel establishes plans (Prov 15:22; 20:18).
- The serpent leads thoughts astray (2 Cor 11:3), and "the god of this world" blinds (2 Cor 4:4).

**3. Within: thoughts acting on the self.**
- "my thoughts answer me, because of my haste within me" (Job 20:2)
- "conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse" (Rom 2:15)
- "my thoughts greatly alarmed me" (Dan 7:28)
- "limping between two different opinions" (1 Kgs 18:21)

**4. From thought into the body.**
- "his thoughts alarmed him; his limbs gave way, and his knees knocked together" (Dan 5:6)
- "my color changed" (Dan 7:28)

**5. Back again: consequences returning to the one who devised.**
- "his evil plan … should return on his own head" (Esth 9:25)
- "the fruit of their devices" (Jer 6:19)
- "let them be caught in the schemes that they have devised" (Ps 10:2)
- "The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance" (Prov 21:5)

**6. Vertical: between God's thinking and human thinking.**
- God searches, knows and discerns thoughts (Ps 139:2, 23; 1 Chr 28:9; Heb 4:12).
- God reckons (Gen 15:6; Ps 32:2).
- God overrules: "you meant evil … God meant it for good" (Gen 50:20); "it is the purpose of the Lord that will stand" (Prov 19:21).
- Human thought can also turn back toward God: "When I think on my ways, I turn my feet to your testimonies" (Ps 119:59).

**The vocabulary itself points to a system.** A single verb, *ḥāšav*, covers designing, devising, thinking and reckoning, so the Hebrew treats these as operations of one faculty rather than separate activities. The English splits apart what the Hebrew holds together (§10).

**A caution about method.** "System" is an interpretive model brought to the texts; the texts don't set out a system themselves. It's a useful lens as long as it is tested against the data rather than imposed on it. Two tests: look for verses that don't fit any of the six flows, and avoid importing modern systems theory as though it were the biblical frame (see the concordism caution in §3).

**A possible next step.** Map the system as nodes (heart/mind, thoughts, speech, body, action, others, God, consequences) and directional flows, with each connection backed by at least one verse from the dataset.

### 12.2 Letting the data show the neighbours (recommended first pull)

Rather than choosing domains by hand, ask the database which other Strong's numbers occur **in the same verses** as the thought vocabulary.

*Claude's first version stalled.* Its `LIKE '% ' || ... || '%'` joins on the space-delimited `span.strong_variant` could not use an index (roughly 45M string comparisons). Leroux's working version (v2) uses the database's own `verse_lexical` table instead. That table has one row per Strong's code per span and is indexed on `strong` and `verse_id`; the query runs in about 1s:

```sql
WITH targets(base) AS (
  VALUES ('H2803'),('H4284'),('H4209'),('H6250'),('H7454'),('H7475'),('H8312'),
         ('H5587'),('H7476'),('H6248'),('G1261'),('G1761'),('G1270'),('G3540'),
         ('G1271'),('G3053')
),
thought_verses AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT vl.verse_id
  FROM verse_lexical vl
  JOIN targets t ON vl.strong LIKE t.base || '%'
  WHERE vl.deleted = 0
)
SELECT c.cluster_code, c.short_name, vl.strong AS strongNumber, s.stepGloss,
       COUNT(DISTINCT vl.verse_id) AS shared_verses
FROM verse_lexical vl
JOIN thought_verses tv ON tv.verse_id = vl.verse_id
JOIN strong s          ON s.strongNumber = vl.strong
JOIN cluster_strong cs ON cs.strong = vl.strong
JOIN cluster c         ON c.cluster_code = cs.cluster_code
WHERE vl.deleted = 0
  AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM targets t WHERE vl.strong LIKE t.base || '%')
GROUP BY c.cluster_code, c.short_name, vl.strong, s.stepGloss
ORDER BY shared_verses DESC;
```
