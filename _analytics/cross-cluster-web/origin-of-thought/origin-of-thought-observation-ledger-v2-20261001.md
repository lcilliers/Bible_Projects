**maḥăšāvāh (H4284): six faces.** These are as in the proposal §2.2, confirmed by all 56 rows, with these additions:
- **Thoughts to be forsaken:** "let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts" (Isa 55:7). The next line gives the answer: "for he will abundantly pardon".
- **Own devices against God's outstretched hands:** "I spread out my hands all the day to a rebellious people, who walk in a way that is not good, following their own devices" (Isa 65:2).
- **Pure words against impure thoughts:** "The thoughts of the wicked are an abomination to the Lord, but gracious words are pure" (Pro 15:26).
- **The crafty frustrated:** "He frustrates the devices of the crafty … He catches the wise in their own craftiness" (Job 5:12–13; 5:13 read as context).
- **Multiplying thoughts, toward us:** "your thoughts toward us; none can compare with you" (Psa 40:5).

**What changes it:** whose thought it is, toward or against whom, whether counsel is taken, haste or diligence. Jer 18:11–18 holds three faces in one passage (proposal §2.2). Jer 18:8 makes God's face conditional.

**mᵊzimmāh (H4209): three faces**, confirmed by all 19 rows (proposal §2.1). Two additions:
- **Quick temper and evil devices in one proverb:** "A man of quick temper acts foolishly, and a man of evil devices is hated" (Pro 14:17). Temper and scheming are set side by side. This is a link to the anger strand.
- **God's purpose is carried by his anger:** "The anger of the Lord will not turn back until he has executed and accomplished the intents of his heart. In the latter days you will understand it clearly" (Jer 23:20; 30:24). The word for discretion and for evil device is here God's *intent*, and his anger serves it. Understanding comes only afterwards. This is a link to the anger strand.

### I.3 The proposal's eight factors, tested against the verses

| # | Factor (proposal §3) | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who is thinking | **Confirmed, refined** | God, the wise, the wicked, a crowd (all words). **Refinement:** "who" alone does not settle it. God's own devising *against* is conditional (Jer 18:8; 26:3). Caiaphas's calculation is taken up as prophecy (Joh 11:51) |

**What the test shows (R):** Ch 11 §4's sentence, *"What decides is not the word but what the inner being is yielding to, or holding firm against"*, holds. The verses fill it out:
- **Thinking does not change character by itself.** It changes by its **direction** (for or against), its **company** (wisdom or arrogance, counsel or a whispering circle), the **state of the heart** it rises from, its **pace**, and **whether it is brought before God**.
- **What it comes back as** shows which character it had.
