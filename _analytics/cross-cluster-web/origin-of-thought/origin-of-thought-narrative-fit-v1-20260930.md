## 1. Short answer

The origin-of-thought work is a **strand** in the sense of the fan-out rule. It is not a separate essay. It cuts across the narrative in the same way the thought vocabulary cuts across clusters (devising → Will & Resolve, thinking → Knowing & Understanding, plotting → Malice & Enmity, reasoning → Reasoning & Interpretation, heart → Inner Seat).

About half of its Scripture findings are **already in the narrative**, placed under the heart and the inner being at work. The other half is **new**. Its strongest new contribution is a **movement**: thought arises → lodges → reckons → devises → comes out in speech → becomes action. That is exactly the book's theme, *Inner Being in Operation*, and no chapter has it yet.

## 3. New — not yet in any chapter

1. **The path from thought to deed (§9.3).** Arising (Dan 2:29; Job 4:13; Luk 24:38) → lodging (Jer 4:14) → reckoning (Isa 53:4) → devising (Ps 36:4) → expression through tongue, lips and eyes (Ps 52:2; Pro 16:30) → action through hand and feet (Mic 2:1; Pro 6:18; Isa 59:7). The same path runs toward good: "When I think on my ways, I turn my feet to your testimonies" (Ps 119:59). Commands address the stages from lodging onward. Arising is not commanded, but it shows the state of the heart.
2. **Thinking as reckoning, i.e. assigning worth (§9.2.2).** Human misreckoning (Gen 38:15; 1Sa 1:13; Isa 53:3–4) against God's reckoning (Gen 15:6; Ps 32:2; Isa 40:15, 17). This links directly to justification language. It has no home in the narrative yet.
3. **Thoughts that arrive, especially at night (§9.1.2–3).** The bed is where thoughts come unbidden (Dan 2:29) and also where people plot on purpose (Ps 36:4; Mic 2:1). The night thoughts are tied to revelation (Dan 2:29–30).
4. **Thought moves the body (§9.2.4).** "his thoughts alarmed him; his limbs gave way" (Dan 5:6); the divided mind "limping between two different opinions" (1Ki 18:21). This fits Ch 6 (flesh) or Ch 11 §3 ("The parts give way in sequence").
5. **Outbound hearing: God hears the plots (§13.1).** "You have heard their taunts … all their plots" (Lam 3:61); Mal 3:16. The narrative has hearing only as the inbound way in. Your comment in the discussion: *"Yes, hearing is an integral part of the outbound thought."*
6. **Design and craft as the good form of devising (§9.2.1).** Exod 26:1; 35:32–35: God fills craftsmen with skill "to devise artistic designs".

- **A. Distribute it.** Treat thought as a strand, like the fear and anger strands. It updates Ch 3, 10, 11, 12, 13 (and 6, 7, 9 lightly), each with a version bump and a claim-register entry, following the index's own "How later work updates this".

**My recommendation: B.** Ch 10 already describes the inner being at work by activity, and it has no "thinking" activity. A new chapter (C) would repeat Ch 3's heart material. Under your prose rule, any chapter text is written only on your specific instruction.
