## Differences and inferences

- morph reviewed for G3429: all 3 occurrences are passive/deponent (V-APN, V-PNI-3S x2) — no active-voice occurrence exists in this corpus. This matches LSJ's own note that the verb's live NT usage is passive, 'of a man' and 'of a woman' alike — the voice pattern is consistent across all occurrences, not a distinction to explore further, but stated here per the round-2 rule that a checked absence of variation is itself worth recording.
  *[strong=G3429]*

- The aorist indicative at Matt 5:28 is meaning-critical, not incidental: Jesus's teaching depends on the tense specifically stating the act as already, completely done at the moment of the inward look — a present or future tense here would teach something weaker (that lust leads toward adultery, not that it already constitutes it). This is exactly the kind of morph-driven distinction round 1 had no rule requiring it to surface.
  *[strong=G3431 · occurrences=[Matt.5.28 surface='adultery' morph=V-AAI-3S]]*

- James 2:11 itself carries two morphs of moicheuō in the same verse — an aorist subjunctive prohibition ('do not commit adultery,' repeating the command) and a present active indicative ('if you do not commit adultery but do murder') — the same verse shifts from quoting the command to describing the reader's actual, ongoing situation, marked by the tense/mood change alone.
  *[strong=G3431 · occurrences=[Jas.2.11 surface='commit adultery' morph=V-PAI-2S]]*

- morph reviewed for G4202's 23 occurrences: all noun-case variation (genitive of source, dative of circumstance, accusative of object, nominative as subject) driven by ordinary sentence syntax, not by a distinct sense of the word itself — no stem/voice distinction applies since it never appears as a verb in this corpus.
  *[strong=G4202]*

- 1 Cor 10:8 carries porneuō twice with two different morphs in one verse: a present active subjunctive ('let us not indulge,' hortatory — the Corinthians' live, present temptation) and an aorist active indicative ('as some of them did,' the wilderness generation's completed past act) — the tense shift alone marks the boundary between the historical warning-example and the present exhortation it supports.
  *[strong=G4203 · occurrences=[1Cor.10.8 surface='sexual immorality' morph=V-PAS-1P; 1Cor.10.8 surface='did' morph=V-AAI-3P]]*

- Rev 19:2 and Col 3:5 both render porneia as bare 'immorality' rather than 'sexual immorality' — the ESV drops the 'sexual' qualifier at exactly the two points where the word is used most abstractly/metaphorically (Babylon's corrupting influence on the earth; a general vice-list entry next to 'impurity, passion, evil desire'), while every occurrence naming a concrete act or a named person keeps 'sexual.' The English surface itself is tracking the literal/metaphorical distinction, even though the underlying Greek word doesn't formally mark it.
