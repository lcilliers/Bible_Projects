## Cross-strong synergy

1. **Transgression is explicitly defined as law-relative, not free-standing.** Rom 4:15 states this
   almost as a formal rule: "where there is no law there is no transgression" (G3847) — the concept
   requires a prior boundary to cross. Both root words for "transgress" in this family are literally
   spatial-boundary words: G3845 (parabainō, "step past") and H5674D (avar, "pass over/through/by")
   — H5674D's meaning block runs 25 senses, nearly all literal motion (crossing a river, passing a
   border, a "passer-by"), with the moral "transgress a covenant/command" sense appearing as one
   derived branch among many (Judg 2:20, Dan 9:11, Hos 6:7). The metaphor is the same across both
   languages: sin as a line stepped over.
2. **"One violation voids the whole standard" — the same argument recurs verbatim from the guilt
   subgroup.** G3848's Jas 2:11 ("If you do not commit adultery but do murder, you have become a
   transgressor of the law") is the identical argument already found under G1777 in the guilt
   subgroup (Jas 2:10, "become guilty of all of it") — the same author applying the same
   whole-law-is-one-unit logic to two different M10 gloss-families independently.
3. **The two largest members are defined more by their *remedy* than by the act.** G3900
   (paraptōma) is Paul's central Adam/Christ contrast term (Rom 5:15-20) and is otherwise almost
   entirely a *forgiveness*-context word — "forgive... their trespasses" (Matt 6:14-15, Eph 1:7,
   Col 2:13), "not counting their trespasses against them" (2 Cor 5:19). H6588 (pesha, 58 verses,
   the single largest strong across all 14 families surveyed so far) shows the identical shape: its
   own meaning block explicitly labels sub-senses "as recognised by sinner," "as God deals with
   it," "as God forgives" — and the verses bear this out (Ps 32:1 "blessed is the one whose
   transgression is forgiven"; Ps 51:1/3 "I know my transgressions, my sin is ever before me" —
   the same recognition-language pattern already found in the guilt subgroup's H0816; Mic 7:18,
   Isa 43:25, Isa 44:22 all "blot out"/"pass over" transgression as a divine act). **Isaiah 53:5**
   ("he was pierced for our transgressions") lands in this family at the exact same verse already
   cited under the guilt subgroup's H5375J and H0817 — pesha converges with the bearing/
   guilt-offering complex at a single shared text.
4. **H6588 also runs the same three-register split found in family J (unfaithfulness):** its own
   meaning block distinguishes transgression "against individuals," "nation against nation," and
   "against God" — identical structure to H4603/H4604's "against man"/"against God" split. Amos's
   "for three transgressions... and for four" formula (Amos 1:3-2:6) applies pesha specifically at
   the nation-against-nation register, functioning as an indictment formula against foreign powers,
   not individual ethics.
5. **H6586 (pasha) fuses "transgress" and "rebel" as one verb, and its own verses run political
   revolt (Moab against Israel, Edom against Judah, 2 Kgs 1:1/3:5/3:7/8:22) alongside straightforward
   religious confession (Lam 3:42, Ps 51:13) without any separation** — the same word describes a
   vassal state revolting from its sovereign and a person confessing sin against God, suggesting the
   underlying figure is sovereignty/rebellion-against-a-ruler applied to both political and
   theological registers alike.
