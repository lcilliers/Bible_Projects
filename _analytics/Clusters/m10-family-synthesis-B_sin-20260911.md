## Cross-strong synergy

1. **The base verbs in both languages share the identical root metaphor: missing a target.**
   Greek hamartanō's own LSJ entry gives its primary sense as literal — "miss the mark, especially
   of a spear thrown" — before any moral extension; its own meaning-tree entry states this directly:
   "primarily to miss a mark; to be in error... to sin" (G0264). Hebrew chata (H2398) carries the
   same convergent image in its own meaning block: "to sin, **miss**, miss the way, go wrong, incur
   guilt." Unlike every other M10 family surveyed — which converge on boundary-crossing
   (transgression), weight-bearing (guilt, iniquity), or physical twisting (corruption) — "sin"
   itself is uniquely defined by a **failed-aim** metaphor, independently attested in both source
   languages. This is the single cleanest, most textually-explicit root-metaphor found across all
   14 families.
2. **The chattah/hamartia noun family repeats the same three-way sense split found in nearly every
   other Hebrew moral-failure root surveyed (H6588 pesha, H5771 avon, family G).** H2399's meaning
   block: "sin / guilt for sin / punishment for sin." H2403H/I: "sin / condition-or-guilt-of-sin /
   punishment-for-sin / sin-offering." The same three-part structure (state / guilt-consequence /
   punishment-consequence) recurring across four or more independently-catalogued Hebrew roots
   suggests either a real feature of how this whole semantic domain is organized in Hebrew, or a
   uniform authoring convention applied across the underlying reference source — worth
   distinguishing before treating it as a substantive finding about the *text* rather than the
   *lexicon*.
3. **H2398 (chata) documents a distinctive causative dimension not found elsewhere in the M10
   corpus: one person's sin actively causing a group's sin.** The repeated Kings formula — Jeroboam
   "sinned and... made Israel to sin" (1 Kgs 15:30, 16:2, 16:13, 16:26, 21:22) — treats sin as
   something a leader can transmit to a whole nation through their example/policy, not merely
   commit individually. This sits alongside the now-familiar Ezekiel individual-responsibility
   formula (3:21, 33:12, 33:16 — "the righteous... when he sins," reappearing again in this family
   as it did in D and G) — the same corpus holds both a strictly individual model and a
   leader-transmits-sin model side by side.
4. **G0266 (hamartia, 145 verses) carries by far the most theologically distinctive material in
   this exercise: Paul's Romans 5-8 treatment of sin as a near-personified ruling power**, not
   merely an act — "sin reigned in death" (5:21), "sold under sin" (7:14), "sin that dwells within
   me" (7:17, 20), "the law of sin" (7:23, 8:2), "slaves of sin" (6:6, 17, 20). No other M10 family
   surveyed treats its concept as an active agent exercising dominion; every other family's
   "moral-failure" words function as acts, states, or burdens, never as a ruling force with its own
   will. This is a qualitative outlier worth flagging on its own, independent of the family's
   broader lexical pattern.
5. **The sin-offering system runs through this family exactly as it did through the guilt subgroup
   (H0817) — the same noun serves double duty for the wrong and its cultic remedy.** H2401's
   meaning block gives "sin, sin offering" as one entry; H2403A-I's Leviticus citations are
   dominated by sacrificial procedure (Lev 4:3's "sin offering," Exod 29:36). This mirrors the
   guilt subgroup's H0817 pattern precisely.

## Note for #1680

"Sin" (this family) is textually the *most* distinctly evidenced of the fourteen — its
missed-the-mark metaphor is explicit and cross-linguistic, unlike several other families whose
unifying image had to be inferred. If the method needs one family to anchor a worked example of
what a well-evidenced single distilled meaning looks like, this is the strongest candidate — with
the caveat that Paul's ruling-power register (point 4) is different enough in kind that folding it
into the same distilled statement as the OT's act/state/offering material may still lose something
real.
