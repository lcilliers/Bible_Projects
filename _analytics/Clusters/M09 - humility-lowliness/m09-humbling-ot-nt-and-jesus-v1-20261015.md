## 2. What differs: the contrast the researcher asked about, "if any"

There are differences, but they are of emphasis and of who carries the humbling. None is a break in what humbling is. Each is counted from the readings.

### 2a. Toward God, and toward one another

- **In the OT readings**, humbling is overwhelmingly before God.
  - In all 20 verses of batch 1 group I it is done before God, or in answer to his word, by the verse or its passage.
  - Humility toward another person is thin. It appears in Pro 25:6–7 (the seat), Pro 16:19 (*"a lowly spirit with the poor"*), Num 12:3 (Moses meek when spoken against), 2Sa 6:22 (David abased in Michal's eyes) and Gen 16:9 (Hagar told to submit).
- **In the NT readings**, three groups are about how one stands toward others: humility toward one another (batch 4 III, 7), gentleness in meeting others (batch 4 IV, 11), and submission among people (batch 5 II, 20). That is 38 of the 77 NT verses.
  - *"in humility count others more significant than yourselves"* (Phili 2:3)
  - *"Clothe yourselves, all of you, with humility toward one another"* (1Pe 5:5)
  - *"submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ"* (Eph 5:21)
- **Even here the NT keeps the God-ward line.** In every NT relationship verse a ground is given that faces God or Christ (batch 5 §2 II).

### 2b. Who humbles, and what follows

- **In the OT accounts, those who humble themselves are mostly kings and the people**, in the narrative of 2 Chronicles and Kings: Rehoboam and the princes, Hezekiah, Manasseh, Josiah, and those who did not (Amon, Zedekiah, Belshazzar, Pharaoh).
  - What follows is national and measured: wrath turned *"so as not to make a complete destruction"* (2Ch 12:12), and disaster delayed to *"his son's days"* (1Ki 21:29).
- **In the NT the one who humbles himself is any person.** What follows is named as:
  - *"justified"* (Luk 18:14)
  - *"grace"* (Jam 4:6)
  - *"exalt you … at the proper time"* (1Pe 5:6)
  - *"rest for your souls"* (Mat 11:29)

### 2c. Submission

- **The NT has a submission verb, *hypotassō*, 32 verses read.** It is used of submitting to God (Jam 4:7; Heb 12:9) and among people.
- **The OT verses read have nothing of that range.** The nearest are Gen 16:9 (*"submit to her"*), Psa 81:11 (*"Israel would not submit to me"*) and 2Ch 30:8 (*"yield yourselves to the Lord"*).
- **Limit:** this is within the pull. A broader OT search for "yield" and "obey" was not made.

### 2d. The contrite and broken heart

- **In the pull, the contrite and broken heart or spirit is OT only** (batch 3: 14 read, all OT).
- **The NT counterparts read are acts:**
  - the tax collector who *"beat his breast"* (Luk 18:13)
  - *"Be wretched and mourn and weep"* before *"Humble yourselves"* (Jam 4:9–10)
  - Paul humbled before the unrepentant, to *"mourn over many"* (2Cor 12:21)
- **Limit:** Greek words for "broken" or "contrite" were not pulled, so this is a fact about the pull, not about the NT.

### 2e. The ground and the example

- **In the OT the ground for humbling is God himself and his word:**
  - *"The Lord is righteous"* (2Ch 12:6)
  - *"when you heard"* (2Ki 22:19)
  - *"trembles at my word"* (Isa 66:2)
- **In the NT a new ground is named: Christ, and Christ's own way:**
  - *"Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus"* (Phili 2:5)
  - *"Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example"* (1Pe 2:21)
  - *"out of reverence for Christ"* (Eph 5:21)
  - *"by the meekness and gentleness of Christ"* (2Cor 10:1)
  - *"learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart"* (Mat 11:29)
- **The source named also changes.** Gentleness is *"the fruit of the Spirit"* (Gal 5:22–23), and the mind of the flesh *"does not submit … indeed, it cannot"* (Rom 8:7). The OT verses read name God reviving the lowly (Isa 57:15) and the heart being humbled (Lev 26:41). They do not name an inner source given for it.

**This is the main difference.** It is where Jesus stands, set out in §3.

## 3. The role of Jesus

Taken from the verses, in what each says of him. Route A, 13.1 (Jesus is part of the divine, researcher 2026-10-15), with the human side in 10.14.

**3.1 He teaches it.**
- He says *"whoever humbles himself will be exalted"* in three settings: the choice of seats (Luk 14:11), the refusal of titles (Mat 23:12) and the two men at prayer (Luk 18:14).
- He gives it a face in the tax collector (Luk 18:13).
- He sets a child in the midst: *"Whoever humbles himself like this child is the greatest"* (Mat 18:4).
- He moves the disciples' joy from spirits being subject to them to *"your names are written in heaven"* (Luk 10:20).

**3.2 He is it.**

| Verse | What it says of him |
| --- | --- |
| Mat 11:29 | *"I am gentle and lowly in heart"*, his own word on his heart |
| Mat 21:5 (Zec 9:9) | *"your king is coming to you, humble"* |
| Luk 2:51 | *"he went down with them … and was submissive to them"*, after *"I must be in my Father's house"* (2:49) |
| Act 8:33 (Isa 53) | *"In his humiliation justice was denied him"*, the lamb that *"opens not his mouth"* (8:32) |
| Phili 2:6–8 | *"though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant … he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross"* |
| Heb 2:9 | *"him who for a little while was made lower than the angels, namely Jesus"* |
| 1Cor 15:28 | *"then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who put all things in subjection under him, that God may be all in all"* |

**3.3 What he taught happens to him.** *"he humbled himself … Therefore God has highly exalted him"* (Phili 2:8–9). *"made lower than the angels … crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death"* (Heb 2:9). **I read:** the shape of the saying he taught (humbles himself, is exalted) is the shape told of him. Phili 2:9's *"Therefore"* joins his humbling to his exaltation. The tie back to the saying in Luke is mine.

**3.4 He is the ground and the example for humility toward others.** Phili 2:3–5 makes *"in humility count others more significant"* rest on *"this mind … in Christ Jesus"*. Also 1Pe 2:21; Eph 5:21; 2Cor 10:1; Mat 11:29 (§2e).

**3.5 All things are put under him, and he is subject to the Father.**
- *"God has put all things in subjection under his feet"* (1Cor 15:27; Eph 1:22; 1Pe 3:22; Heb 2:8).
- **The bridge to the OT is in the text.**
  - Psa 8 says it of *"man"*: *"Yet you have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings and crowned him with glory and honor … you have put all things under his feet"* (Psa 8:5–6).
  - Heb 2 quotes the psalm and reads it as *"namely Jesus"* (2:6–9). It adds: *"At present, we do not yet see everything in subjection to him. But we see him"* (2:8–9).
  - "Made lower" is H2637 in the psalm and G1642 in Hebrews, both T3.
- **I read:** the lowering said of man in Psa 8 is said of Jesus in Heb 2, and the subjection of all things to man is said of the Son. At the end, the Son *"himself"* is subjected to the Father (1Cor 15:28).

**3.6 Others lower themselves before him.**
- John the Baptist: *"He must increase, but I must decrease"* (Joh 3:30). G1642 is the same word as Jesus *"made lower"* in Heb 2:7, 9. Its setting: *"I am not the Christ, but I have been sent before him"* (3:28), and *"this joy of mine is now complete"* (3:29).
- **I read:** John's lowering of himself before Jesus is said with joy, not loss.

**3.7 At his coming, God looks on a low estate.**
- Mary: *"he has looked on the humble estate of his servant"* (Luk 1:48), and *"he has brought down the mighty from their thrones and exalted those of humble estate; he has filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he has sent away empty"* (1:52–53).
- **I read:** Mary's words stand with Hannah's: *"he brings low and he exalts. He raises up the poor from the dust"* (1Sa 2:7–8). The text does not quote Hannah.

## 4. For Ch 13 (13.1 *Divine*)

The verses give God's own lowering in both testaments. Proposed for 13.1, under its own heading when the weave is instructed (title naming the subject, e.g. *Lowering himself*):

- **OT:**
  - God *"looks far down"* and raises the poor (Psa 113:6–7)
  - *"though the Lord is high, he regards the lowly"* (Psa 138:6)
  - he dwells with the contrite and lowly *"to revive"* (Isa 57:15)
  - *"your gentleness made me great"* (2Sa 22:36; Psa 18:35)
  - *"I have been broken over their whoring heart"* (Eze 6:9)
- **In the Son:**
  - in the form of God, *"emptied himself … humbled himself … obedient to the point of death"* (Phili 2:6–8)
  - *"gentle and lowly in heart"* (Mat 11:29)
  - *"made lower than the angels"*, crowned *"because of the suffering of death"* (Heb 2:9)
  - *"submissive to them"* (Luk 2:51)
  - *"the Son himself will also be subjected"* (1Cor 15:28)
  - the humble king (Mat 21:5)
  - his humiliation, silent (Act 8:32–33)
- **God's acts toward the humble and the proud** (route A, already read):
  - gives favor and grace (Pro 3:34; Jam 4:6)
  - sees and answers the one who humbles himself (2Ch 12:7; 1Ki 21:29)
  - humbles to test (Deu 8)
  - brings the proud low (Isa 2; Psa 18:27; Dan 4:37)
  - looks on a humble estate (Luk 1:48)
- **13.1 already holds** Dan 4:17 and 4:37 (*Willing*), Psa 51:16–17 (*Delight*), Jam 4:4–6 (*Jealousy*) and Isa 58:3, 5 (*Choosing*). None is placed for God's lowering.

## 5. Limits, and one gap to name

- **Words not pulled.** Jesus' humility is also told without any lowering word:
  - *"the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve"* (Mar 10:45; Mat 20:28)
  - the washing of the disciples' feet (Joh 13)
  - *"though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor"* (2Cor 8:9, which is in M09's poor/needy split, code S)

  The serve and servant words were not in this fan-out. Phili 2:7 (*"the form of a servant"*) shows they bear on it.

- **The OT side was read only through the words pulled.** "Bow down" (H7812) was left as a reading question (batch 1). It was not read.
