## 1. In short

Batch 1 was about the act: humbling oneself, being humbled, the proud brought low. Batch 2 is about **the humble**, those whom the verses call humble, lowly or meek, and about **humility** as something a person has, seeks or walks in.

## 2. The readings

### I. The humble before God (13 read)

**The humble seek.**
- *"Seek the Lord, all you humble of the land, who do his just commands; seek righteousness; seek humility; perhaps you may be hidden on the day of the anger of the Lord"* (Zep 2:3). Those already called humble are told to seek humility. **I read:** in this verse humility is not a thing held once and for all. The humble keep seeking it, under the threat of the day of anger (2:2).
- *"I will leave in your midst a people humble and lowly. They shall seek refuge in the name of the Lord"* (Zep 3:12). They are left after God removes *"your proudly exultant ones"* (3:11). They are described by what they do and do not do: *"they shall do no injustice and speak no lies"* (3:13).
- *"The afflicted shall eat and be satisfied; those who seek him shall praise the Lord"* (Psa 22:26) and *"you who seek God, let your hearts revive"* (Psa 69:32). In both, the humble or afflicted stand beside "those who seek". (Psa 22:26 is the afflicted sense, group VI.)

**The humble hear what God did for another, and are glad.**
- *"My soul makes its boast in the Lord; let the humble hear and be glad"* (Psa 34:2). David's own deliverance follows: *"I sought the Lord, and he answered me"* (34:4). The superscription sets it after *"he changed his behavior before Abimelech"*.
- *"When the humble see it they will be glad"* (Psa 69:32). "It" is the deliverance of the speaker, who has said *"I am afflicted and in pain"* (69:29).
- **I read:** in both psalms, one man's boast is in the Lord, not in himself, and the humble are those who are glad at it. Their gladness is at what God did for someone else.

**The meek, beside not fretting.** *"Refrain from anger, and forsake wrath! Fret not yourself; it tends only to evil. For the evildoers shall be cut off, but those who wait for the Lord shall inherit the land … But the meek shall inherit the land and delight themselves in abundant peace"* (Psa 37:8–11).
- The meek inherit what *"those who wait for the Lord"* inherit.
- Meekness stands where the psalm has just said *"Refrain from anger … Fret not yourself"* about the prospering wicked.
- Compare Num 12:3 (carried): Moses *"very meek"* where he is spoken against.
- **I read:** in both places meekness stands where another provokes, and the one who is meek leaves the answer to God.

**What God does for the humble:**

| Pro 3:34 | *"to the humble he gives favor"* | *"Toward the scorners he is scornful"* |
| Psa 138:6 | *"though the Lord is high, he regards the lowly"* | *"the haughty he knows from afar"* |

| Isa 11:4 | *"decide with equity for the meek of the earth"* | the shoot of Jesse, who *"shall not judge by what his eyes see"* (11:3) |

- **Psa 25:9** sits between *"Remember not the sins of my youth"* (25:7) and *"pardon my guilt, for it is great"* (25:11). The one who asks to be taught as one of the humble calls himself a sinner, and *"he instructs sinners in the way"* (25:8). **I read:** in this psalm the humble are the teachable, and the teachable are those who own their guilt.
- **Pro 3:34** is quoted in Jam 4:6 and 1Pe 5:5 (batch 4).
- **Psa 138:6:** **I read:** the distance is the reverse of what height would suggest. The Lord who is high regards the lowly near at hand, and keeps the haughty at a distance.

### II. Humility: before honor, with the fear of the Lord (5 read)

**The noun *ʿănāwāh*** (H6038, M05 by tag) is in four of the five verses: Pro 15:33, 18:12, 22:4 and Zep 2:3.

| Verse | Humility beside | Set against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pro 15:33 | *"The fear of the Lord is instruction in wisdom, and humility comes before honor"* | |
| Pro 18:12 | *"humility comes before honor"* | *"Before destruction a man's heart is haughty"* |
| Pro 22:4 | *"The reward for humility and fear of the Lord is riches and honor and life"* | |
| Pro 11:2 | *"with the humble is wisdom"* (H6800B) | *"When pride comes, then comes disgrace"* |

- **What the verses join to humility:** the fear of the Lord (15:33; 22:4), wisdom (11:2; 15:33), and honor that comes after it (15:33; 18:12; 22:4).
- **What they set against it:** the haughty heart before destruction (18:12), and pride before disgrace (11:2). Batch 1 had *"Pride goes before destruction"* (Pro 16:18).
- **The verses around it are about hearing.**
  - *"The ear that listens to life-giving reproof will dwell among the wise. Whoever ignores instruction despises himself, but he who listens to reproof gains intelligence"* (15:31–32), just before 15:33.
  - *"If one gives an answer before he hears, it is his folly and shame"* (18:13), just after 18:12.
  - **I read:** in these two places humility sits beside listening before speaking and taking reproof. The proverbs do not join them in one saying.

**Walking humbly.** *"With what shall I come before the Lord, and bow myself before God on high? Shall I come before him with burnt offerings …? … He has told you, O man, what is good; and what does the Lord require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?"* (Mic 6:6–8).
- The question is about what to bring. The answer is a way to walk.
- *"humbly"* (H6800A, its only use) is *"with your God"*: a walk in company, not a gift handed over.

**Set against religious acts in three passages:**
- the fast (Isa 58:3–7, batch 1)
- offerings (Mic 6:6–8)
- the house and the sacrifices (Isa 66:1–3, group III)

In each, what God asks for or looks to is set against the religious act. Each passage states its own contrast. They are counted here, not joined.

### III. A lowly and contrite spirit: where God dwells and looks (3 read)

**Isa 57:15:** *"For thus says the One who is high and lifted up, who inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy: 'I dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit, to revive the spirit of the lowly, and to revive the heart of the contrite.'"*
- Two dwellings are named: the high and holy place, and *"with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit"*.
- The lowliness is *"of spirit"*, and what is revived is *"the spirit of the lowly"* and *"the heart of the contrite"*. Both are in the inner being.
- The passage goes on: *"I will not contend forever … for the spirit would grow faint before me"* (57:16). And of the backslider: *"I have seen his ways, but I will heal him"* (57:18).
- The contrite word is in M09 (H1793A) and in M10 (H1792). The lowly word is H8217.

**Isa 66:2:** *"All these things my hand has made … But this is the one to whom I will look: he who is humble and contrite in spirit and trembles at my word."*
- Set against *"what is the house that you would build for me"* (66:1), and against offerings that are *"like one who blesses an idol"* by those who *"have chosen their own ways"* (66:3).
- The one God looks to is humble (H6041, M24), contrite in spirit (H5223, M73), and *"trembles at my word"*. No M09 word is in the verse. It is in this batch because the humility words are filed elsewhere (overview §3D/3E.1).

**Isa 61:1:** the anointed one is sent *"to bind up the brokenhearted"*, beside *"good news to the poor"*.
- The brokenhearted are bound up, not rebuked. **I read:** in Isa 57 the contrite are revived, and in Isa 61 the brokenhearted are bound up. The broken heart is met with healing in both.
- The poor side is held by OT-105.

**The heart side** (the broken and contrite heart in Psa 51:17, Psa 34:18 and Psa 147:3) comes in batch 3.

### IV. Lowliness in the one who leads, and in God (4 read, 2 carried)

**A humble king.** *"Behold, your king is coming to you; righteous and having salvation is he, humble and mounted on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey"* (Zec 9:9).
- The next verse: *"I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim and the war horse from Jerusalem … he shall speak peace to the nations"* (9:10).
- The humble king comes without the chariot and war horse, and speaks peace. "Humble" is H6041.

**A king who rides out for meekness.** *"In your majesty ride out victoriously for the cause of truth and meekness and righteousness"* (Psa 45:4). Meekness is a cause the king fights for, in majesty, with *"arrows … sharp in the heart of the king's enemies"* (45:5). **I read:** majesty and meekness are not set against each other here.

**God's own *ʿănāwāh*.**
- *"You have given me the shield of your salvation, and your gentleness made me great"* (2Sa 22:36).
- *"your right hand supported me, and your gentleness made me great"* (Psa 18:35).
- The noun is the same family as *"humility comes before honor"* (H6038 / H6037). The ESV renders it "gentleness" here because it is God's. David credits his greatness to it, in a song that is otherwise about strength: *"He trains my hands for war"*.
- **Change of character:** the word for the humility God asks of a person is used of God's own way toward David. Batch 1 had the same turn with H8213: God *"looks far down"* (Psa 113:6). **I read:** two words for lowering are used of God, and in both the verse that follows (Psa 113:7) or the verse itself (Psa 18:35) says what his lowering does for someone else.

**Carried:** Moses *"very meek"* (Num 12:3), and David *"I will be abased in your eyes"* (2Sa 6:22). Both are leaders, and both are from the no-other-M-code reading.

### V. Made low for what they did (4 read)

- **Mal 2:9:** *"I make you despised and abased before all the people, inasmuch as you do not keep my ways but show partiality in your instruction."* The priests who *"caused many to stumble"* (2:8) are abased by God. Being abased answers partiality.
- **Eze 17:14:** *"that the kingdom might be humble and not lift itself up, and keep his covenant that it might stand."* The humble state is set by a covenant under oath. The king *"rebelled … by sending his ambassadors to Egypt"*, and *"whose oath he despised"* (17:15–16). The verse names the kingdom's humbleness as the condition of its standing. The rebellion is the lifting up.
- **Eze 29:14–15:** Egypt *"shall be a lowly kingdom … the most lowly of the kingdoms, and never again exalt itself above the nations"*. The reason is turned toward Israel: *"it shall never again be the reliance of the house of Israel"* (29:16). Route A, with the HIB side in Israel's reliance.

### VI. The same words for the afflicted and poor (7, to OT-105)

H6035 is one word. The ESV renders it in four ways in the M09 verses:

| ESV rendering | Verses | Where the verse speaks of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| afflicted | Psa 9:12; 10:12; 10:17; 22:26; Amo 2:7 | their cry, their oppression, God not forgetting them |
| humble | Zep 2:3; Psa 25:9; 34:2; 69:32; 76:9; 147:6; 149:4; Pro 3:34 | God's favor, teaching and saving, and their seeking and gladness |
| meek | Num 12:3; Psa 37:11; Isa 11:4; 29:19 | |
| poor | Isa 61:1; Pro 14:21; 16:19; Job 24:4 | |

This is a fact about the translation. The Hebrew word does not change.

The seven verses decided "thread" are those where the verse is about the afflicted being oppressed or crying out. They are already coded in the poor/needy split (P or G) and carried by OT-105. Two of them name the heart: *"you will strengthen their heart"* (Psa 10:17), and *"May your hearts live forever!"* (Psa 22:26).

**I read:** whether the humble and the afflicted are the same people seen from two sides is a question these verses raise but do not answer. It is recorded for OT-106 (the poor person's own side), which carries the researcher's question on inner lack.
