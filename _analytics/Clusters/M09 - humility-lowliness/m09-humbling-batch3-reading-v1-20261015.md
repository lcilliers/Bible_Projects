## 1. In short

The same words, broken or crushed, of the heart or spirit, appear in two quite different settings.

| Group | Verses | What breaks the heart or spirit | What follows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I. The broken and contrite heart before God | 4 | one's own sin (Psa 51; 38) or troubles (Psa 34) | God does not despise it, is near, saves, heals and binds up |
| II. A heart or spirit broken by what it bears | 6 | reproach, scorn, slavery, mockery, being hunted, the evil the prophet sees and the word he carries | despair, no comforter, unable to listen, days extinct |
| III. The crushed spirit in Proverbs | 3 | *"sorrow of heart"* | the bones dry up; it cannot be borne |
| IV. God broken over a heart | 1 | the heart that whores away from him | the escaped remember, and loathe themselves |
| V. Data | 2 | "crippled in his feet" | |

**I read:** what the verses themselves give is the cause of the breaking and whether the broken heart is brought before God. They do not divide contrition from despair by name. That division is the reader's.

## 2. The readings

### I. The broken and contrite heart before God (4 read)

**Psa 51:17:** *"The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise."*
- **What it is set against:** *"For you will not delight in sacrifice, or I would give it; you will not be pleased with a burnt offering"* (51:16).
- **What comes before:** *"Deliver me from bloodguiltiness"* (51:14). The spirit and heart are broken over the speaker's own sin.
- **What it is called:** *"The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit"*. It takes the place of the offering.
- **The next verses:** *"then will you delight in right sacrifices"* (51:19). The offerings are not abolished. They follow the broken heart.
- This is a fourth passage where what God looks to is set against a religious act. The others are Isa 58:3–7, Mic 6:6–8 and Isa 66:1–3 (batches 1 and 2). In Psa 51 the contrast is the speaker's own, and the next verses take it back up.
- "Contrite" here is H1794 (M10). "Broken" is H7665 (M24/T3). No M09 word is in the verse.

**Psa 38:8:** *"I am feeble and crushed; I groan because of the tumult of my heart."*
- **The cause:** *"my iniquities have gone over my head"* (38:4), and *"because of my foolishness"* (38:5).
- **Around him:** *"My friends and companions stand aloof"* (38:11).
- **What he does with it:** *"O Lord, all my longing is before you; my sighing is not hidden from you"* (38:9).
- The crushing is from his own sin, and the groaning of the heart is laid before God.

**Psa 34:18:** *"The Lord is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit."*
- **Its setting:** *"When the righteous cry for help, the Lord hears"* (34:17), and *"Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the Lord delivers him out of them all"* (34:19).
- Here the broken heart is the righteous man's, under affliction, not over his sin.
- God is *"near"* to it. Compare Isa 57:15, *"I dwell … with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit"* (batch 2).
- "Crushed" here is the M09 word H1793A.

**Psa 147:3:** *"He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds."*
- It stands between *"he gathers the outcasts of Israel"* (147:2) and *"The Lord lifts up the humble"* (147:6, batch 1).
- The broken heart is a wound, and God binds it. Compare Isa 61:1, *"to bind up the brokenhearted"* (batch 2).

### II. A heart or spirit broken by what it bears (6 read)

**Psa 69:20:** *"Reproaches have broken my heart, so that I am in despair. I looked for pity, but there was none, and for comforters, but I found none."*
- **What breaks it:** what others say. *"You know my reproach, and my shame and my dishonor"* (69:19).
- **What follows:** despair, and looking for pity and finding none.
- Earlier in the same psalm the speaker is *"afflicted and in pain"*, and *"When the humble see it they will be glad"* (69:29, 32; batch 2).

**Psa 109:22:** *"For I am poor and needy, and my heart is stricken within me."*
- Around it: *"I am an object of scorn to my accusers; when they see me, they wag their heads"* (109:25), and *"My knees are weak through fasting; my body has become gaunt"* (109:24).
- The stricken heart is the inner side of *"poor and needy"*, said of himself as the ground of his plea: *"Help me, O Lord my God!"* (109:26).
- This bears on OT-106, the researcher's question about poor and needy as an inner state and as an appeal.

**Psa 109:16:** the accuser *"did not remember to show kindness, but pursued the poor and needy and the brokenhearted, to put them to death"*.
- The brokenhearted are a party, pursued by one who would not remember kindness. They stand with the poor and needy.
- This is OT-105, the poor as seen by another (split code P). Here the one who sees them pursues them.

**Job 17:1:** *"My spirit is broken; my days are extinct; the graveyard is ready for me."* Next: *"Surely there are mockers about me, and my eye dwells on their provocation"* (17:2). The broken spirit is joined to the sense that life is ending.

**Exo 6:9:** *"Moses spoke thus to the people of Israel, but they did not listen to Moses, because of their broken spirit and harsh slavery."*
- What they did not listen to was the promise: *"I will bring you out … I will take you to be my people … you shall know that I am the Lord"* (6:6–7).
- Here the broken spirit, from slavery, stops the hearing of the word of deliverance.
- Moses draws the conclusion for himself: *"the people of Israel have not listened to me. How then shall Pharaoh listen to me"* (6:12).
- **I read:** this broken spirit closes the ear. Psa 51's broken spirit is offered to God, and the God of Psa 34 is near to the crushed in spirit. The words are the same. The cause and the outcome are opposite.

**Jer 23:9:** *"Concerning the prophets: My heart is broken within me; all my bones shake; I am like a drunken man, like a man overcome by wine, because of the Lord and because of his holy words."*
- **The cause, twice:** *"because of the Lord and because of his holy words"*, and what the prophet sees: *"the land is full of adulterers … Both prophet and priest are ungodly"* (23:10–11).
- The prophet's heart is broken by the word he carries and the evil it exposes.
- **I read:** this breaking is not over his own sin, nor under another's blow. It is the heart of one who hears God's word against what he sees.

### III. The crushed spirit in Proverbs (3 read)

All three use H5218B (T2), "crushed", of the spirit:

| Verse | The crushed spirit | Set against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pro 15:13 | *"by sorrow of heart the spirit is crushed"* | *"A glad heart makes a cheerful face"* |
| Pro 17:22 | *"a crushed spirit dries up the bones"* | *"A joyful heart is good medicine"* |
| Pro 18:14 | *"a crushed spirit who can bear?"* | *"A man's spirit will endure sickness"* |

What the three say together, each in its own words:
- The cause is sorrow of heart.
- It reaches the body: the face in 15:13, the bones in 17:22.
- It is beyond what a person can carry. The spirit can carry the body's sickness, but not its own crushing.

In 15:13 and 17:22 the opposite is a glad or joyful heart. No verse here brings the crushed spirit to God or says what heals it. These verses are already cited in Ch 3, Ch 6, 10.1 and Ch 11.

### IV. God broken over a heart (1 read)

**Eze 6:9:** *"then those of you who escape will remember me among the nations where they are carried captive, how I have been broken over their whoring heart that has departed from me and over their eyes that go whoring after their idols. And they will be loathsome in their own sight for the evils that they have committed."*
- **Who is broken:** God (H7665, the same verb as *"broken spirit"*), over the heart that has departed from him.
- **The human side:** those who escape *"will remember me"*, and *"they will be loathsome in their own sight"*.
- **Change of character:** this is the third word for lowering or breaking used of God:

| Batch | Verse | Word | Of God |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Psa 113:6 | H8213 | God *"looks far down"* |
| 2 | 2Sa 22:36 | H6038 | *"your gentleness made me great"* |
| 3 | Eze 6:9 | H7665 | *"I have been broken"* |

Each verse states its own. They are counted here, not joined. For 13.1.

### V. Data (2)

2Sa 4:4 and 2Sa 9:3: Mephibosheth *"crippled in his feet"*. This is H5223, the word that in Isa 66:2 is *"contrite in spirit"*. Same word, of the feet and of the spirit (change of character). David's *"kindness of God"* to him (9:3) is a different word.
