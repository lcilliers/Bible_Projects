Two things are new in the Greek:
- **Christ as the one who is humble.** He humbled himself, and he calls himself lowly in heart. Humility toward others is grounded in having *"this mind … which is yours in Christ Jesus"* (Phili 2:5).
- **Humility as the way one person bears with another**, which is the largest group here.

## 2. The readings

### I. Humbling oneself, and being exalted (4 read, 3 carried)

**Jesus says the same thing in three settings.** *"everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but the one who humbles himself will be exalted"* comes in:
- the choice of seats (Luk 14:11, carried)
- the refusal of titles (Mat 23:12, carried)
- the two men at prayer (Luk 18:14, read here)

**Luk 18:10–14 gives the act a face.**
- **The Pharisee:** *"standing by himself, prayed thus: 'God, I thank you that I am not like other men … or even like this tax collector. I fast twice a week; I give tithes of all that I get.'"*
- **The tax collector:** *"standing far off, would not even lift up his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast, saying, 'God, be merciful to me, a sinner!'"*
- **Jesus' verdict:** *"this man went down to his house justified, rather than the other."*

What the parable shows:
- The exalting of self is measuring oneself against others before God, and listing one's acts.
- The humbling of self is owning oneself a sinner and asking for mercy, with the body following: standing far off, eyes down, beating the breast.
- **I read:** this is batch 1's *"The Lord is righteous"* (2Ch 12:6) and Lev 26's confession, made into a single prayer. Batch 1's fasting is also on the Pharisee's lips. Batch 1 found fasting can go with a humbling God does not take knowledge of (Isa 58). In Luk 18 it is again on the side that is not justified.

**Mat 18:1–4: humbling oneself like a child.** To *"Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?"* Jesus sets a child in the midst: *"unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter … Whoever humbles himself like this child is the greatest."* Humbling here is a turning, and it answers the question of who is greatest.

**Jam 4:6 and 1Pe 5:5–6 quote Pro 3:34 (batch 2): *"God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble."***
- **In James** it follows the passions at war within and *"friendship with the world is enmity with God"* (4:1–4). The humbling runs on as submitting, drawing near, cleansing, purifying hearts and mourning (4:7–10; the no-other-M-code reading).
- **In Peter:** *"Humble yourselves, therefore, under the mighty hand of God so that at the proper time he may exalt you, casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you"* (1Pe 5:6–7).
  - Humbling oneself *"under the mighty hand"* goes in one sentence with casting one's anxieties on him.
  - **I read:** here humbling oneself is placing oneself under God's hand and letting go of what one carries, on the ground that *"he cares for you"*.
  - The exalting is *"at the proper time"*. It is God's to time.

### II. Christ lowly, humbled, meek (5 read)

**Phili 2:5–9:** *"Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant … And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross. Therefore God has highly exalted him."*
- The pattern of the saying in group I happens in Christ: he humbles himself, and then *"Therefore God has highly exalted him"*.
- The humbling is spelled out as not grasping, emptying himself, taking a servant's form, and obeying to death.
- It is given as *"this mind"* for the readers, introduced by *"in humility count others more significant than yourselves"* (2:3; group III).
- Cited in Ch 2 (for death) but not for humility.
- **For 13.1 and 10.14.** It joins batch 1's God who *"looks far down"* and batch 2's *"your gentleness made me great"*.

**Mat 11:28–29:** *"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls."*
- This is Christ's description of his own heart: *"gentle and lowly in heart"*. The lowliness is placed in the heart, as Pro 16:19 and 29:23 place it in the spirit (batch 1).
- It is the reason given for *"learn from me"*. The outcome is *"rest for your souls"*.
- The verse before: the Father has *"hidden these things from the wise and understanding and revealed them to little children"* (11:25).

**Mat 21:5:** *"your king is coming to you, humble, and mounted on a donkey"*. This is Zec 9:9 (batch 2) fulfilled in the entry to Jerusalem.

**Act 8:32–33:** *"Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter and like a lamb before its shearer is silent … In his humiliation justice was denied him."* This is Isa 53, read by the eunuch and opened by Philip as *"the good news about Jesus"* (8:35). Here the lowering is suffered, not chosen in the words of the verse, and he is silent under it.

**2Cor 10:1:** *"I, Paul, myself entreat you, by the meekness and gentleness of Christ — I who am humble when face to face with you, but bold toward you when I am away!"*
- Paul appeals by Christ's meekness.
- In the same breath he quotes the charge made against him: *"humble"* face to face (here, timid), and bold at a distance.
- **Change of character:** the humility-word in an opponent's mouth, as a weakness.

### III. Humility toward one another (7 read)

| Verse | Humility named | Beside it | Against it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phili 2:3–4 | *"in humility count others more significant than yourselves"* | *"Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others"*; *"being of the same mind"* (2:2) | *"selfish ambition or conceit"* |
| Rom 12:16 | *"associate with the lowly"* | *"Live in harmony with one another"* | *"Do not be haughty … Never be wise in your own sight"* |
| Eph 4:2–3 | *"with all humility and gentleness"* | *"with patience, bearing with one another in love, eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit"* | |
| Col 3:12–13 | *"Put on … humility, meekness"* | compassionate hearts, kindness, patience, *"bearing with one another … forgiving each other"* | after *"put … away: anger, wrath, malice"* (3:8) |
| 1Pe 3:8–9 | *"a humble mind"* | unity of mind, sympathy, brotherly love, *"a tender heart"* | *"Do not repay evil for evil"* |
| 1Pe 5:3, 5 | *"Clothe yourselves, all of you, with humility toward one another"* | the younger *"subject to the elders"* | the elders *"not domineering over those in your charge"* |
| Act 20:19 | Paul *"serving the Lord with all humility and with tears and with trials"* | | |

What these verses say:
- **Humility is directed:** *"toward one another"*. It is a way of counting others: *"more significant than yourselves"*.
- **It goes in company:** with gentleness, patience, bearing with, forgiving, sympathy and a tender heart, every time.
- **It is named against:** selfish ambition, conceit, haughtiness, being wise in one's own sight, and domineering.
- **It is put on, or worn:** *"Put on"* (Col 3:12) and *"Clothe yourselves"* (1Pe 5:5).
- **It keeps unity:** *"harmony"*, *"unity of the Spirit"*, *"unity of mind"*.
- **It runs both ways in authority** (1Pe 5): the younger are subject, and the elders do not domineer. Both are under one command to humility.
- **Paul's own:** in Act 20:19 humility is the manner of *"serving the Lord"*, and it comes with tears and trials.

### IV. Gentleness and meekness in how one meets others and the word (11 read)

**In meeting someone who is wrong or opposed:**
- *"restore him in a spirit of gentleness. Keep watch on yourself, lest you too be tempted"* (Gal 6:1). The verse joins the gentle restoring to watching oneself. Two verses on: *"if anyone thinks he is something, when he is nothing, he deceives himself"* (6:3).
- *"correcting his opponents with gentleness. God may perhaps grant them repentance"* (2Ti 2:25). The correcting is gentle, and the outcome is left to God.
- *"make a defense … yet do it with gentleness and respect"* (1Pe 3:15).
- *"Shall I come to you with a rod, or with love in a spirit of gentleness?"* (1Cor 4:21). Said to those who *"are arrogant"* (4:18).
- *"to be gentle, and to show perfect courtesy toward all people. For we ourselves were once foolish"* (Tit 3:2–3). The ground of the gentleness is the memory of what *"we ourselves"* were.

**In receiving the word:** *"receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls"* (Jam 1:21), after *"let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger"* (1:19). This repeats what batch 2 found in Proverbs, where humility sits beside hearing and taking reproof (Pro 15:31–33; 18:12–13).

**In wisdom:** *"show his works in the meekness of wisdom. But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not boast"* (Jam 3:13–14). Meekness is the mark of true wisdom. Selfish ambition in the heart is its counterfeit, as in Phili 2:3 (group III).

**Where it comes from, in three verbs:**
- **Given:** *"the fruit of the Spirit is … gentleness"* (Gal 5:22–23). In the same passage: *"Let us not become conceited"* (5:26).
- **Pursued:** *"Pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, steadfastness, gentleness"* (1Ti 6:11). It is pursued in flight from *"the love of money"* (6:10).
- **Put on:** Col 3:12 (group III).

**Where it is:** *"let your adorning be the hidden person of the heart with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which in God's sight is very precious"* (1Pe 3:4). It is in the heart, hidden, and precious *"in God's sight"*.

**The blessing:** *"Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth"* (Mat 5:5). This is Psa 37:11 (batch 2), between *"poor in spirit"* (5:3) and *"those who mourn"* (5:4).

### V. A low estate, and where the boast goes (8 read, 1 carried)

**A reversal, and a boast turned around:**
- *"Let the lowly brother boast in his exaltation, and the rich in his humiliation, because like a flower of the grass he will pass away"* (Jam 1:9–10).
- *"God chose what is low and despised in the world, even things that are not, to bring to nothing things that are, so that no human being might boast in the presence of God … 'Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord'"* (1Cor 1:28–31).
- **I read:** in both, the low is where God's choosing and lifting are seen. The boast is moved from what one has to what God does. The rich man's *"humiliation"* is something to boast in, because it tells him the truth: he passes like grass.

**God looks on a low estate.** Mary: *"he has looked on the humble estate of his servant"* (Luk 1:48). Luk 1:52 is carried (*"exalted those of humble estate"*). Mary's own low estate is what God looked on. Batch 1 found God seeing the one who humbles himself (2Ch 12:7; 1Ki 21:29).

**Paul, brought low and lowering himself:**
- *"I know how to be brought low, and I know how to abound … I have learned … to be content"* (Phili 4:11–12). Being brought low is a circumstance here, met with contentment, *"through him who strengthens me"* (4:13).
- *"did I commit a sin in humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I preached God's gospel to you free of charge?"* (2Cor 11:7). A lowering taken on for others' good. **I read:** this has the shape of Phili 2: lowered so that another is raised.
- *"I fear that when I come again my God may humble me before you, and I may have to mourn over many of those who sinned earlier and have not repented"* (2Cor 12:21). Here Paul is humbled by others' unrepented sin, and the humbling is joined to mourning over them. Compare Jer 23:9 (batch 3): the prophet's heart broken over what he sees.
- *"God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus"* (2Cor 7:6). G5011 here means downcast, after *"fighting without and fear within"* (7:5). God's comfort came through a person's arrival.

### VI. The humility-word turned to a show (2 read)

**Col 2:18, 23.** G5012, the noun Phili 2:3, Eph 4:2, Col 3:12 and 1Pe 5:5 use for humility, is rendered *"asceticism"* here:
- *"Let no one disqualify you, insisting on asceticism and worship of angels … puffed up without reason by his sensuous mind"* (2:18)
- *"These have indeed an appearance of wisdom in promoting self-made religion and asceticism and severity to the body, but they are of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh"* (2:23)

What Col 2 says of this practice:
- The one who insists on it is *"puffed up"*.
- It has *"an appearance of wisdom"*.
- It is *"self-made"*.
- It does not stop the flesh.

**I read:** the humility-word here names a practice that inflates the one who practises it. It is the reverse of the humility Col 3:12 tells the same readers to put on, a chapter later. Compare batch 1's Isa 58: a fast called humbling oneself, which God did not take knowledge of. **Change of character (#1919):** one Greek noun names humility toward others and also a self-made severity that puffs up.
