## 1. In short

These Hebrew words name three movements, and the verses tell them apart by who acts:

| Group | Verses | Who lowers whom | The heart in it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I. Humbling oneself before God | 20 | a person lowers himself, before God or before God's word | named: "your heart was penitent / tender", "their uncircumcised heart is humbled", "humbled your heart", "set your heart to understand and humbled yourself" |
| II. God humbles: testing, teaching, answering | 9 | God lowers a person or people | named: "to know what was in your heart", "he bowed their hearts down" |
| III. The proud brought low, the lowly raised | 22 | God brings down what lifts itself up, and lifts the low | the target is named: "haughty looks", "lofty pride", "the eyes of the haughty", "pompous pride", "lowly in spirit" |
| IV. Taking the lower place | 2 | a person takes a low seat, or is told to | |
| V. Humbled or made to submit by another person | 4 | an enemy, a mistress or a king lowers someone | |
| VI. Subdued in war | 14 | a people is beaten down | mostly data; 3 forward |
| VII. Low in other senses | 8 | mountains, cities, old age, God looking down | mostly data |

In every group with an inner bearing, the verses set it against a rising. Humbling oneself answers pride of heart (2Ch 32:26). Failing to humble oneself goes with a lifted-up self, a hardened heart and a stiff neck (Dan 5:22–23; 2Ch 36:12–13). Being humbled by God is set against the heart that is lifted up in plenty (Deu 8:14). The proud are brought low so that *"the Lord alone will be exalted"* (Isa 2:11, 17).

## 2. The readings

### I. Humbling oneself before God (20; 19 read, 1 carried)

These are the accounts of the kings in Chronicles and Kings, the conditions in Lev 26 and 2Ch 7, Daniel, Ezra, Pharaoh, Belshazzar, and the fast of Isa 58.

**It always faces someone.**
- *"before me"* (1Ki 21:29)
- *"before the Lord"* (2Ki 22:19)
- *"before the God of his fathers"* (2Ch 33:12)
- *"before your God"* (Dan 10:12)
- Zedekiah's failure is put as *"He did not humble himself before Jeremiah the prophet, who spoke from the mouth of the Lord"* (2Ch 36:12). Humbling before the prophet is humbling before the word he carries.
- Most verses in this group name before whom. Of the rest, 2Ch 12:6 and 2Ch 30:11 answer a word just brought, and Lev 26:41 goes with confession (26:40). Isa 58:3–5 is a fast addressed to God.

**It answers a word heard.**
- Rehoboam and the princes humble themselves when Shemaiah brings *"You abandoned me, so I have abandoned you"*. What they say is *"The Lord is righteous"* (2Ch 12:5–6).
- Josiah humbles himself *"when you heard how I spoke against this place"* (2Ki 22:19; 2Ch 34:27).
- Ahab does so *"when Ahab heard those words"* (1Ki 21:27).
- Belshazzar is charged: *"you … have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this"* (Dan 5:22). He had the word, in his father's story, and did not bow to it.

**The heart is where it happens.**
- *"your heart was penitent"* (2Ki 22:19); *"your heart was tender"* (2Ch 34:27)
- *"if then their uncircumcised heart is humbled"* (Lev 26:41)
- *"you … have not humbled your heart"* (Dan 5:22)
- *"Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart"* (2Ch 32:26). The verse before says *"his heart was proud"* (32:25). Here humbling is the heart's turn back from its own pride: the same heart, in the other direction.
- *"from the first day that you set your heart to understand and humbled yourself before your God"* (Dan 10:12). Here humbling stands beside setting the heart to understand. It is not a response to a rebuke.

**What it is set against:**
- pride of heart (2Ch 32:25)
- *"you have lifted up yourself against the Lord"* (Dan 5:23), as his father's *"heart was lifted up and his spirit was hardened so that he dealt proudly"* (5:20)
- *"He stiffened his neck and hardened his heart against turning"* (2Ch 36:13)
- *"Do not now be stiff-necked … but yield yourselves"* (2Ch 30:8), answered by mocking in some and humbling in others (30:10–11; carried)
- *"How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me?"* (Exo 10:3), said to Pharaoh, whose heart is hardened (10:1)

**What goes with it:**
- saying *"The Lord is righteous"* (2Ch 12:6)
- tearing clothes, sackcloth, fasting, going about *"dejectedly"* (1Ki 21:27)
- weeping (2Ki 22:19)
- entreating and praying (2Ch 33:12–13)
- fasting and seeking (Ezr 8:21)
- the condition *"humble themselves, and pray and seek my face and turn from their wicked ways"* (2Ch 7:14)
- confession, and making amends for iniquity (Lev 26:40–41)

In Ezra the fast to humble themselves follows a choice not to lean on the king: *"I was ashamed to ask the king for a band of soldiers … since we had told the king, 'The hand of our God is for good on all who seek him'"* (Ezr 8:22).

**Distress does not decide it.**
- Manasseh, captured, *"when he was in distress, he entreated the favor of the Lord … and humbled himself greatly"* (2Ch 33:12).
- Ahaz, humbled by the Lord, *"In the time of his distress he became yet more faithless"* (2Ch 28:22; group II).
- The same pressure meets two hearts and goes two ways. **I read:** the verses give no cause for the difference beyond the heart itself. The same is true at 2Ch 30:10–11.

**Fathers and sons.** *"he did not humble himself before the Lord, as Manasseh his father had humbled himself, but this Amon incurred guilt more and more"* (2Ch 33:23). Belshazzar *"knew all this"* of his father (Dan 5:22). One generation's humbling does not pass to the next.

**God sees it.**
- *"When the Lord saw that they humbled themselves"* (2Ch 12:7)
- *"Have you seen how Ahab has humbled himself before me?"* (1Ki 21:29)
- *"I also have heard you"* (2Ki 22:19)
- *"God was moved by his entreaty"* (2Ch 33:13)
- *"your words have been heard"* (Dan 10:12)
- *"he listened to our entreaty"* (Ezr 8:23)

**I read:** the seeing that OT-105 found for the poor is here for the one who humbles himself. The verses name it; that the two are alike is my observation.

**What it obtains is real, and measured.**
- *"I will grant them some deliverance … Nevertheless, they shall be servants to him"* (2Ch 12:7–8)
- *"I will not bring the disaster in his days; but in his son's days"* (1Ki 21:29)
- Josiah is gathered *"in peace"*, and the disaster still comes (2Ki 22:20)
- The wrath turns *"so as not to make a complete destruction"* (2Ch 12:12)

**It does not last by itself.** Of Rehoboam, two verses after his humbling: *"he did evil, for he did not set his heart to seek the Lord"* (2Ch 12:14).

**What follows in Manasseh:** *"Then Manasseh knew that the Lord was God"* (2Ch 33:13). Knowing follows the humbling and the answer. It does not come before them.

**A humbling God does not take knowledge of: Isa 58:3–5.**
- The people ask: *"Why have we fasted, and you see it not? Why have we humbled ourselves, and you take no knowledge of it?"*
- The answer: *"in the day of your fast you seek your own pleasure, and oppress all your workers"*, and *"you fast only to quarrel and to fight"*.
- The form is named and set aside: *"Is it to bow down his head like a reed, and to spread sackcloth and ashes under him?"*
- The fast God chooses is turned to others: *"to loose the bonds of wickedness … to share your bread with the hungry and bring the homeless poor into your house; when you see the naked, to cover him"* (58:6–7).
- Compare Ahab. His sackcloth and fasting were seen and answered (1Ki 21:27–29).
- **I read:** the outward acts are the same. What differs, by the two passages, is what goes with them. Ahab acts *"when Ahab heard those words"*, against himself. In Isa 58 the fast is kept alongside self-pleasure and oppression. The humbling that is not taken knowledge of is one that leaves the seen poor unseen (58:7). This joins OT-105.
- 10.1, 10.7 and 13.1 already cite Isa 58:3, 5. The reading here is for the humbling side.

### II. God humbles: testing, teaching, answering (9 read)

**Deu 8:2–3, 16 states the purpose.**
- *"that he might humble you, testing you to know what was in your heart, whether you would keep his commandments or not"* (8:2)
- *"And he humbled you and let you hunger and fed you with manna … that he might make you know that man does not live by bread alone"* (8:3)
- *"that he might humble you and test you, to do you good in the end"* (8:16)

Being humbled is set against what plenty does to the heart: *"then your heart be lifted up, and you forget the Lord"* (8:14), and *"Beware lest you say in your heart, 'My power and the might of my hand have gotten me this wealth'"* (8:17).

The next chapter goes on: God subdues the nations (9:3), *"Do not say in your heart … 'It is because of my righteousness'"* (9:4–6). This is why Deu 9:3, an H3665 "subdue" verse, is read here and not as war. The passage turns the subduing into a warning about what the heart will say of itself.

So, in Deuteronomy, being humbled by God is:
- a test that shows the heart
- a lesson about what a person lives by
- *"to do you good in the end"*

The danger it guards against is the heart that lifts itself up and credits itself.

**Psa 107:12:** *"he bowed their hearts down with hard labor"*. This follows *"they had rebelled against the words of God, and spurned the counsel of the Most High"* (107:11), and comes before *"Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble"* (107:13). What God bows down is the heart.

**Being humbled, and the two answers to it:**
- 2Ch 28:19 *"the Lord humbled Judah because of Ahaz"*, and Ahaz grew *"yet more faithless"* (28:22). Compare Manasseh in group I.
- Psa 55:19: *"God will give ear and humble them … because they do not change and do not fear God"*. "They" are the companion whose *"speech was smooth as butter, yet war was in his heart"* (55:21).

**Being humbled, from the inside: Job 30:11.**
- *"Because God has loosed my cord and humbled me, they have cast off restraint in my presence."*
- Before it: *"They abhor me … they do not hesitate to spit at the sight of me"* (30:10).
- Here being humbled by God lays a man open to the contempt of others. It is said by the one it happens to, and his sense is that God did it.

**Dan 4:37:** *"those who walk in pride he is able to humble"*. Nebuchadnezzar says this after his *"reason returned"* (4:34, 36). It is the humbled man's own word on what was done to him.

### III. The proud brought low, the lowly raised (22 read)

**What is brought low is named, and it is the bearing of a person, not only a rank:**
- *"The haughty looks of man … the lofty pride of men"* (Isa 2:11, 17)
- *"all that is proud and lofty … all that is lifted up"* (Isa 2:12)
- *"the eyes of the haughty"* (Isa 5:15)
- *"the pomp of the arrogant … the pompous pride of the ruthless"* (Isa 13:11)
- Moab's *"pompous pride together with the skill of his hands"* (Isa 25:11)
- *"your eyes are on the haughty to bring them down"* (2Sa 22:28); *"the haughty eyes you bring down"* (Psa 18:27)

**Its setting.**
- Isa 2: a land *"filled with silver and gold … filled with idols; they bow down to the work of their hands"* (2:7–8).
- Isa 5: revellers who *"do not regard the deeds of the Lord"* (5:12).

The outcome is stated twice: *"the Lord alone will be exalted in that day"* (Isa 2:11, 17). People *"hide in the dust from before the terror of the Lord"* (2:10). Being brought low here is the lifted-up thing made to give way to the One who is high.

**Who can do it.**
- Job is told: *"look on everyone who is proud and abase him … bring him low"* (Job 40:11–12). He is told this as a challenge he cannot meet: *"Then will I also acknowledge to you that your own right hand can save you"* (40:14).
- *"not from the east or from the west … comes lifting up, but it is God who executes judgment, putting down one and lifting up another"* (Psa 75:6–7)
- *"he brings low and he exalts"* (1Sa 2:7)
- *"I bring low the high tree, and make high the low tree"* (Eze 17:24)
- *"Exalt that which is low, and bring low that which is exalted"* (Eze 21:26), as the crown is taken from the profane prince
- God *"sets over it the lowliest of men"* (Dan 4:17)
- *"The Lord lifts up the humble; he casts the wicked to the ground"* (Psa 147:6)
- *"You save a humble people"* (2Sa 22:28; Psa 18:27; "humble" is H6041)

**The proverbs say it from the person's side.**
- *"One's pride will bring him low, but he who is lowly in spirit will obtain honor"* (Pro 29:23). Here pride itself brings the man low. No other agent is named.
- *"It is better to be of a lowly spirit with the poor than to divide the spoil with the proud"* (Pro 16:19), after *"Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall"* (16:18).
- In both, lowliness is *"of spirit"*. In 16:19 it is a choice of company: with the poor, not with the proud.

**Someone else's reading of a man brought low.** Eliphaz to Job: *"For when they are humbled you say, 'It is because of pride'; but he saves the lowly"* (Job 22:29). **I read:** one man explains another's being brought low as pride, and the verse sets God's saving of the lowly beside it. Whether the explanation is right is the question of the whole book; this verse only shows that someone reads it so.

**Isa 26:5–6:** *"he has humbled the inhabitants of the height, the lofty city … The foot tramples it, the feet of the poor, the steps of the needy."* The proud city brought low is walked on by the poor. It sits beside *"You keep him in perfect peace whose mind is stayed on you"* (26:3).

**Change of character.** In this group H8213 is God's act against pride. In group VII the same verb has God looking down (Psa 113:6).

### IV. Taking the lower place (2 read)

- **Pro 25:6–7:** *"Do not put yourself forward in the king's presence or stand in the place of the great, for it is better to be told, 'Come up here,' than to be put lower in the presence of a noble."* This is the same teaching as Luk 14:8–11 (carried, no-other-M-code reading A). The lowering is chosen in advance, before another must do it.
- **Jer 13:18:** *"Say to the king and the queen mother: 'Take a lowly seat, for your beautiful crown has come down from your head.'"* In the passage it comes after *"be not proud, for the Lord has spoken"* (13:15), and with the prophet's own *"my soul will weep in secret for your pride"* (13:17). The low seat is commanded because the crown has already fallen. The command is to take, inwardly, the place that has already been given outwardly.

### V. Humbled or made to submit by another person (4 read)

- **Judg 16:5:** the lords of the Philistines bribe Delilah: *"see where his great strength lies … that we may bind him to humble him"*. Here humbling is subjugation, sought through betrayal.
- **Gen 16:9:** *"Return to your mistress and submit to her."* Hagar had fled after *"Sarai dealt harshly with her"* (16:6). The angel's command comes with a promise and *"the Lord has listened to your affliction"* (16:11). The submission is commanded by God's messenger, under a mistress who had been harsh. The verse does not say she was wronged or right. Route A and B.
- **Psa 89:22:** *"the wicked shall not humble him"*, God's promise to David. Route A.
- **Dan 5:19:** *"whom he would, he raised up, and whom he would, he humbled"*. This is Nebuchadnezzar's power, *"because of the greatness that he gave him"* (5:18). The next verse turns it on him: *"when his heart was lifted up … he was brought down"* (5:20). The man who humbled whom he would was himself humbled.

### VI. Subdued in war (14: 9 data, 2 carried, 3 forward)

H3665 as *"subdued"*: Moab, Jabin, Midian, the Philistines, the Ammonites, *"all your enemies"*.
- In most of these the word is the end of a battle account, with no inner act in the verse or passage. They are data.
- Three of these verses (1Sa 7:13; 2Sa 8:1; 1Ch 18:1) were marked forward to M09 from M72 group D. They are now decided as data. Neh 9:24 was carried earlier.

**Forward:** three passages join an inner act that is not humbling:
- **2Ch 13:18:** *"the men of Judah prevailed, because they relied on the Lord"*. Reliance; for the strand that reads trust.
- **Psa 81:14:** *"I would soon subdue their enemies"*, if only: *"Israel would not submit to me. So I gave them over to their stubborn hearts"* (81:11–12). Not submitting to God and the stubborn heart; M30.
- **Psa 106:42:** *"they were brought into subjection"* after *"they were rebellious in their purposes"*; then *"he looked upon their distress, when he heard their cry"* (106:43–44). M30, with God's seeing in 13.1.

Isa 25:5 is data for M09. Its neighbour *"you have been a stronghold to the poor"* (25:4) belongs to OT-105.

### VII. Low in other senses (8: 1 read, 2 forward, 5 data)

- **Psa 113:6, read (route A):** *"Who is like the Lord our God, who is seated on high, who looks far down on the heavens and the earth?"* The verb is H8213, the same one that brings the proud low in group III. Here it is God's own looking down from on high. The next verse is *"He raises the poor from the dust"* (113:7). **I read:** the one who brings the high low is also the one who looks low to raise the poor. The verse gives the verb; it does not draw the link.
- **Forward:**
  - Isa 57:9 *"sent down even to Sheol"*. The passage goes on: *"you did not say, 'It is hopeless' … Whom did you dread and fear, so that you lied, and did not remember me, did not lay it to heart?"* (57:10–11). For M01 and M14.
  - Ecc 12:4 *"all the daughters of song are brought low"*, old age before death. For M25, Ch 2.
- **Data:** Isa 29:4, Isa 32:19, Isa 40:4, Isa 25:12, Dan 7:24.
