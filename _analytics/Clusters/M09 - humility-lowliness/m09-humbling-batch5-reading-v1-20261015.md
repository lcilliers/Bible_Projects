**Submission names a place under someone.** The verses tell it from the person's side (groups I–III) and from God's ordering of all things under Christ (IV–V).

## 2. The readings

### I. Submitting oneself to God, or refusing (5 read)

- **Jam 4:7:** *"Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you."*
  - It stands between *"God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble"* (4:6; batch 4) and *"Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you"* (4:10).
  - In this passage submitting and humbling oneself are said in one run of commands, to the same people, before the same God. **I read:** James puts no line between them. The commands run on: submit, resist, draw near, cleanse, purify the heart, mourn, humble yourselves.
- **Heb 12:9:** *"we have had earthly fathers who disciplined us and we respected them. Shall we not much more be subject to the Father of spirits and live?"*
  - Being subject is to God's discipline, and he *"disciplines us for our good, that we may share his holiness"* (12:10).
  - It *"seems painful rather than pleasant, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness"* (12:11).
  - This is batch 1's Deu 8 in another key: God humbles *"to do you good in the end"*.
- **Rom 10:3:** *"being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and seeking to establish their own, they did not submit to God's righteousness."* They had *"a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge"* (10:2). The refusal to submit is the building of one's own righteousness. Compare the Pharisee's list in Luk 18:11–12 (batch 4).
- **Rom 8:7:** *"For the mind that is set on the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to God's law; indeed, it cannot."*
  - This is the one verse that says a person *cannot* submit.
  - The way out given is the Spirit: *"You, however, are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if in fact the Spirit of God dwells in you"* (8:9).
  - Already cited in 10.7 and Ch 12.
- **2Cor 9:13:** *"your submission that comes from your confession of the gospel of Christ, and the generosity of your contribution."* The submission comes *"from your confession"*, and it shows in generosity toward the saints in need. This ties to OT-105 (the poor as seen by another).

### II. Submission among people, and the ground given for it (19 read, 1 carried)

**The relationships named:**
- the governing authorities (Rom 13:1, 5; Tit 3:1; 1Pe 2:13)
- masters (1Pe 2:18; Tit 2:9)
- husbands (Eph 5:22, 24; Col 3:18; 1Pe 3:1, 5; Tit 2:5)
- order in the assembly (1Cor 14:34; 1Ti 2:11)
- those devoted to the service of the saints (1Cor 16:16)
- leaders (Heb 13:17)
- *"one another"* (Eph 5:21)

**The ground given, verse by verse.** The verses nearly always say *why*, and the why faces God:

| --- | --- |
| Rom 13:1 | *"there is no authority except from God"* |
| Rom 13:5 | *"not only to avoid God's wrath but also for the sake of conscience"* |
| 1Pe 2:13 | *"for the Lord's sake"*; *"Live as people who are free … as servants of God"* (2:16) |
| 1Pe 2:18–21 | *"mindful of God"*; *"Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example"* |
| Eph 5:21 | *"out of reverence for Christ"* |
| Eph 5:22 / Col 3:18 | *"as to the Lord"* / *"as is fitting in the Lord"* |
| 1Pe 3:5 | *"the holy women who hoped in God"* |
| 1Pe 3:1 | *"that … they may be won without a word"* |
| Tit 2:5 | *"that the word of God may not be reviled"* |

| Heb 13:17 | the leaders *"are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account"* |
| 1Cor 16:16 | Stephanas' household *"devoted themselves to the service of the saints"* |

**What is set beside it.** Where the passage goes on, it lays a duty on the other side:
- *"Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"* (Eph 5:25)
- *"do not be harsh with them"* (Col 3:19)
- *"showing honor to the woman"* (1Pe 3:7)
- the leaders *"will have to give an account"* (Heb 13:17)
- batch 4's elders *"not domineering"* (1Pe 5:3)

These are counted as each passage states them, not joined into a scheme.

**What it is joined to inwardly:**
- *"conscience"* (Rom 13:5)
- *"mindful of God"* (1Pe 2:19)
- *"reverence for Christ"* (Eph 5:21)
- *"hoped in God"*, and *"do not fear anything that is frightening"* (1Pe 3:5–6)
- in 1Pe 3:1–4 the submission of a wife is set beside *"the hidden person of the heart with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit"* (batch 4)

**Under the unjust.** *"Servants, be subject to your masters with all respect, not only to the good and gentle but also to the unjust. For this is a gracious thing, when, mindful of God, one endures sorrows while suffering unjustly … because Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example"* (1Pe 2:18–21).
- Submission here is bearing wrong, with God in mind and Christ as the pattern.
- Compare Gen 16:9 (batch 1): Hagar told to return and submit to a mistress who had dealt harshly, with *"the Lord has listened to your affliction"*.

**One's own spirit, under oneself.** *"the spirits of prophets are subject to prophets. For God is not a God of confusion but of peace"* (1Cor 14:32–33). The speaker governs what moves in him, for the sake of order and peace. **I read:** this is submission inward. The spirit is under the person, not driving him.

**The limit.** *"to them we did not yield in submission even for a moment, so that the truth of the gospel might be preserved for you"* (Gal 2:5). These were *"false brothers secretly brought in … that they might bring us into slavery"* (2:4). Here refusing to submit is the right act, and the ground is again given: the truth of the gospel.

### III. The insubordinate (1 read, 2 data)

- **Tit 1:10:** *"many who are insubordinate, empty talkers and deceivers"*. Insubordination comes with empty talk and deceit, *"upsetting whole families by teaching for shameful gain"* (1:11).
- **Data:** Tit 1:6 (an overseer's children *"not open to the charge of … insubordination"*) and 1Ti 1:9 (*"the lawless and disobedient"*, in the list the law is for). The word is listed in both verses, not shown at work.

### IV. All things subjected to Christ; the Son subject (7 read; route A, 13.1)

**God puts all things under Christ:**
- *"God has put all things in subjection under his feet"* (1Cor 15:27)
- *"he put all things under his feet and gave him as head over all things to the church"* (Eph 1:22)
- *"at the right hand of God, with angels, authorities, and powers having been subjected to him"* (1Pe 3:22)
- *"it was not to angels that God subjected the world to come"* (Heb 2:5)

**The Son himself is subjected:** *"When all things are subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who put all things in subjection under him, that God may be all in all"* (1Cor 15:28). The one under whom all things are put is himself subject to the Father. The verse gives the end: *"that God may be all in all"*.

**Not yet seen, and the one made lower:** *"putting everything in subjection under his feet … At present, we do not yet see everything in subjection to him. But we see him who for a little while was made lower than the angels, namely Jesus, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death"* (Heb 2:8–9).
- The subjection of all things is not yet seen. What is seen is Jesus *made lower*, and crowned *because of* the suffering of death.
- This is the shape of Phili 2:8–9 (batch 4): lowered, then exalted.
- **The word "made lower" (Heb 2:7, 9) is G1642 *elattoō* "to make less", filed under T3. It was not in this fan-out's pull.** It is named for the OT/NT and Jesus analysis that follows.

**The boy Jesus, submissive:** *"he went down with them and came to Nazareth and was submissive to them. And his mother treasured up all these things in her heart"* (Luk 2:51).
- This follows *"Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house?"* (2:49).
- The Son who knows his Father's house goes down and is subject to Mary and Joseph.
- Then *"Jesus increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with God and man"* (2:52).
- Cited in Ch 4 (for Mary's heart), not for his submission.

### V. Spirits and creation subjected (3 read; route A with B)

- **Luk 10:17, 20:** *"Lord, even the demons are subject to us in your name!"*, and Jesus: *"Nevertheless, do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven."*
  - The subjection of spirits is real, and *"in your name"*.
  - The joy is moved from the power that subjects to the gift received.
  - **I read:** this is the same turn as Jam 1:9–10 and 1Cor 1:31 (batch 4): the boast or the joy moved from what one does to what God gives.
  - The passage goes on: Jesus thanks the Father for revealing these things *"to little children"* (10:21).
  - Cited in 10.1, Ch 11 and 13.3.
- **Rom 8:20:** *"the creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of him who subjected it, in hope that the creation itself will be set free."*
  - A subjection that was *"not willingly"*, set *"in hope"*. With it, *"we ourselves … groan inwardly as we wait"* (8:23).
  - For 13.4, and for the HIB's groaning (Ch 11).

## 4. What batch 5 hands to the OT/NT and Jesus analysis

The researcher asked for this to be given special attention after batch 5:
- the contrast, if any, of humbling between the OT and the NT
- the role Jesus played in it
- Jesus as part of the divine, given attention in Ch 13

From this batch:
- **Christ under whom all things are put** (1Cor 15:27; Eph 1:22; 1Pe 3:22; Heb 2:5, 8) and **the Son himself subject** (1Cor 15:28; Luk 2:51).
- **Jesus "made lower" and crowned because of the suffering of death** (Heb 2:7, 9). The word G1642 *elattoō* (T3) is outside the pull. It also stands in *"He must increase, but I must decrease"* (Joh 3:30). Both are to be checked in the analysis.
- **Christ as the example of submission under wrong** (1Pe 2:21).
- **The ground given for submission is Christ** (*"reverence for Christ"*, *"as to the Lord"*, *"for the Lord's sake"*).
- **The OT words read in batches 1–3 carry no submission verb of this kind.** The nearest are Gen 16:9 (Hagar told to submit), Psa 81:11 (*"Israel would not submit to me"*) and 2Ch 30:8 (*"yield yourselves to the Lord"*). The OT side of the analysis is to be checked against those.
