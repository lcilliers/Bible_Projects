# M09: the 27 verses with no other M-code, read in their passages

**Date:** 2026-10-15 · **Escalation:** #2148 · **Status:** reading only. Nothing is woven, and no narrative or register change is made.

**Instruction (researcher, chat 2026-10-15, verbatim):** *"lets dig deeper into verses with no other M-code, I am trying to see what they are saying"*.

**Method.** Each verse was read in its passage: the verses around it, and the parallel accounts where there are any. There was no framework or template (`feedback_read_verse_for_inner_act_not_event_template`). The M09 word was read by its part in what moves in the passage (rule 73). The researcher's observation on poor and needy (overview v2 §3B.1) is tested at the end; it was not laid over the readings. A reading beyond what a verse states is marked **I read**. Quotes are ESV, from iba.db `verse`.

Source list: [`m09-cluster-verses-crossref-v1-20261015.csv`](m09-cluster-verses-crossref-v1-20261015.csv) (blank `other_mcodes`).

## 1. What they are saying, in short

"No other M-code" is a fact about the single verse, not about what it says. In almost every case the inner act sits in the verses next to it. Examples: the proud in Jam 4:6, partiality and "evil thoughts" in Jam 2:4, "very sad" in Luk 18:23, "received him joyfully" in Luk 19:6, and "indignantly" in Mar 14:4. Read in their passages, the 27 fall into six groups:

| | What the verses say | Verses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | **Humbling oneself, and being exalted or brought low.** It is a choice of place, before God or before people, and the outcome lies with another. | Mat 23:12, Luk 14:11, Jam 4:10, 2Ch 30:11, 2Sa 6:22, Luk 1:52, Num 12:3 (7) |
| B | **"The poor" spoken of by others to judge an act of devotion,** and a motive hidden behind the words. | Mar 14:5, Mat 26:9, Mat 26:11, Joh 12:5, Joh 12:6, Joh 12:8 (6; one event in three Gospels) |
| C | **Giving out of poverty, measured by what is left, not by the amount.** | Mar 12:42, Luk 21:3 (2; one event in two Gospels) |
| D | **Wealth given to the poor as the hinge of a life turned,** or not turned. | Luk 18:22, Luk 19:8 (2) |
| E | **How the poor are seen and treated by others.** | Jam 2:3, Luk 16:20, Job 31:19, Pro 28:3, Lev 25:25, Lev 25:47, Jer 39:10 (7) |
| F | **The M09 word with no humility or poverty sense here** (data). | Judg 11:33 "subdued", Lev 14:37 "deeper", 1Ti 3:4 "submissive" (3) |

The M09 word is nowhere the whole story. In A it names the act. In B to E it names the party around whom another's act turns.

## 2. The readings

### A. Humbling oneself, and being exalted or brought low (7)

**Luk 14:11:** *"For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted."*
- **Setting:** a parable told *"when he noticed how they chose the places of honor"* (14:7). The guest who sits high may be moved down *"with shame"*. The guest who sits low may hear *"Friend, move up higher"* and *"be honored in the presence of all"* (14:9–10).
- Humbling here is choosing the lower place yourself, before anyone sets you there. Honour or shame is then given by the host, not taken.
- In the same passage Jesus turns to the host: *"invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind … because they cannot repay you"* (14:13–14). The humble guest and the guests who are poor stand side by side in one teaching. The guest gives up a claim to a seat. The host gives where nothing comes back.
- Not cited in the narrative.

**Mat 23:12:** the same saying, in another setting.
- **Setting:** Jesus forbids the titles rabbi, father and instructor (23:8–10): *"The greatest among you shall be your servant"* (23:11). It is followed at once by the woes on the hypocrites.
- Here humbling oneself is refusing a title that sets you over the brothers. The saying sits between the servant and the hypocrite.
- 23:3–4 are cited in 10.7. 23:12 is not.

**Jam 4:10:** *"Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you."*
- This is the end of a run of commands that begins at 4:6, *"God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble"*. The commands are: *"Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil … Draw near to God … Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded. Be wretched and mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned to mourning"* (4:7–9).
- In this passage, humbling oneself is not one act but the whole movement the verses list: submitting, resisting, drawing near, cleansing, purifying the heart, mourning. *"Before the Lord"* names who it faces.
- The passage opens with the quarrels that come from *"your passions … at war within you"* (4:1). Jam 4:1–8 are cited across 10.6, 10.7, 10.13, Ch 11, 12 and 13.1. 4:10 is not.

**2Ch 30:11:** *"However, some men of Asher, of Manasseh, and of Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem."*
- **Setting:** Hezekiah's couriers call: *"Do not now be stiff-necked as your fathers were, but yield yourselves to the Lord"* (30:8), and *"if you return to the Lord … he … will not turn away his face"* (30:9). The cities *"laughed them to scorn and mocked them"* (30:10).
- Humbling stands opposite to being stiff-necked and to mocking. It is shown by coming. In Judah, the next verse says, *"The hand of God was also on Judah to give them one heart"* (30:12).
- **I read:** the same call meets two hearts, mocking and humbling, and the verse names no reason for the difference.
- Not cited.

**2Sa 6:22:** *"I will make myself yet more contemptible than this, and I will be abased in your eyes. But by the female servants of whom you have spoken, by them I shall be held in honor."*
- David answers Michal's scorn (6:20) with *"It was before the Lord, who chose me … and I will celebrate before the Lord"* (6:21).
- Abasement here is taken on willingly, for the sake of worship. It is measured in two pairs of eyes: Michal's, where he is abased, and the servants', where he is honoured.
- 6:16, 6:20 and 6:21 are cited in 4, 10.7, 11 and 13.1. 6:22, where David speaks of his own abasement, is not.

**Luk 1:52:** *"he has brought down the mighty from their thrones and exalted those of humble estate"*
- This is in Mary's song, which begins with her own case: *"he has looked on the humble estate of his servant"* (1:48).
- Just before it: *"he has scattered the proud in the thoughts of their hearts"* (1:51). Just after it: *"he has filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he has sent away empty"* (1:53).
- Here the humble estate is a low standing, and the act is God's: route A, 13.1. Mary's own low estate is the HIB side (route B).
- In the same song pride is placed *"in the thoughts of their hearts"*, and lowliness of estate stands next to hunger.
- 1:46, 1:47 and 1:51 are cited. 1:52 is not.

**Num 12:3:** *"Now the man Moses was very meek, more than all people who were on the face of the earth."*
- The verse breaks into the account. Miriam and Aaron *"spoke against Moses"* and said, *"Has the Lord indeed spoken only through Moses?"*, *"And the Lord heard it"* (12:1–2). The Lord then calls the three out and speaks for Moses (12:4–8).
- Nothing in the account has Moses answer.
- **I read:** in this passage the meekness stands where a man is spoken against and does not defend himself, and the Lord takes up his cause. This is a reading of the placement; the verse itself only states the fact.
- 12:8 is cited in 10.8. 12:3 is not.

### B. "The poor" spoken of by others to judge an act of devotion (6)

The anointing at Bethany: Mar 14:3–9; Mat 26:6–13; Joh 12:1–8.
- **The objection:** the ointment *"could have been sold … and given to the poor"* (Mar 14:5; Mat 26:9; Joh 12:5).
- **Who raises it, and how:**
  - Mark: *"some … said to themselves indignantly"*, and *"they scolded her"* (14:4–5).
  - Matthew: *"the disciples … were indignant"* (26:8).
  - John: Judas, and John gives the motive: *"He said this, not because he cared about the poor, but because he was a thief, and having charge of the moneybag he used to help himself to what was put into it"* (12:6).
- **Jesus' answer:** *"you always have the poor with you, and whenever you want, you can do good for them. But you will not always have me"* (Mar 14:7; Mat 26:11; Joh 12:8).

What the verses say:
- The poor are absent from the scene. They are named by others, as a measure to judge a woman's act.
- In John the words of care are a cover. The stated concern is the poor, and the real one is the bag.
- In Mark and Matthew the speakers are "indignant". This is the anger that 10.13 already cites at Mat 26:8, 10, and 10.1 cites at Mat 26:8.
- Jesus does not set the poor aside. He places doing good for them on wanting (*"whenever you want"*, Mar 14:7). This verse was marked forward to M09 from M18 (OT-97).

None of the six M09 verses is cited. Of the three accounts, only Mat 26:8 and 26:10 are.

### C. Giving out of poverty (2)

**Mar 12:42; Luk 21:3:** *"a poor widow came and put in two small copper coins"*. Jesus says she *"has put in more than all those who are contributing … For they all contributed out of their abundance, but she out of her poverty has put in everything she had, all she had to live on"* (Mar 12:43–44; Luk 21:3–4).
- The measure is what is left, not what is given: *"out of their abundance"* against *"out of her poverty … all she had to live on"*.
- In Mark the scene follows directly on the scribes *"who devour widows' houses and for a pretense make long prayers"* (12:40). The poor widow gives everything at the treasury, while those who devour widows pray at length for show.
- **I read:** the verses name no inner word for her. What they give is the measure Jesus sets.
- Mar 12:38–44 are cited in 10.5 (the long prayers and the widow; M18 group G). The M09 verse 12:42 and the Luke parallel are not.

### D. Wealth given to the poor as the hinge of a life (2)

**Luk 18:22:** *"One thing you still lack. Sell all that you have and distribute to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me."*
- The ruler has kept the commandments *"from my youth"* (18:21). *"When he heard these things, he became very sad, for he was extremely rich"* (18:23).
- The lack is named in the extremely rich man. He lacks *one thing*, and it is not in his means.
- The poor are where his wealth is to go. His sadness is what the call meets in him.

**Luk 19:8:** *"Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor. And if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold."*
- Zacchaeus *"received him joyfully"* (19:6). The crowd *"grumbled"* (19:7). Jesus says, *"Today salvation has come to this house … the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost"* (19:9–10).
- Giving to the poor and restoring what was taken are said unasked, in Jesus' presence.

Luke places the two a chapter apart. The rich man who lacked one thing *"became very sad"*. The rich man who was a guest's host *"received him joyfully"* and gave. 19:10 is cited in Ch 2. 18:18–30 is not cited anywhere.

### E. How the poor are seen and treated by others (7)

**Jam 2:3** (*"You stand over there"*):
- **The act:** partiality. A gold ring and fine clothing are seated, shabby clothing is sent to stand (2:2–3).
- **What James calls it:** *"have you not then made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts?"* (2:4).
- **Against it:** *"has not God chosen those who are poor in the world to be rich in faith"* (2:5), and *"you have dishonored the poor man"* (2:6).
- The poor man is the party acted on. The inner act, judging with evil thoughts, is in those who seat him.
- 2:4–6 are cited in 10.7, 10.10, Ch 11 and 13.1. 2:3 is not.

**Luk 16:20** (Lazarus at the gate):
- *"laid"* there, *"covered with sores"*, he *"desired to be fed with what fell from the rich man's table"* (16:20–21).
- The rich man *"feasted sumptuously every day"* (16:19), a gate's width away.
- After death the places are reversed (16:22–26).
- The parable is cited heavily (Ch 2, 10.3, 10.6). 16:20, where the poor man is first set at the gate, is not.

**Job 31:19** (Job's oath):
- *"if I have seen anyone perish for lack of clothing, or the needy without covering"*.
- The oath rests on 31:15: *"Did not he who made me in the womb make him?"*
- Seeing the needy is where responsibility begins: *"if I have seen"*.
- Cited in 10.10 *Friends, company and care*.

**Pro 28:3:**
- *"A poor man who oppresses the poor is a beating rain that leaves no food."*
- Being poor does not keep a man from oppressing the poor. The oppressor and the oppressed share the same lack.
- Two verses on: *"Better is a poor man who walks in his integrity than a rich man who is crooked"* (28:6).
- Not cited.

**Lev 25:25, 47:** *"If your brother becomes poor and sells part of his property"*; *"your brother beside him becomes poor and sells himself"*.
- Poverty here is a state that comes on a brother. The law answers it with a redeemer (25:25, 48–49), with the jubilee, and with *"You shall not rule over him ruthlessly but shall fear your God"* (25:43).
- The inner command is to the one who holds power over the poor brother.
- 25:43–55 are cited in 10.10, Ch 11 and 10.4 (M72). The M09 verses are not.

**Jer 39:10:**
- *"some of the poor people who owned nothing"* are left in the land, and given vineyards and fields, by the conqueror's captain.
- Poverty is the plain state of owning nothing. It is the reason they are left when the rest are taken.
- **I read:** the verse has no inner-being bearing of its own. Route C (data), unless read with the fall of Jerusalem.

### F. The M09 word with no humility or poverty sense here (3), data

- **Judg 11:33:** *"So the Ammonites were subdued"*. This is H3665 in its military sense (§4 of the overview). The passage moves straight to Jephthah's vow and his daughter: *"you have brought me very low"* (11:35). That "brought me very low" is H3766 *kāraʿ*, not an M09 word. Whether 11:34–36 is read for its own inner act is a matter for another strand.
- **Lev 14:37:** *"deeper than the surface"*, of a disease in a house wall. Data.
- **1Ti 3:4:** *"keeping his children submissive"*. This is the overseer's household. The passage lists *"gentle"* (3:3) and warns that a recent convert *"may become puffed up with conceit"* (3:6). The M09 word here is the children's submission, managed by the father. **I read:** it is not the overseer's own humility. Data for M09.

## 3. The researcher's observation tested (overview v2 §3B.1)

| Dimension | What these 27 verses show |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Poor as a state of means | **Corroborated, clearly.** "owned nothing" (Jer 39:10); "becomes poor and sells" (Lev 25:25, 47); "out of her poverty … all she had to live on" (Mar 12:44); the shabby clothing (Jam 2:2). |
| 2. Lack in, or perceived by, the inner being, not verified by means | **Not named with a poverty word in these 27.** The nearest is the reverse case: the "extremely rich" ruler who *"still lacks"* one thing (Luk 18:22–23). He has the means, but his lack is elsewhere. The clearer test verses are outside this set (Rev 3:17, Pro 13:7, Mat 5:3, 2Co 6:10; overview §3B.1). |
| 3. Needy as an expression to get sympathy or help | **Not in these 27, from the poor themselves.** No poor person here speaks. Lazarus "desired to be fed" but says nothing. What appears is another face: **others speak of the poor**, and in John the words of care are a pretext (Joh 12:6). Whether "needy" said by the needy is aimed at help is a matter for the "I am poor and needy" Psalms (Psa 86:1, 70:5, 40:17, 109:22) and Pro 18:23 *"The poor use entreaties"*. |

**A further face these verses add, not in the observation:** the poor as the place where another's inner act is shown and measured. That inner act may be care, partiality, oppression, pretext, giving or refusing (groups B, D and E). In these verses the poor are mostly seen, spoken of, given to or wronged. They seldom act or speak themselves; the poor widow (C) is the exception.

## 4. What this means for "no other M-code" as a sorting aid

M64's 41 choose verses with no other M-code were parked as having "no significant additional bearing" (OT-93). The M09 set is different:
- 23 of the 27 carry inner-being bearing in their passage (groups A to E, less Jer 39:10).
- Seven of them are the plainest humility verses in the cluster (group A).
- The other M-codes sit in the neighbouring verses, for example M08 pride in Jam 4:6, Luk 1:51 and Luk 14:7–10, and M02 anger in Mar 14:4 and Mat 26:8.

For M09, "no other M-code" does not mark verses to set aside.

## 5. Where these stand against the narrative
