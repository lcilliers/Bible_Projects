### AN-H4: 2Ki 3:27, the wrath with no bearer

10.13 says only: the verse "is held". Read outward:
- **The king's act.** The king of Moab "saw that the battle was going against him". He tried to
  break through "but they could not" (3:26). "Then he took his oldest son who was to reign in his
  place and offered him for a burnt offering on the wall", in sight of the besiegers. He gave up his
  own heir and succession at the point of defeat.
- **The wrath.** *qeṣep* (H7110A). Where "wrath came upon" a people with no bearer named, the
  passage gives a breach first: Num 1:53; 18:5; Jos 9:20; 22:20; 1Ch 27:24; 2Ch 24:18; 32:25. In
  2Ch 32:25–26 the same wrath is then called "the wrath of the Lord". In 2Ki 3 no breach is named,
  and Elisha had said, "He will also give the Moabites into your hand" (3:18).
- **The outcome.** "And they withdrew from him and returned to their own land". The campaign ends
  short of the word.
- **I read** that the verse leaves open whose wrath it is. The construction elsewhere is God's
  after a breach. The setting would also allow Moab's fury, roused by the sacrifice, or Israel's own
  horror at it. The verse does not decide.

## 2. Found by reading outward: anger in the body, the teeth

A set-based pull of the gnashing words (*ḥāraq* H2786; *brychō* G1031; *brygmos* G1030) shows the
teeth as where anger and hostility show, as the hands are for fear (10.12):
- **anger:** "The wicked man sees it and is angry; he gnashes his teeth and melts away" (Psa 112:10;
  cited in 10.6); "The wicked plots against the righteous and gnashes his teeth at him" (Psa 37:12;
  cited); Act 7:54 (cited)
- **gloating and mockery:** "All your enemies rail against you; they hiss, they gnash their teeth"
  (Lam 2:16; uncited); "like profane mockers at a feast, they gnash at me with their teeth" (Psa
  35:16; uncited)
- **God's anger as the sufferer feels it:** "He has torn me in his wrath and hated me; he has
  gnashed his teeth at me" (Job 16:9; uncited)
- **"weeping and gnashing of teeth"**, seven times in the judgment sayings (Mat 8:12; 13:42; 13:50;
  22:13; 24:51; 25:30; Luk 13:28; all uncited). Luke gives the moment: "when you see Abraham and
  Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God but you yourselves cast out" (13:28).
  I read the gnashing there as standing with the weeping, at what is seen and lost. The verses do
  not say whether it is anguish or rage.
