# God's heavy hand: human perception and its source (v1, first pass)

**Date:** 2026-09-29 · **Author:** Claude Code · **Status:** first pass, for researcher review. Escalation #1892. Nothing has been written to the DB.
**From:** #1890 (`wa-investigation-god-hardening-own-heart-findings-v1-20260929.md` §4.1).
**Researcher's questions (2026-09-29, verbatim):** "God does not have a hard heart. From the verses reviewed you did pick up that man perceives God as having a hard/heavy hand (actions speak to the inner state). a) is this man perceiving Gods actions as hard? b) is the source of this heavy handedness in another character of God (e.g. His righteousness)"

## 0. What was read

- **The 42 verses** in the #1890 ledger classed `GOD-ATTR-HARD-STRONG-HEAVY` (36), `GOD-NEGATED` (4) and `GOD-HARD-CLAIMED-BY-HUMAN` (2).
- **Each was read with ±3 verses of context** (260 verses).
- **For each verse I recorded three things:**
  1. who says it (a human speaker, the narrator, or God)
  2. whether the verse frames it as someone's **experience**
  3. what the context names as its **source**
- **Limit:** this set is bounded by the hardening vocabulary. "The hand of the Lord" texts that use **no** hardening or heaviness word are **not in it**. Examples: "the hand of the Lord has gone out against me" (Rut 1:13); "your hand has come down on me" (Psa 38:2). See §4.

## 1. Two kinds of text, which must be kept apart

| Kind | Verses | Who speaks | How it is framed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Heavy / hard" hand, chains, sword, breath** (*kaved*, *qashah*, *qasheh*) | 1Sa 5:6, 5:7, 5:11; Psa 32:4; Lam 3:7; Isa 27:1, 27:8; Isa 30:27 ("thick" smoke); Jer 20:7 ("stronger"); Mat 25:24 ("a hard man"); Mal 3:13 ("hard words") | humans under it, or the narrator describing them. **In this set, God never calls his own hand heavy or hard** | as **experience**: terror, affliction, wasting, being walled in, fear |
| **"Strong / mighty hand and outstretched arm"** (*chazaq*) | the Exodus formula: Exo 6:1; 13:9; Deu 3:24; 4:34; 5:15; 6:21; 7:8; 7:19; 9:26; 11:2; 26:8; Jos 4:24; 1Ki 8:42; 2Ch 6:32; Neh 1:10; Psa 89:13; 136:12; Isa 40:10; Jer 21:5; 32:21; 50:34; Eze 20:33–34; Dan 9:15; Exo 32:11; Pro 23:11 | God, Moses, the creed, prayers | as **deliverance remembered**, taught to children (Deu 6:21), and pleaded in prayer (Deu 9:26; Dan 9:15) |

## 2. Question (a): is it man perceiving God's actions as hard?

**Largely yes.** The texts themselves locate the "hardness" in **the human side**. They show four things.

1. **The hard or heavy hand is felt by the one it is against, or by the one who is hiding from it.**
   - The Philistines under the ark: "his hand is **hard** against us and against Dagon our god" (1Sa 5:7). The narrator states the experience ("heavy … he terrified and afflicted them", 5:6; "a deathly panic … very heavy", 5:11), and the Philistines read it as intent to kill: "to kill us and our people" (5:10).
   - The psalmist: "day and night your hand was **heavy** upon me; my strength was dried up" (Psa 32:4). **The context gives the cause, and it is inside the psalmist:** "**when I kept silent**, my bones wasted away" (32:3). **The heaviness ends at confession:** "I acknowledged my sin … and you forgave" (32:5). The psalm opens with the outcome God intended: "Blessed is the one whose transgression is forgiven" (32:1).
2. **The text corrects the perception, often in the same passage.**
   - **Lam 3.** "He has made my chains **heavy** … he shuts out my prayer … He is a bear lying in wait for me" (3:7–10). **The same poem then says:** "the Lord will not cast off forever, for, though he cause grief, he will have compassion according to the abundance of his steadfast love; **for he does not afflict from his heart**" (3:31–33).
     - **This is the text that separates the action from the heart.** The chains are felt as heavy, and the poet confesses that the affliction does not come from God's heart.
     - It answers the researcher's "actions speak to the inner state" directly. **Here the action and the heart are explicitly not the same.**
   - **Mat 25:24–26.** "Master, I knew you to be a **hard** man". The servant names the source of his perception himself: "**so I was afraid**, and I went and hid your talent" (25:25). The master answers "You wicked and slothful servant!" (25:26). **The perception of hardness is born of fear, and the parable condemns it.**
   - **Isa 59:1–2.** "the Lord's hand is **not shortened**, that it cannot save, or his ear **dull** [*kaved*]". The felt absence of God's hand has a stated cause: "**your iniquities have made a separation between you and your God**, and your sins have hidden his face from you". **What is felt as God's weakness or deafness is the human separation.**
   - **Mal 3:13–15.** "Your words have been **hard** against me". The hard words are the human verdict on God: "It is vain to serve God … Evildoers … prosper". **The hardness is in the human perception of God's ways**, and 3:16 answers with those who feared the Lord and "a book of remembrance".
3. **The one who is overpowered for good perceives God as stronger, not as hard.** "O Lord, you have deceived me … **you are stronger than I**, and you have prevailed" (Jer 20:7). The force is **the word**: "a burning fire shut up in my bones" (20:9). **The perception is of a strength he cannot resist, not of cruelty.** Compare M02 §15, the prophet as vessel.
4. **The same hand is "hard" or "saving" depending on which side one stands.** Toward Egypt it is "great deeds of terror" (Deu 4:34; 26:8; Jer 32:21). Toward Israel it is "brought us out" (Deu 6:21). In Jer 21:5, **Israel finds itself on Egypt's side of the formula**: "I myself will fight against you with **outstretched hand and strong arm**, in anger and in fury". **The hand does not change; the person's position before it changes.**

## 3. Question (b): is the source in another character of God, such as his righteousness?

**Yes.** The contexts name the source, and **righteousness is one of several**. It is always named **beside** love and faithfulness, never alone.

| Source the context names | Verses |
|---|---|
| **Righteousness and justice**, with steadfast love and faithfulness | "You have a mighty arm; strong is your hand … **Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne; steadfast love and faithfulness go before you**" (Psa 89:13–14). "The Lord our God is **righteous in all the works that he has done**" (Dan 9:14), then "who brought your people out … with a mighty hand" (9:15), then "according to all your righteous acts, let your anger … turn away" (9:16) |
| **Love and the sworn oath** | "it is **because the Lord loves you and is keeping the oath** … that the Lord has brought you out with a mighty hand" (Deu 7:8). The same passage goes on: "repays to their face those who hate him" (7:10) |
| **Steadfast love** (*chesed*) | "with a strong hand and an outstretched arm, **for his steadfast love endures forever**" (Psa 136:12). The refrain covers the striking of the firstborn (136:10) and the overthrow of Pharaoh (136:15). "He does not retain his anger forever, **because he delights in steadfast love**" (Mic 7:18) |
| **Advocacy for the weak** | "their **Redeemer is strong**; he will plead their cause" (the fatherless, Pro 23:11; the oppressed, Jer 50:34) |
| **Discipline** | "consider the **discipline** of the Lord your God, his greatness, his mighty hand" (Deu 11:2); "that he might discipline you" (Deu 4:36) |
| **Covenant purpose, through judgment** | "with a mighty hand … and with wrath poured out **I will be king over you** … I will enter into judgment with you … **and I will bring you into the bond of the covenant**" (Eze 20:33–37) |
| **Removal of sin** | after "his fierce breath", "**by this the guilt of Jacob will be atoned for**" (Isa 27:8–9) |
| **The shepherd's arm** | "his **arm** rules for him … He will tend his flock like a shepherd; he will gather the lambs **in his arms**" (Isa 40:10–11). **The same arm rules and carries** |
| **Anger** (named, and set inside a purpose) | Jer 21:5 ("in anger and in fury"); Isa 30:27 ("burning with his anger"). The purpose that follows: "the way of life and the way of death" (Jer 21:8); "when the Lord binds up the brokenness of his people, and heals the wounds inflicted by his blow" (Isa 30:26) |
| **Holiness against idols** [Claude reading: inferred, not stated] | 1Sa 5: the heavy hand comes when the ark is set beside Dagon, and Dagon falls (5:3–4). No sin of Ashdod's people is named |

**So the answer to (b):**
- **The heaviness has its source in God's character**, and the texts name **righteousness and justice** (Psa 89:14; Dan 9:14, 16) **together with** steadfast love, faithfulness, the oath, and the cause of the weak.
- **Anger is named too, but never as the last word.** It stands inside a purpose: covenant, atonement, healing.
- **Nowhere in this set is the source a hardness in God.** Lam 3:33 states outright that it is not **from his heart**.

## 4. Proposed next pass (not started)

The set here was bounded by hardening and heaviness words. **The fuller answer is in the "hand of the Lord / your hand against / upon me" texts**, which use no such word. Examples: Rut 1:13 ("the hand of the Lord has gone out against me"); Job 19:21 ("the hand of God has touched me"); Psa 38:2; 39:10; 1Sa 7:13; Eze 1:3 and 3:14 (the hand on the prophet).

**Proposal:** take every verse where *yad* / *cheir* ("hand") or *zeroa* ("arm") belongs to God. Read each for the same three things, keeping the human-perception and God-declaration verses apart. **This is a proposal only**, and it is the researcher's call whether and when to run it. #1891 and M02 unit 2 are queued.
