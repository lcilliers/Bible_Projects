# Special investigation: does Scripture speak of God hardening his own heart? Findings (v1)

**Date:** 2026-09-29 · **Author:** Claude Code · **Status:** for researcher review. Escalation #1890, which runs the method approved under #1889. Nothing has been written to the DB.
**Method:** `wa-investigation-god-hardening-own-heart-method-v1-20260928.md`. Researcher, 2026-09-29 (verbatim): "approved, use STEP, raise the coverage gap escalation". The coverage gap is raised as #1891.
**Ledger:** `wa-investigation-god-hardening-own-heart-ledger-v1-20260929.csv` has 644 verses. Each has a class assigned by reading, and the key verses carry a note.

## 1. Answer

**No text in Scripture, as searched here, speaks of God's own heart, spirit or soul being hardened, made heavy or made stubborn, whether by God himself or by anyone else.**

This is the documented negative the brief asked for. The researcher called it "a material finding in its own right". What makes it well evidenced:
- **Every verse** of the hardening vocabulary (§2) was read, and God is **never** the owner of a hardened seat.
- **Every verse** where the heart or soul is plausibly God's (§3) was read, and none hardens.
- **STEP and `iba.db` hold exactly the same verses** for every code searched (§5).

**What the texts do say** is more specific than a bare absence. There are four patterns (§4):
1. **God hardens human hearts**, and humans harden their own.
2. **Hardness, heaviness or strength is said of God's hand, arm, sword and breath, never of his heart.**
3. **The same root, used of God, means glory, not hardness.**
4. **God's heart is described in ways that are the opposite of hardening:** it grieves, recoils in compassion, and "does not afflict from his heart".

The one text where God's own inner seat refuses a people is **Jer 15:1**, which is probably the text behind the researcher's recollection. See §4.4.

## 3. God's own heart, soul and spirit: the other direction

## 4. What the texts show instead

### 4.1 Hardness belongs to God's hand, arm, sword and breath, not his heart

- "The hand of the Lord was **heavy**" (1Sa 5:6, 11); "his hand is **hard** against us" (1Sa 5:7); "your hand was **heavy** upon me" (Psa 32:4).
- The "**strong** hand" (*yad chazaqah*) of the Exodus (Exo 6:1; 13:9; Deu 4:34 …; see the ledger, class `GOD-ATTR-HARD-STRONG-HEAVY`, 36 verses in all).
- The "**thick** [*koved*, heaviness] rising smoke" of his coming in anger (Isa 30:27).
- "his **hard** and great and strong sword" (Isa 27:1).
- **The nearest a hardening word comes to God's own spirit:** "he removed them with **his fierce [*qasheh*, hard] breath** [*ruach*] in the day of the east wind" (Isa 27:8). Read in context, this *ruach* is **the east wind as God's instrument**, not God's inner spirit. It is recorded as the closest case, not as a positive.
- **God's heart, when it is described as strong, is strong in understanding, not hard:** "God is mighty … mighty in strength of heart [ESV "understanding"]" (Job 36:5); "He is wise in heart and mighty in strength — **who has hardened himself against him**, and succeeded?" (Job 9:4). **In Job 9:4 God's heart is wise, and the hardening belongs to the human who resists him.**

### 4.2 The same root, used of God, means glory

- *Kaved*, "to be heavy", is the word for Pharaoh's hardened heart (Exo 8:15, 32; 9:34; 10:1).
- In the same narrative, God says "I will **harden** [*chazaq*] Pharaoh's heart … and I will **get glory** [*kaved*] over Pharaoh" (Exo 14:4, 17). **The root that makes the human heart heavy makes God glorious.**
- Other examples: Lev 10:3; Isa 26:15; Hag 1:8; Eze 28:22; 39:13.

### 4.3 What is denied of God with a hardening word

- "He does not **retain** [*chazaq*] his anger forever, because he delights in steadfast love" (Mic 7:18).
- "the Lord's hand is not shortened … or his ear **dull** [*kaved*, heavy]" (Isa 59:1).
- "he does not afflict from his heart" (Lam 3:33).
- "my soul shall not abhor you" (Lev 26:11).

**Where a hardening or heaviness word is used of God's own disposition, it is negated.**

### 4.4 The one text of refusal in God's own seat: Jer 15:1

"Though Moses and Samuel stood before me, yet **my heart** [*nephesh*, literally "my soul"] **would not turn toward this people**. Send them out of my sight."
- **God's inner seat refuses a people**, and the refusal is proof even against the greatest intercessors.
- **It is not hardening.** No hardening word is used. The expression is "my soul is not toward" them.
- **This is probably the text behind the recollection** of God's heart hardening. [Claude reading] It is the only verse found where God's own seat is turned **away**. Beside it stand the aversion texts: Lev 26:30; Psa 11:5; Isa 1:14; Amo 6:8.

### 4.5 A human says God is hard, and is wrong

- "Master, **I knew you to be a hard man**, reaping where you did not sow" (Mat 25:24). This is the wicked servant's view of the parable's master (a figure for God), and the parable condemns it.
- In Mal 3:13 the Lord says "**Your words** have been hard against me". **The hardness is in the human speech about God.**

## 5. Coverage

- **STEP against `iba.db`:** every code was searched in STEP by its exact lettered code. Bare numbers such as H2388 or H3513 return nothing in STEP. The two verse lists were then compared.
  - **Result: identical for every code.** No verse is in STEP but missing from the DB, and none is in the DB but missing from STEP.
  - This covers the hardening codes of all three passes (the 11 added codes were compared separately, also identical: for example H2389 54 = 54 and H3515 40 = 40), and the heart/soul codes (H3820A 550 = 550; H3824 230 = 230; G2588 149 = 149; H5315G 230 = 230 …). That is **43 codes, all identical**.
- **The limit:** STEP and `iba.db` come from the same tagged text. **A verse absent from that text is absent from both**, so this comparison cannot see it. That is the question of #1891 (29,760 verses against a canon of about 31,100). **The finding holds for the tagged text as it stands.** If #1891 finds missing verses, they should be checked against this question.

## 6. Limits, stated so they can be checked

1. **The God's-heart screen (§3) is an English screen.** Its filter was: a divine name or a "my/his/your heart/soul/mind" pronoun.
   - 490 heart/soul verses fell outside it and were not read.
   - A God-owned heart in those 490 would have to be expressed with no pronoun and no divine name, for example "the heart" with God as an antecedent from an earlier verse. **Not ruled out by reading. Considered unlikely.**
   - Reading them would close this limit completely, at a cost of about 490 short verses.
2. **The spirit (*ruach*, *pneuma*) was not screened as God's seat** beyond the hardening vocabulary. That vocabulary finds any hardening word, whatever it is joined to, so a hardened divine spirit would have appeared in §2 (Isa 27:8 did).
3. **The vocabulary is Strong's-based.**
   - Expressions without a hardening word would be missed where the owner is human, for example "heart of stone" (Eze 11:19; 36:26, *leb ha'eben*) and "fat heart" (*shamen*).
   - **For God they are covered by §3,** which reads God's seat verses whatever words they use.
   - The four candidate additions in the method (stiff-necked, *shamen*, heart of stone, shut/close) were covered like this. Stiff-necked is inside H7186 and G4644. The other three are human-only images and do not appear in any of God's seat verses.
4. **The English text is the ESV.** The Hebrew and Greek readings cited in brackets are from lexical knowledge and are marked [lexical] where they go beyond the DB tags: Job 36:5 *lev*; Jer 15:1 *nephesh*; Hos 11:8 *nehpak*.
