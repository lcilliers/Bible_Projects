## 1. What OT-105.8 holds

OT-105 (#2149) is *The poor as seen by another: what it moves*. It is the thread split off by the M09
strand on your instruction: *"observing someone else as being poor triggers/or should trigger

The passage was read in full (ESV, iba.db `verse`): Luke 14:12–24.

## 2. The reading

**The passage has two parts.**
- **Jesus to his host (14:12–14).** "When you give a dinner or a banquet, do not invite your friends
  or your brothers or your relatives or rich neighbors, lest they also invite you in return and you
  be repaid. But when you give a feast, invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind, and you
  will be blessed, because they cannot repay you. For you will be repaid at the resurrection of the
  just."
- **The parable (14:15–24).** A guest says, "Blessed is everyone who will eat bread in the kingdom of
  God!" (14:15), and Jesus answers with a parable.
  - The invited "all alike began to make excuses": a field, five yoke of oxen, a wife (14:18–20).
  - "So the servant came and reported these things to his master. Then the master of the house
    became angry and said to his servant, 'Go out quickly to the streets and lanes of the city, and
    bring in the poor and crippled and blind and lame'" (14:21).
  - Then the highways and hedges, "that my house may be filled", and the end: "none of those men who
    were invited shall taste my banquet" (14:23–24).

**The main operations:**
- **In 14:21, anger at a refusal turned into a wider invitation.** The anger has a stated ground, the
  excuses reported (14:18–21), and a stated outcome: the poor brought in, and the invited shut out
  (14:24). The poor are the ones the angry master turns to.
- **In 14:12–14, the reason to invite the poor:** "because they cannot repay you". The giver is told to
  give where no return can come, and is promised repayment "at the resurrection of the just". This is
  the thread's own question, what seeing the poor moves in a person, given as a command with its
  reason.

## 3. What the narrative already holds

- **10.13 *Anger*, *Other parties*** quotes 14:21 under "The master in Jesus' parables", in one
  sentence: "In another parable the anger turns into a wider invitation". It quotes the anger and the

- **10.7** has 14:7–10 (the lowest place) and 14:28–31 (counting the cost). **10.15** has 14:8–10.
- **Not in the book:** 14:12–14 (invite those who cannot repay), 14:18–20 (the excuses) and 14:24.

## 4. The impact

**For M02 (a completed cluster):** one gap. 10.13 tells the master's anger without what it rested on.
Anger in the verses has a ground, and here the passage gives it: invited guests who "all alike began
to make excuses". One clause in 10.13 would complete the telling: the anger follows the report of the
excuses, and ends with "none of those men who were invited shall taste my banquet". This keeps the
ground the verse joins to the anger (the M72 lesson: a role never licenses dropping what the verse
joins to the act).

**For OT-105 itself (the poor as seen by another): the key verse is 14:12–14, not 14:21.**
- It states what seeing the poor should move in a person: inviting those who cannot repay, *because*
  they cannot, with repayment looked for from God.
- It belongs where OT-105 is picked up: when giving, generosity or reward is read (OT-105's target
  clusters M05, M46, M26 and others, none yet read), or in 10.10 *Relating to others*.
- Under the rule for this review, it should **stay on OT-105** as a verse handle, not be woven from
  the M02 side.

**The excuses (14:18–20)** are polite refusals ("Please have me excused") given for a choice already
made. They bear on 10.7 (choosing) and 10.11 (what is covered). The verses give the excuses only, not
what lay behind them. They are noted here, not proposed for weaving.
