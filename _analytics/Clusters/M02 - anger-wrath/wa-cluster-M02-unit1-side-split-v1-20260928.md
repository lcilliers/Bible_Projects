**If the §3 recommendations are accepted, the sizes are:**
- **unit 1: about 245 verses.** That is 219 human, plus 20 from B1/B2/B4/B5/B8/B9, plus Jon 4:2 from B7. This is 1.7 times M20's reading.
- **unit 2: about 395 verses.** That is 303 divine, plus B3 (11), B6 (4) and B7 less Jon 4:2 (77).

## 2. How the split was made

- **Every verse was read.** The side comes from who is angry in the verse, not from the Strong's number: the same word, *aph* or *chemah*, falls on both sides.
- **Impersonal "wrath"** with no bearer named (for example "lest wrath come upon the congregation", Num 1:53; "the day of wrath", Pro 11:4) was read in context. Where the context makes it God's, it is **divine**. Where it does not, it is **B5**.
- **"Provoking the Lord to anger"** (the long *ka'as* series in Kings and Jeremiah) is **divine**. The anger is God's, and the human side is the provocation. The pilot §2 table puts these in "the seat of the offender", which is unit 2's subject.
- **Meribah as a place name:** the verses about the people's quarrel are **human** (Exo 17:7; Num 20:13, 24; 27:14; Deu 32:51). Deu 33:8 and Psa 81:7, where God does the striving or testing, are **divine**. The pure boundary markers (Eze 47:19; 48:28) are **none**.
- **"Venom" / "poison" (*chemah*)** as a figure for the enemies' malice is **human** (Deu 32:33; Psa 58:4; 140:3). Venom that God sends as literal poison (Deu 32:24) is **none**. "My spirit drinks their poison", from the arrows of the Almighty (Job 6:4), is **divine**.
- **A spot-check of the divine bucket** listed every divine verse that names no divine party (16 verses). All 16 were re-read, and all are God's wrath in context.

## 3. Borderline: 118 verses in 9 classes. These are not decided silently.

Each class has a recommendation, and **the researcher's ruling is asked for.** An asterisk (\*) marks a seated verse.

**B1. Human anger attributed to God's Spirit or to God (6), recommended for unit 1.** The anger sits inside a human, but the text says it comes from God.
- Num 25:11: Phinehas, "jealous with my jealousy"
- Judg 14:19\* and 1Sa 11:6\*: "the Spirit … rushed upon him … hot anger"
- Jer 6:11: "full of the wrath of the Lord; I am weary of holding it in"
- Jer 15:17: "you had filled me with indignation"
- Eze 3:14\*: "in the heat of my spirit, the hand of the Lord being strong upon me"

This is the pilot §2.3 "vessel", widened, and batch A §2.4 covers the Spirit verses.

**B2. Divine and human anger in one verse (5), recommended for unit 1 for the human part and unit 2 for the divine part.**
- Num 11:10: the Lord's anger, and Moses displeased
- 2Ch 28:9: the Lord angry, and Israel's rage "that has reached up to heaven"
- Psa 7:6: God's anger against "the fury of my enemies"
- Psa 76:10: "the wrath of man shall praise you"
- Rev 11:18: "the nations raged, but your wrath came"

**B3. A human agent carrying out God's wrath (11), recommended for unit 2.**
- 1Sa 28:18: Saul did not carry out God's fierce wrath
- Isa 10:5–6: Assyria, "the rod of my anger"
- Isa 13:3, 5: "my mighty men to execute my anger"
- Jer 25:15: Jeremiah gives out the cup
- Jer 50:25: the weapons of his wrath
- Eze 23:25: "they shall deal with you in fury"
- Eze 25:14: "by the hand of my people … according to my anger"
- Rom 13:4, 5\*: the authority as "an avenger who carries out God's wrath", with the conscience

**Note:** Eze 23:25 is the one verse here where the agents' own fury is named. It could go to unit 1.

**B4. Jesus as the one who is angry (2). The researcher's ruling is needed.**
- Mar 3:5\*: "with anger, grieved at their hardness of heart"
- Mar 10:14: indignant

The pilot recorded Mar 10:14's party as divine and left it unresolved. **Recommendation:** read both in unit 1 as their own item, because the verse gives the inner operation (anger with grief) in human terms. Mar 3:5 is seated.

**B5. The bearer is unclear (6), recommended for unit 1, with each side settled by reading the context.**
- Deu 32:27: the enemy's "provocation", which God fears
- 2Ki 3:27: "great wrath against Israel" after Mesha's sacrifice (whose wrath?)
- Job 19:29: "wrath brings the punishment of the sword"
- Job 36:18: "beware lest wrath entice you into scoffing" (Job's own wrath, or God's?)
- Job 40:11: God invites Job to "pour out the overflowings of your anger" (a human invited to wield anger like God's)
- Pro 11:23: "the expectation of the wicked [ends] in wrath"

**B6. God's anger against Moses (4), the case named in the handoff, recommended for unit 2.**
- Exo 4:14\*
- Deu 1:37; 3:26; 4:21 ("the Lord was angry with me because of you")

These are God's anger borne by a named leader, so they belong to unit 2 as a class. They are flagged because the handoff named "the anger of the Lord was kindled against Moses" as a borderline example.

**B7. A human addressing God about God's anger, or acting toward it (78), recommended for unit 2, except Jon 4:2 → unit 1.** The class covers:
- intercession (Gen 18:30, 32; Exo 32:11–12; Deu 9:18–20; Psa 106:23; Jer 18:20\*)
- the lament psalms and Lamentations addressed to God (Psa 6:1; 38:1, 3\*; 60:1; 74:1; 79:5; 80:4; 85:4–5; 88:7, 16; 89:38, 46; 90:7, 9, 11; 102:10; Lam 2:21–22; 3:43; 5:22; …)
- imprecations addressed to God (Psa 56:7; 59:13; 69:24; 79:6; Jer 10:25; 18:23; Lam 3:66)
- a stated human act toward God's anger: fear (Deu 9:19), shame (Ezr 8:22), humbling (2Ch 12:7, 12; 32:26\*), a resolve in the heart (2Ch 29:10\*), inquiry (2Ki 22:13; 2Ch 34:21), bearing it (Mic 7:9), false assurance (Jer 2:35), hiding from it (Rev 6:16–17), leaving vengeance to it (Rom 12:19)
- the creed addressed to God (Neh 9:17; Psa 86:15; Mic 7:18)

**Why unit 2:** the handoff defines unit 2 as "divine anger as borne by the human", and this class is exactly that, with the human act stated.

**The exception, Jon 4:2**, is Jonah's angry prayer quoting "slow to anger" back at God. It is one passage with Jon 4:1, 4 and 9, which are human, so it should be read with them. The full reference list is in the CSV (`side = B7`).

**B8. Human anger or trouble that God induces (3), recommended for unit 1.**
- Deu 32:21: "I will provoke them to anger with a foolish nation"
- Rom 10:19, which quotes it
- Eze 32:9\*: "I will trouble the hearts of many peoples"

The operation is the human's, and the cause is God.

**B9. A parable figure who stands for God (3), recommended for unit 1, marked as figures.**
- Mat 18:34: the master "in anger"
- Mat 22:7: "the king was angry"
- Luk 14:21: the master of the house

The pilot §1.3 read these as anger turning straight into action. By contrast, Luk 15:28 (the elder brother) is a human figure and is on the human side.

## 4. Not read: none (39) and other (2)

- **none**, grouped by kind:
  - lament forms, *qinah* (2Sa 1:17; 2Ch 35:25; Jer 9:10, 20; Eze 2:10; 19:1, 14; 26:17; 27:2, 32; 28:12; 32:2, 16; Amo 5:1; 8:10)
  - the theophany smoke (Exo 19:18; Psa 104:32; 144:5)
  - heat and warmth (Jos 9:12; Job 37:17; Psa 58:9; Lam 5:10; Hos 7:5)
  - wind, storm and sea (Psa 11:6\*; 124:5\*; Isa 54:11; Jon 1:11, 13, 15; Hab 3:14; Zec 7:14; Hos 13:3)
  - splinter and twig (Hos 10:7; Joe 1:7)
  - boundary Meribah (Eze 47:19; 48:28)
  - literal venom (Deu 32:24)
  - "repaired" (Neh 3:20)
  - "might attack" (Est 8:11\*)

  The pilot §0 already recorded the three seated verses (Psa 11:6, 124:5, Est 8:11) as spurious seat links.
- **other:** Rev 12:12 (the devil "in great wrath") and Rev 12:17 (the dragon "became furious").
- **Hab 3:14** ("came like a whirlwind to scatter me") is enemies rushing in. It is put under none because the word is a figure of motion. If the researcher counts it as hostility, it moves to human.
