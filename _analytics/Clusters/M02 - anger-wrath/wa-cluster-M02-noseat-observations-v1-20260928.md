## 0. What was read

- **681** verses carry an M02 word. **66** of them also carry an M47 seat word, and those are the M47 cross-cluster set. The other **615** have no seat word.
- **Read in full:** the verses of every Strong's that is never seated (§1, 37 Strong's, about 110 verse entries).
- **Read for the implicit-seat Strong's (§2):** every verse of *aph*, *chemah*, *charah* and *ka'as*, plus the human-bearer verses of the rest. The divine-bearer formulae ("the anger of the Lord was kindled against …") were read as a series, not one by one.
- **Context** was read for about 25 passages.
- **Rules applied:** the same as M20, so see the pilot index:
  - the implicit seat counts only where the seat word elsewhere is used in an inner-being sense
  - the programme's inner-being definition (prose section 5)
  - the subject is the human inner being (prose section 1090, `hib.set`)
  - `phenomenon.set` hidden-behind-act
- **Links that do not count** (non-inner-being senses of the M47 word): Pro 25:23, Eze 13:13, 19:12 and Psa 11:6 (wind); Num 11:33 (meat); Eze 16:26 (lustful "flesh"); Eze 7:19 (*nephesh* as hunger); Psa 124:5 ("us"); Est 8:11 (lives as targets). No M02 Strong's loses its implicit status through this, because each still has at least one genuine link.
- **Tag notes, one line each:**
  - *qinah* H7015 "dirge" (16 verses) is a **lament form**, grief put into formal speech, not anger
  - *qetseph* H7110B and *qetsaphah* H7111 "splinter / twig" are homonyms, with no inner being
  - *cham* H2525 "warm" (bread, garments) has no inner being
  - *saar* H5590 "rage" is mostly the **sea** raging (Jon 1:11, 13)

## 1. Strong's with no seat anywhere — observations

### 1.1 *Thumos* G2372 (18 verses): the passion word that is never seated

- **What it is.** In Greek, *thumos* is itself the old word for the heated, passionate spirit, so it carries its own inner place (compare M20 §1.3, the seat inside the word). It is **never** found with an M47 word in the same verse.
- **Three settings, three senses:**
  - **Vice lists** (2Cor 12:20; Gal 5:20; Eph 4:31; Col 3:8). The inner place is given **by the list's frame**, not by a seat. In Gal 5:19 anger is among "the works of the flesh", with 5:17 (flesh against Spirit) nearby. In Col 3:8 it is put away along with speech "from your mouth". **Flesh and mouth take the seat's place.**
  - **Crowd rage** (Luk 4:28, "all in the synagogue were filled with wrath"; Act 19:28, "enraged and … crying out, 'Great is Artemis'"). This is the **collective as container**: "filled", with no organ named. The shout carries it.
  - **Divine wrath in Revelation** (14:10, 19; 15:1, 7; 16:1, 19; 19:15). Wrath becomes **a substance**: wine, a cup, bowls, a winepress. The subject is divine. The human side is **drinking it** (14:10; 16:19). There is no inner operation of God to read, only its effect poured out.
- **How the sense shifts.** Rev 14:8 and 18:3 have "the wine of the **passion** of her sexual immorality". The same word means intoxicating passion, and the drinking image is shared with God's wrath.
- **Heb 11:27** is a no-seat verse about **Moses's** inner operation (faith, not being afraid). The king's anger is only its occasion: "for he endured as seeing him who is invisible".

### 1.2 *Aganakteō* G0023 / *aganaktēsis* G0024: indignation (8 verses)

- **What it is.** A reaction **triggered by something seen or heard**: "when the ten heard it" (Mat 20:24; Mar 10:41); "when the chief priests … saw" (Mat 21:15); "when the disciples saw it" (Mat 26:8); "when Jesus saw it" (Mar 10:14).
- **What carries it.** **Speech**: "Why this waste?" (Mat 26:8). In Mar 14:4, "said **to themselves** indignantly" (*pros heautous*): once again the inner place is given **by a reflexive pronoun** (as in M20, Act 10:17). The **status rivalry** between disciples (Mat 20:24; Mar 10:41) is relational.
- **Before and after.** Luk 13:14: the synagogue ruler, indignant at Jesus, **turns and addresses the crowd**. The anger is displaced onto a third party.
- **Positive value.** 2Cor 7:11 lists "what indignation, what fear, what longing, what zeal" as the fruit of "godly grief", a sequence of inner operations valued as good. Mar 10:14 is **Jesus's** indignation. The bearer class is recorded as divine in the party data; it is noted here and not resolved.

### 1.3 *Orgizō* G3710 and the NT anger verbs (*thumoō*, *cholaō*, *parorgizō*, *thumomacheō*, *orgilos*)

- **Anger turns straight into action.** This is especially so in the parables and in narrative:
  - "in anger his master delivered him to the jailers" (Mat 18:34)
  - "The king was angry, and he sent his troops" (Mat 22:7)
  - the master "became angry and said … bring in the poor" (Luk 14:21)
  - Herod "became furious, and he sent and killed all the male children" (Mat 2:16)

  **There is no interval in which a seat would be named.** The act is the evidence of the state.
- **The body refuses.** Luk 15:28: the elder brother "was angry and **refused to go in**. His father came out and entreated him". The complaint that follows (15:29–30) exposes a hidden grievance, "these many years I have served you". The inner state was **held behind service**, and anger releases it (hidden-behind-act).
- **The inner act is judged like the outer.** In Mat 5:21–22 anger is set beside murder, and it incurs the same "liable to judgment".
- **Anger bounded by time.** Eph 4:26–27, "Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger, and give no opportunity to the devil". The neighbouring 4:23 has "renewed in the spirit of your minds". The implicit seat here is the passage's.
- **The body inside the word.** *Cholaō* (Joh 7:23) is built on *cholē*, "bile": the Greek carries an inner organ inside the verb, as *thumos* does.
- **Relational causation.** In Eph 6:4, fathers "provoke to anger" (*parorgizō*), exactly parallel to M20's Col 3:21 (provoke → discouraged). In Rom 10:19, **God** provokes Israel to jealousy and anger: an inner operation induced from outside, with God as the cause.

### 1.4 Human rage with a body face: *pa·neh / na·phal* ("face fell"), *che·ma* (Aramaic), *be·nas*, *za·eph* (sullen), *za·aph* (rage)

- **The face carries it.**
  - Gen 4:5–6: "Cain was very angry, and his face fell … Why are you angry, and why has your face fallen?" God reads the anger **off the face**. What follows in 4:7 is an inner struggle stated as an image: "sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is for you, and you must rule over it".
  - Dan 3:19: "Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury, and the expression of his face was changed". The rage is **externalised onto the furnace**, "heated seven times more".
- **The body withdraws, and the seat is supplied by another person.** 1Ki 21:4: Ahab went home "vexed and sullen … lay down on his bed and turned away his face and would eat no food". In 21:5 Jezebel says, **"Why is your spirit so vexed that you eat no food?"** The narrator gives no seat. The wife names one, reading it off the body. (1Ki 20:43, "vexed and sullen", is the same pair.)
- **The same pattern with other words:**
  - 1Sa 20:34: Jonathan "rose from the table in fierce anger and ate no food … for he was grieved for David". Anger, grief and fasting come together.
  - 2Ki 5:11–14: Naaman "was angry and went away … turned and went away in a rage". His inner thought is quoted ("Behold, I thought that he would surely come out"). **The servants talk him round** (5:13), and "his flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child" (5:14). The movement runs from rage to persuasion to obedience to restoration, with no seat word anywhere.
  - 2Ch 16:10 (Asa "in a rage with" the seer); Dan 2:12 (the king "angry and very furious"). Anger **issues in orders**: imprisonment, execution.
- **Divine anger in the same words:** Jer 3:12, "I will not look on you in anger", literally "cause my face to fall". The same body idiom is used of God.

### 1.5 *Eris* G2054, *dichostasia*, *logomachia*, *paradiatribē*, *paroxusmos*, *prokaleō*: strife words

- **Mostly vice lists and church-order texts.** The inner place is given **by the frame**:
  - 1Cor 3:3, "you are still **of the flesh** … behaving only in a human way"
  - 1Ti 6:4–5, "puffed up with conceit … depraved **in mind**". *Nous* is excluded from M47 by ruling 4, which is why these do not count as seated.
  - Gal 5:26 ("provoking one another, envying one another") sits between 5:24–25 (flesh crucified, walk by the Spirit) and 6:1.
- **An inner motive behind a public act.** Phili 1:15, "Some indeed preach Christ from envy and rivalry, but others from good will". The same act has different inner sources.
- **The same word with opposite value.**
  - *Paroxusmos* is the "sharp disagreement" that parts Paul and Barnabas (Act 15:39), and also "stir up one another **to love** and good works" (Heb 10:24).
  - *Erethizō* is "your zeal has stirred up most of them" (2Cor 9:2), and also "do not provoke your children" (Col 3:21).

  This adds to the candidate synthesis theme "one word with opposite value".

### 1.6 *Ashan* H6225 "smoke" (6 verses), *meribah* H4809H / H4808, and the remaining single-verse words

- ***Ashan*.** The anger word is **literally smoke**: Sinai "wrapped in smoke" (Exo 19:18), and mountains that "smoke" at his touch (Psa 104:32; 144:5). Where it is anger, it is **divine anger answering a human heart**. In Deu 29:20, "the anger of the Lord and his jealousy will smoke against that man". The man is described in 29:18–19: one "whose heart is turning away … blesses himself in his heart, saying, 'I shall be safe, though I walk in the stubbornness of my heart'". **The seat in the passage is the human's secret self-blessing, and the anger answers it.**
- ***Meribah*.** A **place name that stores an inner event**: "the waters of Meribah, where the people of Israel quarreled with the Lord" (Num 20:13). In Eze 47:19 and 48:28 it is a boundary marker with no inner content left. In Psa 106:32 the next verse is seated (106:33, "they made his spirit bitter", Moses's spirit, spirit class U).
- **Gen 13:8.** "Let there be no strife between you and me … for we are kinsmen": strife refused on the ground of kinship.
- **Job's vexation** (*ka'as* H3708B, 4 verses). "Oh that my vexation were weighed" (6:2), and "my eye has grown dim from vexation" (17:7). The **eye** carries it, and the whole body: "all my members are like a shadow". Job 5:2, "vexation kills the fool", shows the inner state as lethal.
- **Rev 3:15–16, *zestos*.** "Neither hot nor cold … lukewarm … I will spit you out of my mouth". The inner state is given as **temperature**, and the response as the body rejecting food.

## 2. Strong's with an implicit seat — what the genuine links show

The implicit links for M02 are genuine. **But the seat they point to is usually not the angry party's own.** Of the 66 seated verses, the genuine links show three kinds of seat:

The seat is implicit in **the offender**, and the anger is its answer. Jer 7:19, "Is it I whom they provoke? … Is it not themselves, to their own shame?", **turns the provocation back onto the provokers' own inner being** |

### 2.1 Human anger narratives among the implicit-seat words (*aph, charah, chemah, qatsaph*)

- **Where the seat arrives in the next verse:**
  - Neh 5:6 → 5:7: "I was very angry when I heard their outcry … I took counsel with myself" (Hebrew: *my heart* counselled me). **The anger is followed by deliberation, and the seat arrives with the deliberation.**
  - 1Sa 1:6–7 (*ka'as*, Hannah provoked, "wept and would not eat") → 1:15–16: "a woman troubled in spirit … pouring out my soul … speaking out of my great anxiety and vexation". **The seat is given by the sufferer herself** when she explains her behaviour.
- **Where a pronoun stands in for the seat:**
  - Est 1:12: "his anger burned **within him**" (Hebrew *bo*, "in him", with no seat word)
  - Est 3:5: Haman "was filled with fury". Est 5:9 (seated) is the same scene: "joyful and glad of heart. But when Haman saw Mordecai … he was filled with wrath". The implicit seat for 3:5 is **Haman's own heart, established two chapters later.**
- **Anger that releases the grievance it hid, spoken to God:** Jon 4:1–3, "it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was angry. And he prayed … I knew that you are a gracious God … slow to anger … please take my life from me". **Jonah quotes God's own "slow to anger" (*erek appayim*) against him.** The prophet's anger comes from a creed he knows well. God's reply, "Do you do well to be angry?" (4:4, 9), leads to "angry enough to die".
- **Anger with an act:**
  - Moses "saw the calf … anger burned hot, and he threw the tablets" (Exo 32:19)
  - Balaam struck the donkey (Num 22:27)
  - Balak "struck his hands together" (Num 24:10)
  - Saul's verbal assault on Jonathan (1Sa 20:30)
  - David's verdict on the rich man, "the man who has done this deserves to die" (2Sa 12:5). The next verse turns it: "You are the man."
- **Two-bearer passages:** Num 11:10, "the anger of the Lord blazed hotly, and Moses was displeased". Moses then complains to God (11:11): the prophet is **caught between** divine anger and the people's weeping.

### 2.2 Wisdom sayings

"Slow to anger" (Pro 14:29; 15:18; 16:32; 19:11) is literally "long of nostrils". It is the **same idiom as God's self-description** in Exo 34:6 (Num 14:18; Neh 9:17; Psa 86:15; 103:8; 145:8; Jon 4:2; Nah 1:3). **A human virtue and a divine attribute share one body idiom.** The sayings also treat anger as **governable from outside**:
- "a soft answer turns away wrath" (15:1)
- "a gift in secret averts anger" (21:14)
- "the wise turn away wrath" (29:8)
- "a wise man will appease" the king's wrath (16:14)
- Jam 1:19–20, "slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God", followed at once by "the implanted word, which is able to save your souls" (1:21, seated)

### 2.3 The human vessel of divine anger

- Jer 6:11: "I am full of the wrath of the Lord; I am weary of holding it in."
- Jer 15:17: "I sat alone … for you had filled me with indignation." In 15:16 (seated): "your words became to me a joy and the delight of my heart."

**God's anger is carried inside a human being.** The prophet is its container, and it wears him out. The seat in 15:16 is the one that ate the words. This is the one place in M02 where divine anger becomes **a human inner operation**.

## 3. What carries the inner being when there is no seat (M02)

1. **The act.** Anger in the narratives turns straight into a deed: jailing, killing, striking, breaking the tablets. There is no pause in which a seat is named (§1.3; §2.1).
2. **The face and body.** The face falls, the expression changes; lying on the bed, the face turned away, food refused (§1.4).
3. **Speech.** "Why this waste?"; Jonah's prayer; Naaman's "I thought" (§1.2; §2.1).
4. **Another person names the seat.** Jezebel to Ahab; Hannah explaining herself to Eli; God asking Cain (§1.4; §2.1).
5. **Reflexive "in himself / to themselves"** (Est 1:12; Mar 14:4).
6. **The seat is inside the word.** *Thumos*, *cholaō*, and *aph* itself, which means nostril or face.
7. **Frame words.** "Flesh" (Gal 5; 1Cor 3), "mind / *nous*" (1Ti 6), "mouth" (Col 3:8).
8. **The seat belongs to the other party.** Divine anger is answered by, or answers, a **human heart**: the provoker's (cause) or the sufferer's (effect). This is the dominant pattern of the 615 verses, most of which have **God as the one who is angry**. Under the programme's definition God is not the human-inner-being subject, so the inner being in those verses lies **with the human party**. There it is usually implicit: idolatry and turning as the cause, dismay and lament as the effect.
9. **No inner operation.** Dirge, splinter, warm bread, the raging sea (§0).

**Answer to the key question (M02):**
- **Yes, human anger is repeatedly shown with no seat.** It is carried by act, face, speech, or another person's naming. Where the seat appears, it often comes **in the next verse, as a consequence or an explanation** (Neh 5:7; 1Ki 21:5; 1Sa 1:15).
- **The bulk of M02, though, is divine anger.** Its implicit seat is not God's but **the human heart it answers or strikes.** That differs from M20, where the no-seat bearer was almost always the human who was perplexed or anxious.
- **The cross-correlation holds, but it points across parties.** A seated verse for *aph* or *chemah* usually establishes *someone's* heart. It is not always the heart of the one who is angry.
