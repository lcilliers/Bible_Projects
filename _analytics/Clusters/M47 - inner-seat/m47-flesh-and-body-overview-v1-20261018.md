## 1. Two facts on your observations

- **The glorified body is in the verses**, but none of them was among the 27, because none carries an
  M47 word:
  - "who will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious body" (Php 3:21)
  - "It is sown in dishonor; it is raised in glory" (1Co 15:43); "raised a spiritual body" (15:44)
  - "this perishable body must put on the imperishable" (15:53)
  - "a building from God, a house not made with hands" (2Co 5:1–4)

  They are told in Ch 2 (*Changed*, *Sown and raised*, *Waited for*), as death and resurrection, not
  as the body.
- **2 Corinthians 4:11 uses "flesh", not "body":** "that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in
  our mortal flesh". You read it as the physical self, beside Rom 8:11 ("your mortal bodies"). So
  "mortal flesh" and "mortal bodies" already show the two words used of the same thing. That is the
  interchangeable side of your question, in the first pair you noticed.

## 4. Proposed approach

This follows the fear and M09 pattern: read in batches, then one cross-batch account, then the
chapter. Your two questions run through every batch:
- **Where are body and flesh used interchangeably, and where are they separated in meaning or
  connotation?**
- **Does body dying and flesh dying mean different things, and how?**

Each verse is read in its passage, by its main act (rule 73), with no preset list of senses. The
faces are recorded as the verses give them, with the circumstances in which each appears (#1919).

0. **Completeness check.** Compare the six main Strong's with STEP, and add any missing verses
   (#2138 route).
1. **Batch 1: the 14 verses with both words**, and parallel passages where one gospel or letter says
   "flesh" and another "body". This is where the interchange question is sharpest, so it goes first.
2. **Batch 2: *sōma* (NT body), 122 verses**, including the body of Christ, the Lord's supper, the
   raised body and the glorified body.
3. **Batch 3: *sarx* and the "fleshly" words (NT), 136 verses.**
4. **Batch 4: *bāśār*, *šᵉʾēr* and the Hebrew body words (OT), about 290 verses**, likely split by
   sense as the reading shows (for example "all flesh", kin, meat, the body).
5. **Batch 5: the corpse words, 67 verses.** These are probably mostly Ch 2 (death) or data. Your
   call whether they are in scope at all.
6. **Cross-batch account:** the faces of flesh and of body; where they meet and where they part;
   dying.
7. **Ch 7 rebuilt as flesh and body** (title yours). Every place that now cites these verses is
   reconciled:
   - what moves into Ch 7
   - what stays (for example Ch 2 for death and resurrection) with a pointer
   - the claims reconciled under rule 77, for these concepts only (#2152)
