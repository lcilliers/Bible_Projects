## 1. What OT-106.3 holds

OT-106 (#2150) is *Poor and needy: the poor person's own side*. It carries your observation of
2026-10-15 (#2148), verbatim: *"poor - to be poor, lacking resources, is a state or status naming a
persons availability of external resources (wealth); it could also be a inner being characteristic
where being or perceiving in the inner being of lack perceived but may not be true or could be true,
but the verification is not in external resources … needy is an expression with an objective to get
simpathy or help. This description for you need to be corroborated in the verses"*.

Item 3 is its M47 handle: **"poor joined to spirit or heart (Mat 5:3; Pro 16:19; Psa 109:22)"**. The
M09 split has five more verses coded **S** (the poor person's own side) that carry an M47 word:
Psa 69:32, Psa 34:2, 2Sa 12:3, Exo 30:15 and Pro 13:8. All eight were read in their passages (ESV,
iba.db `verse`).

## 2. What is already in the book

- **Mat 5:3** "Blessed are the poor in spirit" is in Ch 6 *The human spirit*, in a list of lowly
  against proud: "proud or poor in spirit". 5:4 ("those who mourn") is not cited.
- **Pro 16:19** "It is better to be of a lowly spirit with the poor" is told in four places (Ch 6,
  10.7, 10.14, Ch 12).
- **Psa 109:22** "I am poor and needy, and my heart is stricken within me" is in Ch 4 (the heart
  broken) and 10.14 (*By what others do*).
- **Psa 69:32 and 34:2** are in 10.14 *The humble*, Ch 2, Ch 4, Ch 5 and 10.15.
- **Exo 30:15** (rich and poor give the same half shekel) is in Ch 5.

The verses are cited, but the book **nowhere asks your question of them**: what being poor is on the
person's own side.

## 3. What the passages say on that question

**Proverbs 13:7–8: poverty shown, and poverty real, are not the same.** *The clearest corroboration.*
> "One pretends to be rich, yet has nothing; another pretends to be poor, yet has great wealth. The
> ransom of a man's life is his wealth, but a poor man hears no threat."

The verse sets the poverty a person shows against what he has. "Pretends" is the reflexive of the
verb for being rich and being poor: one makes himself rich, another makes himself poor. The
"verification", in your word, is in what he has, not in what he presents. 13:8 then gives poverty a
side of its own: the poor man "hears no threat". Neither verse is in the book.

**Matthew 5:3: poverty placed in the spirit.**
> "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are those who mourn,
> for they shall be comforted."

The verse locates the poverty "in spirit", not in means. That fits your second dimension. The book
lists it as lowliness (Ch 6). It does not say that the verse moves poverty inside, or that "those who
mourn" follows at once.

**Psalm 109:21–26: the poor man's own words, and the appeal.**
> "For I am poor and needy, and my heart is stricken within me. I am gone like a shadow at evening; I
> am shaken off like a locust. My knees are weak through fasting; my body has become gaunt, with no
> fat. I am an object of scorn to my accusers … Help me, O Lord my God! Save me according to your
> steadfast love!"

"Poor and needy" is said of himself, by the one who prays. It is joined at once to the heart ("my heart
is stricken") and the body (gaunt), and it ends in an appeal: "Help me". That is your third
dimension, needy as an appeal for help, and the appeal is made to God. The book quotes 109:22 alone,
as a broken heart, without the self-description or the appeal around it.

**Psalm 34:4–6 and 69:29–33: the poor man cries, and is heard.**
- "I sought the Lord, and he answered me and delivered me from all my fears … This poor man cried, and
  the Lord heard him and saved him out of all his troubles" (34:4, 6).
- "But I am afflicted and in pain; let your salvation, O God, set me on high! … For the Lord hears
  the needy" (69:29, 33).

The poor man is the one who speaks, and his fear is among what he is delivered from (34:4). 34:2 and
69:32 are in the book. 34:4–6 and 69:29, 33 are not.

**2 Samuel 12:3: what the poor man has, and loves.**
> "but the poor man had nothing but one little ewe lamb … It used to eat of his morsel and drink from
> his cup and lie in his arms, and it was like a daughter to him."

Nathan's parable gives the poor man's side as attachment to the little he has, against the rich man
who "was unwilling to take one of his own flock" (12:4). Not in the book.

**Exodus 30:15** (the same half shekel for rich and poor, "to make atonement for your lives") is
already in Ch 5 and adds nothing here.
