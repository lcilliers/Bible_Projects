## 2. How I read it (please correct)

- **The unit moves from the seat to the IB operation.** The seat stays, but as **one method among several** by which an operation is carried or shown.
- **"Different methods of operation"** = the ways the pilot found an inner operation being carried in the text (table in §3).
- **"Internally to the IB"** = how one inner operation leads to, joins, or displaces another inside the same person. Examples:
  - fear leads to hiding (Gen 3:10)
  - perplexity leads to a question, then waiting for an interpreter (Act 10:17–19)
  - anger leads to deliberation, "my heart counselled me" (Neh 5:6–7)
  - perception is blocked, then fear, then rivalry (Luk 9:45–46)
- **"Across the surface"** = movement over the boundary between inner and outer, in both directions:
  - **outward (expression):** the inner shows in face, body, act or speech (Cain's fallen face; Ahab on his bed; the grumblers' words)
  - **inward (impact):** something outside reaches in (a whisperer's words "go down into the inner parts of the body", Pro 18:8; fathers provoke and children lose heart, Col 3:21; God hides his face and the psalmist is dismayed, Psa 30:7)
  - **across persons:** one person's operation is read, named or caused by another. Jezebel names Ahab's spirit (1Ki 21:5); the anger of one answers the heart of another (M02 §2).
- **"Is affected"** = what changes or stops an operation:
  - a word ("a soft answer turns away wrath", Pro 15:1)
  - a gift (Pro 21:14)
  - an interpreter arriving (Luk 24:4; Act 2:14)
  - handing it over (Phili 4:6 → 4:7; 1Pe 5:7)
  - a time limit ("do not let the sun go down on your anger", Eph 4:26)
  - servants' persuasion (Naaman, 2Ki 5:13)
