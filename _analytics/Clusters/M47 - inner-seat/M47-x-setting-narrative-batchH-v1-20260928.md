## 0. Data notes (short)

1. **Pre-filter — "Lord of hosts" (H6635B, M72): 28 pairs in 22 verses.** This is a divine title, not dominion as a relation. The group is reported in **§1.9**, not dropped.
2. **Misleading tags** (noted, and not read as the cluster's sense):
   - **"choirmaster" (H5329 "to conduct", M76):** 10 psalm superscriptions (Psa 9:1, 11:1, 14:1, 36:1, 42:1, 45:1, 53:1, 57:1, 62:1, 69:1). The M47 word belongs to the psalm body. The heading has no bearing on it.
   - **"hand: power" (H3027H, M72): 49 verses.** Mostly the idiom "into the hand of" (Jeremiah's "into the hand of those who seek their life"; Joshua's conquest lists). It is read as **agency**, and only where the hand acts on an inner component (§1.1, §1.4).
   - **Quantity words in M46:** "abundance / greatness / multitude" of iniquity or idols (Jer 13:22; Hos 9:7; Eze 14:4), "numerous" (Deu 10:22), "to no avail" (Dan 11:27), "good for nothing" (Jer 13:10), "retinue" (1Ki 10:2). None of these is about wealth.
   - **Words in M76 and M26 used in other senses:**
     - "pity" (Psa 69:20) — *nûd*, a sympathetic shaking of the head
     - "intoxicated" (Pro 5:20) — *šāgâ*, see §4.2
     - "appointed" (Jon 4:8) and "count on" (2Co 10:2)
   - **"heart" = "midst":** "the heart of the seas" (Eze 27:27).
   - **"spokes" (Eze 10:12)** is tagged hand.
3. **The death words are outside the M-codes** (see the handoff §3), as in Batch G.

## 1. The king's heart and the subject's heart (M72)

### 1.1 The most powerful heart is the least its own

**Evidence:**
- **Held and turned:**
  - "**The king's heart is a stream of water in the hand of the Lord; he turns it wherever he will**" (Pro 21:1)
  - "the Lord … **had turned the heart of the king of Assyria** to them" (Ezr 6:22)
  - "who **put such a thing as this into the heart of the king**" (Ezr 7:27)
  - "God has **put it into their hearts** … handing over their royal power to the beast" (Rev 17:17)
  - "his wisdom, which **God had put into his mind**" (2Ch 9:23)
- **Stirred:** Cyrus, Pul, the kings of the Medes (1Ch 5:26; 2Ch 36:22; Ezr 1:1; Jer 51:11) → Batch G §11.
- **Hardened:**
  - "the Lord **hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt**" (Exo 14:8; 4:21)
  - "the Lord your God **hardened his spirit and made his heart obstinate**, that he might give him into your hand" (Deu 2:30) [H1] (Sihon)
- **Cut off and taken away:**
  - "who **cuts off the spirit of princes**" (Psa 76:12) [H1]
  - "He **takes away understanding** [*heart*] **from the chiefs** … and makes them wander" (Job 12:24)
- **Unsearchable to men, not to God:**
  - "the heart of kings is **unsearchable**" (Pro 25:3)
  - "the mind of the king of Syria was greatly troubled" — Elisha tells "the words that you speak in your bedroom" (2Ki 6:11–12; see Batch A and Batch E)

**[Claude reading]** In the text the king's heart is the heart with the most power over others. It is also the heart shown **most often as acted upon**: God turns it, puts things into it, stirs it, hardens it, and cuts it off. The unsearchable heart of Pro 25:3 is unsearchable **to subjects**. To God it is a stream in his hand.

### 1.2 The heart that claims a throne

- "You said **in your heart**, 'I will ascend to heaven … **I will set my throne on high**'" (Isa 14:13)
- "**in his own mind he shall become great** … he shall even rise up against the Prince of princes" (Dan 8:25)
- "**in her heart she says, 'I sit as a queen**, I am no widow'" (Rev 18:7)
- "the **arrogant heart of the king of Assyria**" (Isa 10:12); "Your heart was proud because of your beauty … I exposed you **before kings**" (Eze 28:17)
- "when **his heart was lifted up and his spirit was hardened** … he was brought down from his **kingly throne**" (Dan 5:20). Then "**his mind was made like that of a beast** … **until he knew that the Most High God rules** the kingdom of mankind" (5:21). See Batch G §14.10.
- **The remedy in the law of the king:** he is to write out the law and "read in it all the days of his life … **that his heart may not be lifted up above his brothers**" (Deu 17:19–20).
- **The subject's version:** "Haman said **to himself** [*in his heart*], 'Whom would the king delight to honor more than me?'" (Est 6:6). Context 6:7–12: he describes the honour, **is made to give it to Mordecai**, and "hurried to his house, mourning and with his head covered" (6:12).

[Claude reading] Isa 14, Dan 8 and Rev 18 all use the same form: **the heart speaks a claim to rule**, and the fall follows. Deu 17 gives the only preventive, and it is a daily one: reading, so that the heart is not raised. Haman's inner sentence is the courtier's form of the same claim, and the reversal follows within the same scene.

### 1.3 Hearts won and lost for a king — the Absalom arc

**Context read:** 2Sa 13:37–39; 14:1, 24, 33; 15:1–6, 13; 19:1–14.

| Step | Text |
|---|---|
| The king's longing | "the spirit of the king **longed to go out to Absalom**, because he was comforted about Amnon" (13:39). [verify] ESV supplies "spirit"; the verse is not in M47. |
| The heart read by another | "Joab … **knew that the king's heart went out to Absalom**" (14:1) → Joab stages the Tekoite woman's case |
| The heart held back | "he is **not to come into my presence**" (14:24) → after two years, "the king **kissed** Absalom" (14:33) |
| Hearts stolen | Absalom at the gate: "your claims are good and right, but **there is no man designated by the king to hear you**" (15:3). "he would … take hold of him and **kiss** him" (15:5). "**So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel**" (15:6). |
| The report | "**The hearts of the men of Israel have gone after Absalom**" (15:13) |
| Grief after victory | "O my son Absalom" (19:4). Joab: "your servants, who have this day **saved your life** … you love those who hate you" (19:5–6) |
| Hearts swayed back | "**You are my bone and my flesh**. Why then should you be the last to bring back the king?" (19:12–13) → "**he swayed the heart of all the men of Judah as one man**" (19:14) |

**What the sequence shows [Claude reading]:**
1. **Allegiance is a heart-matter, and it moves by attention.** Absalom wins hearts by standing where the king is absent (15:2–4) and by the kiss the king himself gave him (14:33 → 15:5). The heart follows whoever **hears the cause**.
2. **The king's own heart drives the plot.** The heart that "went out to Absalom" (14:1) opens the way for the son who steals every other heart. After the victory the same heart grieves, and nearly loses the army (19:6–7).
3. **Flesh as a claim on the heart.** David recovers Judah's heart with "bone and flesh" — kinship offered as grounds for allegiance.
   - **The same claim in the opposite direction:** Abimelech — "Remember also that **I am your bone and your flesh**" (Judg 9:2) → "**their hearts inclined to follow Abimelech**, for they said, 'He is our brother'" (9:3) → he kills his seventy brothers "on one stone" (9:5).
   - **One appeal to kinship-flesh wins hearts for restoration in one case and for fratricide in the other.** [Claude reading]
- **Other occurrences of allegiance in the heart:**
  - "with **a whole heart to make David king** … of **a single mind** to make David king" (1Ch 12:38)
  - "The hand of God was also on Judah to give them **one heart** to do what the king … commanded" (2Ch 30:12)
- **Jeroboam — the promise, then the fear.**
  - Before: "you shall **reign over all that your soul desires**" (1Ki 11:37), on the condition of 11:38, "if you will … walk in my ways".
  - "Jeroboam **said in his heart**, 'Now the kingdom will turn back …' **the heart of this people will turn again** to their lord … and they will kill me" (12:26–27).
  - After: "So the king took counsel and **made two calves of gold**" (12:28).
  - [Claude reading] The king fears **the people's heart** turning, so he turns their worship. His own heart's fear overrides the promise he was given. See Batch C §1.1 (soul-desire as the scope of rule).

### 1.4 The king's life, the subject's life

- **Esther — the soul reversed.** Context read: Est 4:12–16; 5:6–14; 7:3–8.
  - Mordecai: "**Do not think to yourself** [*in your nephesh*] that in the king's palace you will escape" (4:13) → "if I perish, I perish" (4:16).
  - Then, at the wine: "**let my life be granted me** for my wish, and my people for my request" (7:3).
  - The king "arose in his wrath from the wine-drinking … **Haman stayed to beg for his life** from Queen Esther" (7:7).
  - [Claude reading] Within one banquet the *nephesh* asked for passes **from Esther to Haman**. Esther's inner reckoning ("do not think in your soul") is corrected first. Only then does she stake her life. See §7.6 for the wine-threshold.
- **Ben-hadad and the prophet's parable.** Context read: 1Ki 20:31–43.
  - "Perhaps he will **spare your life**" (31) → "Please, **let me live**" → "**Does he still live? He is my brother**" (32)
  - The disguised prophet: "Guard this man; if … he is missing, **your life shall be for his life, or else you shall pay a talent of silver**" (39)
  - "**your life shall be for his life**, and your people for his people" (42) → Ahab "went to his house **vexed and sullen**" (43; the same phrase as 21:4 — Batch F §7)
  - [Claude reading] **A life has a price in silver in the parable, but not in the verdict.** Ahab spares one life by calling him "brother", and forfeits his own life for it. This bears on Batch G §5.4 (§R-V).
- **The king's anger costs a life:** "whoever provokes him to anger **forfeits his life**" (Pro 20:2). "let my life be **precious in your sight**" (2Ki 1:13–14; see Batch C).
- **The formula "into the hand of those who seek their life"** (Jer 19:7; 21:7; 22:25; 34:20–21; 38:16; 44:30; 46:26) → Batch G §5.3.
- ◆ **A reward for murder refused:** the murderers of Ish-bosheth: "your enemy, **who sought your life**. The Lord has avenged my lord the king" (2Sa 4:8) → David: "As the Lord lives, **who has redeemed my life** out of every adversity" → he has them killed (4:9–12). **The claim of vengeance on David's behalf is answered by an oath on David's own redeemed soul.**

### 1.5 Rule turned inward

- "**he who rules his spirit** [is better] than he who takes a city" (Pro 16:32) [H1]
- "If **the anger** [*spirit*] **of the ruler** rises against you, do not leave your place, for calmness will lay great offenses to rest" (Ecc 10:4) → Batch F §9.5 ("holding the spirit back")
- "let **the peace of Christ rule** [*umpire*] **in your hearts**" (Col 3:15)
- "having **his desire under control** [*authority over his own will*] … determined this **in his heart**" (1Co 7:37)
- "after it was sold, was it not **at your disposal** [*authority*]? Why is it that **you have contrived this deed in your heart**?" (Act 5:4)
- "while **applying my heart** … when **man had power over man** to his hurt" (Ecc 8:9); God "does not give him **power to enjoy** them" (Ecc 6:2)
- "**Let every soul be subject** to the governing authorities" (Rom 13:1)

[Claude reading] Words for rule and authority are applied **to the self's own components**: a man rules his spirit, peace rules the heart, a person has authority over his own will. Ananias shows the reverse: **authority over what he owned, with the heart left unruled**.

### 1.6 Authority over spirits — who commands whom

- "**He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey him**" (Mar 1:27; Luk 4:36). Authority over unclean spirits is **given** to the twelve (Mat 10:1; Mar 6:7) [O].
- **The formula without the authority.** Context read: Act 19:13–16. "I adjure you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims" → "Jesus I know … **but who are you?**" → the man with the evil spirit "**mastered all of them**" (19:16) [O]. **The spirit masters those who use the name without the relation.**
- **Authority to give the Spirit, offered for money.** Context read: Act 8:13–24.
  - Simon "believed … was amazed" (13)
  - "he **offered them money**, saying, '**Give me this power** also'" (18–19) [D1]
  - "**your heart is not right before God** … pray … that … **the intent of your heart** may be forgiven" (21–22)
  - "in the gall of bitterness and in the bond of iniquity" (23)
- **A spirit as someone's income:** "a slave girl who had **a spirit of divination** and brought **her owners** much gain" (Act 16:16) [O]
- "the **prince of the power of the air**, **the spirit that is now at work** in the sons of disobedience" (Eph 2:2) [O]

[Claude reading] Here spirit sits beside authority and money. **Spirits obey Jesus; a spirit overpowers those who borrow his name; the Spirit cannot be bought, and the attempt exposes the heart; a spirit is exploited for profit.** In each case the question is who has authority over which spirit.

### 1.8 David's heart before kings

- **Two flights to Achish, both begun in the heart.**
  - Context 1Sa 21:10–15: the servants of Achish recall the song → "**David took these words to heart and was much afraid** of Achish" (12) → he feigns madness (13).
  - Context 1Sa 26:21–27:4. **Saul's oath comes first:** "I will no more do you harm, because **my life was precious in your eyes** this day. **Behold, I have acted foolishly, and have made a great mistake**" (26:21). David: "as your life was precious this day in my sight, **so may my life be precious in the sight of the Lord**" (26:24). Saul: "Blessed be you, my son David!" (26:25).
  - **Immediately after:** "Then **David said in his heart**, 'Now I shall perish one day by the hand of Saul'" (27:1) → he goes back to Achish (27:2) → "he no longer sought him" (27:4).
  - [Claude reading] David does not answer Saul's promise with trust. He answers it by putting his life in the Lord's sight (26:24) and then **with a private sentence in his heart** that the promise will fail. The heart's reading of the king proves right: Saul stops only when David is out of reach. This adds context to the "said in heart" series (depiction v1 §2.1c; Batch B §0).
- **The heart approved, then corrected overnight.** Context 2Sa 7:1–5:
  - "Go, **do all that is in your heart**, for the Lord is with you" (7:3)
  - "But **that same night** the word of the Lord came to Nathan" (7:4)
  - David's own later account: "**I had it in my heart** to build … But God said to me, 'You may not'" (1Ch 28:2–3)
  - [Claude reading] The prophet endorses the heart's intention. God then revises it without condemning it. This is the only place in the batch where **a good intention of the heart is set aside, not judged**.
- **The heart that despised the king.** "Michal … saw King David leaping and dancing … and **she despised him in her heart**" (2Sa 6:16; 1Ch 15:29).
  - After: it becomes speech — "How the king of Israel honored himself today" (6:20).
  - David: "**I will make myself yet more contemptible** … by them I shall be held in honor" (6:21–22).
  - "Michal … had no child to the day of her death" (6:23).
  - Heart-contempt → speech → a permanent sequel. See Batch C.

## 2. Wealth and the inner seat (M46)

### 2.1 Heart and riches — the value depends on where the heart is set

- **Negative:**
  - "**your heart has become proud in your wealth**" (Eze 28:5)
  - "Beware lest you **say in your heart**, 'My power and the might of my hand have gotten me this wealth'" (Deu 8:17)
  - "if riches increase, **set not your heart on them**" (Psa 62:10)
  - "you did not serve the Lord … with **gladness of heart**, because of **the abundance of all things**" (Deu 28:47)
- **Positive:**
  - "**your heart shall thrill and exult**, because … **the wealth of the nations** shall come to you" (Isa 60:5)
  - "he did with all his heart, and **prospered**" (2Ch 31:21)
- **Wealth not asked for:** "Because **this was in your heart**, and you have not asked for possessions, wealth, honor, or **the life** of those who hate you" (2Ch 1:11; 1Ki 3:11)
- **◆ Wealth does not satisfy a heart set on a grievance.** Context read: Est 5:9–14.
  - Haman goes out "**joyful and glad of heart**", sees Mordecai, and is "filled with wrath" (5:9).
  - He "recounted … **the splendor of his riches**, the number of his sons, all the promotions" (5:11). Then: "**Yet all this is worth nothing to me**, so long as I see Mordecai" (5:13).
  - The gallows follow (5:14).

[Claude reading] The same wealth thrills the heart (Isa 60), makes it proud (Eze 28) and is claimed by it as its own work (Deu 8). The relation depends on **whether the heart sets itself on the wealth** (Psa 62:10) or on the one who gives it. Haman shows a third case: **wealth recited to a heart that is fixed on an injury gives no comfort.**

### 2.2 The price of a life, again

- "**The ransom of a man's life is his wealth**, but a poor man hears no threat" (Pro 13:8)
- "each shall give **a ransom for his life** … **The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less** … to make atonement for your lives" (Exo 30:12–16; context read) → Batch D; Batch E
- "your life shall be for his life, **or else you shall pay a talent of silver**" (1Ki 20:39) → §1.4
- Satan: "**Skin for skin! All that a man has he will give for his life**" (Job 2:4) → "only **spare** [*guard*] **his life**" (2:6). Context read: Job 2:3–7.
- "they have **devoured human lives**; they have **taken treasure**" (Eze 22:25)

[Claude reading] Batch G §5.4 read Mic 6:7 and Mat 16:26 as a refusal to set any price on the soul. **This batch shows the law and the narratives do set a price, and state it:**
- a ransom in wealth (Pro 13:8)
- a half-shekel that is **equal for every life** (Exo 30:15)
- a talent of silver in a parable that turns into "life for life" (1Ki 20:39–42)

Satan's axiom (Job 2:4) is that **a man will give everything for his *nephesh***. The difference from G §5.4 is the direction of the transaction: **a life can be ransomed within the covenant, but a soul cannot be bought back from God** (Mat 16:26). See §R-V.

### 2.3 Fatness — good in the soul, dulling in the heart

- **Soul made fat = satisfied:**
  - "the **soul** of the diligent **is richly supplied** [*made fat*]" (Pro 13:4)
  - "**Whoever brings blessing** [*the soul of blessing*] **will be enriched** [*made fat*]" (Pro 11:25) [lexical: *dāšēn* H1878]
- **Heart made fat = dull:** "**Make the heart of this people dull** [*fat*] … lest they … understand with their hearts" (Isa 6:10) [lexical: *šāman* H8080]
- **Flesh:** "the **fat of his flesh will grow lean**" (Isa 17:4). "**fatter in flesh**" on vegetables (Dan 1:15).

[Claude reading; lexical] **One image changes value with the component.** A fat soul is satisfied; a fat heart has stopped perceiving. The two roots differ, but the picture is the same. Candidate for the synthesis item "one word, opposite moral value" (§7.4).

### 2.4 Riches moved inward (NT)

- "according to **the riches of his glory** he may grant you to be **strengthened with power through his Spirit in your inner being**" (Eph 3:16) [D1]
- "having **the eyes of your hearts** enlightened, that you may know … **the riches of his glorious inheritance**" (Eph 1:18)
- "that **their hearts** may be encouraged … to reach all **the riches of full assurance** of understanding" (Col 2:2)
- "Let the word of Christ **dwell in you richly** … singing … **spiritual** songs, with thankfulness **in your hearts**" (Col 3:16)
- **The reverse:**
  - "**The fruit for which your soul longed** has gone from you, and all your delicacies and your splendors are lost" (Rev 18:14)
  - Simon's money for the Spirit (Act 8:18; §1.6)

[Claude reading] In Paul, the wealth vocabulary moves **into** the heart and the inner being. Babylon's soul loses the wealth it longed for. Simon tries to move it the other way, buying the Spirit.

### 2.5 ◆ Fringe (M46)

## 3. Slavery, service and burden (M78)

"if that servant **says to himself** [*in his heart*], '**My master is delayed**,' and begins to beat the … servants, and **to eat and drink and get drunk**" (Luk 12:45; Mat 24:48–49). Context read: Luk 12:42–46; Mat 24:49–51.

- The unfaithful servant says **in his heart** that the master is delayed → beats → eats and drinks with drunkards → the master "will come on a day when he does not expect him" (46).

  - [Claude reading] **Despair under a burden (like Batch G §6) is answered by distributing the Spirit, not by removing the burden.**
- "it has **seemed good to the Holy Spirit** and to us to lay on you **no greater burden**" (Act 15:28) [D1]

## 4. Way and wandering (M76)

### 4.1 Heart and way — which leads?

- **The heart directs the way:**
  - "**direct your heart in the way**" (Pro 23:19)
  - "My son, **give me your heart**, and let your eyes observe my ways" (Pro 23:26)
- **The way follows the heart:**
  - "he went on backsliding **in the way of his own heart**" (Isa 57:17)
  - "**Walk in the ways of your heart** and the sight of your eyes. **But know** that … God will bring you into judgment" (Ecc 11:9) → Batch F §7.4
  - "if … **my heart has gone after my eyes**" (Job 31:7)
- **Heart and way given together:**
  - "I will give them **one heart and one way**" (Jer 32:39)
  - "I will **run in the way** of your commandments **when you enlarge my heart**" (Psa 119:32)
  - "**Teach me your way** … **unite my heart** to fear your name" (Psa 86:11)
- **Set the heart on the ways:** "**Consider** [*set your heart on*] **your ways**" (Hag 1:5, 7) — the prophet's summons is for the heart to look at the way
- **Weighing:**
  - "All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but **the Lord weighs the spirit**" (Pro 16:2) [H2]
  - "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but **the Lord weighs the heart**" (Pro 21:2)
  - "I the Lord **search the heart** … to give every man **according to his ways**" (Jer 17:10); "render to each **whose heart you know, according to all his ways**" (2Ch 6:30)
- **The heart blames God for the ruined way:** "When a man's folly **brings his way to ruin**, **his heart rages against the Lord**" (Pro 19:3)
- **"Lifted up" in the Lord's ways:** "**His heart was courageous** [*lifted up*] **in the ways of the Lord**" (2Ch 17:6) → Batch D (the "high heart", pride or courage by direction)

[Claude reading] The relation runs in both directions. The heart can lead the way (Pro 23:19), be dragged by the eyes (Job 31:7), or go its own way (Isa 57:17). The best state is **one heart with one way** (Jer 32:39), **enlarged** so that the way can be run (Psa 119:32). **Eyes judge the way; God weighs the heart or spirit behind it** (Pro 16:2; 21:2).

### 4.2 Going astray — five agents

1. **The heart itself:** "a people who **go astray in their heart**" (Psa 95:10)
2. **The spirit, with a way back:** "those who **go astray in spirit will come to understanding**" (Isa 29:24) [H1]
3. **A foreign spirit:**
   - "**a spirit of whoredom has led them astray**" (Hos 4:12) [X]
   - "The Lord has **mingled within her a spirit of confusion** … as **a drunken man staggers**" (Isa 19:14) [X]
4. **God:** "O Lord, **why do you make us wander from your ways and harden our heart**?" (Isa 63:17). Context 63:1–6, 15–17: this complaint follows **the divine warrior's "day of vengeance was in my heart"** (63:4) and a plea that "the stirring of your inner parts and your compassion are held back" (63:15). → §6.1
5. **The loss of understanding:** "He takes away understanding [*heart*] from the chiefs … and **makes them wander** in a trackless waste" (Job 12:24)

**One verb, three circumstances — *šāgâ* (err / stray / be intoxicated)** [lexical; Claude reading]:
- **Unwitting error:** "the person [*nephesh*] **who makes a mistake**, when he sins unintentionally" (Num 15:28) — atonement is provided
- **A confession that is not trusted:** Saul: "I have acted foolishly, and **have made a great mistake**" (1Sa 26:21) → David still flees (§1.8)
- **Intoxication:** "Why should you **be intoxicated**, my son, with a forbidden woman" (Pro 5:20). The verse before uses the same verb for the wife: "be intoxicated always in her love" (5:19) [verify].

The same verb covers the innocent slip, a king's self-accusation and erotic absorption. **In each the self is carried off course without full deliberation.**

- ◆ "**My heart staggers**; horror has appalled me; **the twilight I longed for** has been turned for me into trembling" (Isa 21:4). Context 21:1–4: a stern vision "**as whirlwinds** … sweep on"; "my loins are filled with anguish … **I am bowed down so that I cannot hear**". **The prophet's own heart reels from the vision it received.**
- ◆ "his **breath** is like an overflowing stream … a bridle that **leads astray**" (Isa 30:28) — God's breath as the thing that sends the nations astray
- "all flesh **had corrupted their way**" (Gen 6:12) → G §1.3

### 4.3 Guarding the way keeps the soul

## 5. Keeping and guarding (M74)

### 5.1 Guarding one's own inner seat

- "**Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life**" (Pro 4:23). **Context 4:20–27 — the guarding moves outward:**
  - words kept "**within your heart**" (21) → "life … and **healing to all their flesh**" (22)
  - **keep the heart** (23)
  - → the mouth: "put away … crooked speech" (24)
  - → the eyes: "look directly forward" (25)
  - → the feet: "Ponder the path of your feet" (26–27)
- "take care, and **keep your soul diligently, lest you forget** … lest they depart from your heart" (Deu 4:9) — **guarding the soul means guarding memory**
- "**guard yourselves in your spirit**, and let none of you be faithless to the wife of your youth" (Mal 2:15–16) [U+H1; H1]
- "**watch yourselves lest your hearts be weighed down** with dissipation and drunkenness" (Luk 21:34) → §7.6
- "restore him **in a spirit of gentleness**. **Keep watch on yourself**" (Gal 6:1) [X]
- "Take care lest there be **an unworthy thought in your heart**" (Deu 15:9); "Take care **lest your heart be deceived**" (Deu 11:16)
- "Be very careful, therefore [*guard well your souls*], **to love the Lord**" (Jos 23:11) — distance 0: the "careful" word *is* the nephesh phrase

**The opposite sense of the same verb — *nāṣar*** [lexical; Claude reading]:
- "**Keep** [*nāṣar*] **your heart** with all vigilance" (Pro 4:23)
- "the woman meets him, dressed as a prostitute, **wily** [*nāṣar*, guarded] **of heart**" (Pro 7:10). Context 7:11–12: "loud and wayward … **at every corner she lies in wait**".
- **A guarded heart is the wise man's protection in Pro 4 and the seductress's concealment in Pro 7.** The same verb, in the same book, three chapters apart. Candidate for the synthesis item "one word, opposite value" (§7.4).

### 5.2 Who keeps the soul — the same verb in every direction

### 5.3 Keeping something in the heart — holding what is not yet understood

[Claude reading] "Keeping in the heart" follows alarm (Daniel) or incomprehension (Mary, 2:50). **The heart holds what the mind cannot yet resolve.** In both cases the keeping is the end of the scene: nothing is said or done yet.

## 6. Judgment, vengeance and justification (M26)

### 6.1 Vengeance — its value depends on whose heart holds it

| Holder | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **God's heart** | "**the day of vengeance was in my heart**, and **my year of redemption** had come" (Isa 63:4). Context 63:1–6: "I have trodden the winepress **alone** … I looked, but **there was no one to help** … so **my own arm** brought me salvation". |
| **God's nephesh** | "**shall I not avenge myself** [*shall not my soul avenge itself*] on a nation such as this?" (Jer 5:9, 29; 9:9) → Batch E |
| **The persecuted prophet** | "O Lord of hosts, who **tests the righteous, who sees the heart and the mind**, **let me see your vengeance upon them**, for **to you have I committed my cause**" (Jer 20:12; 11:20) |
| **The nations' soul** | "the Philistines … **took vengeance with malice of soul**" (Eze 25:15) → Batch E, F, G |
| **The anxious heart, comforted** | "Say to those who have **an anxious heart**, 'Be strong; fear not! Behold, **your God will come with vengeance** … **He will come and save you**'" (Isa 35:4) |
| **A stirred human spirit, as instrument** | "The Lord has **stirred up the spirit of the kings of the Medes** … that is **the vengeance of the Lord**" (Jer 51:11) [H1] |

**Jeremiah's sequence — context read, Jer 20:7–14:**
- "you have deceived me" (7)
- "there is **in my heart as it were a burning fire** shut up in my bones … I cannot [hold it in]" (9)
- "my close friends, **watching for my fall** … '**take our revenge on him**'" (10)
- → "**let me see your vengeance** … **to you have I committed my cause**" (12)
- → "**Sing to the Lord; praise the Lord!** For he has delivered **the life of the needy**" (13)
- → "**Cursed be the day on which I was born!**" (14)

Jer 11:18–21 has the same prayer, and names "the men of Anathoth, **who seek your life**" (21).

[Claude reading]
1. **Vengeance changes value with the heart that holds it.** In the Philistines' soul it is malice. In God's heart it stands in one line with **redemption** (Isa 63:4). To the anxious heart it is **rescue** (Isa 35:4).
2. **The prophet's heart does not take vengeance; it hands vengeance over.** The friends want "our revenge" (20:10). Jeremiah asks to *see* God's (20:12). The handing over ("to you have I committed my cause") is what makes the praise of 20:13 possible. **Two verses later the heart falls to cursing its birth** (20:14). The inner seat swings from fire, to entrusting, to praise, to despair in five verses (cf. the despairing soul, Batch G §6).
3. **What follows the divine heart's vengeance is complaint** (Isa 63:15–17): the people ask where God's compassion has gone and why he makes them wander (§4.2).

### 6.2 The heart as judge of the self

- **Context read: 1Jo 3:18–22.**
  - "let us not love in word … but in deed" (18)
  - "By this we shall … **reassure our heart before him**" (19)
  - "**whenever our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart**, and he knows everything" (20)
  - "if **our heart does not condemn us, we have confidence** before God" (21) → "whatever we ask we receive" (22)
- "**You are those who justify yourselves** before men, but **God knows your hearts**" (Luk 16:15)
- **Context read: Job 32:1–3.**
  - "he was **righteous in his own eyes**" (1)
  - "Elihu … burned with anger at Job **because he justified himself** [*his nephesh*] **rather than God**" (2)
  - "also at Job's three friends because they had found no answer, although they had declared Job to be in the wrong" (3)
- "Faithless Israel **has shown herself** [*her nephesh*] **more righteous** than treacherous Judah" (Jer 3:11) — comparative justification
- "I do not say this **to condemn you**, for … **you are in our hearts, to die together and to live together**" (2Co 7:3)

[Claude reading] The heart can **condemn** the self (1Jo 3:20) or **justify** it (Job 32:2; Luk 16:15). In both cases the text places a higher court above it: "God is greater than our heart"; "God knows your hearts". 1Jo 3 is the gentler reading: the self-condemning heart is **reassured**, not overruled harshly. In 2Co 7:3 condemnation is set aside because the other person is **held in the heart**.

### 6.3 Flesh and justification

- "**by works of the law no human being** [*flesh*] **will be justified**" (Rom 3:20; Gal 2:16)
- "God has done what the law, **weakened by the flesh**, could not do … in **the likeness of sinful flesh** … **he condemned sin in the flesh**" (Rom 8:3)
- "**manifested in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit**" (1Ti 3:16) [U]
- "you were **justified** in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and **by the Spirit of our God**" (1Co 6:11) [D1]
- "**unnatural desire** [*other flesh*] … undergoing **a punishment** of eternal fire" (Jude 7)

[Claude reading] Within this cluster *flesh* means three things: all humanity, which cannot be justified; the place where sin is condemned; and **Christ's own flesh**, which is vindicated by the Spirit. This extends Batch G §7 (flesh valued by circumstance).

### 6.4 Counting

- "So teach us **to number our days that we may get a heart of wisdom**" (Psa 90:12) → Batch B
- "he **poured out his soul to death** and **was numbered with the transgressors**" (Isa 53:12); "**Out of the anguish of his soul** he shall see and be satisfied … make many **to be accounted righteous**" (53:11)
- "it is not **the children of the flesh** who are the children of God, but the children of the promise **are counted** as offspring" (Rom 9:8)

### 6.5 ◆ Fringe (M26)

- **Balaam:** "Who can **count** the dust of Jacob …? **Let me** [*my nephesh*] **die the death of the upright**" (Num 23:10) — the hired curser's soul wishes for the end of those he was hired to curse

"**I have occasioned the death of all the persons of your father's house**" (22) → "Stay with me … **for he who seeks my life seeks your life. ## 7. Cross-cutting observations for Batch H [Claude reading]

1. **The most powerful heart is shown as the least its own.** The king's heart is turned, filled, stirred, hardened and cut off by God (§1.1). When it claims a throne for itself, it falls (§1.2). Deu 17:20's daily reading is the one preventive.
2. **Allegiance is a heart-matter, and it moves by attention and kinship.** Absalom steals hearts by hearing their causes. David sways them back by "bone and flesh". Abimelech uses the same flesh-claim to incline hearts toward fratricide (§1.3).
3. **The social relations of this batch supply the vocabulary for the self's relation to itself:**
   - **rule:** one rules one's spirit; peace rules the heart (§1.5)
   - **service:** one serves from the heart, or serves sin with the flesh (§3.1)
   - **guarding:** one keeps one's heart, soul and spirit (§5.1)
   - **judgment:** the heart condemns or justifies the self (§6.2)

   **Setting-words turned inward describe the inner seat as governed.** This is the batch's main contribution.
4. **More words with opposite value by circumstance** (for synthesis item 3):
   - *nāṣar*: keep the heart (Pro 4:23) / the "guarded" wily heart (Pro 7:10) (§5.1)
   - *šāmar*: keep the law / God keeps the soul / enemies watch for the life (§5.2)
   - vengeance: malice in the soul / redemption in God's heart / comfort to the anxious heart (§6.1)
   - fatness: a satisfied soul / a dull heart (§2.3)
   - *šāgâ*: unwitting error / self-accusation / intoxication (§4.2)

6. **The eating-and-drinking threshold, extended** (Batch F §7):

   - **The threshold refused at the heart:** "Daniel **resolved** [*set on his heart*] that he would not defile himself with **the king's food, or with the wine**" (Dan 1:8) → "**fatter in flesh**" than those who ate it (1:15). Compare Uriah (Batch F §7.5).

   - **The warning:** "watch yourselves lest **your hearts be weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness**" (Luk 21:34).
   - **The counter-statement:** "the kingdom of God is **not a matter of eating and drinking** but of … joy in the Holy Spirit" (Rom 14:17).
7. **David's heart reads kings rightly.** Both flights to Achish begin in the heart (1Sa 21:12; 27:1). The second follows immediately on Saul's oath and confession (26:21–25) (§1.8).
