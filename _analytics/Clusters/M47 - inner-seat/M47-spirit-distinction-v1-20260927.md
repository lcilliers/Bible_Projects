## 1. Classes and the evidence each requires

| Class | Meaning | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | divine Spirit — **explicit** | a marker in the verse itself: "of God / of the Lord / Holy / my-his-your Spirit (God speaking) / one-same Spirit / the Lord who is the Spirit" |
| **D2** | divine Spirit — **from context** | an action or attribute in the verse that the text elsewhere gives to God's Spirit, or the adjacent verse names it (e.g. Luk 2:27 after 2:25–26) |
| **D3** | divine **per ESV capitalisation only** | the verse itself does not decide it. The ESV translators' reading is recorded, but it is **not treated as evidence** |
| **H1** | human spirit — **explicit** | a human possessor or state in the verse: "my / his / your spirit", "a man's spirit", "broken / contrite / haughty spirit" |
| **H2** | human spirit — **from context** | e.g. paired with body or soul, departs at death, "spirits of all flesh", "spirits of prophets" |
| **O** | **other** spirit-being | unclean, evil, harmful, lying, deceitful, ministering (angelic), apparition, the cherubim's "spirit" |
| **X** | **disposition / quality** | "a spirit of jealousy / whoredom / skill / wisdom / stupor / grace …", where the source is not identified as a being |
| **T** | **temper** (rûaḥ = anger / self-control) | H7307J; one case (Mic 2:7) is the **Lord's** temper |
| **W** | wind / breath | |
| **B** | **boundary** | God-given breath/spirit in the human: "the spirit of God is in my nostrils" (Job 27:3) |
| **D-attr** | divine **as attributed by a pagan speaker** | "the spirit of the holy gods is in you" (Daniel 4–5) |
| **U** | **not determinable** | the text allows more than one reading. The reason is given for each row |

A verse can carry **two classes** where two referents are present, e.g. Rom 8:16 "The Spirit himself [D1] bears witness with our spirit [H1]".

## 3. Where the distinction cannot be made from the text — the explicit list (U and D3)

Grouped by *why* the text leaves it open:

**a. "In the Spirit / in spirit"** — visionary or prayer state
- Rev 1:10; 4:2; 17:3; 21:10; 1Co 14:2; Act 19:21; 20:22.
- Could be the person's own spirit (cf. 1Co 14:14 "my spirit prays") or God's Spirit.

**b. The flesh–Spirit antithesis** — D3
- Gal 5:5, 16–18, 22, 25; 6:8; Rom 8:4–6, 13; Gal 3:3; 4:29; Rom 7:6; Joh 3:5.
- The ESV reads these as divine. The verses alone don't decide it, although the paragraphs contain explicit D1 verses (Gal 4:6; Rom 8:9).

**c. Christ and the dead "made alive / living in the spirit"** — spirit as a mode of existence
- 1Pe 3:18; 4:6; 1Co 15:45 ("a life-giving spirit"); 1Ti 3:16 ("vindicated by the Spirit / in spirit").
- Also Rom 1:4 ("Spirit of holiness") and Heb 9:14 ("eternal Spirit"), which are debated.

**d. Human spirit or Holy Spirit indwelling**
- Jam 4:5 ("the spirit that he has made to dwell in us"); Rom 8:10 ("the Spirit/spirit is life"); Rom 2:29 (circumcision "by the Spirit / in spirit").
- 1Co 6:17 ("one spirit with the Lord") is a genuine boundary: the human spirit is joined to the Lord.

**e. Prophetic "spirits" to be tested**
- 1Co 12:10; 1Jo 4:1; 2Th 2:2; 2Co 11:4 ("a different spirit"); Rev 19:10 ("the spirit of prophecy").

**f. "Seven spirits (of God)"** — Rev 1:4; 3:1; 4:5; 5:6
- The "of God" marker is present, but the plural leaves divine Spirit vs angelic spirits open.

**g. Life-breath vs spirit**
- Ecc 8:8 ("No man has power to retain the spirit"); Ecc 11:5 ("the spirit comes to the bones in the womb"); Job 34:14 ("gather to himself his spirit and his breath" — God's own, or the one he gave).

**h. Old Testament "the Spirit" without a marker, where the prophet's own spirit is also possible**
- Eze 2:2; 3:24 ("the Spirit entered into me and set me on my feet") — D3.
- Num 27:18 ("a man in whom is the Spirit").
- 1Ch 28:12 ("all that he had in mind" = "by the spirit" [lexical]).
- Hos 9:7 ("the man of the spirit").

**i. "Spirit of X" where the X could be divine or a disposition**
- Eph 1:17 ("the Spirit/a spirit of wisdom and revelation"); 2Co 4:13 ("spirit of faith"); Isa 4:4 ("spirit of judgment / burning"); Jer 51:1 ("spirit / wind of a destroyer").

**j. Order of being** — Isa 31:3: "flesh, and not spirit" // "man, and not God". Spirit here names the divine order of being rather than any one spirit.

## 4. What the distinction shows [Claude reading]

1. **The Hebrew human spirit is prominent.** In H7307G the human spirit (H1 + H2, about 90 rows) is at least as present as the divine Spirit (D1 + D2, about 70 rows). In the Greek G4151G the divine Spirit dominates, and the human spirit is a minority (about 50 rows).
2. **Dispositions (X) are almost all Hebrew:** jealousy, whoredom, confusion, deep sleep, justice, skill, wisdom, grace. The same construction ("a spirit of …") sits on the boundary between a mood that arrives and a being that is sent (§3i).
3. **Many verses carry two spirits at once.** These are the clearest evidence of how the text relates them:
   - **Witness together:** "The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit" (Rom 8:16).
   - **Knowing by analogy:** "the spirit of that person, which is in him … the Spirit of God" (1Co 2:11).
   - **Exchange:** the Spirit of the Lord departs; a harmful spirit from the Lord comes (1Sa 16:14).
   - **Contrast:** "spirit of slavery" vs "Spirit of adoption" (Rom 8:15); "spirit of the world" vs "Spirit who is from God" (1Co 2:12); "Spirit of truth" vs "spirit of error" (1Jo 4:6).
   - **Co-presence:** the Spirit lifts the prophet, who goes "in the heat of my spirit" (Eze 3:14); the Spirit of the Lord falls on Ezekiel, and God knows "what comes into your mind [rûaḥ]" (Eze 11:5).
   - **Creation:** "The Spirit of God has made me" // "the breath of the Almighty gives me life" (Job 33:4).
4. **Boundary cases (B, and some U) sit exactly where God's giving meets human life:** breath in the nostrils (Job 27:3), the spirit returning to God (Ecc 12:7, H2), the spirit God "made to dwell in us" (Jam 4:5).
5. **Divine temper.** H7307J is used once of the Lord: "Has the Lord grown impatient?" (Mic 2:7). ◆ fringe.
