## 2. The readings

### Isaiah 57:15: high, and with the lowly spirit

> "For thus says the One who is high and lifted up, who inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy: 'I
> dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit, to
> revive the spirit of the lowly, and to revive the heart of the contrite.'"

**The main operation** is God's dwelling with, and reviving, a lowly spirit and a contrite heart. The
height word is God's own height, set beside where he also dwells.

**Already carried, in seven places:**
- Ch 2 *Life that returns*
- Ch 4 *The heart is the source* (the heart revived)
- Ch 6 *The human spirit*
- 10.14 *A lowly and contrite spirit*
- 10.15 *The words* (the same height verbs for God and for the proud heart)
- 13.1 *Lowering himself*
- Ch 14 *Revived, restored, renewed*

It also has claims C-167, C-189 and C-202. 57:16 is carried in five places, and 57:17 in 10.9.
**Nothing more is needed for the verse or its M47 words.**

**What the passage holds that is not in the book (57:18–21):**
- **Healing after the ways are seen.** "I have seen his ways, but I will heal him; I will lead him and
  restore comfort to him and his mourners, creating the fruit of the lips. Peace, peace, to the far
  and to the near" (57:18–19). It comes straight after the man who "went on backsliding in the way
  of his own heart" (57:17, in 10.9 *When God hides his face*). The "but" joins them: God has seen
  the heart's way, and he heals anyway.
- **A heart that cannot be quiet.** "But the wicked are like the tossing sea; for it cannot be quiet,
  and its waters toss up mire and dirt. There is no peace, says my God, for the wicked" (57:20–21).
  This is the figure of an inner state: restless, unable to be still, throwing up what is in it. The
  book already has the arrogant man "never at rest" (Hab 2:5, 10.15) and the quieted soul of Psalm
  131 (10.14). It does not have this picture.

### 1 Samuel 16:7: man looks on the outward appearance

> "But the Lord said to Samuel, 'Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature,
> because I have rejected him. For the Lord sees not as man sees: man looks on the outward
> appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart.'"

**The main operation** is seeing: what man looks at and what God looks at. The height word ("the
height of his stature") is the outward thing Samuel looked at.

**Already carried:** Ch 4 *The heart is hidden, and God sees it* and Ch 9 *Inner and outer* quote the
second half ("man looks … the Lord looks on the heart"). **Nothing more is needed for the M47 word.**

**What the passage holds that is not in the book (16:6):** the seer's own thought, formed from what
he saw. "When they came, he looked on Eliab and thought, 'Surely the Lord's anointed is before him'"
(16:6). The rebuke in 16:7 answers that thought. Even the prophet, sent by God to choose, made up his
mind from the outside. The book quotes the principle but not the mistake it corrects. The passage
then runs through seven sons (16:8–10) to the youngest, out with the sheep (16:11–12).

## 3. The impact

**For OT-97.71 itself: nothing new.** Both verses are already carried for their M47 words, Isa 57:15
in seven places and 1Sa 16:7 in two. The item can be closed as **already carried**.

**Found by reading the passages, and not in the book:**

| Passage | What it adds | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Isa 57:18–19 | God has seen the heart's own way (57:17), "but I will heal him", and restore comfort | 10.9 *When God hides his face*, after 57:17; or 13.1 |
| Isa 57:20–21 | the wicked "like the tossing sea; for it cannot be quiet": an inner state of no rest and no peace | 10.15 *The heart that says what God says* (beside "never at rest", Hab 2:5) or Ch 12; set against the quieted soul (10.14) |

Each is a quotation with short framing. The tossing sea is a figure, and saying what it pictures in
the inner being needs an "I read" and a claim (rule 77). The other two stay close to the text.
