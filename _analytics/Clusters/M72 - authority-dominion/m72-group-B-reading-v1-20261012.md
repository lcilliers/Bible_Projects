## 2. Readings

### 2.1 R1. What rules within a person (12: 6 read, 6 carried)

**Already carried:**
- *"he who rules his spirit"* (Pro 16:32: Ch 6, 10.13, Ch 11 §7)
- *"Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts"* (Col 3:15: Ch 11 §7)
- *"Its desire is for you, and you must rule over it"* (Gen 4:7: Ch 10, 10.6)
- *"having his desire under control"* (1Cor 7:37: 10.6)
- *"No man has power to retain the spirit"* (Ecc 8:8: Ch 2)
- *"God does not give him power to enjoy them"* (Ecc 6:2: 10.6)

**Psa 119:133, Rom 6:14.** The trial read these, and its readings stand.

**Psa 19:13.** *"Keep back your servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me! Then I shall be blameless, and innocent of great transgression."*
- **The passage.** The verse before is *"Who can discern his errors? Declare me innocent from hidden faults"* (19:12). The verse after is *"Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in your sight"* (19:14).
- **Roles:**
  - "have dominion" (M72) is the **characteristic**.
  - "presumptuous" (M08) qualifies the sins: it names the kind that can rule.
  - "innocent" (M26) and "transgression" (M56) are the outcome he asks for.
- **Reading.** The psalmist names two kinds of sin:
  - **Hidden faults.** He cannot discern them, so he asks to be declared innocent of them.
  - **Presumptuous sins.** These are sins done knowingly and proudly. He fears these could take dominion over him, so he asks God to hold him back from them.

  Only the second kind is spoken of as able to rule.
- **I read:** a sin that rules is the one a person walks into knowingly. It is not the one he cannot see.
- **Parties and route:** the psalmist, and God asked to keep him (B).
- **Home:** Ch 11 §7, in the face *what would rule within*. 10.9 also takes it (asking God). It points to 10.2, which cites 19:12.

**1Cor 6:12.** *"'All things are lawful for me,' but not all things are helpful. 'All things are lawful for me,' but I will not be dominated by anything."*
- **The passage.** The verses after are about food and the stomach (6:13) and the body *"not meant for sexual immorality, but for the Lord"* (6:13). Then: *"your bodies are members of Christ"* (6:15).
- **Roles:**
  - "dominated" (G1850, M72) is the **characteristic**.
  - "lawful" is the ground. It carries no M-code, but the Greek words are related: *exestin* is "it is lawful", and *exousiazō* is "to have authority".
- **Reading.** Paul quotes the saying twice, and twice he sets a limit beside it. The first limit is what helps. The second is that he will not be brought under the authority of anything.
- **I read:** the thing a man is free to do can come to have authority over him.
- **Parties and route:** Paul, and the things he is free to use (B).
- **Home:** Ch 11 §7, *what would rule within*. It points to 10.6 *Wanting that owns the one who wants* (Gen 4:7).

**Eph 6:12.** *"For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers over this present darkness, against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places."*
- **The passage, before:** *"Masters, do the same to them, and stop your threatening, knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven"* (6:9). Then *"be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might"* (6:10), and *"stand against the schemes of the devil"* (6:11).
- **The passage, after:** *"withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand firm"* (6:13). The armour follows: truth, righteousness, the readiness of the gospel of peace, and *"the shield of faith"* (6:14–16).
- **Roles:**
  - "authorities" (M72) names the opponent: authorities that are not flesh and blood.
  - "flesh" (M47), "spiritual" (M47) and "evil" (M58) qualify what the struggle is not, and what it is.
  - The human act is to wrestle, to stand and to withstand. These carry no M-code.
- **Reading:** the believer is set against authorities, and the act asked of him is to stand. The means given are truth, righteousness, faith and the strength *"of his might"*, which is God's.
- **Pointer:** three verses earlier (6:9), a human master is told to stop threatening, *"knowing"* that he has a Master in heaven. In one passage, a master's rule over others is checked by the Master above him, and the believer stands against authorities above the human ones.
- **Parties and route:** the powers (A, 13.3) and the believer (B).
- **Home:** 13.3 for the powers. Ch 11 §7 for standing against them. 6:9 goes to R2 (§2.2).

**Ecc 5:19.** *"Everyone also to whom God has given wealth and possessions and power to enjoy them, and to accept his lot and rejoice in his toil — this is the gift of God."*
- **The passage.** *"what I have seen to be good and fitting is to eat and drink and find enjoyment"* (5:18; cited in Ch 11 §4). *"God keeps him occupied with joy in his heart"* (5:20; cited in 10.1 and 10.3).
- **Roles:**
  - "power" (H7980 *šālaṭ*, M72) is the **characteristic**: power over what one has, *to enjoy it*.
  - "wealth" and "possessions" (M46) are its object.
  - "rejoice" (M04) is what it makes possible.
  - "gift" (M39) is its source.
- **Reading:** having goods is one thing. Having power over them, so that they can be enjoyed, is another, and the verse calls that power the gift of God. Ecc 6:2 (carried in 10.6) is the same case turned round: God gives the wealth *"yet God does not give him power to enjoy them, but a stranger enjoys them"*.
- **Face:** mastery over one's own things is given or withheld. Ownership does not hold it.
- **Home:** 10.6 *Aimed at nothing, and enjoyment given*, beside 6:2. Ch 11 §7 face. 13.1 (God gives).

**What the group shows (faces of ruling within):**
- **The person rules.** *"he who rules his spirit"* (Pro 16:32); *"you must rule over it"* (Gen 4:7); *"having his desire under control"* (1Cor 7:37); *"I will not be dominated by anything"* (1Cor 6:12).
- **God is asked to keep the person.** *"let no iniquity get dominion over me"* (Psa 119:133); *"let them not have dominion over me"* (Psa 19:13).
- **It is stated as a fact, under grace:** *"sin will have no dominion over you"* (Rom 6:14). Two verses earlier the same thing is a command: *"Let not sin therefore reign"* (6:12).
- **It is given or withheld by God:** the power to enjoy (Ecc 5:19; 6:2).
- **There is no power at all:** *"No man has power to retain the spirit"* (Ecc 8:8).
- **Something else is to rule:** *"Let the peace of Christ rule"* (Col 3:15).

**Why the faces differ, as the verses give it:**
- What would rule is named differently: the spirit, sin's desire, presumptuous sins, iniquity, anything lawful.
- The one who acts differs: the man himself, God asked, or grace.
- **I read:** where the thing that would rule is sin, the verses either ask God or speak of grace. Where it is a man's own spirit or desire, they say he rules it. Ecc 8:8 sets the outer limit: death and the spirit are not under any man's power.

### 2.2 R2. Ruling another: the manner (18: 15 read, 3 carried)

**Already carried:**
- Lev 25:43 and Neh 5:15 (10.10 *The fear of God and the weak*)
- Isa 14:6 (10.10 *Anger between people*)

**1Pe 5:3.** The trial read it, and its reading stands (see below).

**The law's limit, given three times (Lev 25:43, 46, 53).**
- **The three verses:**
  - *"You shall not rule over him ruthlessly but shall fear your God"* (25:43).
  - *"over your brothers the people of Israel you shall not rule, one over another ruthlessly"* (25:46).
  - For an Israelite sold to a foreigner: *"He shall not rule ruthlessly over him in your sight"* (25:53).
  - The ground given before and after: *"For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt"* (25:42; 25:55).
- **Roles:**
  - "rule" (M72) is the **characteristic** each time.
  - "ruthlessly" (M06 "severity") is the manner forbidden.
  - "fear" (M01) is what is set against it (25:43).
- **Reading:** each verse gives the limit a different ground:
  - in 25:43, **the ruler's own fear of God**
  - in 25:46, **brotherhood** ("one over another")
  - in 25:53, **the onlooker** ("in your sight"). The Israelite who sees a foreign master rule harshly over his brother is made responsible for it.

  Behind all three stands **whose servant the ruled one is**: "they are my servants". The one ruled over belongs to God first.
- **Home:** 10.10 *The fear of God and the weak* (widen with 25:46 and 25:53). Ch 11 §7, face *the limits of rule*.

**Eze 34:4.** *"The weak you have not strengthened, the sick you have not healed, the injured you have not bound up, the strayed you have not brought back, the lost you have not sought, and with force and harshness you have ruled them."*
- **The passage.** *"Ah, shepherds of Israel who have been feeding yourselves! Should not shepherds feed the sheep?"* (34:2). *"You eat the fat, you clothe yourselves with the wool"* (34:3). *"So they were scattered, because there was no shepherd"* (34:5).
- **Roles:**
  - "ruled" (H7287A, M72) is the **characteristic**.
  - "force" (M23) and "harshness" (M06 "severity", the word of Lev 25:43) are its manner.
  - "weak", "sick" (M73), "injured" (M24) and "sought" (M83) are the care left undone.
- **Reading:**
  - The charge has two parts: what the shepherds did not do, five kinds of care, and how they did rule, with force and harshness.
  - The ground is in 34:2–3. They fed themselves.
  - The result is stated as if there were no rulers at all: "because there was no shepherd" (34:5, 8). The rulers were there; the shepherding was not.
  - The figure measures rule by care of the weakest: the weak, the sick, the injured, the strayed, the lost.
- **I read:** rule that serves the ruler is counted as no shepherd at all.
- **Home:** 10.10, a new section **"Ruling another"**. Ch 11 §7. Ch 12 (the self-feeding).

**Pro 28:15–16.** *"Like a roaring lion or a charging bear is a wicked ruler over a poor people."* Next verse: *"A ruler who lacks understanding is a cruel oppressor, but he who hates unjust gain will prolong his days."*
- **Roles:**
  - "ruler" (M72) is the subject. The verse is about how he rules, so it carries the characteristic.
  - "wicked" (M58) qualifies him.
  - "poor" (M09) are the ruled.
  - "groan" (M03) is tagged on "roaring".
- **Reading:**
  - The figure is seen from below. The poor meet such a ruler as a beast meets its prey.
  - 28:16 names the inner ground of such rule: a lack of understanding. Its opposite is hating unjust gain.
- **Home:** 10.10 *Ruling another*. Ch 11 §7.

**Ecc 8:9.** *"All this I observed while applying my heart to all that is done under the sun, when man had power over man to his hurt."*
- **Roles:**
  - "had power" (H7980, M72) is the **characteristic**.
  - "heart" (M47) is the observer's: "applying my heart".
  - "hurt" (M58) is the outcome.
- **Reading:**
  - The Preacher sets his heart to watch one thing: man ruling man. What he sees is hurt.
  - Whose hurt is not said. "His" can be the ruled or the ruler, and I leave it open.
  - Two verses later he names what follows when evil is not checked: *"the heart of the children of man is fully set to do evil"* (8:11).
- **Home:** 10.10 *Ruling another*. Ch 11 §7.

**2Sa 23:3.** *"The God of Israel has spoken; the Rock of Israel has said to me: When one rules justly over men, ruling in the fear of God,"*
- The verse is David's last words, given as God's word to him. The ruler is described by two things: justice, and *"the fear of God"*. 23:4, which completes the sentence, is not in the table.
- **Roles:** "rules" (M72) is the **characteristic**, "justly" (M12) and "fear" (M01) its manner.
- **Home:** 10.10 *The fear of God and the weak* (beside Lev 25:43). Ch 11 §7.

**Lordship turned round (Mat 20:25; Mar 10:42; Luk 22:25).**
- **The verses:**
  - *"You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them. It shall not be so among you"* (Mat 20:25–26).
  - Luke: *"The kings of the Gentiles exercise lordship over them, and those in authority over them are called benefactors. But not so with you"* (Luk 22:25–26).
- **The settings:**
  - Matthew and Mark: the request for the seats at his right and left, then the ten *"indignant at the two brothers"* (Mat 20:24).
  - Luke: *"A dispute also arose among them, as to which of them was to be regarded as the greatest"* (22:24), at the last supper.
- **Roles:**
  - "lord it over" (G2634, G2961) and "exercise authority" (G2715, G1850) are the **characteristic**: the rule set aside.
  - "called" (M42) in Luke is the name such rulers take.
- **Reading:**
  - Jesus does not say the Gentile rulers are wrong to rule. He says *"It shall not be so among you"*.
  - Luke adds what such rule claims for itself: the name "benefactors".
  - In its place: *"let the greatest among you become as the youngest, and the leader as one who serves"*, and *"I am among you as the one who serves"* (Luk 22:26–27).
  - The disciples' inner acts are wanting the first place, indignation, and dispute. 10.7 and 10.10 already carry these (Mat 20:21–22, 26, 27–28; Mar 10:44–45).
- **What M72 adds:** the picture of lordship that the service is set against, and the good name such rule takes.
- **Home:** Ch 11 §7, face *rule turned round*. Pointers to 10.7 and 10.10.

**1Pe 5:3** (trial) belongs to the same face. It names the motives of oversight: *"not under compulsion, but willingly … not for shameful gain, but eagerly"* (5:2).

**2Cor 1:24.** *"Not that we lord it over your faith, but we work with you for your joy, for you stand firm in your faith."*
- **The passage, before:** *"it was to spare you that I refrained from coming again to Corinth"* (1:23).
- **Roles:**
  - "lord it over" (G2961, M72) is the **characteristic**, denied.
  - "faith" (M13) is its object.
  - "joy" (M04) is what the work is for.
- **Reading:**
  - Paul has authority, but he says faith is not something he rules. The reason he gives is that *"you stand firm in your faith"*: their faith stands on its own ground, not his.
  - In place of lordship he puts working with them, for their joy.
- **Home:** Ch 11 §7, *rule turned round*. 10.10.

**1Cor 7:4.** *"For the wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband does. Likewise the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife does."*
- **The passage.** *"Do not deprive one another, except perhaps by agreement for a limited time, that you may devote yourselves to prayer; but then come together again, so that Satan may not tempt you because of your lack of self-control"* (7:5).
- **Roles:**
  - "have authority over" (G1850, M72) is the **characteristic**, twice and both ways.
  - "self-control" (7:5) is what is at risk.
- **Reading:**
  - Authority over one's own body is held by the other, and the verse says it the same way of each, with *"Likewise"*.
  - It is given up by agreement, and only *"for a limited time"*.
  - The ground given is the lack of self-control that Satan can use (7:5). So mutual authority guards each one against what neither rules alone.
- **Home:** 10.10. Ch 11 §7. It points to R1 (self-control).

**1Ti 2:12.** *"I do not permit a woman to teach or to exercise authority over a man; rather, she is to remain quiet."*
- **The passage, before:** men are to pray *"without anger or quarreling"* (2:8), and women to dress *"with modesty and self-control"* (2:9). *"Let a woman learn quietly with all submissiveness"* (2:11).
- **The passage, after:** two grounds, *"For Adam was formed first, then Eve; and Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and became a transgressor"* (2:13–14).
- **Roles:**
  - "exercise authority" (G0831, M72) is the **characteristic**, here not permitted.
  - "teach" (M15) is joined to it.
  - "quietness" (M33) is what is asked instead.
- **Reading:**
  - The verse states the restriction and gives its own two grounds, the order of forming and the deception.
  - The inner acts the passage names are learning quietly, self-control, and being deceived.
  - Beyond these I read nothing (rule 69).
- **Home:** 10.10, stated with its own grounds. Ch 11 §7.

**Est 1:22.** He sent letters *"that every man be master in his own household and speak according to the language of his people."*
- **The passage.** Vashti refused to come (1:12; cited in 10.1, 10.13 and Ch 11). The counsel was that the noble women *"will say the same to all the king's officials, and there will be contempt and wrath in plenty"* (1:18). The decree was to have this result: *"all women will give honor to their husbands"* (1:20). *"This advice pleased the king"* (1:21).
- **Roles:**
  - "master" (H8323A, M72) is the **characteristic**, here ordered by decree.
  - "speak" (M65) is joined to it.
- **Reading:**
  - The rule of each house is made a matter of royal law.
  - The motive given is fear of contempt and wrath (1:18), and the aim is honour (1:20). Honour, an inner act, is commanded by decree.
- **Pointer:** Dan 6:26 (§2.7), where a king decrees fear of God.
- **Home:** 10.10 *Ruling another*. Ch 11 §7. It points to 10.13 (1:12).

**Pro 22:7.** *"The rich rules over the poor, and the borrower is the slave of the lender."*
- **Roles:**
  - "rules" (M72) is the **characteristic**.
  - "rich" and "poor" (M46, M09) give its ground.
- **Reading:**
  - The proverb states how things are, without commanding or forbidding. Wealth gives rule, and debt makes a man a slave.
  - Two verses later: *"Whoever has a bountiful eye will be blessed, for he shares his bread with the poor"* (22:9).
- **Face:** rule that comes from money and debt.
- **Home:** 10.10 *Ruling another*. Ch 11 §7.

**Eph 6:9** (from §2.1, a passage verse with no B word): *"Masters, do the same to them, and stop your threatening, knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven, and that there is no partiality with him."*
- What checks the master is knowing that he has a Master too.
- **Home:** 10.10 *Ruling another*, beside Lev 25:43.

**What the group shows (faces of ruling another):**
- **The manner forbidden:** ruthlessly, harshly, with force (Lev 25; Eze 34:4); domineering (1Pe 5:3); lording it (Mat 20:25; 2Cor 1:24).
- **What checks it:**
  - the ruler's fear of God (Lev 25:43; 2Sa 23:3; Neh 5:15)
  - brotherhood (Lev 25:46)
  - the onlooker (Lev 25:53)
  - the Master in heaven (Eph 6:9)
  - the ruled belong to God (Lev 25:42, 55)
- **The inner ground of harsh rule:** feeding oneself (Eze 34:2), a lack of understanding and love of gain (Pro 28:16), anger (Isa 14:6).
- **Where rule comes from:** money and debt (Pro 22:7); royal decree (Est 1:22); mutual giving in marriage (1Cor 7:4).
- **Turned round:** greatness as service (Luk 22:26–27); working with others for their joy (2Cor 1:24); leading by example, willingly and eagerly (1Pe 5:2–3).

### 2.3 R3. Wanting rule, refusing it, resenting it (14: 10 read, 4 carried)

**Already carried:**
- Luk 4:6, the devil's offer of all authority (13.3)
- Dan 11:3–4, *"rule with great dominion and do as he wills"* (10.7 *Willing*)
- 2Pe 2:10, *"despise authority"* (Ch 7)

**Joseph's rule: hated, then received unawares (Gen 37:8; 42:6).**
- **Gen 37:8.** After the sheaves dream: *"Are you indeed to reign over us? Or are you indeed to rule over us?" So they hated him even more for his dreams and for his words.*
  - The hatred was already there: *"they hated him"* because the father loved him more (37:4; in 10.6).
  - After the second dream, *"his brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the saying in mind"* (37:11; in 10.3).
- **Gen 42:6.** *"Now Joseph was governor over the land … And Joseph's brothers came and bowed themselves before him with their faces to the ground."* Then: *"Joseph remembered the dreams that he had dreamed of them"* (42:9). *"Joseph recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him"* (42:8).
- **Roles:**
  - "rule" (M72) is the **characteristic**.
  - "hated" (M06) is the response to it.
  - "dreams" and "words" are what provoked it.
- **Reading:**
  - The brothers' question is about a younger brother's rule over them. Hatred answers it.
  - The rule they hated came about anyway. They bowed without knowing whom they bowed to, and Joseph remembered.
  - The account ends with Joseph reading it as God's doing: *"He has made me … ruler over all the land"* (45:8; carried in 10.10).
- **Face:** another's rule resented in prospect.
- **Home:** 10.6 *Jealousy and envy* (with 37:4). Ch 11 §7. 10.3 (Joseph remembered, 42:9).

**Gideon refuses rule (Judg 8:22–23).**
- **The verses.** *"Rule over us, you and your son and your grandson also, for you have saved us from the hand of Midian."* Gideon said, *"I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you; the Lord will rule over you."*
- **What follows at once:**
  - He asks for the earrings of the spoil (8:24).
  - *"Gideon made an ephod of it … And all Israel whored after it there, and it became a snare to Gideon and to his family"* (8:27).
- **Roles:** "rule" (M72), three times in his answer, is the **characteristic**. "Saved" (M45) is the people's ground for offering it.
- **Reading:**
  - The people offer rule as the return for being saved.
  - Gideon refuses it for himself and his son, and names the Lord as the one who will rule.
  - The next verses record the gold and the ephod, and that it became a snare. The text gives no reason for the ephod, and I give none.
- **Home:** Ch 11 §7, face *rule offered and refused*. 10.7 (refusing is an act). 13.1 for "the Lord will rule over you", as God's character only.

**Abimelech seeks rule (Judg 9:2).**
- **The verse.** *"Which is better for you, that all seventy of the sons of Jerubbaal rule over you, or that one rule over you?" Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh.*

### 2.9 X. Single verses (6 read)

**Luk 10:19–20.** *"Behold, I have given you authority to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall hurt you. Nevertheless, do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven."*
- **The passage.** The seventy-two *"returned with joy"* (10:17). *"In that same hour he rejoiced in the Holy Spirit"* (10:21).
- **Roles:** "authority" (M72) is given. "rejoice" is the **characteristic** that is redirected.
- **Reading:**
  - Jesus does not take the authority back. He moves their joy from the authority to their names written in heaven.
  - The authority is real. It is not to be the ground of joy.
- **Home:** 10.1 (*Delight* or joy; not yet cited). Ch 11 §7. 13.3 (spirits subject).

**Luk 12:5.** *"But I will warn you whom to fear: fear him who, after he has killed, has authority to cast into hell. Yes, I tell you, fear him!"*
- **The passage.** *"do not fear those who kill the body, and after that have nothing more that they can do"* (12:4). *"Fear not; you are of more value than many sparrows"* (12:7).
- **Reading:**
  - Fear is measured by the reach of authority. One whose authority ends at the body is not to be feared. One whose authority reaches past death is.
  - In the same passage: "Fear not".
- **Home:** 10.12, beside Mat 10:28, the parallel already cited there twice.

**Hos 12:4.** *"He strove with the angel and prevailed; he wept and sought his favor. He met God at Bethel, and there God spoke with us"*.
- **The passage.** *"in his manhood he strove with God"* (12:3). *"So you, by the help of your God, return, hold fast to love and justice, and wait continually for your God"* (12:6).
- **Lexical note.** The M72 word is H7786 ("to reign"), on the surface "strove". It is a loose association, recorded and not re-tagged (rule 70).
- **Reading:**
  - Jacob's prevailing is told as weeping and seeking favour. The prophet draws the call to Israel from it: return, hold fast, wait.
- **Home:** 10.9 (seeking God's favour). 13.2 (the angel).

**Jer 30:21.** *"Their prince shall be one of themselves; their ruler shall come out from their midst; I will make him draw near, and he shall approach me, for who would dare of himself to approach me? declares the Lord."*
- **Roles:**
  - "ruler" (M72) is the one who approaches.
  - The phrase ESV renders "dare of himself" carries the M47 **heart** word: who would *pledge his heart* to approach. "Heart" is the characteristic, and "ruler" names who.
- **Reading:**
  - The ruler's nearness to God is God's doing: "I will make him draw near".
  - No one comes near of his own heart's daring.
- **Home:** 10.9 (approaching God). Ch 4 (the heart). 13.1.

**Psa 49:14.** *"Like sheep they are appointed for Sheol; death shall be their shepherd, and the upright shall rule over them in the morning."*
- **The passage.** *"But God will ransom my soul from the power of Sheol"* (49:15; in Ch 2). *"Be not afraid when a man becomes rich"* (49:16).
- **Reading:**
  - The rule of the rich ends at death, where death shepherds them. The upright rule "in the morning".
  - The psalm draws the inner act from this: be not afraid of the rich.
- **Home:** Ch 2 (beside 49:15). 10.12 (be not afraid).

**1Cor 11:10.** *"That is why a wife ought to have a symbol of authority on her head, because of the angels."*
- The verse gives two grounds, "That is why" (11:7–9) and "because of the angels", and does not explain the second. *"Judge for yourselves"* (11:13).
- **Reading:** I record it and add nothing.
- **Home:** 13.2, as a mention of angels only. No inner-being placement.

## 3. God as ruler: 57 verses for 13.1 (God's character only)
