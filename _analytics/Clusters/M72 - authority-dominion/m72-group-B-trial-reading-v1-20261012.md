## 1. Where the authority comes from: Pilate (Joh 19:10–11)

*"So Pilate said to him, 'You will not speak to me? Do you not know that I have authority to release you and authority to crucify you?' Jesus answered him, 'You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given you from above. Therefore he who delivered me over to you has the greater sin.'"*

The passage before: *"When Pilate heard this statement, he was even more afraid"* (19:8), after *"he has made himself the Son of God"* (19:7). He asks *"Where are you from?"*, and Jesus gives *"no answer"* (19:9). After: *"From then on Pilate sought to release him"*, and then the cry, *"you are not Caesar's friend"* (19:12).
- **Roles.**
  - "Authority" (M72) is the **characteristic**. The main operation is Pilate laying claim to his authority, and Jesus naming where it comes from.
  - "Know" (M15) is the knowledge Pilate presses on Jesus.
  - "Release" (M59) is the object of the authority.
  - "Sin" (M56) measures the one who handed him over.
- **Reading:** Pilate speaks of his authority when he is afraid (19:8) and has had no answer (19:9). He states it twice, release and crucify. Jesus does not deny it. He names its source, "given you from above", and that changes how guilt is weighed: "therefore" the one who handed him over has the greater sin. After this Pilate seeks to release him, and the crowd moves him with another authority, Caesar's (19:12).
- **I read:** the claim of authority comes out of fear, not out of security. The authority is real, but it is received, and the one who holds it is still moved by fear of a higher one.
- **Parties and route:** a human authority, God's authority above it (A, named only as "from above"), and the human inner acts of fear, claim and guilt (B).
- **Home:** Ch 11 §7, under a new face, *where authority comes from*. 10.12 *Fear* (19:8, 12): fear acting through the one in authority. 13.1 for "given you from above", as God's character only.

## 2. Ruling the self (Pro 16:32; Psa 119:133; Rom 6:14)

**Pro 16:32.** *"Whoever is slow to anger is better than the mighty, and he who rules his spirit than he who takes a city."*
- **Roles:**
  - "Rules" (M72) is the **characteristic** in the second line. "Spirit" (M47) is its object, the seat of the operation.
  - "Anger" (M02), in "slow to anger", is the paired act in the first line.
  - "Better" (M18) is the measure.
- **Reading:** the proverb sets an outward conquest against an inward one, and puts the inward one first. Ch 11 §7 and 10.13 already carry it. **Confirmed;** nothing to add beyond the face.
- **OT-16:** this is one of its 4 B verses, and the anger reading (10.13) and Ch 11 hold it. Closed for M72.

**Psa 119:133.** *"Keep steady my steps according to your promise, and let no iniquity get dominion over me."* The verses around it: *"I open my mouth and pant, because I long for your commandments"* (119:131); *"Redeem me from man's oppression, that I may keep your precepts"* (119:134).
- **Roles:** "get dominion" (M72) is the **characteristic**. "Iniquity" (M58) is what would rule him, and "promise" (M65) is the ground of the plea.
- **Reading:** the psalmist does not claim to rule himself here. He asks that no iniquity rule him, and asks God to steady his steps. Within the same plea he asks to be redeemed from *man's* oppression (119:134). Two dominions stand side by side, one inner (iniquity) and one outer (oppression). He asks God against both, and the purpose of both requests is keeping God's word.
- **Placement now:** not cited. **Home:** Ch 11 §7 (*Ruling*), and 10.9 (asking God).

**Rom 6:14.** *"For sin will have no dominion over you, since you are not under law but under grace."* Before it: *"Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body, to make you obey its passions"* (6:12). After it: *"you are slaves of the one whom you obey"* (6:16).
- **Roles:** "have dominion" (M72) is the **characteristic**. "Sin" (M56) is the ruler that is denied. "Law" (M54) and "grace" (M50) are the two realms, the ground.
- **Reading:** Paul speaks of sin as a ruler that reigns and makes the body obey its passions (6:12). Its dominion is said to be over, and the reason given is a change of rule: *"under grace"*. He also turns it into a command, *"Let not sin … reign"*. So the end of sin's dominion is stated as a fact (6:14) and given as a task (6:12–13), and in 6:16 obeying is what makes a person a slave.
- **I read:** the question of who rules within is answered by whom one presents oneself to (6:13, 16).
- **Placement now:** not cited (Rom 7 is in 10.7 *Willing*). **Home:** Ch 11 §7, with a pointer to 10.7 (Rom 7:15–21, a will that is not master) and Ch 14.

## 3. Ruling another (Lev 25:43; 1Pe 5:3; Pro 29:2)

**Lev 25:43.** *"You shall not rule over him ruthlessly but shall fear your God."* The ground comes before it: *"For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt"* (25:42).
- **Roles:**
  - "Rule over" (M72) is the **characteristic**.
  - "Ruthlessly" (M06) qualifies how the ruling is done: harsh rule is the thing forbidden.
  - "Fear" (M01) is what is set against it.
- **Reading:** the check on rule over another is the ruler's fear of God, and the reason is whose servant the ruled one is. Already in 10.10 *The fear of God and the weak*. **Confirmed.** The face for M72: rule limited by the ruler's own fear.

**1Pe 5:3.** *"not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock."* The verses around it: *"exercising oversight, not under compulsion, but willingly … not for shameful gain, but eagerly"* (5:2); *"Clothe yourselves, all of you, with humility toward one another, for 'God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble'"* (5:5).
- **Roles:** "domineering" (M72) is the **characteristic**, here forbidden. The verse holds no other M-code. The passage around it gives willingness and eagerness (5:2) and humility (5:5).
- **Reading:** oversight is to be done in one way, by example, and not in the other way, by domineering. The motives named are willingness, not compulsion, and eagerness, not gain. The passage ends on humility for all, elders included.
- **Pointer:** Luk 22:25–26, *"The kings of the Gentiles exercise lordship over them … But not so with you"* (also B), is the same contrast. It is kept separate here and read with the B set.
- **Placement now:** not cited. **Home:** 10.10, with the inner motives of one who leads; Ch 11 §7.

**Pro 29:2.** *"When the righteous increase, the people rejoice, but when the wicked rule, the people groan."*
- **Roles:**
  - "Rule" (M72) is a **qualifier** here, the setting: who is in power.
  - "Rejoice" (M04) and "groan" (M03) are the characteristics, the people's inner response.
  - "Righteous" and "wicked" (M12, M58) qualify the rulers.
- **Reading:** the verse is about what a people feels under its rulers. This is **the impact of authority on a characteristic** (your §5 question).
- **Triage:** the M72 word is supportive, so the verse goes **forward to M04 / M03**. The verse is not cited.

## 4. God's rule (Dan 4:17)

*"to the end that the living may know that the Most High rules the kingdom of men and gives it to whom he will and sets over it the lowliest of men."* The passage:
- the same sentence again (4:25)
- the king's boast, *"Is not this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power … for the glory of my majesty?"* (4:30)
- the end: *"my reason returned to me, and I blessed the Most High … for his dominion is an everlasting dominion"* (4:34); *"those who walk in pride he is able to humble"* (4:37)
- **Roles:**
  - "Rules" (M72 B), with "Most High" (E) as its subject, is God's act.
  - "Kingdom" (A) is a qualifier.
  - "Know" (M15) is the purpose: that the living may know.
  - "Will" (M64) and "lowliest" (M09) qualify the giving.
- **Reading:** God's rule is stated for the sake of human knowing. The human act in the passage is Nebuchadnezzar's: his boast of his own power and glory, then reason returning, then blessing the Most High.
- **Test of the line:** the B word here is said of God, so for M72 it is read as God's character only (13.1, already carried in *Willing*). The human side, pride in one's own power and the humbling, is not carried by the B word. It sits in 4:30–37 and belongs to the strands that read pride (M08) and knowing (M15).
- **Proposed rule for B:** where God is the ruler, the B word is read for God's character only (13.1). The human act around it is placed by its own operation, or marked forward.
