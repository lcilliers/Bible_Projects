## 2. The readings

### Gen 31:29: power to harm, held back by God's word

> "It is in my power to do you harm. But the God of your father spoke to me last night, saying, 'Be
> careful not to say anything to Jacob, either good or bad.'"

**The passage (31:24–30).** God came to Laban "in a dream by night" (31:24). Laban overtakes Jacob
and accuses him of tricking him and carrying off his daughters "like captives of the sword" (31:26).
He says he has the power, and then why he will not use it.

**The main operation:** a man who could harm holds back, because God told him to. He says both
things to the man he could harm.

**In the narrative now:** Jacob's side of the passage is told ("Because I was afraid", 31:31). Laban's
31:24 and 31:29 are not cited.

**Impact:** none for M72. This is harm held back, which is **M58**, where OT-98.4 and OT-98.5 already
carry it. It stays there.

### 1Sa 15:28: the kingdom torn away after the word was rejected

> "The Lord has torn the kingdom of Israel from you this day and has given it to a neighbor of yours,
> who is better than you."

**The passage (15:26–30):**
- Samuel: "you have rejected the word of the Lord, and the Lord has rejected you from being king"
  (15:26).
- Saul seizes the skirt of Samuel's robe "and it tore" (15:27). Samuel turns the tear into the word
  of 15:28.
- Then "the Glory of Israel will not lie or have regret" (15:29).
- Saul answers: "I have sinned; yet honor me now before the elders of my people" (15:30).

**The main operation** is God's: the kingdom taken from the one who rejected his word. The same
word, "rejected", is used of both. Saul's acts are the grasp at the robe and the request for honour
after the confession.

**In the narrative now:** Saul's confession is told ("I feared the people and obeyed their voice",
15:24), with 15:9, 15:11, 15:21 and 15:32. Verses 15:26–30 are not cited.

**Impact:**
- **13.1 *Ruling*, "He gives authority and takes it away":** 15:26 and 28, where the rejection
  answers a rejection. This is God's character, within your M72 ruling.
- 15:29 ("will not … have regret") stands in the same chapter as "I regret that I have made Saul
  king" (15:11) and "the Lord regretted" (15:35). If 15:29 is quoted, all three must be quoted
  together and left standing as the text has them, not reconciled.
- **Saul's "yet honor me now before the elders"** (15:30) is honour sought from people straight
  after confessing to God. It is not a fear or M72 act. It belongs to the strand that reads honour
  (M22), and should be marked forward there, not woven now.

### Ezr 7:9, 8:18; Neh 2:8: "the good hand of my God was on me"

> "on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem, for the good hand of his God was on
> him. For Ezra had set his heart to study the Law of the Lord, and to do it and to teach his
> statutes and rules in Israel." (Ezra 7:9–10)

> "And by the good hand of our God on us, they brought us a man of discretion" (Ezra 8:18)

> "And the king granted me what I asked, for the good hand of my God was upon me" (Nehemiah 2:8)

**The passages.**
- **Ezra 7.** The phrase runs through the chapter:
  - "the king granted him all that he asked, for the hand of the Lord his God was on him" (7:6)
  - the journey completed (7:9), then straight away "For Ezra had set his heart …" (7:10)
  - Ezra's own words at the end: "who extended to me his steadfast love before the king … **I took
    courage, for the hand of the Lord my God was on me**, and I gathered leading men" (7:28)
- **Ezra 8.** The hand brings him the men he needed (8:18). He will not ask the king for soldiers
  because he has said, "The hand of our God is for good on all who seek him" (8:22). 8:21–22 are in
  10.14.
- **Nehemiah 2.** Prayer (1:11; 2:4), the request (2:5), and the grant named as God's hand (2:8). All
  of this is in 10.7 *Whose measure*.

**The main operation** is the person's: **naming God's hand as the reason** for what came about, and
what that knowing does in him. Two inner acts sit right beside the phrase, and neither is in the
narrative:
- **Ezra 7:10, a heart set.** "Ezra had set his heart to study the Law of the Lord, and to do it and to
  teach." The setting of the heart is an act of direction. It is verse-stated, and named in three
  steps: study, do, teach. The verse opens with "For", straight after the good hand (7:9). I read the
  "For" as giving the hand a reason in Ezra's heart; the text does not spell that out further.
- **Ezra 7:28, courage taken.** "I took courage, for the hand of the Lord my God was on me." Knowing
  God's hand on him is the stated ground of his courage, and the courage turns at once into action:
  "and I gathered".

**Impact:**
- **10.7 *Choosing and setting direction*:** Ezra 7:10, setting the heart to study, do and teach. It is
  a clear verse for the heart's direction, and it is not in the book. Rehoboam, who "did not set his
  heart to seek the Lord" (2 Chronicles 12:14), is in 10.13 and 10.14, and is its opposite.
- **10.12 *Fear*, "What changes it", or 10.9:** Ezra 7:28, courage taken *because* God's hand is on
  him. Ezra 8:22 already shows Ezra choosing not to lean on the king's soldiers. 7:28 gives the inner
  ground.
- **13.1 *His favor*:** the good hand of God on a person, named by the person as the reason (Ezr 7:6,
  9, 28; 8:18; Neh 2:8). Under your ruling the hand is read for God's character only. The verses say
  it is "for good" on "all who seek him" (8:22).

### Neh 2:18: telling of God's hand moves others to act

> "And I told them of the hand of my God that had been upon me for good, and also of the words that
> the king had spoken to me. And they said, 'Let us rise up and build.' So they strengthened their
> hands for the good work."

**The passage (2:17–18).** Nehemiah names the trouble: "You see the trouble we are in … that we may no
longer suffer derision" (2:17). Then he tells two things: God's hand, and the king's words.

**The main operation:** a telling that moves the hearers. They answer with their own resolve ("Let
us rise up and build"), and the verse says what followed: "they strengthened their hands". I read
the strengthened hands as resolve taken, the figure the verse uses.

**In the narrative now:** not cited.

**Impact:** **10.10 *Relating to others*** (or 10.5 *Speaking*, for what speech carries): what one
person tells of God's dealing with him gives others resolve. It sits beside 10.12's Ezra 7:28 (his
own courage), as the same ground passed on. The strengthened hands are M23 (strength); a forward
note is needed there, not a weave now.

### Isa 52:7: "Your God reigns", as good news

> "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news, who publishes peace,
> who brings good news of happiness, who publishes salvation, who says to Zion, 'Your God reigns.'"

**The passage (52:7–10).** The watchmen "lift up their voice; together they sing for joy" (52:8). The
waste places break into singing, "for the Lord has comforted his people" (52:9).

**The main operation:** God's reign announced, and heard as good news, with joy. The reign is God's
(13.1). The joy and the comfort are the hearers' (M04, M70).

**In the narrative now:** not cited.

**Impact:**
- **13.1 *Ruling*:** "His reign, and the nations' rage" (Rev 11:17–18) has God's reign with wrath.
  Isa 52:7–9 is the other face: his reign is good news, joined to peace, salvation and comfort. This
  sits with "The ruler to come", where "His rule comes with peace".
- The hearers' joy belongs to M70 (good news), which is OT-97.40, an item still open on a cluster
  not yet read. No weave of the joy now.

### 1Ki 2:15 (pointer): "the kingdom was mine"

> "You know that the kingdom was mine, and that all Israel fully expected me to reign. However, the
> kingdom has turned about and become my brother's, for it was his from the Lord."

**The passage (2:13–23).** Adonijah comes "peacefully" (2:13). He says the kingdom was his, grants
that it was his brother's "from the Lord", and asks for Abishag (2:17). Solomon hears the request
as a claim: "Ask for him the kingdom also" (2:22). It costs Adonijah his life (2:23).

**The main operation:** a loss acknowledged in words ("from the Lord") and a request that Solomon
reads as reaching for the same thing. The verses give Solomon's reading. They do not say what
Adonijah intended.

**In the narrative now:** not cited.

**Impact:** none for M72. A possible place is 10.11 *Hiding and disclosing* (a request read as
covering an aim), but the verses give only Solomon's reading. It needs care, and I would leave it
as a pointer for now.

**In short:** under your ruling, OT-97.38 has nothing for M72, and the item can be closed as already
decided by #1998. Reading the passages for their acts found real gaps, though. **Ezra 7:10 and 7:28
are clear inner-being verses that the book does not have**, and Neh 2:18 and Isa 52:7–9 are missing
too. These came to light only because the passages were opened again.

**If woven,** each placement is a quotation with short framing. The "I read" on Ezra 7:10's "For" and
on the strengthened hands would each need a claim (rule 77). The fear claims in 10.12 and the claims
on God's rule in 13.1 would be reconciled in the same pass.

**Forward notes that would be needed:** 1Sa 15:30 → M22 (honour); Neh 2:18 "strengthened their
hands" → M23 (strength).
