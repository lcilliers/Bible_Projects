## 2. Read again from your side: the standing, and what works with it

I went back over the readings with your suggestion in mind: authority as a state or status, working with other characteristics and parties. This is what I found in my own reading.

**Where my reading was biased.** In §2 of the reading I marked the B word as the **characteristic** in most verses. Read again, the B word names the act in only a handful of verses: Pro 16:32, Gen 4:7, 1Co 6:12, 1Co 7:37, Psa 19:13, Psa 119:133 and Rom 6:14. Even in these it is joined to another act: slowness to anger, sin's desire, one's own desire, what is lawful. **In the rest, another act carries the verse, and the authority is the standing that act moves in or toward.** By the guide, those B words were qualifiers, giving a setting, a ground or an object. I assigned the role by the strand's word, which the guide warns against.

**What this shows, as far as the verses go:**
- **The standing is nearly always given, received, taken or withheld.** The source is named: God, the chief priests, money, a decree, a spouse, the people. A word search finds a giving, receiving or taking verb in the same verse in 71 of the 275. That is a search, not a reading.
- **The same standing is filled by opposite acts.** It holds fear or faith, pride or wonder, fury or restraint. What fills it changes what it does to others: harm, or building up.
- **Several parties are present.** These are the source, the holder, the one ruled, and often an onlooker. The verses frequently say how the holder stands toward his own source, as in "knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven".

This supports your reading more than my first account did. It is not yet tested across the verses in the forward list (47) or the God list (57), and those were triaged under the same bias. Your re-read (OT-100) is the right check.
