## Cross-strong synergy — a genuine two-pole split, not one meaning

The verse evidence splits cleanly into two registers that share the gloss "error/deception" but are
not the same concept:

**Pole 1 — innocent, unwitting, or hidden error.** G0051's *only* citation (Heb 9:7) is explicitly
"the unintentional sins of the people," addressed through the sanctuary system, not moral failure
in the culpable sense. H7691 (Ps 19:12) is a direct plea about error the person *cannot see in
themselves* — "who can discern his errors? Declare me innocent from hidden faults" — error here is
defined by its invisibility to the one who commits it. H4879 (Job 19:4) is explicitly private and
self-contained: "my error remains with myself," offered as a defence that it harms no one else.
H8417 extends the category to a cosmic scale — even God's own angels "he charges with error"
relative to God's own standard (Job 4:18).

**Pole 2 — deliberate deception, exercised as an act on someone else.** G4106 (planē) is the
richest single entry: from "fraud" (the guards' report of a stolen body, Matt 27:64) to organized
false teaching (Eph 4:14, 2 Thess 2:11's "strong delusion," 1 John 4:6's "spirit of error" set
against "the Spirit of truth") to a term for entrenched moral corruption (Rom 1:27, Jude 11 — Cain/
Balaam/Korah as paradigm cases). G1185 (deleazō) supplies the operative image for this pole: baiting
a hook — enticement works by appeal to "his own desire" (Jas 1:14) or "sensual passions" (2 Pet
2:18), i.e. it succeeds through the target's own appetite, not force. H8442 (Isa 32:6) folds error
into a cluster with folly, iniquity, and ungodliness — deliberate, not accidental.

**The bridge case, and the most consequential finding: H6601B (pathah) is morally bidirectional in
its own verse set.** Its 19 verses include straightforwardly negative enticement to sin (Prov 1:10
"if sinners entice you," Job 31:9/27's self-indictment for a heart "enticed toward a woman," Judg
14:15's plot against Samson) — but also **God's own tender wooing of Israel using the identical
verb**: "I will allure her... and speak tenderly to her" (Hos 2:14), and the heavenly-court scene
where a spirit volunteers to "entice" Ahab to his death with God's own authorization (1 Kgs
22:20-22, repeated 2 Chr 18:19-21), plus Ezekiel 14:9's striking claim that God himself "deceived"
a false prophet as an act of judgment. On this evidence, "entice/deceive" (pathah) is not
intrinsically a vice-word at its root — it names a persuasion-mechanism that verses apply to
seduction into sin, redemptive wooing, and divine judicial deception alike, with the moral valence
supplied entirely by context, not by the word.

## Note for #1680

This family is the clearest evidence yet against collapsing a gloss-family into one distilled
meaning: not only does it split into an innocent/hidden pole and a deliberate/agentive pole, but
its single richest Hebrew member (pathah) is demonstrably used by the text itself in morally
opposite directions. A method that assigns one meaning-value to "error/deception/enticement" as a
family would flatten a distinction the verses are actively making.
