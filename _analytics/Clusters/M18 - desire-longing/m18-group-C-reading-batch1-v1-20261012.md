## 2. Batch 1 readings

### 2.1 R7. I do not do what I want (Rom 7:15–21)

**The passage:**
- *"For I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate. Now if I do what I do not want, I agree with the law, that it is good. So now it is no longer I who do it, but sin that dwells within me"* (Rom 7:15–17).
- *"For I have the desire to do what is right, but not the ability to carry it out. For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I keep on doing. Now if I do what I do not want, it is no longer I who do it, but sin that dwells within me. So I find it to be a law that when I want to do right, evil lies close at hand"* (7:18–21).

**Roles:**
- want (*thelō*, M18) is the characteristic: the will
- hate (M06) is set against it
- "agree" (M44) with the law, that it is good (M05)
- sin (M56) "dwells within"

**Reading:**
- The will is real, and it is on the side of the good. Paul says three things about it: he wants the good, he hates what he does, and his wanting agrees with the law.
- The will is not able to carry itself out. "Not the ability to carry it out" names the gap, and the doing goes the other way.
- Paul does not deny the will. He says that what does the opposite is "no longer I", but sin that dwells within.
- He calls it a "law" he finds: whenever he wants the right, evil is close at hand.
- I read the will here as a true inner act that is not master in its own house.

**What is already in the narrative:** the delight in the inner being and the war in the members (7:22–23, 10.1), the cry (7:24, Ch 2), "the desire … but not the ability" and the divided service (7:18, 25, Ch 7). The willing itself (7:15–17, 19–21) is not.

**Home:** 10.7 *Willing*, a new paragraph before *A will under another*: **"A will that cannot carry itself out."** It points to 10.1, Ch 2 and Ch 7.

**Change of character (#1919):** the same verb, "want", is the will set on godliness (2 Tim 3:12, 10.7). Here it is the will that wants the good and cannot do it.

### 2.2 D1. Doing the will of God (23 verses)

The current 10.7 *A will under another* holds four pieces: Psa 143:10, Col 1:9, Eph 6:6 and Gethsemane. The group adds what the verses say about God's will as something a person prays, knows, does or fails to do.

**Praying it.**
- *"Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven"* (Mat 6:10). It is the prayer taught, before bread and forgiveness (6:11–12).
- At Gethsemane, the second time: *"My Father, if this cannot pass unless I drink it, your will be done"* (26:42). It comes after *"The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak"* (26:41). The words he taught are the words he prays.
- Asking within it: *"if we ask anything according to his will he hears us"* (1Jo 5:14). The other side: *"If you abide in me, and my words abide in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you"* (Joh 15:7). The second verse leaves the wish open, but only for one in whom his words abide.
- I read the two together. The asking is free where the asker's will has been shaped by his words.

**Knowing it.**
- *"If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God"* (Joh 7:17). The willing comes first, and the knowing follows it.
- Paul was *"appointed … to know his will"* (Act 22:14), and was told the next moment *"why do you wait?"* (22:16).
- *"Do not be foolish, but understand what the will of the Lord is"* (Eph 5:17), with *"be filled with the Spirit"* (5:18).
- Knowing can stand without doing: *"you … know his will and approve what is excellent"* (Rom 2:18), said to one who *"boast[s] in God"* (2:17) and teaches others (2:19–21).
- The servant *"who knew his master's will but did not get ready or act according to his will"* is beaten more than the one who did not know (Luk 12:47–48).

**Saying and doing.**
- *"Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father"* (Mat 7:21). Those who said "Lord" had prophesied and done mighty works (7:22).
- The two sons: one said *"I will not, but afterward he changed his mind and went"*, the other *"I will, sir, but did not go"*. *"Which of the two did the will of his father?"* (Mat 21:29–31). The first son's "I will not" is a will that turned. Jesus applies the change of mind to belief: *"you did not afterward change your minds and believe him"* (21:32).

**What it is, where the verses say:**
- giving thanks in all circumstances (1Th 5:18)
- sanctification, abstaining from sexual immorality, each knowing how to control his body *"not in the passion of lust"* (1Th 4:3–5)
- by doing good, silencing the ignorance of foolish people (1Pe 2:15)
- separation from what broke faith, after confession (Ezr 10:11)
- **Suffering inside it:** *"it is better to suffer for doing good, if that should be God's will"* (1Pe 3:17), followed by *"For Christ also suffered"* (3:18).
- **Endurance to the end of it:** *"you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised"* (Heb 10:36).

**Who does it, and who is related by it.**
- *"whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother"* (Mat 12:50; Mar 3:35). It is said with his hand stretched toward his disciples, while his family stands outside.
- *"if anyone is a worshiper of God and does his will, God listens to him"* (Joh 9:31). The man born blind says it against those who said *"we do not know where he comes from"* (9:29).
- **God working it in them:** *"equip you with everything good that you may do his will, working in us that which is pleasing in his sight"* (Heb 13:21). Beside it stands Phi 2:13 in 10.12 (*"God … works in you, both to will and to work"*).

**Route A:** *"Bless the Lord, all his hosts, his ministers, who do his will!"* (Psa 103:21). This is angels doing his will. Home: 13.2.

**Homes:**
- 10.7 *Willing*: a new subsection after *A will under another*, **"Doing the will of God"**, in the parts above
- the family verses (Mat 12:50; Mar 3:35): a pointer in 10.10
- Psa 103:21: 13.2
- Rom 12:2 stays in Ch 14 by pointer
- Phi 2:13 stays in 10.12 by pointer

### 2.3 D2. Plans held under God's will (8 verses)

10.7 *Planning* already has Paul's "often intended … prevented" (Rom 1:13) and James 4:15 ("If the Lord wills"). The group shows the same thing in Paul's own plans, and in what holds them back:
- **Said at parting:** *"I will return to you if God wills"* (Act 18:21), after he *"declined"* to stay longer (18:20).
- **Said with purpose:** *"I will come to you soon, if the Lord wills, and I will find out not the talk of these arrogant people but their power"* (1Co 4:19). Then *"What do you wish? Shall I come to you with a rod, or with love?"* (4:21). Their will is asked too.
- **His wish, and what he hopes:** *"I do not want to see you now just in passing. I hope to spend some time with you, if the Lord permits"* (1Co 16:7). He stays in Ephesus because *"a wide door … has opened to me, and there are many adversaries"* (16:9).
- **Prayed for:** *"always in my prayers, asking that somehow by God's will I may now at last succeed in coming to you. For I long to see you"* (Rom 1:10–11). *"strive together with me in your prayers … so that by God's will I may come to you with joy"* (15:30, 32).
- **Hindered:** *"we endeavored the more eagerly and with great desire to see you face to face, because we wanted to come to you, I, Paul, again and again, but Satan hindered us"* (1Th 2:17–18). The hindrance is named, and it is not God's will but Satan.
- **Yielded by others:** Paul *"would not be persuaded, we ceased and said, 'Let the will of the Lord be done'"* (Act 21:14), after *"What are you doing, weeping and breaking my heart?"* (21:13). Here the friends' will gives way, to the Lord's will as they read it in Paul's resolve.
- **A wish set beside a gift:** *"I wish that all were as I myself am. But each has his own gift from God"* (1Co 7:7). He states his wish and does not press it on others.

**Reading:**
- The verses keep three things apart:
  - Paul's own wanting, which is strong and repeated
  - God's will, prayed for and deferred to
  - what hinders, named once as Satan
- I read the plan as held open toward God, without the wanting being given up.

**Home:** 10.7 *Planning*, extending the "Paul plans the same way" paragraph.

### 2.4 J5. Jesus' own will (8 verses; route A, with B)

- **The gathering refused.** *"O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the city that kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to it! How often would I have gathered your children together as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and you were not willing!"* (Mat 23:37; Luk 13:34).
  - His will and theirs use one verb: "would I" and "you were not willing".
  - The picture is a hen gathering. The result: *"your house is left to you desolate"* (23:38).
  - The door is left open: *"until you say, 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord'"* (23:39).
  - Homes: 13.1 *Willing* for his will (A), and 10.7 *Willing*, *Refusing God refuses something good*, for "being gathered" (B).
- **A longing in his will.** *"I came to cast fire on the earth, and would that it were already kindled! I have a baptism to be baptized with, and how great is my distress until it is accomplished!"* (Luk 12:49–50). The will is joined with distress. Home: 13.1 *Willing*.
- **His will for those given him.** *"Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory"* (Joh 17:24). It is spoken as his will to the Father, grounded in *"you loved me before the foundation of the world"*. Home: 13.1 *Willing*.
- **Calling those he wanted.** *"he went up on the mountain and called to him those whom he desired, and they came to him. And he appointed twelve … so that they might be with him"* (Mar 3:13–14). Home: 13.1 *Choosing* (with 10.7 *Being chosen*).
- **The owner's will, and the begrudging eye.** *"Take what belongs to you and go. I choose to give to this last worker as I give to you. Am I not allowed to do what I choose with what belongs to me? Or do you begrudge my generosity?"* (Mat 20:14–15).
  - "Do you begrudge" is literally "is your eye evil", the reverse of the good eye of Pro 22:9 (10.6 *Through the eye*).
  - The owner's will is generosity. The complaint (20:12) rests on comparison: "you have made them equal to us".
  - Homes: 13.1 *Willing* (A, a parable), and 10.6 *Through the eye*, beside Pro 22:9 (B).
- **Meaning to pass by.** *"He meant to pass by them, but when they saw him walking on the sea they thought it was a ghost, and cried out … But immediately he spoke to them and said, 'Take heart; it is I. Do not be afraid'"* (Mar 6:48–50). The verse states the intention and does not explain it. Their terror is met at once with his word. Home: 10.12, beside "Fear not", as a pointer only. I place nothing on the intention itself.
