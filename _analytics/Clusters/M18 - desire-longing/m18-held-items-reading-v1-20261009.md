## 1. Jealousy and zeal (OT-95: 10 verses)

The verses do not show one jealousy. They show several, distinguished by **whose** jealousy it is and **for whom or against whom** it burns.

**Jealousy for another (1Ki 19:10, 14; Num 11:29).**
- Elijah says the same words twice, word for word: *"I have been very jealous for the Lord, the God of hosts … and I, even I only, am left, and they seek my life, to take it away"*. The first time is in the cave (19:10). The second is after the wind, the earthquake, the fire and "the sound of a low whisper" (19:12–14).
- The encounter did not change how he described himself.
- God does not argue with him. He gives him work: *"Go, return on your way … you shall anoint"* (19:15–16).
- His zeal is joined in the same sentence to being alone and to his life being hunted. **I read** zeal for God that has turned into the sense of standing alone. God answers it with work, not with a correction.

Joshua's is also jealousy for another: *"Are you jealous for my sake? Would that all the Lord's people were prophets, that the Lord would put his Spirit on them!"* (Num 11:29). It is refused by the very person it is for.

**Compare.**

| | Elijah | Joshua |
|---|---|---|
| Jealous for | God | Moses |
| What the jealousy wants | God's covenant guarded | The gift kept to one man ("stop them", 11:28) |
| What it meets | No rebuke; sent back to work | Refused: Moses wants the Spirit given to all |

**I read** that jealousy for another can guard and can restrict. The difference is whether it wants God's gift kept narrow.

**Envy of the wicked: seen, then embittered (Psa 73:1, 3, 20–22; Pro 23:17).**
- Psalm 73 begins with its conclusion: *"Truly God is good to Israel, to those who are pure in heart"* (73:1). This is OT-96 *ṭôb*. "Good" is a confession about God, set over the whole psalm.
- Then: *"But as for me, my feet had almost stumbled … For I was envious of the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked"* (73:2–3). Envy comes in by sight, as desire does in 10.6.
- In the sanctuary (73:17, already in 10.4) the prosperity is seen again: *"Like a dream when one awakes, O Lord, when you rouse yourself, you despise them as phantoms"* (73:20, H5782). What was envied turns out to be a dream, and God's rousing is the waking.
- Then the psalmist names his own state: *"When my soul was embittered, when I was pricked in heart, I was brutish and ignorant; I was like a beast toward you"* (73:21–22).

**I read** envy as a sight of another's good that embitters the soul. The psalm does not cure it with a better argument. It cures it with a different seeing, in God's presence.

Proverbs gives the same answer as a command, set in time: *"Let not your heart envy sinners, but continue in the fear of the Lord all the day. Surely there is a future, and your hope will not be cut off"* (Pro 23:17–18). Envy looks at what others have now. The answer looks at "a future" and "hope".

**Jealousy among the wise (Jam 3:14–16).** *"But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not boast and be false to the truth. This is not the wisdom that comes down from above, but is earthly, unspiritual, demonic. For where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there will be disorder and every vile practice."*
- It is set against *"Who is wise and understanding among you?"* (3:13). So it is the jealousy of people who would be counted wise.
- "Bitter" is a taste. Psalm 73 has "embittered" too.
- It is "in your hearts", and it shows outwardly as "disorder".
- James names its source in three words. The middle one, "unspiritual", is literally "soulish".

**God's jealousy, for his people and over them (Eze 36:5–6; Isa 42:13–14; Jam 4:5; Eze 8:3).**
- **For them, against those who scorned them:** *"I have spoken in my hot jealousy against the rest of the nations and against all Edom, who gave my land to themselves as a possession with wholehearted joy and utter contempt"* (Eze 36:5). *"I have spoken in my jealous wrath, because you have suffered the reproach of the nations"* (36:6). His jealousy answers someone else's joy at taking what is his.
- **Roused after long restraint:** *"The Lord goes out like a mighty man, like a man of war he stirs up his zeal … For a long time I have held my peace; I have kept still and restrained myself; now I will cry out like a woman in labor; I will gasp and pant"* (Isa 42:13–14). Explore the figures. In two verses God's zeal is a warrior and a woman giving birth. **I read** zeal that both fights and brings forth, held back a long time and then released with a cry. Isaiah sets the two pictures side by side. The joining is his.
- **Over them:** *"You adulterous people! Do you not know that friendship with the world is enmity with God? … Or do you suppose it is to no purpose that the Scripture says, 'He yearns jealously over the spirit that he has made to dwell in us'? But he gives more grace"* (Jam 4:4–6).
  - The picture is a husband's jealousy over a divided loyalty, aimed at "the spirit that he has made to dwell in us".
  - The next words are "But he gives more grace". This jealousy is answered with grace, not only with wrath. Compare Eze 36, where jealousy turns outward against enemies.
  - **I read** God's jealousy as the same burning with two objects: those who harm his people, and his people's own divided heart.
- **What provokes it:** *"the seat of the image of jealousy, which provokes to jealousy"* (Eze 8:3). It stands at the entrance of the inner court, with *"the glory of the God of Israel … there"* (8:4). The image provokes by being set up in the place that belongs to him.

**What changes the character of jealousy** (for Ch 11 §4). The word is the same in each case. What differs is shown in the verses:
- **Whose it is:** God's, Elijah's, Joshua's, a husband's (Pro 6:34–35, in 10.1), a sister's (Gen 30:1, in 10.6)
- **For whom or against whom**
- **Whether it wants another's good kept narrow** (Joshua), **or guards what is truly owed** (God's for his people; Elijah's for the covenant)
- **Whether it ends in grace** (Jam 4:6) **or in a rotting** (*"envy makes the bones rot"*, Pro 14:30, in Ch 4)

**Where:**
- **10.6, new section *Jealousy and envy*:** the human faces, with pointers to Job 5:2, Pro 6:34–35, 14:30, 27:4, Gen 30:1 and Song 8:6 where they already stand.
- **13.1, new section *Jealousy*:** God's (Eze 36:5–6; Isa 42:13–14; Jam 4:5–6; Eze 8:3; Deu 6:15 pointer to 10.13).
- **10.9:** Psalm 73's envy undone in God's presence.
- **Ch 11 §4:** the faces.

## 2. Generic "good" (OT-96: 10 verses)

**Hezekiah's plea (Isa 38:3; 2Ki 20:3).** *"Please, O Lord, remember how I have walked before you in faithfulness and with a whole heart, and have done what is good in your sight." And Hezekiah wept bitterly.* God answers: *"I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears"* (Isa 38:5; 2Ki 20:5).
- He appeals to his record: a whole heart, and good in God's sight.
- God's answer names his prayer and his tears. It does not name the record.
- **I read** that the verses keep a plea about one's own good separate from what God says he answered. I do not go further than that.

**Where:** Ch 2 *Life asked for* (Isa 38:16–17 are already there), and 10.9.

**God's look (Gen 1:21).** *"And God saw that it was good."* This goes with Gen 1:25 (reading M25 §6): God's look of approval, then the woman's look in Gen 3:6.

**Where:** with Gen 1:25 (13.4; see §5 of that reading).

**A heart that is a trap (Ecc 7:26).** *"And I find something more bitter than death: the woman whose heart is snares and nets, and whose hands are fetters. He who pleases God escapes her, but the sinner is taken by her."*
- It is the Preacher's own finding, after *"I turned my heart to know and to search out"* (7:25), *"which my soul has sought repeatedly"* (7:28).
- The figure is a hunter's: snares and nets for a heart, fetters for hands.
- A heart can be a trap set for another person's desire. Escape is tied to "he who pleases God", not to strength of will.

**Where:** 10.6, beside Pro 6:25 ("let her capture you with her eyelashes"). It is the same picture of capture.

**The soul's portion, and waiting (Lam 3:24–26).** *"'The Lord is my portion,' says my soul, 'therefore I will hope in him.' The Lord is good to those who wait for him, to the soul who seeks him. It is good that one should wait quietly for the salvation of the Lord."*
- The soul speaks to itself, which is a voice to the soul (Ch 11 §5).
- "Portion" is the same as in Psa 16:5.
- Twice the goodness belongs to waiting. **I read** that waiting is where the soul's hope is placed, and that "good" is said both of the Lord and of the waiting.

**Where:** 10.9 (waiting), Ch 11 §5, and 13.1 *He is good*.

**Pressure, and the prayer for good (Psa 125:3–4).** *"For the scepter of wickedness shall not rest on the land allotted to the righteous, lest the righteous stretch out their hands to do wrong. Do good, O Lord, to those who are good, and to those who are upright in their hearts!"* The verses show that the rule of the wicked, left in place, can push the righteous into wrong. The prayer asks God to do good to the upright in heart.

**Where:** 10.8 *Right and wrong* (pressure toward wrong), and 13.1.

**A mixed verdict (2Ch 19:2–3).** *"Should you help the wicked and love those who hate the Lord? Because of this, wrath has gone out against you from the Lord. Nevertheless, some good is found in you, for you destroyed the Asheroth out of the land, and have set your heart to seek God."* A love wrongly given and a heart set to seek God stand in one king, and the prophet's verdict holds both.

**Where:** 10.7 *Setting the heart*.

**Filled with one's own ways (Pro 14:13–14).** *"Even in laughter the heart may ache … The backslider in heart will be filled with the fruit of his ways, and a good man will be filled with the fruit of his ways."* This is the same filling as *"have their fill of their own devices"* (Pro 1:31, in 10.7).

**Where:** 10.7 *What follows a choice*.

**Knowing in heart and soul (Jos 23:14–15).** *"you know in your hearts and souls, all of you, that not one word has failed of all the good things that the Lord your God promised"*. Then: *"just as all the good things … have been fulfilled for you, so the Lord will bring upon you all the evil things"*.
- The knowledge comes from experience, held in heart and soul.
- Joshua turns it into a warning: the same faithfulness holds for the threats.

**Where:** 10.2 *Knowing*.

## 3. The heat word *ḥāmam* (OT-15: 19 verses)

**The heart that grows hot, and then speaks (Psa 39:1–3).**
- *"I said, 'I will guard my ways, that I may not sin with my tongue …' I was mute and silent; I held my peace to no avail, and my distress grew worse. My heart became hot within me. As I mused, the fire burned; then I spoke with my tongue"*.
- What he speaks is a prayer: *"O Lord, make me know my end"* (39:4).
- The sequence is the verses' own: silence held, distress growing, the heart hot, musing as the fire, and then speech. The heat does not come out in sin, which was his fear. It comes out to God.

**Where:** 10.5 *The pause between* or *From the heart to the mouth*, and Ch 4 *The heart feels*.

**Heat that kills (Deu 19:4–6).** *"lest the avenger of blood in hot anger pursue the manslayer … though the man did not deserve to die, since he had not hated his neighbor in the past"*. The Hebrew says "while his heart is hot". The law builds cities of refuge because the heat of a grieving relative will not tell the innocent from the guilty. The law allows for the inner state and sets a distance between it and the act ("because the way is long").

**Where:** 10.13 (anger provoked) and 10.10.

**Burning with desire (Isa 57:3–5).** *"you who burn with lust among the oaks, under every green tree, who slaughter your children in the valleys"*. The verses before are mockery: *"Whom are you mocking? … stick out your tongue"* (57:4, H6026, the M25 read-back verse held with OT-15). The burning is for other gods, under every green tree, and it reaches as far as the children. Desire for an idol goes from heat to killing.

**Where:** 10.6, beside Jer 2:24 ("in her heat sniffing the wind"), which is the same picture of heat.

**The oven (Hos 7:6–7).** Ch 4 and 10.13 already have *"with hearts like an oven they approach their intrigue; all night their anger smolders"*. 7:7 completes it: *"All of them are hot as an oven, and they devour their rulers … and none of them calls upon me"*. The heat ends in devouring, and in the absence of prayer.

**Where:** 10.13, one clause added.

**Warmth that does not come (Hag 1:5–6, 14).** *"Consider your ways. You have sown much, and harvested little. You eat, but you never have enough; you drink, but you never have your fill. You clothe yourselves, but no one is warm."* "Consider your ways" is literally "set your heart" (Ch 4).
- Effort does not satisfy while *"this house lies in ruins"* (1:4).
- In the same chapter: *"the Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel … and they came and worked on the house"* (1:14, H5782, §4).
- **I read** Haggai 1 in two halves. Wanting for oneself is never satisfied. Then the spirit is stirred toward God's house. The chapter places them in that order.

**Where:** 10.6 (want not satisfied, beside Ecc 5:10 and Isa 55:2), and Ch 6 (§4).

**Warmth from a fire that also made a god (Isa 44:15–20).** *"He takes a part of it and warms himself … Also he makes a god and worships it … Also he warms himself and says, 'Aha, I am warm, I have seen the fire!'"* (44:15–16). Then: *"they know not, nor do they discern, for he has shut their eyes … and their hearts, so that they cannot understand"* (44:18). Explore the figure: the same log gives comfort and a god. **I read** a satisfied body and a closed heart in one man, side by side. He is pleased with the warmth and does not see what he has done with the rest. 10.7 already cites Isa 44.

**Where:** 10.7 (beside its Isa 44 citation) and Ch 12 *Idols taken inside*.

**No fire to sit before (Isa 47:13–14).** *"You are wearied with your many counsels … No coal for warming oneself is this, no fire to sit before!"* Counsel that cannot save is a fire that gives no warmth.

**Where:** 10.7 *Planning* (counsel that cannot deliver).

**Friends who vanish in the heat (Job 6:15–17).** *"My brothers are treacherous as a torrent-bed … When they melt, they disappear; when it is hot, they vanish from their place."* Explore the figure. A wadi is full in winter, when no one needs it, and dry in summer, when the caravans come (6:18–19). **I read** friends who are there when they are not needed and gone in the heat when they are.

**Where:** 10.10.

**Two kept warm (Ecc 4:9–12).** *"if two lie together, they keep warm, but how can one keep warm alone?"* It comes after *"woe to him who is alone when he falls"* (4:10).

**Where:** 10.10.

**Warming the needy (Job 31:19–20).** This is inside Job's oath of innocence: *"if I have seen anyone perish for lack of clothing … if he was not warmed with the fleece of my sheep"*. The figure is a body that "blessed me" for the warmth. Care for another's body is set among the matters a man swears to before God.

**Where:** 10.10.

**Carried by existing places:**
- 1Ki 1:1–2 (David, old, not warm): Ch 2
- 2Ki 4:34 (the child's flesh warm): Ch 2 *Sleep and waking*, 2Ki 4:31–35
- Jer 51:39 ("While they are inflamed … then sleep a perpetual sleep"): Ch 2, LD-152

**No inner-being bearing (route C):** Exo 16:21 (the sun hot), Neh 7:3, Eze 24:11 (the pot heated).

**Nature (13.4):** *"For she leaves her eggs to the earth and lets them be warmed on the ground, forgetting that a foot may crush them … She deals cruelly with her young … because God has made her forget wisdom"* (Job 39:14–17; 39:17 checked). The ostrich as God describes her to Job.

## 4. Rousing (H5782, #1942: 46 verses; unit 4 §E)

### 4.1 God called to wake, and God calling back

The psalm calls are in 10.9 *Calling on God to wake* as a summary. Read one by one, they rest on different grounds:

| Verse | Ground the call rests on |
|---|---|
| Psa 7:4–6 | Innocence, sworn: "if I have repaid my friend with evil … Arise, O Lord, in your anger … awake for me" |
| Psa 35:23 | A cause: "for my vindication, for my cause" |
| Psa 44:22–23 | Suffering for God's sake: "for your sake we are killed all the day long … Awake!" |
| Psa 59:3–4 | "for no fault of mine" |
| Psa 80:1–2 | His office: "Shepherd of Israel … stir up your might and come to save us" |
| Isa 51:9 | His past: "awake, as in days of old … Was it not you who … dried up the sea" |

Against them, Bildad makes God's rousing conditional: *"if you are pure and upright, surely then he will rouse himself for you"* (Job 8:6). It is a friend's claim, and the book does not leave his counsel standing.

**What happens next in Isaiah.** The call *"Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the Lord"* (51:9) is answered with the same words aimed at the people:
- *"Wake yourself, wake yourself, stand up, O Jerusalem, you who have drunk … the cup of his wrath"* (51:17)
- *"Awake, awake, put on your strength, O Zion"* (52:1)

**I read** that the people's call for God to wake is answered by God calling them to wake. Isaiah's repeated words show this. The interpretation is mine.

The same people cannot rouse themselves: *"There is no one who calls upon your name, who rouses himself to take hold of you; for you have hidden your face from us"* (64:7). Yet the next verse appeals: *"But now, O Lord, you are our Father; we are the clay"* (64:8).

**Where:** 10.9 *Calling on God to wake* (rewritten from the summary), with a pointer to 10.13 (Isa 51:17; 64:7).

### 4.2 God stirs the spirit: toward building, and toward destroying

**Toward building.** *"the Lord stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he made a proclamation"* (Ezr 1:1; 2Ch 36:22). Then *"everyone whose spirit God had stirred to go up"* (Ezr 1:5). Not everyone went: the stirred ones rose. Haggai gives the order:
1. They *"obeyed the voice of the Lord … And the people feared the Lord"* (Hag 1:12).
2. *"I am with you, declares the Lord"* (1:13).
3. *"And the Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel … and the spirit of all the remnant of the people. And they came and worked"* (1:14).

The stirring follows obedience, fear and a word of presence, and it ends in work. Of Cyrus: *"I have stirred him up in righteousness … he shall build my city and set my exiles free, not for price or reward"* (Isa 45:13).

**Toward destroying.**
- *"So the God of Israel stirred up the spirit of Pul king of Assyria"*, after *"they broke faith with the God of their fathers"* (1Ch 5:25–26)
- *"the Lord stirred up against Jehoram the anger of the Philistines"* (2Ch 21:16), after a written warning (21:12–15)
- *"I will stir up the spirit of a destroyer against Babylon"* (Jer 51:1)
- *"The Lord has stirred up the spirit of the kings of the Medes, because his purpose concerning Babylon is to destroy it"* (51:11)

Each time the verses give the ground: broken faith, a king's sin, or his purpose and vengeance for his temple.

**Compare:**
- **The place God acts is the spirit** (Ch 6), whether the outcome is building or destroying.
- **The stirred person still acts as himself.** Cyrus makes a proclamation and writes it down. The Medes *"have no regard for silver and do not delight in gold"* (Isa 13:17), and Cyrus works *"not for price or reward"* (45:13). Both stirred agents are men who cannot be bought. **I read** that God stirs a spirit already suited to the task. The verses do not say whether the stirring makes it so.
- **Desire turned into judgement:** *"I will stir up against you your lovers from whom you turned in disgust"* (Eze 23:22), right after *"Thus you longed for the lewdness of your youth"* (23:21). What was longed for becomes the instrument of judgement. This is the same face as Num 11, already in 10.6: what is wanted against God is given, and becomes the penalty.
- **His people as his weapon:** *"I have bent Judah as my bow; I have made Ephraim its arrow. I will stir up your sons, O Zion"* (Zec 9:13). **Restored:** *"I will stir them up from the place to which you have sold them"* (Joe 3:7). **One who will call on his name:** *"I stirred up one from the north … and he shall call upon my name"* (Isa 41:25; 41:2).

**Where:**
- **Ch 6** *It is stirred, and it is hardened*, widened: both directions, Haggai's order, and the stirred who cannot be bought.
- **13.1, new section *Stirring*:** God acting on the spirits of kings and peoples, and the grounds given.
- **10.6:** Eze 23:21–22, beside Num 11.
- **Ch 11 §4** *Who rouses, and toward what* already names Ezr 1:5 and Jer 51:1, 11. One clause is added for Haggai's order.

### 4.3 The inner being stirred

- **The heart awake while the body sleeps (Song 5:2–4):** *"I slept, but my heart was awake. A sound! My beloved is knocking. 'Open to me …'"* She delays: *"I had put off my garment; how could I put it on? I had bathed my feet; how could I soil them?"* Then *"my heart was thrilled within me"* (5:4). **I read** a wakeful heart held back by the body's comfort. Being awake inside does not by itself open the door.
  - **Where:** Ch 4 *The heart decides and is moved*, and 10.6 (wanting a person).
- **Love not roused before its time (Song 2:7; 3:5; 8:4; 8:5):** *"I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem … that you not stir up or awaken love until it pleases"*.
  - It is said three times, after love-sickness (2:5), after finding and holding (3:4), and after longing (8:1–2). It is addressed to others.
  - "Until it pleases" is the delight word: love has its own time and its own wanting.
  - The one awakening in the book is the beloved's: *"Under the apple tree I awakened you"* (8:5).
  - Straight after: *"love is strong as death, jealousy is fierce as the grave … Many waters cannot quench love … If a man offered for love all the wealth of his house, he would be utterly despised"* (8:6–7).
  - **I read**: not to be roused early or by others; once roused, it cannot be put out and cannot be bought. The Song puts these side by side.
  - **Where:** 10.6 (where Song 8:6 already stands), 10.10 (love), Ch 11 §4 (already has 2:7).
- **Hatred rouses, love covers (Pro 10:12):** *"Hatred stirs up strife, but love covers all offenses."* Explore the figures: one stirs up, the other covers over. Hatred brings things to the surface. Love lays something over them. (Already in Ch 11.)
  - **Where:** 10.10 *Anger between people*.
- **Rousing oneself rightly, and wrongly (Job 17:8; 31:29):**
  - *"The upright are appalled at this, and the innocent stirs himself up against the godless. Yet the righteous holds to his way"* (17:8–9).
  - In his oath: *"If I have rejoiced at the ruin of him who hated me, or exulted when evil overtook him (I have not let my mouth sin by asking for his life with a curse)"* (31:29–30).
  - Job rouses himself against godlessness and disowns rousing himself at an enemy's fall. **Compare:** the same act of rousing oneself, approved in the first and refused in the second. The difference is the object: wrong itself, or a person's ruin.
  - **Where:** 10.10, and Ch 11 §4.
- **The heart roused for war (Dan 11:25):** *"he shall stir up his power and his heart"*. 10.7 *Planning* has 11:24–27. One phrase is added: the heart is stirred as a resource of war.
- **The ear woken each morning (Isa 50:4–5):** *"The Lord God has given me the tongue of those who are taught, that I may know how to sustain with a word him who is weary. Morning by morning he awakens; he awakens my ear to hear as those who are taught. The Lord God has opened my ear, and I was not rebellious"*.
  - The sequence is the verses': the ear woken, then the tongue that sustains. Hearing comes before speaking, and it is daily.
  - *"I was not rebellious"* comes before *"I gave my back to those who strike"* (50:6).
  - **Where:** 10.2 (hearing), and 10.5 (*Speech is heard*).
- **Rousing oneself to praise (Psa 57:6–8; Judg 5:12):**
  - *"my soul was bowed down … My heart is steadfast, O God, my heart is steadfast! I will sing and make melody! Awake, my glory! Awake, O harp and lyre! I will awake the dawn!"*
  - The self calls itself awake, from a bowed soul, through a steadfast heart, and gets ahead of the day.
  - Deborah is called by others: *"Awake, awake, Deborah! … break out in a song!"* (Judg 5:12).
  - **Compare:** rousing oneself, and being roused by others, both to song.
  - **Where:** 10.9, and Ch 11 §5 (voices to the soul: "Awake, my glory").
- **Woken by an angel to see (Zec 4:1–2):** *"the angel who talked with me came again and woke me, like a man who is awakened out of his sleep. And he said to me, 'What do you see?'"*
  - **Where:** 13.2 *Angels* (an angel acting on a person), and 10.2.

### 4.4 Waking in the inner being (H6974, LD-155)

- **Waking only to want more (Pro 23:33–35):** *"Your eyes will see strange things, and your heart utter perverse things … 'They struck me,' you will say, 'but I was not hurt; they beat me, but I did not feel it. When shall I awake? I must have another drink.'"* The first thing he wants on waking is the thing that numbed him. Feeling is gone ("I did not feel it"), and the want is left.
  - **Where:** 10.6 (craving that repeats), and 10.1 (not feeling).
- **A dream of eating (Isa 29:8):** *"As when a hungry man dreams, and behold, he is eating, and awakes with his hunger not satisfied"*. This is the picture for the nations that fight against Zion. In the next verses: *"the Lord has poured out upon you a spirit of deep sleep"* (29:10).
