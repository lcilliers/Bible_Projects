## 1. The faces of the word (#1919, Ch 11 §4)

*ḥāpēṣ* is rendered delight, desire, wish, please, pleasure, favor and purpose in these verses. The rendering follows who delights and in what.

| Face | Verses | What differs |
|---|---|---|
| God's delight, given as a reason | 2Sa 22:20; Isa 42:21; Num 14:8 | a reason for rescue; a reason for magnifying his law; a ground for courage |
| God's delight, refused to a thing offered | Isa 1:11; Psa 40:6; 51:16, 19; Hos 6:6 | what is set in its place: an open ear, a broken heart, lasting love |
| God's delight, quoted by people | Psa 22:8; 41:11; 2Sa 15:26 | a taunt; a thing known by its outcome; a thing left to him |
| A person's delight in God's word | Psa 112:1; 119:16, 35, 47; Rom 7:22 | beside fear, beside not forgetting, beside asking to be led, beside a war in the members |
| A person's delight in God himself | Psa 73:25; Isa 58:14; Job 22:26; 27:10 | after envy; after giving up one's own pleasure; in place of gold; absent, so calling only in distress |
| Delight that looks right and is not | Isa 58:2 | delight to draw near, beside oppression |
| Delight on the wrong thing, or missing | Jer 6:10; Job 21:14; Psa 109:17; 2Sa 24:3 | scorned word; not wanting him for lack of profit; no delight in blessing; a king's delight questioned |
| Delight in a person | Gen 34:19; Est 2:14; Est 6:7–11 | haste into the cost; a king's delight deciding a life; delight imagined for oneself |
| Delight claimed falsely | 1Sa 18:22 | a word of delight that is a snare |
| Wishing | Job 9:3; 13:3; 33:32; Deu 25:7–8 | to contend with God; to justify another; not wanting a duty |
| A ruler's pleasure | Psa 115:3; Ecc 8:3 | the same words of God and of a king |
| A child's delight | Isa 66:11–12; 11:8 | nursing, dandled, playing where there is no harm |

## 2. What God does not delight in, and what he does (13.1 *Delight*)

Five verses each say that God does not delight in sacrifice. Each puts something else in its place.

| Verse | What is not delighted in | What is set beside it |
|---|---|---|
| Isa 1:11–13 | "the blood of bulls"; "I have had enough" | "I cannot endure iniquity and solemn assembly" |
| Psa 40:6–8 | "sacrifice and offering" | "you have given me an open ear"; "I delight to do your will … your law is within my heart" |
| Psa 51:16–17 | "you will not delight in sacrifice, or I would give it" | "a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise" |
| Hos 6:4–6 | "not sacrifice" | "I desire steadfast love", against "Your love is like a morning cloud". Both "love" words are H2617A (verse_lexical) |
| Psa 51:19 | (turned) | "then will you delight in right sacrifices": after the broken heart, the same offerings please him |

- **Probe:** 51:19 shows that the offering itself is not what he refuses. David says he would give it (51:16). The verses do not say God has no use for offerings. They say what he wants first, and every verse names something inward: an ear opened, the law in the heart, a broken heart, love that lasts.
- **Hosea:** what he desires is the very thing their love lacks. Their *ḥesed* passes like dew; he desires *ḥesed*. **I read** that his desire is aimed at the lack.
- **What he delights in:** "steadfast love, justice, and righteousness" (Jer 9:24). The verse before forbids boasting in wisdom, might and riches. The one who may boast boasts "that he understands and knows me". "His delight is not in the strength of the horse, nor his pleasure in the legs of a man" (Psa 147:10), beside "those who fear him" (147:11, already in 10.9). Strength is turned down twice, once as a boast and once as a delight.
- **Aimed at the inner being:** "you delight in truth in the inward being, and you teach me wisdom in the secret heart" (Psa 51:6), said by one "brought forth in iniquity" (51:5). His delight looks for truth inside, and he teaches there. The verse also belongs in Ch 4.

## 3. Whom he delights in, and why (13.1 *Delight*)

- **A reason for rescue:** "he rescued me, because he delighted in me" (2Sa 22:20; Psa 18:19 carried), from enemies "too mighty for me".
- **A name:** "you shall be called My Delight Is in Her … for the Lord delights in you" (Isa 62:4), in place of Forsaken and Desolate, "as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride" (62:5).
- **A man's steps:** "The steps of a man are established by the Lord, when he delights in his way; though he fall, he shall not be cast headlong" (Psa 37:23–24). His delight does not stop a fall; it stops the fall from being final.
- **Read by a stranger:** the queen of Sheba, who "did not believe the reports until I came and my own eyes had seen it", says "Blessed be the Lord your God, who has delighted in you … that you may execute justice and righteousness" (1Ki 10:7, 9; 2Ch 9:8 carried). She reads God's delight from what she saw, and names what it is for.

## 4. He does what pleases him (13.1)

- "Our God is in the heavens; he does all that he pleases" (Psa 115:3), answering "Where is their God?" and set against idols that "have mouths, but do not speak".
- "The Lord was pleased, for his righteousness' sake, to magnify his law" (Isa 42:21). The verses around it speak of a servant who "sees many things, but does not observe them" and a people with "none to say, Restore!". His pleasure has a reason, and the people around it do not see.
- **Purposing:** "it shall accomplish that which I purpose" (Isa 55:11). The word is the delight word. His word carries out his delight and does not return empty.
- **The same words of a king:** "he does whatever he pleases" (Ecc 8:3), as a reason not to be hasty before him. **I read** the two set side by side as one form of words, used once of God and once of a man with power. It goes to 10.10, and to the kings who "did as he pleased" in Daniel, which are read with group C.

## 5. God's delight in people's mouths (10.9)

- **As courage:** "If the Lord delights in us, he will bring us into this land … do not fear the people of the land" (Num 14:8–9). The congregation answered by reaching for stones (14:10). Their courage rested on his delight, not on how big the people were.
- **As something known:** "By this I know that you delight in me: my enemy will not shout in triumph over me" (Psa 41:11), from a man whose "close friend … has lifted his heel against me" (41:9). He learns of the delight from what did not happen.
- **As a taunt:** "let him rescue him, for he delights in him!" (Psa 22:8). The words that are thanks in Psa 18:19 are mockery here. He does not answer the mockers. He answers God: "Yet you are he who took me from the womb" (22:9). **Change of character:** the same claim has opposite values in two mouths.
- **Not presumed:** "if he says, I have no pleasure in you, behold, here I am, let him do to me what seems good to him" (2Sa 15:26). David sends the ark back. He does not hold God to a delight; he puts himself in it.

## 6. A person's delight on God's word and on God (10.6)

- **With fear:** "Blessed is the man who fears the Lord, who greatly delights in his commandments!" (Psa 112:1). Fear and delight are said of one man in one breath (10.12).
- **Still asking to be led:** "Lead me in the path of your commandments, for I delight in it. Incline my heart to your testimonies, and not to selfish gain!" (Psa 119:35–36). He gives his delight as the reason to be led. In the next breath he asks for his heart to be inclined. **I read** that delight does not make a man self-steering.
- **With remembering:** "I will delight in your statutes; I will not forget your word" (119:16), "as much as in all riches" (119:14). And "I find my delight in your commandments, which I love" (119:47).
- **Inward, and at war:** "I delight in the law of God, in my inner being, but I see in my members another law waging war against the law of my mind" (Rom 7:22–23). The delight is real and it is inner, and it does not carry the members. Ch 7 has 7:25, and Ch 2 has 7:24.
- **On God himself, after envy:** "there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you. My flesh and my heart may fail" (Psa 73:25–26), from the man who "was envious of the arrogant" (73:3).
- **After one's own pleasure is given up:** "If you turn back your foot … from doing your pleasure on my holy day … then you shall take delight in the Lord" (Isa 58:13–14). Delight in him comes after the turning.
- **In place of gold, on a condition:** "if you lay gold in the dust … then the Almighty will be your gold … then you will delight yourself in the Almighty and lift up your face to God" (Job 22:24–26). It is Eliphaz speaking, and he offers it to Job as a condition.
- **Missing, so calling only in distress:** "Will God hear his cry when distress comes upon him? Will he take delight in the Almighty? Will he call upon God at all times?" (Job 27:9–10). **I read** the questions as setting the cry in distress against calling at all times. Delight is what makes the difference.
- **The meek:** "delight themselves in abundant peace" (Psa 37:11), in the psalm whose 37:4 opens 10.6.
- **As a child's:** "that you may nurse and be satisfied from her consoling breast; that you may drink deeply with delight … you shall be carried upon her hip, and bounced upon her knees. As one whom his mother comforts, so I will comfort you" (Isa 66:11–13). The figure is a child's delight at being fed and dandled. The comforting in the last line is God's own, as a mother's. **I read** the delight as one that is received, not worked for. It goes to *Thirst answered*, with a note to 13.1.

## 7. Delight that looks right, on the wrong thing, or missing (10.6 *Delight*)

- **Looks right:** "they seek me daily and delight to know my ways, as if they were a nation that did righteousness … they delight to draw near to God" (Isa 58:2). The next verse, already in 10.6, is "you seek your own pleasure, and oppress all your workers" (58:3). The delight is real enough to be named twice. **I read** that delight in religion can sit beside oppression without either one cancelling the other in the person's own eyes.
- **Scorned word:** "the word of the Lord is to them an object of scorn; they take no pleasure in it", with ears that "cannot listen" (Jer 6:10). The prophet is set beside them: "I am full of the wrath of the Lord; I am weary of holding it in" (6:11). This also goes to 10.2.
- **Not wanting him, by reckoning:** "Depart from us! We do not desire the knowledge of your ways. What is the Almighty, that we should serve him? And what profit do we get if we pray to him?" (Job 21:14–15), said by those who "spend their days in prosperity" (21:13).
- **No delight in blessing:** "He loved to curse … He did not delight in blessing" (Psa 109:17), from one who "did not remember to show kindness" (109:16). The cursing soaks in "like oil into his bones" (109:18). **I read** the figure as what a man delights in becoming his own substance. This also goes to 10.5.
- **Questioned:** "why does my lord the king delight in this thing?" (2Sa 24:3). The king's word prevailed (24:4). Afterwards "David's heart struck him" (24:10, Ch 8). The delight was questioned by another person, overruled, and then judged by his own heart.

## 8. Delight in a person (10.6 *A person*; 10.10; 10.11)

- **Haste into the cost:** "the young man did not delay to do the thing, because he delighted in Jacob's daughter" (Gen 34:19). The thing was the circumcision the brothers had asked "deceitfully" (34:13). 10.6 has 34:2 and 34:8. The delight that seized her (34:2) now hurries him into a trap.
- **A king's delight decides a life:** "She would not go in to the king again, unless the king delighted in her and she was summoned by name" (Est 2:14). Beside it, each woman was given "whatever she desired" (2:13), and Esther "asked for nothing except what Hegai … advised" (2:15). It goes to 10.10.
- **Imagined for oneself:** Haman spells out the honour he thinks is his: the robes the king has worn, the king's horse, a crown, and a proclamation (Est 6:7–9). Then he is told "do so to Mordecai" (6:10), proclaims it himself (6:11), and goes home "mourning and with his head covered" (6:12). 10.6 has 6:6 ("Whom would the king delight to honor more than me?"). **I read** that the honour he designed for his own wanting became the measure of his shame. It widens *Delight can be misread*.
- **Claimed falsely:** "Speak to David in private and say, Behold, the king has delight in you" (1Sa 18:22), while Saul thought "that she may be a snare for him" (18:21). It goes to 10.11, beside the other false words.

## 9. Wishing (10.9, 10.10)

- **To contend with God:** "If one wished to contend with him, one could not answer him once in a thousand times" (Job 9:3). Four chapters later he says "I desire to argue my case with God", and turns from the friends: "worthless physicians are you all" (13:3–4). **Change of character:** the wish first judged hopeless is later declared. 10.5 has Job 9:34–35.
- **To justify another:** "speak, for I desire to justify you" (Job 33:32), Elihu, "to bring back his soul from the pit" (33:30).
- **Not wanting a duty:** "if the man does not wish to take his brother's wife … if he persists, saying, I do not wish to take her" (Deu 25:7–8). Then the gate, the sandal, the spit, and the name "The house of him who had his sandal pulled off" (25:9–10). The law does not force the wanting. It makes the not-wanting public and gives it a name.
- **Favour demanded:** "Whoever favors Joab, and whoever is for David, let him follow Joab" (2Sa 20:11), called out beside Amasa, just killed. The people "stopped" at the sight, and went on once the body was covered (20:12–13). **I read** that the sight held them back and the covering let them go. It goes to 10.10.

## 10. Nature (13.4)

- "The nursing child shall play over the hole of the cobra, and the weaned child shall put his hand on the adder's den. They shall not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain" (Isa 11:8–9). The delight word is a child's play, where there is no more harm.

## 11. Route C (1)

- Jer 6:2, "The lovely and delicately bred I will destroy". The word describes Zion. No inner act.
