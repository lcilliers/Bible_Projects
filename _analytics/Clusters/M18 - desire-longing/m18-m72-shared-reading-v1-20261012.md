## 3. Read: the 6 where the M18 word carries the account

### 3.1 "If it pleases the king": a request put under another's pleasure (Neh 2:5, 7; Est 1:19; 5:4; 9:13)

10.7 *Whose measure* already asks in whose eyes a choice is good, and has "The king's eyes, or God's" (1Sa 14:36) and "Another's will, given the whole decision" (Est 3:9, 11; 8:8). These five verses are the same words from the asking side. In each, *ṭôb* names the king's own judgement: literally "if it is good to the king". The asker puts the request under the king's pleasure. The roles: the M18 word is the **measure**; "king" (M72) is **standalone**, the one whose pleasure decides. The verses differ in what stands behind the ask.

**Neh 2:5, 7.** *"If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor in your sight, that you send me to Judah … that I may rebuild it."* Before he speaks, *"So I prayed to the God of heaven"* (2:4). After the grant: *"And the king granted me what I asked, for the good hand of my God was upon me"* (2:8). His prayer in 1:11 asked for *"mercy in the sight of this man"*.
- **Reading:** the request is put under the king's pleasure, and the outcome is named as God's hand. The passage states both. Two measures stand in one request, the king's and God's, and Nehemiah names God's as the reason the king's went his way.
- **I read:** the king's pleasure is not set against God's here, as it is with Saul's people (1Sa 14:36). It is the place where God's hand is seen.

**Est 1:19.** *"If it please the king, let a royal order go out … and let the king give her royal position to another who is better than she."* Memucan's ground is fear of what others will do: the women will *"look at their husbands with contempt"* (1:17), *"and there will be contempt and wrath in plenty"* (1:18). *"This advice pleased the king and the princes"* (1:21).
- **Reading:** the counsel is put under the king's pleasure, but what drives it is the fear of contempt spreading. "Better" (also *ṭôb*) here judges a woman by whether she comes when called.

**Est 5:4; 9:13.** *"If it please the king, let the king and Haman come today to a feast."* The king has already said, *"What is your request? It shall be given you, even to the half of my kingdom"* (5:3). At 9:12 he asks again, and she asks again under his pleasure (9:13), and *"the king commanded this to be done"* (9:14).
- **Reading:** the form is kept even when the king has offered everything. Esther does not take the offer as leave to stop asking under his pleasure. Her first request is small (a feast); the real one waits (7:3).
- **I read:** the form keeps the decision with the king each time, after he has offered to give it away.

**Faces of the M18 word (#1919):** in 10.7 the measure was the chooser's own eyes, the Lord's, the people's around, the king's. Here it is the measure an asker hands to the one above. What differs is who is asking and what stands behind it: prayer (Nehemiah), fear of contempt (Memucan), silver (Haman, 3:9, already placed), a life at stake (Esther).

**Home:** 10.7 *Whose measure*, as one more bullet, "The king's pleasure, asked for", pointing to 10.9 for Nehemiah's prayer and God's good hand.

### 3.2 Delight in the one they demanded (Mal 3:1)

*"And the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple; and the messenger of the covenant in whom you delight, behold, he is coming, says the Lord of hosts."* The verse answers 2:17: *"You have wearied the Lord with your words … by saying, 'Everyone who does evil is good in the sight of the Lord, and he delights in them.' Or by asking, 'Where is the God of justice?'"* It is followed by *"But who can endure the day of his coming, and who can stand when he appears? For he is like a refiner's fire"* (3:2).
- **Roles:** "delight" (M18) is a characteristic of the people. "Seek" (M83) is paired with it. "Lord of hosts" (M72) names the speaker. "Covenant" (M32) names the messenger's office.
- **Reading:** the people said God delights in evildoers (2:17). God answers with their own word: the one *"in whom you delight"* is coming. Their delight and their seeking are taken at their word, and then the question is who can stand. They asked for justice; justice comes as a refiner's fire.
- **I read:** the delight is real enough to be named, but it is delight in what they have not yet faced. The same pattern of words stands in Isa 58:2 (*"they delight to draw near to God"*), already in 10.7, where the delight measures an eagerness that is not what God chooses. Both verses say this in their own words; the comparison is mine.
- **Route:** A and B. The coming and the refining are God's (13.1); the delight and the seeking are the people's.
- **Home:** 10.7, beside Isa 58:2 (*Others around a choice*, "An inner act weighed by what God chooses"). 13.1 takes the refiner who comes.

## 5. No carrying M-code: read by the act (8)

**2Sa 11:2.** *"he saw from the roof a woman bathing; and the woman was very beautiful."* Then *"David sent and inquired about the woman"* (11:3), and *"So David sent messengers and took her"* (11:4). It is the spring, *"the time when kings go out to battle … But David remained at Jerusalem"* (11:1).
- **Roles:** "beautiful" (M18) qualifies the one seen. "King's house" (M72) is the setting. The acts, seeing, sending and taking, carry no M-code in these verses.
- **Reading:** the passage names no desire. It gives the acts in order: he saw, he inquired, he sent, he took. The order is the one 10.6 *Coveting* states in Achan's own words (Jos 7:21). Here the sending is done by a king: messengers go and bring her. Micah's woe in 10.6 names this: they covet and take *"because it is in the power of their hand"* (Mic 2:1–2).
- **I read:** the king's power closes the gap between the look and the taking. Nathan's charge later names what David did as despising *"the word of the Lord"* (2Sa 12:9).
- **Home:** 10.6 *Coveting*, after Achan, with the point that the verses name no desire.

**2Sa 18:27.** *"And the king said, 'He is a good man and comes with good news.'"* His next words are *"Is it well with the young man Absalom?"* (18:29).
- **Reading:** the king reads the runner's identity as a sign of good news. What he wants to hear is about Absalom.
- **I read:** wanting colours how a sign is read before the news is heard. **Home:** 10.6 *Wanting speaks, and drives thinking*, and it points to 2Sa 18:33, already placed.

**2Ki 20:13; Isa 39:2.** *"And Hezekiah welcomed them gladly. And he showed them … all that was found in his storehouses. There was nothing in his house or in all his realm that Hezekiah did not show them"* (Isa 39:2).
- **Roles:** "precious" (M18) qualifies the oil. "Realm" (M72) is how far the showing went. "Welcomed" (M41) and "gladly" (M04) qualify the welcome. The act the passage is about, showing everything, has no M-code.
- **Reading:** Isaiah asks *"What have they seen in your house?"*, and Hezekiah answers *"all"* (39:4). The word against it follows: all of it will be carried to Babylon (39:6).
- **Pointer, kept separate:** 2Ch 32:31 says of these envoys that *"God left him to himself, in order to test him and to know all that was in his heart"*.
- **Home:** 10.11 *Hiding and disclosing*: disclosure with no hiding, and what it showed.

**Jer 44:17.** *"For then we had plenty of food, and prospered, and saw no disaster."* This comes after *"we will not listen to you"* (44:16), and is followed by *"since we left off making offerings to the queen of heaven … we have lacked everything"* (44:18).
- **Reading:** they read their past prosperity as the result of the offerings, and their loss as the result of stopping. The vow is held on that reasoning.
- **I read:** the same ruin that 10.13 *Seen and not taken in* says they saw (44:2) is here read the other way.
- **Home:** 10.2, beside the refusal to hear (44:16), pointing to 10.13.

**Gen 40:16.** *"When the chief baker saw that the interpretation was favorable, he said to Joseph, 'I also had a dream'."*
- **Reading:** he speaks because he has seen the other man's outcome. His own is death (40:19). **Home:** 10.5 (what moves a person to speak).

**1Ki 2:38.** Shimei: *"What you say is good; as my lord the king has said, so will your servant do."* Three years later he goes, and Solomon asks, *"Why then have you not kept your oath to the Lord?"* (2:43).
- **Reading:** "good" is his assent. The passage calls it an oath to the Lord. **Home:** 10.5 *The oath*.

**Amo 6:2.** *"Are you better than these kingdoms?"*, put to those *"at ease in Zion"* (6:1), who *"put far away the day of disaster"* (6:3).
- **Reading:** the comparison is the prophet's question to their ease. **Home:** 10.4, beside Amos 6:1.

## 8. What the pairing shows

These points come from the 51 only, and are not counted beyond them.
- In these verses M72 is mostly a person, the one who decides. The M18 word then measures that person's judgement ("if it pleases the king"), or the speech wanted from others (1Ki 22).
- Where M72 carries the act, it is God's: his hand, his reign, a kingdom given and taken. Those verses go forward to M72.
- Two verses show power carrying out what the eye or the will sets on: David (2Sa 11) and Laban's restraint (Gen 31:29, forward).
