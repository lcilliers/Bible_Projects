# M18 × M02 read-back: the additions read for the inner being's workings

**Date:** 2026-10-09 · **Escalation:** #1993 (handoff item C; OT-19) · **Register:** `m18-m02-validation-register-v1-20261009.csv` · **Worksheet:** `m18-m02-validation-worksheet-v1-20261009.md`

**Method:** the same as the M25 reading. The anger ledgers (units 1–5, the cross-ledger overview v2) were the starting point. Each verse is read in its passage. The probing asks what the inner being does, compared with what, why it differs, what lies behind it, whether the relationship changes it, and what the figure pictures. Readings beyond the text are marked **I read**. Wherever the anger ledger says that no verse names envy (F.6), envy is not read in.

**Scope:**
- 51 verses: the 59 M18 × M02 verses, less 5 done with M47 and 3 done with M25.
- Together with those 8, this answers **OT-19** (the 59 verses face by face).

---

## 1. God's jealousy (13.1 *Jealousy*, widened)

The anger ledgers already hold God's jealousy and anger side by side (AG-04, AG-73–76). Read for the jealousy word, the verses say more about what jealousy is.

- **It answers a bond given to another.**
  - *"They stirred him to jealousy with strange gods; with abominations they provoked him to anger"* (Deu 32:16). Before it: *"Jeshurun grew fat, and kicked; you grew fat, stout, and sleek; then he forsook God who made him"* (32:15). After it: *"You were unmindful of the Rock that bore you"* (32:18).
  - *"they moved him to jealousy with their idols"* (Psa 78:58).
  - **I read**: jealousy in God is roused where the bond he made is given to another. The verses name a fullness that kicked, and a forgetting.
- **It is answered in kind.** *"They have made me jealous with what is no god … So I will make them jealous with those who are no people"* (Deu 32:21). The measure of the answer is the offence itself, "no god" for "no people". Paul takes it up (Rom 10:19, in the anger ledger AG-217).
- **Against his own, as against an unfaithful wife.**
  - *"I will judge you as women who commit adultery and shed blood are judged, and bring upon you the blood of wrath and jealousy"* (Eze 16:38).
  - *"I will direct my jealousy against you, that they may deal with you in fury"* (23:25). It is carried by *"your lovers"*, the ones she desired (23:22, 10.6, 13.1 *Stirring*).
- **Against one man who blesses himself.** *"the anger of the Lord and his jealousy will smoke against that man"* (Deu 29:20). He is the one who *"blesses himself in his heart, saying, 'I shall be safe'"* (29:19). The inner act of self-assurance is what the jealousy smokes against. (13.1 *Willing* already has 29:20 for "not willing to forgive".)
- **For Zion.** *"I am jealous for Zion with great jealousy, and I am jealous for her with great wrath"* (Zec 8:2). The next verse is *"I have returned to Zion and will dwell in the midst of Jerusalem"* (8:3). The jealousy for her ends in his dwelling with her.
- **Over the whole earth, and then a pure speech.** *"in the fire of my jealousy all the earth shall be consumed"* (Zep 3:8). Then: *"For at that time I will change the speech of the peoples to a pure speech, that all of them may call upon the name of the Lord"* (3:9). The passage puts the fire and the pure speech side by side. In Zep 1:18, *"Neither their silver nor their gold shall be able to deliver them … In the fire of his jealousy, all the earth shall be consumed"*.
- **Creation shaken.** *"For in my jealousy and in my blazing wrath I declare, On that day there shall be a great earthquake … all the people who are on the face of the earth, shall quake at my presence"* (Eze 38:19–20).
- **Jealous, and slow to anger, side by side.** *"The Lord is a jealous and avenging God … and keeps wrath for his enemies. The Lord is slow to anger and great in power"* (Nah 1:2–3). The verses put both in one breath. I do not reconcile them.
- **It ends, and something follows.**
  - *"So will I satisfy my wrath on you, and my jealousy shall depart from you. I will be calm and will no more be angry"* (Eze 16:42). The next verse names what had enraged him: *"Because you have not remembered the days of your youth"* (16:43).
  - *"And they shall know that I am the Lord—that I have spoken in my jealousy"* (Eze 5:13). The stated end is knowing.
- **Asked about in lament.** *"How long, O Lord? Will you be angry forever? Will your jealousy burn like fire? Pour out your anger on the nations that do not know you"* (Psa 79:5–6). **I read** a people asking for the jealousy to end on them and to turn on others. The ledger reads the same (AG-88).

**Compare** this with what was woven earlier today from Ezekiel 36 and James 4. **I read** one jealousy, aimed in different ways:
- against his people when they give the bond to another
- for them when others scorn them
- over them, met with "more grace" (Jam 4:6)

## 2. A person who shares God's jealousy (10.6 *Jealousy and envy*)

*"Phinehas … has turned back my wrath from the people of Israel, in that he was jealous with my jealousy among them"*. He is given *"my covenant of peace"*, *"because he was jealous for his God and made atonement for the people of Israel"* (Num 25:11–13). 10.9 has it.

**Compare Elijah.** Both are "jealous for" God:
- Phinehas's jealousy is God's own ("with my jealousy"). It turns the wrath back and is met with a covenant of peace.
- Elijah's comes out as *"I, even I only, am left"* (1Ki 19:10). It is met with work and with "seven thousand" (10.6).

**I read**: the same jealousy for God can stand with the people and turn wrath from them, or end in feeling alone.

## 3. Envy of the wicked, and the future (10.6 *Jealousy and envy*)

*"Fret not yourself because of evildoers; be not envious of wrongdoers! For they will soon fade like the grass"*, and then *"Trust in the Lord, and do good … Delight yourself in the Lord … Commit your way to the Lord … Be still before the Lord and wait patiently for him"* (Psa 37:1–7).

- "Fret" is the burning verb, *heat yourself* (10.1). Envy and the inner heat stand together in the first line.
- The answer comes in two parts. One is what will happen to the envied: they "fade". The other is a run of acts toward God: trust, delight, commit, be still, wait.
- **Delight** is among them. Envy wants what another has. The answer gives delight a new object.

Proverbs has the same pairing twice, and both times it answers with the future:
- *"Fret not yourself because of evildoers, and be not envious of the wicked, for the evil man has no future"* (Pro 24:19–20)
- *"Surely there is a future, and your hope will not be cut off"* (23:18, already in 10.6)

Just before 24:19 stands *"Do not rejoice when your enemy falls, and let not your heart be glad when he stumbles"* (24:17). Envy of the wicked who prosper, and gladness when an enemy falls, are set two verses apart. Job disowns the second in his oath (10.10).

**Not envy.** *"The wicked man sees it and is angry; he gnashes his teeth and melts away; the desire of the wicked will perish!"* (Psa 112:10). What he sees is the righteous man's honour (112:9). The anger ledger records that no verse names envy here (F.6). So it goes under *Evil desire*: where the desire of the wicked ends.

## 4. Jealousy as not yet grown (10.6 *Jealousy and envy*)

- *"But I, brothers, could not address you as spiritual people, but as people of the flesh, as infants in Christ. I fed you with milk … for while there is jealousy and strife among you, are you not of the flesh and behaving only in a human way? For when one says, 'I follow Paul,' and another, 'I follow Apollos'"* (1Co 3:1–4).
  - The figure is infancy. Jealousy is named as the sign of not having grown. It shows in attaching oneself to a favourite teacher.
  - **Compare** 1Pe 2:2, already in 10.6: *"Like newborn infants, long for the pure spiritual milk, that by it you may grow up"*. One infancy longs for milk in order to grow. The other stays on milk and quarrels.
- *"The night is far gone … Let us walk properly as in the daytime … not in quarreling and jealousy. But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to gratify its desires"* (Rom 13:12–14). Before it: *"the hour has come for you to wake from sleep"* (13:11).
  - The figure is waking at daybreak. Jealousy belongs to the night.
  - The answer is a garment, "put on", not a stronger will. It goes with refusing to make "provision" for the flesh's desires.

## 5. Where a desire ends (10.6 *Evil desire*)

*"The desire of the righteous ends only in good, the expectation of the wicked in wrath"* (Pro 11:23). With Psa 112:10, "the desire of the wicked will perish!". **I read** the proverbs judging a desire by where it ends.

## 6. What is desired, taken by God (10.6 *Taken away*)

*"He has bent his bow like an enemy … and he has killed all who were delightful in our eyes"* (Lam 2:4). *"The Lord has become like an enemy"* (2:5).
- **Compare** Eze 24:25, already in 10.6, where God foretells it. In Lamentations it is mourned after the fact, and the one who took it is named "like an enemy".

## 7. Whose measure (10.7 *Whose measure*)

*"But Naaman was angry and went away, saying, 'Behold, I thought that he would surely come out to me …' Are not Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel?"* (2Ki 5:11–12).
- His own measure ("better") and his own picture of how it should go ("I thought") are set against the prophet's word.
- His servants answer with a question: *"My father, it is a great word the prophet has spoken to you; will you not do it? Has he actually said to you, 'Wash, and be clean'?"* (5:13). His *"flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child"* (5:14).

**I read** a measure of "better" that nearly lost him the cure, and a question that let him lay it down.

## 8. The will (10.7 *Willing*; 13.1 *Willing*)

- **The will that stays outside.** *"But he was angry and refused to go in. His father came out and entreated him"* (Luk 15:28). The verb is the will word. The older brother's anger takes the form of a will that refuses to go in. The father does not wait inside; he comes out. 10.13 has the anger. 10.7 gets the will.
- **The absolute will with a set end.** *"And the king shall do as he wills. He shall exalt himself and magnify himself above every god … He shall prosper till the indignation is accomplished; for what is decreed shall be done"* (Dan 11:36). **Compare** Nebuchadnezzar, already in 10.7: *"Whom he would, he killed"*, until his heart was lifted up and he was brought down (Dan 5:19–20). In Daniel 11 the absolute will runs until a time already set.
- **God's desire, held in patience.** *"What if God, desiring to show his wrath and to make known his power, has endured with much patience vessels of wrath prepared for destruction, in order to make known the riches of his glory for vessels of mercy"* (Rom 9:22–23). Desire, wrath and patience stand in one question, and it is a question ("What if"). 13.1 already has 9:18–20.

## 9. Favour (13.1 *Delight*; 10.10)

- *"He does not retain his anger forever, because he delights in steadfast love"* (Mic 7:18). The reason his anger ends is what he delights in.
- *"for in my wrath I struck you, but in my favor I have had mercy on you"* (Isa 60:10). Wrath and favour are named in one sentence, with mercy coming from the favour. Compare *"his favor is for a lifetime"* (Psa 30:5, in 10.13).
- **A ruler's favour has a ground.** *"A servant who deals wisely has the king's favor, but his wrath falls on one who acts shamefully"* (Pro 14:35), beside *"his favor is like dew on the grass"* (Pro 19:12, in 10.10).

## 10. Two wishes, and fear (10.10)

*"For I fear that perhaps when I come I may find you not as I wish, and that you may find me not as you wish — that perhaps there may be quarreling, jealousy, anger"* (2Co 12:20).
- Each side holds a wish about the other.
- Paul fears that both will be disappointed, and he names what disappointment would bring.

**I read** expectation between people as the ground on which quarrel and jealousy grow, said by the one who fears it.

---

## 11. Zeal passed on (Ch 11 §6)

*"I know your readiness, of which I boast about you to the people of Macedonia … And your zeal has stirred up most of them"* (2Co 9:2). The zeal of one church stirs another. The stirring verb is the one used of fathers provoking children (Col 3:21; anger ledger AG-239). Here it is aimed at good.

---

## 12. Weight, and rash words (10.5)

*"Oh that my vexation were weighed, and all my calamity laid in the balances! For then it would be heavier than the sand of the sea; therefore my words have been rash"* (Job 6:2–3). Job measures his inner weight and gives it as the reason for his words. **Compare** Psalm 39, woven today: silence held until the heart burned, then speech turned to God. Here the weight is given as the reason the words came out rash.

---

## 13. Error realigned (10.1)

10.1 said *"Grief makes it tender; jealousy makes it hard"* as if the verses said it. They do not say so. Mark 3:5 joins anger with grief. Pro 6:34–35 joins it with jealousy and says the fury refuses every gift. The comparison is mine, so it is restated as a reading.
