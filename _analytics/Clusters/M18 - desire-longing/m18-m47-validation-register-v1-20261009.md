## 3. (a) Why the verses were not narrated

There is one main reason. **The M47 reset narrated the seat words (heart, soul, spirit, flesh) and left the desire side of each verse to a later strand.** This is that strand. So 10.6 *Wanting* was short (38 lines). It had nothing on:
- desire in the heart through the eye (Mat 5:28; Pro 6:25)
- delight and its objects (Psa 119:70; Isa 55:2; Pro 18:2; Psa 40:14)
- the wilderness craving (Num 11)
- evil desire (Pro 21:10; Psa 10:3; Mic 7:3)
- longing for people (1Th 2:17; Rom 1:11)
- what is desired being taken away (Eze 24:21, 25)

Ch 5 had no account of a life held precious (1Sa 26:21; 2Ki 1:13–14; Pro 6:26; Psa 49:7–8).

Two smaller reasons:
- **Batch C set some verses aside as idioms when they were not.** Gal 1:16 was filed as a "flesh" idiom, but the M18 word there is God's own pleasure ("he … was pleased to reveal his Son").
- **Ch 11 cited around a verse.** It cites "Psalm 10:2, 4" and skips 10:3, "the wicked boasts of the desires of his soul".

## 4. (b) New perspectives, validated and added

- **God's delight is a phenomenon of its own. 13.1 now has a section *Delight*.** It covers:
  - what he delights in: blameless ways, uprightness, a purpose in a heart he does not let be carried out, a people precious in his eyes
  - whom he is pleased with: the Son at the baptism, where the Spirit comes down in the same verse
  - that he does what pleases him: Pro 21:1; Job 23:13; Jon 1:14; Isa 53:10

  The verses for it were in batch C §1.12 and in the 51 verses outside batch C. None of them had been narrated.
- **Ecclesiastes answers its own vanity.** 10.6 had only the "striving after wind". The same book names envy as the source of toil (4:4), sets "a handful of quietness" against it (4:6), and names enjoyment as "from the hand of God" (2:24–26). Ch 11 §2 gains the merry heart that God approves (Ecc 9:7). Until now the merry heart was shown only as a threshold of danger.
- **Delight can end, and the law guards the other.** "you shall let her go where she wants", where "where she wants" is her soul (Deu 21:14).
- **The soul's want before God is allowed, with rejoicing** (Deu 14:26). The same idiom is used for the reach of a rule (1Ki 11:37).

## 5. (c) Errors realigned

1. **Ch 5, "exhaustion".** Ch 5 v3 called Job 10:1, Ecc 2:17 and Jon 4:3 "a third kind of hating one's life, which is exhaustion rather than discipleship". The verses give their own ground:
   - Job: bitterness of soul
   - the Preacher: "what is done under the sun was grievous to me"
   - Jonah: anger that God spared Nineveh (4:1–4); faintness comes only with the second wish (4:8)

   The frame came from M47 batch G §6. Ch 5 now gives each ground. Ch 2 already read Jonah correctly.
2. **Ch 4, Pro 21:1.** It was listed among the "materials" for the state a heart is in. It is a heart being directed, "wherever he will", and "will" is the delight word. It is now set apart in Ch 4 and placed in 13.1 *Delight*.

## 6. Held, and why

- **OT-95, jealousy (8 verses).** Elijah's zeal (1Ki 19:10, 14), God's jealousy (Eze 36:5), heart envy (Pro 23:17; Jam 3:14), Jam 4:5, Num 11:29 and Eze 8:3. Jealousy has faces that differ by whose it is (God's, a husband's, envy of sinners). It needs one account, from the jealousy words, rather than pieces now.
- **OT-96, generic *ṭôb* (10 verses).** These wait for your decision on the delight-filter buckets. Four of them are said of God and would go to 13.1 if kept.
