## 1. The triage: where the M18 word stands in each verse

For each verse, every M-coded word was listed. The M18 word was then given its role against the verse's main inner-being operation (guide §4.2).

| Decision | Verses | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **read** | 24 | The M18 word carries the verse's main operation (role: characteristic). It is read in depth below (§2). |
| **forward** | 38 | The M18 word qualifies another characteristic whose M-code is in the verse. The reading is marked forward to that cluster (§3). |
| **forward-carried** | 5 | As forward. The verse is already placed in the narrative by its operation. |
| **no-carrier** | 15 | The M18 word qualifies an act that has **no M-code in the verse**, so no cluster reading will bring it back. This needs your direction (§4). |
| **data** | 12 | The M18 word has no inner-being sense here, for example "every matter", a stone, a feast day or a trade term (§5). |

What the 94 show about the word: the noun *ḥēpheṣ* (H2656) is read as "delight" but often means "purpose", "matter" or "what one wants" in a trade. The adjective *ṭôb* (H2896A) is mostly the other party's judgement ("what seems good to you"). Where the word does carry the verse, it is delight in God's word, delight in people, God's own pleasure or purpose, or the enjoyment of life.

## 2. Read in depth: the 24 verses where the M18 word is central

Each verse was read in its passage. Readings beyond what a verse states are marked **"I read"**. The proposed home follows the main operation. Nothing is woven.

### 2.1 Delight in God's word, held under pressure (Psa 1:2; 119:24, 92, 143; 40:8)

**Psa 1:2.** *"but his delight is in the law of the Lord, and on his law he meditates day and night."*
- **Roles:** delight (M18) is the characteristic. Law (M54) is its object. Meditating (M84) is a paired act.
- **Reading:** the "but" sets this delight against the counsel, the way and the seat of 1:1. Then the tree (1:3) gives the figure of what follows. The verse joins delight and meditation in one breath: what is delighted in is turned over "day and night".
- **I read:** the psalm names the man by what he delights in, more than by what he avoids.
- **Placement now:** cited in 10.4 and 10.9, but not in 10.1 *Delight*, where delight in the law is the account. Missed.
- **Home:** 10.1 *On God's word, and on God*, as a new bullet, "Beside meditating". 10.4 keeps the meditating.

**Psa 119:24.** *"Your testimonies are my delight; they are my counselors."* The verse before reads: *"Even though princes sit plotting against me, your servant will meditate on your statutes"* (119:23).
- **Roles:** delight is the characteristic. Counselors (M16) is the part the testimonies play for him.
- **Reading:** the delight is held while he is plotted against. What he delights in also serves him as counsel, in place of the princes' counsel against him.

**Psa 119:92.** *"If your law had not been my delight, I would have perished in my affliction."* It is followed by *"I will never forget your precepts, for by them you have given me life"* (119:93).
- **Reading:** here delight is what kept him alive. He does not say the law kept him; he says his delight in it did. The next verse turns to not forgetting, and to life given (10.3; Ch 2).

**Psa 119:143.** *"Trouble and anguish have found me out, but your commandments are my delight."* It is followed by *"give me understanding that I may live"* (119:144).
- **Roles:** distress (M24) is present beside the delight. The delight does not remove it.

**The three Psalm 119 verses together:**
- **Faces (#1919):** the same delight is held under plotting (v24), in affliction (v92) and in anguish (v143).
- 10.1 already carries 119:75, 77 ("against his own pain").
- **Home:** 10.1 *On God's word, and on God*, widening the existing affliction bullet with three settings: under plotting and as counsel, as what kept him alive, and beside anguish.

**Psa 40:8.** *"I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart."* It follows *"In sacrifice and offering you have not delighted, but you have given me an open ear"* (40:6).
- **Roles:**
  - delight (M18) is the characteristic
  - "will" (H7522, a second M18 word) is its object
  - law (M54) is inward
- **Reading:** God's not delighting in sacrifice (40:6) and the speaker's delight to do God's will (40:8) use the same root, with God first and the man second. The delight is in **doing**, and the law is "within my heart".
- **Placement now:** in 13.1 *Delight* through Heb 10:5–10, where it is read of Christ. Not in 10.1.
- **Home:** 10.1 *On God's word, and on God* (B), as a new bullet "Joined to doing, with the law inward". 13.1 keeps its reading.

### 2.2 Delight in people (Psa 16:3)

**Psa 16:3.** *"As for the saints in the land, they are the excellent ones, in whom is all my delight."*
- **Passage:** 16:2 comes before, *"You are my Lord; I have no good apart from you"*. 16:4 comes after: *"The sorrows of those who run after another god shall multiply … I will … not … take their names on my lips."*
- **Roles:** delight is the characteristic. The saints (M13) are its object.
- **Reading:** three things stand together: no good apart from God, all delight in his holy ones, and refusing even the names of those who run after other gods.
- **I read:** delight in the saints is the companion of having God as one's only good. It sets whom he keeps company with.
- **Home:** 10.1 *In a person*, with a pointer to 10.10 *Friends, company and care*.

### 2.3 The enjoyment of life, and its limits (Ecc 11:7; Job 21:21)

**Ecc 11:7.** *"Light is sweet, and it is pleasant for the eyes to see the sun."*
- **Passage:**
  - 11:8: *"if a person lives many years, let him rejoice in them all; but let him remember that the days of darkness will be many"*
  - 11:9: *"Rejoice, O young man … let your heart cheer you … But know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment"*
- **Roles:** pleasant (M18) is the characteristic. "To see the sun" is to be alive.
- **Reading:** the pleasure is in life itself, in seeing the light. The writer does not let it stand alone. Rejoicing is bound to remembering the dark days and to the judgment.
- **Face:** pleasure in being alive, held with remembering and judgment.
- **Home:** 10.6 *Aimed at nothing, and enjoyment given*, the Ecclesiastes account of enjoyment.

**Job 21:21.** *"For what do they care for their houses after them, when the number of their months is cut off?"*
- **Passage:** the friends say *"God stores up their iniquity for their children"* (21:19). Job answers, *"Let him pay it out to them, that they may know it. Let their own eyes see their destruction"* (21:19–20).
- **Roles:** the M18 word (*ḥēpheṣ*, "what is his delight in") is the characteristic: the wicked man's concern.
- **Reading:** Job argues that a punishment deferred to the children does not touch a man whose concern ends with his own months. Justice must reach the man himself, before his own eyes.
- **I read:** the verse makes the reach of a person's concern decide what can reach him.
- **Face:** delight as concern for one's own, ending at death.
- **Home:** 10.6 *Wanting and death*.

### 2.4 Generosity in the eye (Pro 22:9)

**Pro 22:9.** *"Whoever has a bountiful eye will be blessed, for he shares his bread with the poor."*
- **Roles:**
  - the "bountiful" eye is literally a "good" eye (*ṭôb*, M18). This is the characteristic: a generous disposition.
  - the poor (M09) receive
  - blessed (M80) is the outcome
- **Reading:** the goodness is said of the eye, the way a person looks on another, and it shows in the hand that shares bread. The same book warns against eating the bread of a man who is stingy (Pro 23:6, already in 10.6 *Possessions*).
- **Face:** "good" as generosity of the eye.
- **Home:** 10.6 *Through the eye*, as the eye's opposite turn from covetous seeing, with a pointer to 10.10.

### 2.5 Coveting dressed as a fair offer (1Ki 21:2)

**1Ki 21:2.** *"Give me your vineyard, that I may have it for a vegetable garden, because it is near my house, and I will give you a better vineyard for it; or, if it seems good to you, I will give you its value in money."*
- **Roles:** the tagged M18 word is only the courtesy of the offer ("if it seems good to you": a qualifier). The verse's operation is Ahab's wanting what is another's, carried by untagged words ("give me … that I may have it"). That is coveting, M18's own characteristic (guide §4.6).
- **Passage:**
  - Naboth: *"The Lord forbid that I should give you the inheritance of my fathers"* (21:3)
  - Ahab *"went into his house vexed and sullen … And he lay down on his bed and turned away his face and would eat no food"* (21:4, already in 10.1 and 10.13)
- **Reading:** the want is put as a fair exchange, a better vineyard or money. The refusal is not a matter of price but of inheritance before the Lord. The want, refused, goes into the body.
- **I read:** the courtesy of the offer covers a want that cannot bear a no.
- **Home:** 10.6 *Coveting*, beside Exo 20:17 ("anything that is your neighbor's") and Mic 2:2.

### 2.6 A people pleased with their king (2Sa 3:36)

**2Sa 3:36.** *"And all the people took notice of it, and it pleased them, as everything that the king did pleased all the people."*
- **Passage:** David mourns Abner, weeps at the grave and swears not to eat till sunset (3:31–35). Then: *"So all the people and all Israel understood that day that it had not been the king's will to put to death Abner"* (3:37).
- **Roles:** "pleased" (M18; the second "pleased" also carries M05) is the characteristic: the people's approval. The king (M72) is its object.
- **Reading:** the people watched what he did. It pleased them, and through it they understood what he had **not** willed.
- **I read:** the verse moves from what is seen, through approval, to knowing another's will.
- **Home:** 10.10 *Loyalty and honour*, with a pointer to 10.2 (understanding).

### 2.7 Seeing what pleases the Lord, and turning (Num 24:1)

**Num 24:1.** *"When Balaam saw that it pleased the Lord to bless Israel, he did not go, as at other times, to look for omens, but set his face toward the wilderness."* It is followed by *"And the Spirit of God came upon him"* (24:2).
- **Roles:**
  - "pleased" is God's (A)
  - Balaam's seeing it is the HIB act (B)
  - bless (M80) is what pleased the Lord
  - "look" (M37) is the old seeking of omens, now dropped
- **Reading:** what Balaam had worked against twice (Num 22–23), he now sees. Once he knows what pleases the Lord, he stops seeking omens and sets his face in a direction.
- **Home:**
  - B: 10.7 (setting direction), as a verse of a course changed by perceiving God's pleasure
  - A: 13.1 *Delight* ("it pleased the Lord to bless")

### 2.8 God's own pleasure and purpose (Isa 44:28; 46:10; 48:14; Psa 135:6)

**Isa 44:28.** *"who says of Cyrus, 'He is my shepherd, and he shall fulfill all my purpose'"*. It closes a chain of "who says": *"who confirms the word of his servant and fulfills the counsel of his messengers"* (44:26).

**Isa 46:10–11.** *"declaring the end from the beginning … saying, 'My counsel shall stand, and I will accomplish all my purpose' … I have spoken, and I will bring it to pass; I have purposed, and I will do it."* The setting is *"Remember this and stand firm … remember the former things of old; for I am God, and there is no other"* (46:8–9).

**Isa 48:14.** *"Who among them has declared these things? The Lord loves him; he shall perform his purpose on Babylon."*
- **Referent crux (guide §4.6):** "his purpose" can be read two ways.
  - It is the Lord's purpose, carried out by the one he loves. This agrees with 44:28: "he shall fulfill all my purpose".
  - It is Cyrus's own pleasure against Babylon.
- I adopt the first, on 44:28. The second is kept on record.

**Roles (all three):** *ḥēpheṣ* (the delight word) is the characteristic, used for God's settled purpose. Counsel (M16, 46:10) is paired with it. Love (M51, 48:14) is said of the one who carries it out.

**Reading (all three):** the purpose is always spoken: "says", "declaring", "I have spoken". It is set against the idols that cannot declare anything (46:5–7; 48:14 "who among them has declared"). It is carried out by a named man.

**Face (#1919):** the word that names a person's delight here names God's purpose. What pleases him is what he will do.

**Home:** 13.1 *Purposing*, as a new paragraph: "His purpose, spoken and carried out by another". The existing account came from the M64 plan words, and this adds the delight word.

**Psa 135:6.** *"Whatever the Lord pleases, he does, in heaven and on earth, in the seas and all deeps."*
- **Passage:** before it, *"For I know that the Lord is great, and that our Lord is above all gods"* (135:5). After it, clouds, lightning and wind (135:7). Later, the idols that cannot speak or see (135:15–18).
- **Reading:** his pleasure and his doing are one. Nothing stands between them, anywhere in creation. Psa 115:3 says the same and is already in 13.1 *Delight*.
- **Home:** 13.1 *Delight*, beside Psa 115:3.

### 2.9 What the Lord does not delight in (1Sa 15:22; Mal 1:10; Ecc 5:4)

**1Sa 15:22.** *"Has the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams."*
- **Passage:** Saul says *"I have obeyed"*, and puts the spoil down to the people, *"to sacrifice to the Lord"* (15:20–21). Samuel answers, and goes on: *"rebellion is as the sin of divination, and presumption is as iniquity and idolatry. Because you have rejected the word of the Lord, he has also rejected you"* (15:23).
- **Roles:**
  - the Lord's delight is the characteristic (A)
  - obeying and listening (M41) is the HIB act weighed (B)
  - burnt offering (M36) is what was put in its place
- **Reading:** what God delights in is set as the measure. A sacrifice offered from what was to be destroyed does not please him. Hearing and doing do.
- **Home:**
  - A: 13.1 *Delight*, beside Hos 6:6 and Psa 51:16 (already there)
  - B: Saul's obedience offered as sacrifice, **forward to M41**

**Mal 1:10.** *"Oh that there were one among you who would shut the doors, that you might not kindle fire on my altar in vain! I have no pleasure in you, says the Lord of hosts, and I will not accept an offering from your hand."*
- **Passage:** the priests *"despise my name"* (1:6). They offer the blind and lame. *"Present that to your governor; will he accept you or show you favor?"* (1:8). After the verse: *"my name will be great among the nations"* (1:11).
- **Roles:** "no pleasure" is God's (A). Accept (M39) is the same act outwardly. The priests' despising (M30) is B.
- **Reading:** he would rather the doors were shut than the fire kindled in vain. The governor comparison measures the contempt: they offer God what no man would accept.
- **I read:** worship that despises is worse to him than none.
- **Home:**
  - A: 13.1 *Delight* (what he has no pleasure in)
  - B: the priests' despising, **forward to M30**

**Ecc 5:4.** *"When you vow a vow to God, do not delay paying it, for he has no pleasure in fools. Pay what you vow."*
- **Passage:** it comes between *"Be not rash with your mouth, nor let your heart be hasty to utter a word before God"* (5:2) and *"It is better that you should not vow than that you should vow and not pay"* (5:5). It closes with *"God is the one you must fear"* (5:7).
- **Roles:** "no pleasure" is God's (A). It is given as the ground for paying the vow. The HIB act, the vow made good, carries no M-code.
- **Reading:** "the fool" in this passage is the one whose mouth runs ahead of his doing (5:2–3).
- **Home:**
  - A: 13.1 *Delight*, with Mal 1:10
  - B: 10.5 *From the heart to the mouth*

### 2.10 The Lord's favor asked for (Psa 90:17)

**Psa 90:17.** *"Let the favor of the Lord our God be upon us, and establish the work of our hands upon us; yes, establish the work of our hands!"*
- **Passage:**
  - *"Return, O Lord! How long? Have pity on your servants!"* (90:13)
  - *"Make us glad for as many days as you have afflicted us"* (90:15)
  - *"Let your work be shown to your servants"* (90:16)
- **Roles:** favor (*nōʿam*, "pleasantness", M18) is the Lord's (A), and it is what is asked for. The work of their hands is the HIB side.
- **Reading:** in a psalm of how short a man's life is (90:3–10, 12), the last request is that something of their own work should last. It is asked through his favor resting on them, set beside "your work" (90:16).
- **Home:**
  - B: 10.9 *Waiting, and pleading*
  - A: 13.1 *Delight*, by pointer

### 2.11 Wisdom delighted in, and delighting (Pro 8:30–31)

**Pro 8:30–31.** *"then I was beside him, like a master workman, and I was daily his delight, rejoicing before him always, rejoicing in his inhabited world and delighting in the children of man."*
- **Genre:** a poem in which Wisdom speaks in person (8:22: *"The Lord possessed me at the beginning of his work"*). It is read as a figure, not taken literally.
- **Roles:** delight (M18) and rejoicing (M04) are paired. They run from God to Wisdom, and from Wisdom to the world and to mankind.
- **Reading:** delight is placed at creation, before anything goes wrong. Wisdom is God's delight, and Wisdom delights in people.
- **I read:** the poem puts delight in mankind into the work of creation itself.
- **Home:** 13.1 *Delight*, as a new bullet on delight at creation, with a pointer to 10.1.

### 2.12 Passions that bend prayer (Jam 4:3)

**Jam 4:3.** *"You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, to spend it on your passions."*
- **Passage:** *"your passions are at war within you … You desire and do not have, so you murder. You covet and cannot obtain, so you fight and quarrel. You do not have, because you do not ask"* (4:1–2). Then 4:4: *"friendship with the world is enmity with God."*
- **Roles:** passions (*hēdonē*, M18) are the characteristic. Asking (M21) is the act they bend.
- **Reading:** James names two failures in prayer, not asking and asking for the passions. The passions are behind the fighting (4:1) and behind the wrong asking (4:3). They are present in the prayer itself.
- **Placement now:** 4:1–2 is in 10.6 *Unmet, and wanting against God*.
- **Home:** 10.6 *Unmet, and wanting against God*, extending the 4:1–2 account to 4:3. Pointer to 10.9 (prayer).

### 2.13 Already carried: confirmed (Isa 58:13)

**Isa 58:13.** One's own pleasure is turned from, and the Sabbath is called a delight, followed by *"then you shall take delight in the Lord"* (58:14).
- **Placement now:** in 10.1 *On God's word, and on God*, "After one's own pleasure". The placement stands.
- **Correction (v2):** v1 said the same word is used twice in the verse with opposite objects. That is wrong. "Your pleasure" (twice, "doing your pleasure", "seeking your own pleasure") is *ḥēpheṣ*. "Call the Sabbath a delight" is a different word, *ʿōneg*. No Ch 11 change is made from this verse.
