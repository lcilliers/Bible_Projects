## 1. Coveting (10.6, new section)

**The commandment always names the neighbour's thing.** *"You shall not covet your neighbor's house; you shall not covet your neighbor's wife … or anything that is your neighbor's"* (Exo 20:17).
- Deuteronomy uses two verbs: "covet" for the wife, and "desire" for the house and the rest (Deu 5:21).
- **I read** coveting as wanting defined by a relationship: it is wanting what is another's.
- The answer Paul gives is a love for the same neighbour: *"You shall not covet … are summed up in this word: You shall love your neighbor as yourself"* (Rom 13:9).

**Seeing, coveting, taking.**
- Achan, in his own words: *"when I saw among the spoil a beautiful cloak … then I coveted them and took them. And see, they are hidden"* (Jos 7:21).
- The woman: *"saw … to be desired … took"* (Gen 3:6).
- The same three acts stand in both verses, and the second adds hiding (10.11).
- Micah adds the night and the power: *"Woe to those who devise wickedness and work evil on their beds! When the morning dawns, they perform it, because it is in the power of their hand. They covet fields and seize them"* (Mic 2:1–2). The covetous wish is planned in bed and carried out when there is power to do it.

**The command names the desire, and sin multiplies it.** *"I would not have known what it is to covet if the law had not said, 'You shall not covet.' But sin, seizing an opportunity through the commandment, produced in me all kinds of covetousness"* (Rom 7:7–8). **I read** the command as making the desire known, and sin as using that very knowledge.

**A snare, and a fruit.**
- *"You shall not covet the silver or the gold that is on them … lest you be ensnared by it"* (Deu 7:25). The gold on an idol is a snare.
- *"Whoever is wicked covets the spoil of evildoers, but the root of the righteous bears fruit"* (Pro 12:12). Taking is set against growing.

**The opposite, lived.** Paul: *"I coveted no one's silver or gold or apparel. You yourselves know that these hands ministered to my necessities … 'It is more blessed to give than to receive'"* (Act 20:33–35). The hands that might have taken work and give.

## 2. Desire as master, leader, deceiver, hunter (10.6 *Wanting that owns the one who wants*, widened)

The verses give desire the verbs of a person acting on the one who wants:

| What desire does | Verse |
|---|---|
| Is obeyed, under sin as king | *"Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body, to make you obey its passions"* (Rom 6:12) |
| Is followed | *"grumblers, malcontents, following their own sinful desires"* (Jude 16); *"scoffers … following their own sinful desires"*, who ask *"Where is the promise of his coming?"* (2Pe 3:3–4) |
| Picks what is heard | *"having itching ears they will accumulate for themselves teachers to suit their own passions, and will turn away from listening to the truth and wander off into myths"* (2Ti 4:3–4) |
| Keeps learning from arriving | *"led astray by various passions, always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth"* (2Ti 3:6–7) |
| Deceives | *"your old self … corrupt through deceitful desires"* (Eph 4:22) |
| Lures | *"each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed by his own desire"* (Jam 1:14). The bait comes before the conceiving of 1:15 |
| Takes captive | *"the treacherous are taken captive by their lust"* (Pro 11:6) |
| Crouches | *"sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is for you, and you must rule over it"* (Gen 4:7) |
| Is inherited | *"your will is to do your father's desires"* (Joh 8:44) |

**Explore the figures.**
- "Itching ears" pictures a want to be scratched. The teachers are gathered to scratch it.
- "Lured and enticed" is the fisherman's or hunter's bait.
- "Crouching at the door" is an animal waiting at the threshold.

**I read** these as the same act seen from different sides. The want leads, and the one who wants follows.

**What is set against it.**
- **Knowing God.** *"not in the passion of lust like the Gentiles who do not know God"* (1Th 4:5); *"the passions of your former ignorance"* (1Pe 1:14). In two verses the uncontrolled desire stands beside not knowing.
- **A renewed mind.** *"to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and to put on the new self"* (Eph 4:23–24).
- **The will of God, which lasts.** *"no longer for human passions but for the will of God. For the time that is past suffices for doing what the Gentiles want to do"* (1Pe 4:2–3). *"the world is passing away along with its desires, but whoever does the will of God abides forever"* (1Jo 2:17). The desires pass away; the doing of his will lasts.
- **A share in his nature.** *"partakers of the divine nature, having escaped from the corruption that is in the world because of sinful desire"* (2Pe 1:4).

## 3. One word for desire, and three settings (10.6 *A person*; Ch 11 §4)

The word *tĕšûqāh* occurs three times:
- *"Your desire shall be for your husband, and he shall rule over you"* (Gen 3:16). This is after the fall, and desire stands with rule.
- *"sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is for you, and you must rule over it"* (Gen 4:7). Here the desire is sin's, aimed at Cain, and the rule is his to exercise.
- *"I am my beloved's, and his desire is for me"* (Song 7:10). It is mutual, and nothing in the verse rules.

The word stays the same. What differs is whose the desire is, and whether rule stands beside it. The verses set these three side by side by their wording. The reading of them together is mine.

## 4. Longing for people (10.6 *A person*, widened)

Paul's longing for the churches is joined each time to something else:
- **Memory:** *"As I remember your tears, I long to see you, that I may be filled with joy"* (2Ti 1:4).
- **Christ's own affection:** *"how I yearn for you all with the affection of Christ Jesus"* (Phi 1:8), after *"I hold you in my heart"* (1:7).
- **Distress for another's distress:** Epaphroditus *"has been longing for you all and has been distressed because you heard that he was ill"* (Phi 2:26).
- **Both ways:** *"you … long to see us, as we long to see you"*. It comforts *"in all our distress and affliction"* (1Th 3:6–7).
- **Prayer:** *"they long for you and pray for you, because of the surpassing grace of God upon you"* (2Co 9:14).
- **Held across years:** *"I have longed for many years to come to you"* (Rom 15:23), after being *"so often … hindered"* (15:22).
- **Passed on through a third person:** God *"comforted us by the coming of Titus, and not only by his coming but also by the comfort with which he was comforted by you, as he told us of your longing"* (2Co 7:6–7).
- **Joy and standing firm:** *"whom I love and long for, my joy and crown, stand firm thus in the Lord"* (Phi 4:1).

**Compare Shechem**, already in 10.6. His longing came after violence. Paul's longing gives, prays, remembers, and carries another's distress.

**Longing for a home, and fear.** Laban: *"you have gone away because you longed greatly for your father's house"*. Jacob: *"Because I was afraid"* (Gen 31:30–31). The longing and the fear stand in two speakers.

**The bride's desire.** *"With great delight I sat in his shadow, and his fruit was sweet to my taste"* (Song 2:3). *"he is altogether desirable. This is my beloved and this is my friend"* (5:16). The pictures are of shade and taste: desire as rest and as sweetness. And *"the king will desire your beauty"*, after *"forget your people and your father's house"* (Psa 45:10–11).

**What is wanted in a person.** *"What is desired in a man is steadfast love"* (Pro 19:22).

## 5. Through the eye, and nothing for the eye (10.6 *Through the eye*)

- **The case begins with a sight.** *"you see among the captives a beautiful woman, and you desire to take her to be your wife"* (Deu 21:11). Its end, "if you no longer delight in her" (21:14), is already in 10.6.
- **Pictures on a wall.** *"She saw men portrayed on the wall, the images of the Chaldeans"* (Eze 23:14). She *"lusted after the Assyrians … all of them desirable young men"* (23:12; 23:6).
- **Nothing to desire.** *"he had no form or majesty that we should look at him, and no beauty that we should desire him. He was despised and rejected by men"* (Isa 53:2–3).
  - **Compare** the woman's look at the tree. That look found something "to be desired" and took it. Here the look found nothing to desire and turned away.
  - **I read** that the one who *"has borne our griefs"* (53:4) is the one the desiring eye passed over.

## 6. Heat after fullness (10.6 *As heat*)

*"When I fed them to the full, they committed adultery … They were well-fed, lusty stallions, each neighing for his neighbor's wife"* (Jer 5:7–8). **Compare** *"Jeshurun grew fat, and kicked"* (Deu 32:15; 13.1 *Jealousy*). Both say that fullness was followed by turning away. The stallion picture makes the desire animal and loud.

## 7. The craving named in a place (10.6 *Unmet, and wanting against God*)

- *"the name of that place was called Kibroth-hattaavah, because there they buried the people who had the craving"* (Num 11:34). The place is named "graves of craving".
- *"they ate and were well filled, for he gave them what they craved. But before they had satisfied their craving"* (Psa 78:29–30). They were filled, and the craving was not satisfied. **I read** that the craving was not the hunger. Filling the stomach did not end it.
- *"they soon forgot his works; they did not wait for his counsel. But they had a wanton craving in the wilderness, and put God to the test"* (Psa 106:13–14). The verses put forgetting and not waiting first. *"he gave them what they asked, but sent a wasting disease among them"* (106:15).
- Paul: *"these things took place as examples for us, that we might not desire evil as they did"* (1Co 10:6).
- **Two hungers at a gate.**
  - Lazarus *"desired to be fed with what fell from the rich man's table"* (Luk 16:21).
  - The younger son was *"longing to be fed with the pods that the pigs ate, and no one gave him anything. But when he came to himself"* (Luk 15:16–17).
  - In both, the want goes unanswered by the one who could answer it. For the younger son, the unmet want is where he comes to himself.

## 8. Longing for God, his word, his day (10.6 *Aimed at God*)

- **Panting.** *"I open my mouth and pant, because I long for your commandments"* (Psa 119:131).
- **Worth and taste.** *"More to be desired are they than gold, even much fine gold; sweeter also than honey"* (Psa 19:10).
- **Chosen, then longed for.** *"I have chosen your precepts. I long for your salvation, O Lord, and your law is my delight"* (Psa 119:173–174).
- **Longing laid open.** *"O Lord, all my longing is before you; my sighing is not hidden from you"* (Psa 38:9). It is said in sickness, with *"my nearest kin stand far off"* (38:11).
- **Longing to see, and not seeing.**
  - *"many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see, and did not see it"* (Mat 13:17).
  - *"you will desire to see one of the days of the Son of Man, and you will not see it … Do not go out or follow them"* (Luk 17:22–23).
  - The longing is real, and it is not to be met by following whoever says "Look, here!"
- **Desiring the day, wrongly and rightly.**
  - *"Woe to you who desire the day of the Lord! Why would you have the day of the Lord? It is darkness, and not light, as if a man fled from a lion, and a bear met him"* (Amo 5:18–19).
  - Jeremiah: *"nor have I desired the day of sickness"* (Jer 17:16).
  - **Compare.** The people want a day they do not understand. The prophet disowns wanting the disaster he has to announce. The figure in Amos is of fleeing one danger straight into another.

## 9. Where desire ends, and what it is set against (10.6 *Evil desire*)

- *"What the wicked dreads will come upon him, but the desire of the righteous will be granted"* (Pro 10:24). Dread and desire stand against each other.
- *"Whoever isolates himself seeks his own desire; he breaks out against all sound judgment"* (Pro 18:1).
- *"All day long he craves and craves, but the righteous gives and does not hold back"* (Pro 21:26). This is the verse after *"The desire of the sluggard kills him"*. The craving that never ends is set against giving that does not hold back.
- *"How long … will scoffers delight in their scoffing"* (Pro 1:22). This goes in the delight list.

## 10. Greed, and its reasons (10.6 *Possessions*)

- **A root, and a wandering.** *"For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. It is through this craving that some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pangs"* (1Ti 6:10).
- **Refuge in the wrong place.** *"See the man who would not make God his refuge, but trusted in the abundance of his riches and sought refuge in his own destruction!"* (Psa 52:7). **I read** the irony as the psalm's own: what he ran to was his ruin.
- **A nest on high.** *"Woe to him who gets evil gain for his house, to set his nest on high, to be safe from the reach of harm! … you have forfeited your life"* (Hab 2:9–10). The figure is a bird nesting out of reach. Greed's motive is named as safety.
- **Greed in the leaders, with a false peace.** *"everyone is greedy for unjust gain; and from prophet to priest, everyone deals falsely. They have healed the wound of my people lightly, saying, 'Peace, peace,' when there is no peace"* (Jer 6:13–14).
- **Greed and forgetting God.** *"you … make gain of your neighbors by extortion; but me you have forgotten"* (Eze 22:12).
- **No contentment.** *"Because he knew no contentment in his belly, he will not let anything in which he delights escape him"* (Job 20:20).
- **At a ruler's table, and a stingy man's.**
  - *"put a knife to your throat if you are given to appetite. Do not desire his delicacies, for they are deceptive food"* (Pro 23:2–3).
  - *"When your eyes light on it, it is gone, for suddenly it sprouts wings"* (23:5).
  - *"do not desire his delicacies, for he is like one who is inwardly calculating. 'Eat and drink!' he says to you, but his heart is not with you"* (23:6–7).
  - The pictures are a knife at the throat, riches with wings, and a host whose heart is elsewhere. The desire for the food is warned against because of what the food hides.
- **Kept and devoured.** *"Precious treasure and oil are in a wise man's dwelling, but a foolish man devours it"* (Pro 21:20).

## 11. What is desired, taken (10.6 *Taken away*)

- **The prophet's own delight.** *"I am about to take the delight of your eyes away from you at a stroke; yet you shall not mourn or weep … So I spoke to the people in the morning, and at evening my wife died"* (Eze 24:16–18). The verses already in 10.6 (24:21, 25) explain the sign to the people. Here it is first a man's wife.
- **Stripped.** *"The enemy has stretched out his hands over all her precious things"* (Lam 1:10).
- **A plea from the ruins.** *"all our pleasant places have become ruins. Will you restrain yourself at these things, O Lord?"* (Isa 64:11–12).
- **To the complacent.** *"Beat your breasts for the pleasant fields"* (Isa 32:12), said to *"you complacent women"* (32:11).
- **Built on oppression, withheld.** *"because you trample on the poor … you have planted pleasant vineyards, but you shall not drink their wine"* (Amo 5:11).
- **Longing that can do nothing.** *"your eyes look on and fail with longing for them all day long, but you shall be helpless"* (Deu 28:32).
- **The moth.** *"When you discipline a man with rebukes for sin, you consume like a moth what is dear to him; surely all mankind is a mere breath!"* (Psa 39:11). The figure is slow and silent loss, not plunder.

## 12. Desire at the end (10.6 *Wanting and death*; Ch 2)

- *"desire fails, because man is going to his eternal home"* (Ecc 12:5). Wanting itself grows old.
- *"they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one … If they had been thinking of that land from which they had gone out, they would have had opportunity to return"* (Heb 11:15–16). The desire is shown by not going back.
- *"Do not long for the night, when peoples vanish in their place"* (Job 36:20). This goes to Ch 2, *Wanting to die*.
- *"he departed with no one's regret"* (2Ch 21:20). A death that no one desired back goes to Ch 2, *What death is*.

## 13. Panting: one verb, many settings (Ch 11 §4)

*šāʾap*, "pant" or "long", is used of:
- a worn slave who *"longs for the shadow"* (Job 7:2)
- *"the thirsty"* who *"pant after his wealth"* (Job 5:5)
- the sun that *"hastens"* to where it rises (Ecc 1:5)
- the wild donkey *"sniffing the wind"* (Jer 2:24; 10.6)
- the psalmist who *"pant[s]"* for the commandments (Psa 119:131)
- God, who will *"gasp and pant"* like a woman in labour (Isa 42:14)

The verb names an urgent, breathing reach. What it reaches for decides its character. The beasts *"pant for you because the water brooks are dried up"* (Joe 1:20; 13.4).

## 14. God's desire (13.1 *Delight*, *Choosing*)

- **A place desired.** *"For the Lord has chosen Zion; he has desired it for his dwelling place: 'This is my resting place forever; here I will dwell, for I have desired it'"* (Psa 132:13–14). The mountains are pictured as envious: *"Why do you look with hatred, O many-peaked mountain, at the mount that God desired for his abode?"* (Psa 68:16).
- **A people loved, not for what they were.** *"It was not because you were more in number than any other people that the Lord set his love on you and chose you, for you were the fewest of all peoples, but it is because the Lord loves you"* (Deu 7:7–8). *"Yet the Lord set his heart in love on your fathers … Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart"* (10:15–16). His set heart is answered by a call on theirs.
- **His hope for them, disappointed.** *"I said, How I would set you among my sons, and give you a pleasant land … And I thought you would call me, My Father, and would not turn from following me. Surely, as a treacherous wife leaves her husband, so have you been treacherous to me"* (Jer 3:19–20).
- **His grief over what is his.** *"they have made my pleasant portion a desolate wilderness. They have made it a desolation; desolate, it mourns to me. The whole land is made desolate, but no man lays it to heart"* (Jer 12:10–11).
- **His keeping.** *"A pleasant vineyard, sing of it! I, the Lord, am its keeper; every moment I water it … I have no wrath"* (Isa 27:2–4).
- **His word on the one who clings to him.** *"Because he holds fast to me in love, I will deliver him"* (Psa 91:14).
- **His timing.** *"When I please, I will discipline them"* (Hos 10:10).
- **His guarding of what is left behind.** *"no one shall covet your land, when you go up to appear before the Lord your God three times in the year"* (Exo 34:24).
- **The nations' treasures.** *"the treasures of all nations shall come in, and I will fill this house with glory … The silver is mine"* (Hag 2:7–8).
- **The Son's desire.** *"I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer"* (Luk 22:15).
- **Job's hope of God's longing.** *"You would call, and I would answer you; you would long for the work of your hands"* (Job 14:15).

## 15. Aims (10.7 *Setting the heart*)

- *"I make it my ambition to preach the gospel, not where Christ has already been named"* (Rom 15:20)
- *"whether we are at home or away, we make it our aim to please him. For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ"* (2Co 5:9–10)
- *"aspire to live quietly, and to mind your own affairs, and to work with your hands"* (1Th 4:11)
- *"If anyone aspires to the office of overseer, he desires a noble task"* (1Ti 3:1)

The word (*philotimeomai*) is the one used for ambition. **I read** what it is aimed at in these verses as unexpected: going where no one has gone, pleasing him wherever one is, and quietness.

## 16. Other placements

- *"their inmost self is destruction; their throat is an open grave"* (Psa 5:9): Ch 12, *The heart as the source*. "Destruction" is the same word as the desire word in Pro 11:6 above. The lexical fact is in the data. That the inner self can be named by it is the verse's own.
- *"An evildoer listens to wicked lips, and a liar gives ear to a mischievous tongue"* (Pro 17:4): 10.2. What one listens to shows what one is.
- *"All who fashion idols are nothing, and the things they delight in do not profit"* (Isa 44:9): Ch 12, *Idols taken inside*.
- *"Be not envious of evil men, nor desire to be with them, for their hearts devise violence"* (Pro 24:1–2): 10.6, *Jealousy and envy*.
