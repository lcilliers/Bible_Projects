## 3. What the passages hold that is not in the book

**Jeremiah 22:16–17: knowing God, said as doing justice for the poor.** *The largest gap.*
> "He judged the cause of the poor and needy; then it was well. Is not this to know me? declares the
> Lord. But you have eyes and heart only for your dishonest gain, for shedding innocent blood, and for
> practicing oppression and violence."

The passage (22:13–17) sets Josiah against his son, who builds "by unrighteousness" and makes his
neighbour work unpaid. God himself calls judging the poor's cause *knowing me*. The son's eyes and
heart are given "only" to gain. This is an inner-being statement in God's own words:
- **10.2 *Knowing and hearing*:** what knowing God is said to be.
- **10.6:** the heart and eyes set on gain.

OT-105 already has Jer 22:16 as item 12.

**Job 31:16–17: the poor's desire not withheld.**
> "If I have withheld anything that the poor desired, or have caused the eyes of the widow to fail, or
> have eaten my morsel alone, and the fatherless has not eaten of it"

These are Job's own words, in the oath God's verdict supports (Job 42:7). Here the M18 word is the
poor man's own desire, which Job swears he did not refuse. "Eaten my morsel alone" names the act of
not sharing. 10.10 already has 31:19–20 from the same oath, and this sits just before it.

**Mark 14:4–7: the poor as a pretext, and doing good "whenever you want".**
> "There were some who said to themselves indignantly, 'Why was the ointment wasted like that? For
> this ointment could have been sold for more than three hundred denarii and given to the poor.' And
> they scolded her. But Jesus said, 'Leave her alone … For you always have the poor with you, and
> whenever you want, you can do good for them. But you will not always have me.'"

Mark gives what Matthew does not: the indignation was "to themselves" first, then spoken as a scolding.
Jesus' answer puts doing good to the poor in the hearers' own will ("whenever you want"). The poor are
named in the scolding. Jesus answers it, and the verses do not say what lay behind it in Mark. John
12:6 says it of Judas, but John 12 is not in this set.

**Ecclesiastes 9:15–16: the poor wise man forgotten and not heard.**
> "But there was found in it a poor, wise man, and he by his wisdom delivered the city. Yet no one
> remembered that poor man. But I say that wisdom is better than might, though the poor man's wisdom
> is despised and his words are not heard."

What seeing someone poor did here: he was **not remembered**, and his words **not heard**, though he
had saved the city. This bears on 10.3 (forgetting) and 10.2 (hearing).

**Esther 9:22: gifts to the poor in the days of gladness.**
> "the month that had been turned for them from sorrow into gladness and from mourning into a
> holiday; that they should make them days of feasting and gladness, days for sending gifts of food to
> one another and gifts to the poor"

Gladness after sorrow is kept by feasting and by giving, to one another *and* to the poor. It belongs
with the Esther passages now in 10.10 and 10.12. On the joy side it is 10.1.

## 4. The impact

**For M18 itself: nothing missing.** Where the M18 word carries the act (Pro 22:9, Mat 19:21, Rom
15:26–27), the verse is already told. Where the word is a qualifier, the act lies elsewhere.

**For the book, five gaps**, all on OT-105's own subject (what seeing the poor moves in a person):

| Passage | What it adds | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Jer 22:16–17 | judging the cause of the poor called "to know me"; eyes and heart only for gain | 10.2 *Knowing and hearing*; pointer from 10.6 |
| Job 31:16–17 | the poor's desire not withheld; not eating one's morsel alone | 10.10 *Friends, company and care*, beside 31:19–20 |
| Mar 14:4–7 | the indignation "to themselves", the poor named in a scolding, doing good "whenever you want" | 10.13 or 10.1 (with Matthew 26:8); 10.10 |
| Ecc 9:15–16 | the poor wise man not remembered, his words not heard | 10.3 *Forgetting* or 10.2 |
| Est 9:22 | gifts to the poor kept with the gladness of deliverance | 10.10, or 10.1 |

**A question of method for OT-105.** OT-105 was set up (your instruction, #2148) to be picked up when
the clusters that carry the response are read (M05, M12, M10, M24, M46, M58, M26), "not analysed
through to the narrative on its own". Weaving these five now, from the M18 handle, would move part of
OT-105 into the book ahead of those strands. The other option is to note each passage on OT-105 and
close item 9 as carried for M18.

| Option | What it means |
|---|---|
| Weave the five, then close item 9 | as in the table; claims where the framing goes beyond the verse; OT-105 keeps its other items |
| Close item 9, keep the five on OT-105 | M18 has nothing missing; add the five passages to OT-105 as verse items for when its clusters are read |
| Weave Jer 22:16–17 only | the one passage where God himself says what knowing him is; the rest stay on OT-105 |
