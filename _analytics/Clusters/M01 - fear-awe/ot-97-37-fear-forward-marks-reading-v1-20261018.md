## 2. The readings

### Est 8:17: fear of the Jews leads others to declare themselves Jews

> "And in every province and in every city, wherever the king's command and his edict reached, there
> was gladness and joy among the Jews, a feast and a holiday. And many from the peoples of the
> country declared themselves Jews, for fear of the Jews had fallen on them."

**The passage.** The second edict (8:9–14) lets the Jews defend themselves. Mordecai goes out in
royal robes and Susa rejoices (8:15–16). The verse then holds two peoples under one decree:
- gladness and joy among the Jews
- fear among the peoples, which moved many of them to declare themselves Jews

**The main operation is fear moving people to change sides**: a public declaration made "for
fear". The verse gives fear as the only reason. It names no conviction and no God. The book of
Esther never names God, so a reading must not say God laid this fear.

**In the narrative now:** nothing. Est 8:15–17 is not cited anywhere. 10.10 *Fear that arrives with
a person* has 9:1–3, the fear of the Jews and of Mordecai.

**What it adds:** a face of dread on a people that neither flees nor opposes. It joins the ones it
fears.
- 10.12 *Dread laid on others* sets two peoples side by side: Rahab, whose heart melted at what she
  heard, and Moab, whose dread sent for a curse. Est 8:17 is a third outcome: taking on the name of
  the feared, "for fear".
- That section's opening says "God lays dread on peoples". Est 8:17 and 9:2 are dread with **no
  named giver**, so the opening would need to be widened, not just added to.

### Est 9:5: doing as they pleased, once fear had fallen

> "The Jews struck all their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying them, and did as they
> pleased to those who hated them."

**The passage.** On the day the enemies "hoped to gain the mastery", "the reverse occurred" (9:1).
"No one could stand against them, for the fear of them had fallen on all peoples" (9:2). The
officials helped, "for the fear of Mordecai had fallen on them" (9:3).

**The main operation in 9:5 is not fear.** It is acting at will ("as they pleased", the M18 word)
against those who hated them. Fear is in 9:2–3, and 9:2 states its effect: no one could stand.
9:5 is what followed.

**In the narrative now:** 10.10 already quotes 9:1–2 and 9:3. 9:5 is not cited.

**What it adds:** only the outcome. If 10.10's paragraph "Mastery can turn on the day it is hoped
for" is extended, 9:5 completes it: with no one able to stand (9:2), they "did as they pleased"
(9:5). There is **nothing new for 10.12**. The fear itself is already told.

### Ecc 12:5: fear in old age

> "they are afraid also of what is high, and terrors are in the way; the almond tree blossoms, the
> grasshopper drags itself along, and desire fails, because man is going to his eternal home, and
> the mourners go about the streets —"

**The passage (12:1–7)** is one figure of old age.
- It is framed by "Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come"
  (12:1) and ends "the dust returns to the earth … and the spirit returns to God who gave it" (12:7).
- Inside it: "the keepers of the house tremble, and the strong men are bent" (12:3); "one rises up
  at the sound of a bird" (12:4); then the fear of heights and of the road (12:5).

**The main operation:** fear that comes as the body fails. I read the "keepers of the house" and
"strong men" as the body's own parts (the figure is not explained in the text). The fears are
ordinary: of high places, of the way out of doors. They come with the trembling of 12:3 and the
startle at a bird in 12:4. The whole is set inside the call to remember the Creator *before* those
days (12:1).

**In the narrative now:**
- Ch 2 *Facing death* quotes 12:5 as the end of life.
- 10.6 *Wanting and death* quotes "desire fails".
- Ch 7 cites 12:3.
- 10.12 only points to Ch 2 ("terrors in old age").
- Claim C-153 rests on the passage, but it is about "the king of terrors" (Job 18:14), not this verse.

**What it adds:** a face missing from 10.12's own account: **fear that grows as the body weakens**.
It is not fear of a threat. The person is the one acted on, as in *Fear that comes upon a person*,
but the cause is age. The 12:1 frame also ties it to remembering (10.3): the call is to remember
the Creator before these fears come. The figurative reading needs "I read" and a register claim
under rule 77.

### Psa 103:11: his steadfast love toward those who fear him

> "For as high as the heavens are above the earth, so great is his steadfast love toward those who
> fear him"

**The passage** (103:8–18) says "those who fear him" three times:
- the love "as high as the heavens" (103:11)
- the Father's compassion (103:13)
- the steadfast love "from everlasting to everlasting on those who fear him" (103:17)

It then says who they are: "to those who keep his covenant and remember to do his commandments"
(103:18). Between these lines: "he knows our frame; he remembers that we are dust" (103:14).

**The main operation is God's steadfast love toward the one who fears him.** The height word (M08)
is a measure. Fear is the standing of the one loved.

**In the narrative now:**
- 10.9 *How God meets those who fear him* quotes 103:13.
- 10.3 and 10.4 quote 103:14.
- 103:11 and 103:17–18 are not cited.

**What it adds:**
- **10.9:** the measure of that love, as high as the heavens and from everlasting to everlasting.
  This sits beside 103:13, which is already there.
- **10.12 *The fear of God*:** the section lists how this fear is held (commanded, learned, given in
  the heart, possible without the heart, owed). Psa 103:17–18 adds a line the verse itself states:
  those who fear him are "those who keep his covenant and remember to do his commandments". This
  joins fear to keeping and remembering (10.3).
