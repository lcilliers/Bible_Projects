## A. What the verses say fear is

No verse in the four ledgers gives a definition of fear. What the verses do state falls under the five headings below. **The headings are my arrangement (R).** Each line under them is stated (S).

| Heading (R) | What the verses state | Verses | basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **It comes upon a person** | "dread came upon me, and trembling"; "Fear and trembling come upon me"; "fear fell upon him"; "Fear seized them all". The person is the one acted on | Job 4:14; Psa 55:5; Luk 1:12; Luk 7:16 | S |
| **It is felt in the heart and the body together** | "he was afraid, and his heart trembled greatly"; "My flesh trembles for fear of you"; "which made all my bones shake"; "fighting without and fear within" | 1Sa 28:5; Psa 119:120; Job 4:14; 2Cor 7:5 | S |
| **It has a cause outside and often a reason inside** | "I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked" | Gen 3:10 | S |
| **The nearest statement of what it is** | "There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear. For fear has to do with punishment". The verse before sets it: "so that we may have confidence for the day of judgment". This is said of fear before judgment, not of every fear (FE-136) | 1Jo 4:17–18 | S; the limit is R |
| **Named as a bondage** | "you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear"; "through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery"; "God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control" | Rom 8:15; Heb 2:15; 2Ti 1:7 | S |
| **As a standing toward God: commanded, learned, given** | "what does the Lord your God require of you, but to fear the Lord your God"; "that they may hear and learn to fear the Lord your God"; "I will put the fear of me in their hearts, that they may not turn from me" | Deu 10:12; Deu 31:12; Jer 32:40 | S |
| **The fear of God, named by what it is** | "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge"; "The fear of the Lord is hatred of evil"; "a fountain of life"; "It was a covenant of fear, and he feared me" | Pro 1:7; Pro 8:13; Pro 14:27; Mal 2:5 | S |
| **Something owed** | "If then I am a father, where is my honor? And if I am a master, where is my fear?"; "respect to whom respect is owed" | Mal 1:6; Rom 13:7 | S |

**What this shows (R):** the verses speak of fear in two ways that do not reduce to each other. In one, fear happens to a person, and the person is acted on. In the other, fear of God is a standing that is required, learned, given and owed. Exo 20:20 puts both in one sentence: "Do not fear, for God has come to test you, that the fear of him may be before you, that you may not sin" (FE-20). I note the two. No verse says they are two kinds.

## F. Fear and the other characteristics

**Researcher's question:** *"does it tightly integrate/relate to/live with other characteristics?"*

**Short answer.** Yes, in two senses, and the two should be kept apart.
1. **In the data:** fear words share verses with **79 of the M-clusters**, and with **40 of them in 20 or more verses** (`fear-web-pull-v1-20261001.csv`, M01 hits paired with every other tagged word in the verse). This is co-occurrence of tags. It points to verses to read. It is not a relation in itself (#1919: a tag shows one face only). Some high counts are tagging, not meaning. M72 (authority) is mostly "king"; M42 (prayer) and M43 (prophecy) are mostly "declares".
2. **In the verses:** fear is **joined by the wording itself** to many other characteristics. The forms the joins take are listed in the table below.

**Ways the verses join fear to another characteristic** (the forms are grammatical, so S; naming them is R):
- a **paired command**: "do not fear or be dismayed", "be strong and courageous"
- **set against with "but"**: "The fear of man lays a snare, but whoever trusts in the Lord is safe"
- **one leading to another**: "so that", "for", "and"
- **the same root, rendered as another characteristic**: *rāgaz* (to tremble) as anger; *phobos* (fear) as respect
