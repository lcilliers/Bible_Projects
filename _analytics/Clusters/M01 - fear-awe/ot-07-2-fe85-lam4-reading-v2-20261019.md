## 1. The verse in the original

"For the chastisement of the daughter of my people has been greater than the punishment of Sodom,
which was overthrown in a moment, and no hands were wrung for her" (Lam 4:6).

The last clause is literally "and hands did not writhe in / on her" (*wᵉlōʾ ḥālû bāh yādāyim*: the
verb H2342A *ḥûl*, qal perfect; "hands" dual, H3027G; *bāh*, "in / on / with her").

**What the root carries** (iba.db `strong_meaning_tree`, and the ESV's own renderings of every
occurrence in `verse_lexical`). *ḥûl* is one root, "to twist, whirl", and the same twisting is:

| Face | ESV renderings (examples) |
|---|---|
| the body writhing in labour or pain | "Writhe and groan, O daughter of Zion, like a woman in labor" (Mic 4:10); Saul "badly wounded" (1Sa 31:3) |
| trembling, of the earth and of people | "tremble before him, all the earth" (Psa 96:9); "who shall hear the report of you and shall tremble and be in anguish because of you" (Deu 2:25) |
| the heart in anguish | "My heart is in anguish within me" (Psa 55:4); the queen "deeply distressed" (Est 4:4) |
| no anguish felt | "You have struck them down, but they felt no anguish" (Jer 5:3) |
| birth | "the God who gave you birth" (Deu 32:18); "I was brought forth in iniquity" (Psa 51:5) |
| dance, whirl, storm | the daughters of Shiloh "dance in the dances" (Judg 21:21); "a whirling tempest" (Jer 23:19) |
| waiting | "Be still before the Lord and wait patiently for him" (Psa 37:7); "wait anxiously for good" (Mic 1:12) |

**Basis, or translator's choice?** The anguish is in the root. The verb is the writhing of pain
and fear, and here its subject is hands. So the tie between hands and anguish is in the Hebrew. The
English idiom "wrung", and "for her", are the translator's rendering of "writhed in / on her". The
Hebrew also allows the clause to be read as "no hands turned on her": Sodom fell "in a moment",
by no human hand, with no siege. I read both as open in the words. The ESV chose the first, which
puts grief or anguish *over* her into the clause.

## 2. Hands as the place where fear and anguish show

Lam 4:6 is not alone. A set-based pull (verses where a hand word stands with the writhing or
anguish words), with the neighbouring verses read, shows the body, and the hands first, as where
fear and anguish are seen. Almost none of these verses is in the narrative:

- **Hands fall with the report.** "We have heard the report of it; our hands fall helpless; anguish
  has taken hold of us, pain as of a woman in labor" (Jer 6:24; cited in 10.12). The king of
  Babylon, "his hands fell helpless; anguish seized him" (Jer 50:43; uncited).
- **Hands and heart together.** "Therefore all hands will be feeble, and every human heart will
  melt. They will be dismayed: pangs and agony will seize them; they will be in anguish like a
  woman in labor … their faces will be aflame" (Isa 13:7–8; uncited). "Every heart will melt, and
  all hands will be feeble; every spirit will faint, and all knees will be weak as water" (Eze 21:7;
  uncited): heart, hands, spirit and knees in one breath. "All hands are feeble, and all knees turn
  to water" (Eze 7:17; uncited).
- **Hands held as in labour.** "Why then do I see every man with his hands on his stomach like a
  woman in labor? Why has every face turned pale?" (Jer 30:6; uncited).
- **Fear aims at the hands.** "Fear not, O Zion; let not your hands grow weak" (Zep 3:16; uncited).
  "For they all wanted to frighten us, thinking, 'Their hands will drop from the work … But now, O
  God, strengthen my hands'" (Neh 6:9; cited in 10.5 and 10.10).
- **A translation going the other way.** "his courage failed" (2Sa 4:1) renders the hand word
  (H3027I, glossed "courage") with "drop" (H7503): literally, his hands dropped. Here the translator
  turned a body word into an inner word. Reading the English alone hides the body.
- **The answer comes through the hands too.** "Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble
  knees" (Isa 35:3); "you have strengthened the weak hands" (Job 4:3); "lift your drooping hands
  and strengthen your weak knees" (Heb 12:12). All are uncited.
