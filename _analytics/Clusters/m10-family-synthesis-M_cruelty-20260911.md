## Cross-strong synergy

Two consistent patterns run across all 24 verses:

1. **Cruelty is defined by the *absence of mercy*, stated explicitly, not implied.** Three of
   H0394's six verses use the near-identical formula "cruel and have no mercy" (Jer 6:23, Jer
   50:42) or "merciless" (Jer 30:14); Prov 12:10 makes the same point as a paradox — "the mercy of
   the wicked is cruel," i.e. even their gentlest disposition still qualifies as cruelty. Cruelty
   here is not a separate vice alongside mercilessness; the texts treat the two as the same fact
   stated twice.
2. **Cruelty/ruthlessness is almost never self-attributed — it is the property of "the other."**
   Every verse in this set describes an external agent: venom (Deut 32:33), the Day of the Lord as
   cosmic judge (Isa 13:9), foreign armies and nations (Jer 6:23, 50:42, Ezek 28:7, 31:12, Isa
   25:3-5), and — dominating H6184's 15 verses — oppressors and adversaries named from the
   position of the sufferer asking to be delivered *from* them (Job 6:23 "redeem me from the hand
   of the ruthless"; Ps 86:14 "a band of ruthless men seeks my life"; Jer 15:21 "redeem you from the
   grasp of the ruthless"). This is structurally the opposite of the guilt subgroup (#1680 v2
   finding): guilt was predominantly *self*-recognized ("he realizes his guilt"); cruelty in this
   data is predominantly *other*-attributed, named by the victim or the narrator, never confessed
   by the cruel party themselves.
3. **H6184 carries a double sense in its own meaning block** ("awe-inspiring... mighty" alongside
   "ruthless") that the verses mostly resolve toward the negative pole (oppressor, adversary,
   tyrant) but Isa 25:3's "strong peoples will glorify you" and the general "mighty" sense suggest
   the same root can describe raw power without the negative charge — worth noting as a possible
   over-wide gloss, similar to the H5375J caveat in the guilt subgroup.
