## The frame the book sets

Before any friend speaks, the book says who Job is, twice:
- the narrator: *"that man was blameless and upright, one who feared God and turned away from evil"* (1:1)
- the Lord: *"there is none like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, who fears God and turns away from evil"* (1:8)

Job's choosing is already named there, in its refusing side: he *"turned away from evil"*.

At the end the book gives a verdict on the friends: *"the Lord said to Eliphaz the Temanite"*: *"My anger burns against you and against your two friends, for you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has"* (42:7). The verdict is about how they spoke *of God*. Elihu is not named in it, either way.

So every evaluation of Job's choices in these speeches is made by someone the book does not let have the last word.

## Job 15:5 — Eliphaz: "your iniquity teaches your mouth, and you choose the tongue of the crafty"

Eliphaz weighs Job by his words. He opens with *"Should a wise man answer with windy knowledge"* (v2), and charges *"you are doing away with the fear of God and hindering meditation before God"* (v4). Then he names the choice: *"For your iniquity teaches your mouth, and you choose the tongue of the crafty"* (v5). He reads it this way:
- something inside, iniquity, teaches the mouth
- the mouth then chooses a crafty tongue

From that he gives the verdict: *"Your own mouth condemns you, and not I; your own lips testify against you"* (v6).

He takes his evaluation further inside. He moves from the mouth to the heart, eyes and spirit: *"Why does your heart carry you away, and why do your eyes flash, that you turn your spirit against God and bring such words out of your mouth?"* (v12–13). For Eliphaz, the words show what the heart has chosen.

His grounds for judging are age and tradition:
- *"Both the gray-haired and the aged are among us, older than your father"* (v10)
- *"what wise men have told, without hiding it from their fathers"* (v18)

His picture of the outcome is the wicked man's end (v20–35).

Set against the book's frame, his charge *"you are doing away with the fear of God"* (v4) runs against the narrator's and the Lord's *"one who feared God"* (1:1, 8). The Lord's verdict in 42:7 is on how Eliphaz spoke of God, not directly on this charge. Still, the man Eliphaz says has chosen the tongue of the crafty is the one the Lord twice calls *"my servant Job"* (42:7–8).

**What this verse shows of choosing, as weighed by another:**
- Another person can read an inner choice from someone's words: the mouth taught by iniquity, the heart that, in his words, *"carry you away"*.
- The reading can be made confidently, with the weight of age and tradition behind it, and still not be the Lord's verdict.

## Job 34:4 — Elihu: "Let us choose what is right; let us know among ourselves what is good"

Elihu is angry with both sides. He *"burned with anger at Job because he justified himself rather than God"*, and at the friends *"because they had found no answer, although they had declared Job to be in the wrong"* (32:2–3).

He calls the listeners to weigh Job's case together, and calls that weighing a choosing: *"Hear my words, you wise men"* … *"for the ear tests words as the palate tastes food"*; *"Let us choose what is right; let us know among ourselves what is good"* (v2–4). The choosing here is not of a course of life. It is a judgement: picking out what is right in the case before them.

The picture is tasting. The ear tests (H0974) as the palate tastes (H2938). Then *"let us know among ourselves what is good"* uses the same words as Barzillai's *"Can I discern what is pleasant and what is not"* (2Sa 19:35): know (H3045), between (H0996), good (H2896). Barzillai said the palate's knowing had gone with age. Elihu uses the palate as the picture for weighing words.

What they are to weigh is Job's own claim. Elihu quotes it: *"I am in the right, and God has taken away my right"* (v5). He judges it at once: *"What man is like Job, who drinks up scoffing like water"*, *"who travels in company with evildoers"* (v7–8). His ground is God's justice: *"far be it from God that he should do wickedness"* (v10); *"his eyes are on the ways of a man, and he sees all his steps"* (v21).

**What this verse shows of choosing, as weighed by another:**
- Weighing another person's choices is itself a choosing, *"what is right"*.
- It is made together (*"let us … among ourselves"*), by testing words as the palate tests food.
- It is a judgement on a case, not a course of life.
- The one who calls for it has already decided (v7–8).

## Job 34:33 — Elihu: "For you must choose, and not I"

Elihu frames a confession and asks whether Job will make it: *"For has anyone said to God"*: *"I have borne punishment; I will not offend any more"*; *"teach me what I do not see; if I have done iniquity, I will do it no more"* (v31–32). Then: *"Will he then make repayment to suit you, because you reject it? For you must choose, and not I; therefore declare what you know"* (v33).

Two things stand in the verse:
- **The refusing word comes back.** *"because you reject it"* uses H3988A, the same verb as *"refuse the evil"* in Isaiah 7:15. Elihu says Job is refusing, and that God will not repay on Job's terms because of it.
- **The choice cannot be made for him.** *"you must choose, and not I"*. Elihu has weighed Job, but he says the choosing itself belongs to Job alone.

Elihu's verdict follows anyway: *"Job speaks without knowledge; his words are without insight"*; *"Would that Job were tried to the end"* (v35–36).

The book records what Job did choose, and when. It is not after Elihu, but after the Lord speaks (38–41):
- *"I have uttered what I did not understand, things too wonderful for me"* (42:3)
- *"I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you; therefore I despise myself, and repent in dust and ashes"* (42:5–6)

*"I despise"* is the same verb, H3988A, as Elihu's *"you reject"*. In the record the verb stands without its own tagged object; the ESV supplies *"myself"*. I read this as Elihu having named the right kind of choice, a refusing, but the choice being made when Job's eye saw the Lord, not under Elihu's argument. The text gives the order (*"now my eye sees you; therefore"*). It does not comment on Elihu.

**What this verse shows of choosing, as weighed by another:**
- The one who weighs can name the choice and press it, but *"you must choose, and not I"*: it belongs to the chooser.
- In Job the choice that was pressed was made when the chooser's eye saw the Lord, and the refusing verb is turned on himself.

## Job 36:21 — Elihu: "this you have chosen rather than affliction"

Elihu speaks *"on God’s behalf"* (v2), and his speech is about what affliction is for:
- *"if they are bound in chains and caught in the cords of affliction, then he declares to them their work … He opens their ears to instruction and commands that they return from iniquity"* (v8–10)
- *"If they listen and serve him, they complete their days in prosperity … But if they do not listen, they perish"* (v11–12)
- *"He delivers the afflicted by their affliction and opens their ear by adversity"* (v15)

In Elihu's account, affliction is how God opens the ear.

Then he turns to Job, and what he sees in him is inward:
- *"you are full of the judgment on the wicked"* (v17)
- *"Beware lest wrath entice you into scoffing"* (v18)
- *"Do not long for the night"* (v20)

His evaluation: *"Take care; do not turn to iniquity, for this you have chosen rather than affliction"* (v21). The choice he sees is a preference, as in Proverbs 16:16 (*"rather than"*). Of the two things before Job, affliction (H6040, the word in v15) and iniquity (H0205), Elihu says Job has taken iniquity rather than accept the affliction as the opening of his ear. The warning *"Take care"* treats the choice as still able to be turned.

He ends by pointing to God: *"who is a teacher like him?"* (v22). "Teacher" here is H4175, from the same root as *"instruct"* in Psalm 25:8, 12 (H3384).

**What this verse shows of choosing, as weighed by another:**
- In suffering, another person can see the choice as a preference: complaint and iniquity taken rather than affliction received as teaching.
- The inner signs he reads are judgement, wrath, scoffing, and longing for the night.
- He warns rather than condemns: *"Take care"*.

## What batch 4 adds to the account of choosing

These are set beside the test and batches 1–3. They stand in their own section: choices weighed by another party.

**New:**
- **Choices can be weighed by another, from the outside in.** Eliphaz reads the inner choice from the mouth, and from the mouth to the heart, eyes and spirit (15:5–6, 12–13). The book does not let that reading stand as the last word. The Lord calls Job *"my servant"*, and rebukes Eliphaz for how he spoke of God (42:7).
- **The weighing is itself a choosing.** *"Let us choose what is right"* (34:4) is a judgement made together, by testing words as the palate tastes food. It uses the same words as Barzillai's knowing between good and bad by taste (2Sa 19:35).
- **The choice belongs to the chooser.** *"you must choose, and not I"* (34:33). The one who weighs can name the choice and press it, but cannot make it.
- **The refusing verb returns at the end.** Elihu's *"you reject"* and Job's *"I despise … and repent"* are the same verb (34:33; 42:6), the verb of *"refuse the evil"* (Isa 7:15). In Job the choice is made when *"now my eye sees you"* (42:5). This stands beside the test reading of Jos 24, where the people chose from what they had seen.
- **Suffering as a place of choosing.** Affliction received as the opening of the ear, or iniquity taken *"rather than"* it (36:15, 21). Elihu reads the choice from inner signs: wrath, scoffing, longing for the night.

**Kept open:**
- Elihu is neither rebuked nor affirmed in 42:7. Whether his evaluations of Job's choices stand is not settled by the text.
- Job's own choosing in his own words is in the wider pull but outside §2a: *"I would choose strangling and death rather than my bones"* (7:15); *"choosing my words with him"* (9:14); *"I chose their way"* (29:25). These are not read here. They will matter when the account sets the weighing of others beside the chooser's own voice.
