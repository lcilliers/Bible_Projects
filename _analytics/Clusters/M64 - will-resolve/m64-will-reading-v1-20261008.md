## Part 1. What the will verses say, read in their passages

### 1.1 The will meets something put before it

In the Hebrew narratives, *ʾāḇâ* almost always stands right after someone has asked, urged, commanded or offered. The passages show who put the thing forward and what it was:
- **A request:**
  - *"Please let us pass through your land"* (Judg 11:17)
  - Sihon was asked to sell food and water and let Israel pass *"only by the road"* (Deu 2:27–29), and *"would not let us pass"* (2:30)
  - Ahaziah: *"Let my servants go with your servants in the ships," but Jehoshaphat was not willing* (1Ki 22:49)
- **Pressing:**
  - The Levite's father-in-law pressed him day after day (Judg 19:7–9), *"But the man would not spend the night"* (19:10)
  - Absalom *"pressed him, but he would not go"* (2Sa 13:25)
- **A command:** Saul's guard was ordered to kill the priests, and they *"would not put out their hand"* (1Sa 22:17). Saul ordered his armor-bearer, who *"would not, for he feared greatly"* (1Sa 31:4).
- **An enticement:** *"If your brother … or your friend who is as your own soul entices you secretly … you shall not yield to him"* (Deu 13:6–8). *"My son, if sinners entice you, do not consent"* (Pro 1:10).
- **A demand:** Ben-hadad demanded wives, children, silver and gold, and the elders said, *"Do not listen or consent"* (1Ki 20:5–8). The king's answer kept a line: *"All that you first demanded of your servant I will do, but this thing I cannot do"* (20:9).
- **A plea:**
  - Tamar: *"No, my brother, do not violate me … But he would not listen to her"* (2Sa 13:12–14)
  - Tamar again, after (13:16)
  - The old man at Gibeah pleading with the men of the city (Judg 19:23–25)
  - Israel calling on Benjamin to give up the guilty (Judg 20:12–13)
- **A warning:** Abner warned Asahel twice, *"Turn aside from following me. Why should I strike you to the ground?"*, and *"he refused to turn aside"* (2Sa 2:21–23).
- **A gift:** water from Bethlehem, brought at the risk of three men's lives (2Sa 23:16–17; 1Ch 11:18–19).
- **God's word, call or rest:** see 1.3.

I read the Hebrew will, in these verses, as the inner act that answers what someone else puts forward. It is seldom shown starting on its own.

### 1.2 What the "no" protects, and what it costs

The same verb is used for refusals that are faithful and for refusals that are violent. The passages show which is which.

**A refusal that keeps faith:**
- The king's servants would not kill the priests of the Lord. Doeg did (1Sa 22:17–18).
- David *"would not put out my hand against the Lord's anointed"* (1Sa 26:23). He sets it beside a prayer: *"as your life was precious this day in my sight, so may my life be precious in the sight of the Lord"* (26:24).
- David *"said longingly, 'Oh, that someone would give me water'"* (2Sa 23:15). When it came, *"he would not drink of it. He poured it out to the Lord and said, 'Far be it from me, O Lord, that I should do this. Shall I drink the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?'"* (23:16–17). His own longing was met, and he refused it for the sake of the men's lives. He gave the reason aloud, before the Lord.
- The faithful refuse enticement and demand (Deu 13:8; Pro 1:10; 1Ki 20:8).

**A refusal that is violent:** Amnon *"would not listen to her, and being stronger than she, he violated her"* (2Sa 13:14). The men of Gibeah *"would not listen"* (Judg 19:25). Here the "would not" is the stronger one shutting out the plea of the weaker.

**A refusal that costs the one who makes it:**
- Asahel would not turn aside, and he died (2Sa 2:21–23).
- The man who *"does not wish to take his brother's wife"*, and *"persists, saying, 'I do not wish to take her'"*, is publicly shamed (Deu 25:7–9).
- Saul and the people *"spared Agag and the best of the sheep … and would not utterly destroy them. All that was despised and worthless they devoted to destruction"* (1Sa 15:9). The refusal was selective. They destroyed what they did not value and kept what they did. God's word follows: *"he has turned back from following me"* (15:11), and *"Samuel was angry, and he cried to the Lord all night"* (15:11).

**A refusal that comes from inside a feeling:**
- **Fear:**
  - The armor-bearer *"would not, for he feared greatly"* (1Sa 31:4; 1Ch 10:4).
  - David *"was afraid of the Lord that day, and he said, 'How can the ark of the Lord come to me?' So David was not willing to take the ark"* (2Sa 6:9–10). Then *"it was told King David, 'The Lord has blessed the household of Obed-edom'"*, and *"David went and brought up the ark of God … with rejoicing"* (6:12). What David heard changed what he was willing to do.
- **Grief:** David fasting for the child, *"the elders of his house stood beside him, to raise him from the ground, but he would not, nor did he eat food with them"* (2Sa 12:17).
- **Jealousy:** *"For jealousy makes a man furious … He will accept no compensation; he will refuse though you multiply gifts"* (Pro 6:34–35).
- **Melted hearts:** *"Yet you would not go up, but rebelled"* (Deu 1:26). Then *"you murmured in your tents"* (1:27) and *"Our brothers have made our hearts melt"* (1:28).

### 1.3 Refusing God: what was offered, and what it was refused for

The passages show that what God offered and was refused was good:
- **The land:** the spies brought fruit and said *"It is a good land"* (Deu 1:25), *"Yet you would not go up"* (1:26).
- **Rest:** *"This is rest; give rest to the weary; and this is repose"; yet they would not hear* (Isa 28:12). *"In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength." But you were unwilling* (Isa 30:15).
- **Being filled:** *"Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it." But my people did not listen to my voice; Israel would not submit to me* (Psa 81:10–11).
- **His Spirit and words:** *"If you turn at my reproof, behold, I will pour out my spirit to you; I will make my words known to you"* (Pro 1:23), before *"you … would have none of my reproof"* (1:25).
- **Cleansing, after reasoning:** *"Come now, let us reason together … though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow … If you are willing and obedient, you shall eat the good of the land; but if you refuse and rebel, you shall be eaten by the sword"* (Isa 1:18–20). This is the one place in the set where the will is invited with "if". Both ways are set out, as in *Life and death set before a person* (10.7).

**What the refusal goes with.** In these passages the "no" to God is placed beside something the people chose or planned instead:
- *"But you were unwilling, and you said, 'No! We will flee upon horses'"* (Isa 30:15–16). The refusal is said in their own words, together with their own plan. The chapter opened with *"stubborn children … who carry out a plan, but not mine"* (30:1). They told the prophets, *"Do not prophesy to us what is right; speak to us smooth things"* (30:10).
- *"Israel would not submit to me. So I gave them over to their stubborn hearts, to follow their own counsels"* (Psa 81:11–12).
- *"Because they hated knowledge and did not choose the fear of the Lord, would have none of my counsel and despised all my reproof, therefore they shall eat the fruit of their way, and have their fill of their own devices"* (Pro 1:29–31).
- *"None of them cast away the detestable things their eyes feasted on"* (Eze 20:8).
- *"a hard forehead and a stubborn heart"* (Eze 3:7).

**Pro 1:29–31 and Psa 81:11–12 put choosing, willing and one's own counsels or devices into a single sentence.** In Proverbs: did not choose, would have none of my counsel, their own devices. In the psalm: would not submit, their stubborn hearts, their own counsels. These are the places where Scripture itself sets the acts side by side. Both say what follows: being given over to one's own counsels, and being filled with one's own devices. They do not say these acts form one sequence in every person, and these notes do not say it either.

**Hardening:**
- *"the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he would not let them go"* (Exo 10:27). Pharaoh had offered part (*"only let your flocks and your herds remain behind"*, 10:24). Moses would not bargain: *"not a hoof shall be left behind"* (10:26).
- *"the Lord your God hardened his spirit and made his heart obstinate"* (Deu 2:30).

In both, the verses name God's act on the heart or spirit beside the person's "would not". They give the purpose: *"that he might give him into your hand"* (Deu 2:30). They do not explain the rest. 13.1 already has the parallel, God acting on hearts (2Th 2:11; Rom 11:8).

### 1.4 The willing heart and the willing spirit

- **Moved from inside:** *"they came, everyone whose heart stirred him, and everyone whose spirit moved him"* (Exo 35:21), then *"All who were of a willing heart brought"* (35:22). Moses had said *"Whoever is of a generous heart, let him bring"* (35:5).
- **After consecration:** *"You have now consecrated yourselves to the Lord. Come near … and all who were of a willing heart brought burnt offerings"* (2Ch 29:31).
- **Willing, together with a plan.** David gives Solomon the plan for the temple. *"All this he made clear to me in writing from the hand of the Lord, all the work to be done according to the plan"* (1Ch 28:19). Then *"Be strong and courageous and do it … with you in all the work will be every willing man who has skill"* (28:20–21). A plan from God and the willing who carry it out stand together.
- **Asked of God:** *"Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me … take not your Holy Spirit from me. Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with a willing spirit"* (Psa 51:10–12). Clean heart, right spirit, Holy Spirit and willing spirit are asked for in one prayer. The willing spirit is something God upholds.
- **Not by compulsion:** Paul would have liked to keep Onesimus, *"but I preferred to do nothing without your consent in order that your goodness might not be by compulsion but of your own accord"* (Phm 13–14). The word in v13 is the will word. In v14 Paul leaves the willing to Philemon, so that the good would be his own.

### 1.5 A will weighed against another's

- **Gen 24.** The servant looks ahead to another person's will: *"Perhaps the woman may not be willing to follow me to this land"* (24:5). Abraham answers with what God will do, *"he will send his angel before you"* (24:7), and with a way out: *"if the woman is not willing to follow you, then you will be free from this oath of mine"* (24:8). The woman's will is allowed for in the plan.
- **2Sa 13:24–28.** Absalom's planning meets David's will. Absalom invited the king, who *"would not go"* (13:25). Then *"Absalom pressed him until he let Amnon and all the king's sons go with him"* (13:27), and he ordered Amnon's death (13:28). The planner presses the will of another until it gives way.
- **2Sa 14:29–31.** *"Joab would not come"*, twice. Absalom set Joab's field on fire, and *"Then Joab arose and went"*. Another way of forcing a will.
- **Pilate.** *"Pilate, wishing to satisfy the crowd, released for them Barabbas"* (Mar 15:15). He had asked them, *"Why? What evil has he done?"* (15:14), and earlier, *"do you want me to release to you the King of the Jews?"* (Joh 18:39). His own judgment was *"I find no guilt in him"* (18:38), and he bent his will to the crowd's.
- **Restrained or encouraged by friends.** *"when Paul wished to go in among the crowd, the disciples would not let him. And even some of the Asiarchs, who were friends of his, sent to him and were urging him not to venture into the theater"* (Act 19:30–31). *"when he wished to cross to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him"* (Act 18:27).
- **Overruled by objection.** *"they wished to set me at liberty … But because the Jews objected, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar"* (Act 28:18–19).
- **Overruled by right.** Diotrephes *"refuses to welcome the brothers, and also stops those who want to and puts them out of the church"* (3Jo 10).

### 1.6 The will that resolves, intends and steers

- **Joseph.** *"being a just man and unwilling to put her to shame, resolved to divorce her quietly. But as he considered these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream"* (Mat 1:19–20). He was unwilling to shame her, he resolved, and he went on considering. Then God spoke into the considering.
- **Herod.** *"when he saw that it pleased the Jews, he proceeded to arrest Peter … intending after the Passover to bring him out to the people. So Peter was kept in prison, but earnest prayer for him was made to God by the church"* (Act 12:3–5). His intention is set beside the church's prayer, and the intention was not carried out (12:6–11).
- **Paul.** *"I wanted to come to you first … I wanted to visit you on my way to Macedonia"* (2Co 1:15–16). Then *"Do I make my plans according to the flesh"* (1:17).
- **The centurion.** *"wishing to save Paul, kept them from carrying out their plan"* (Act 27:43).
- **The pilot's will.** *"they are guided by a very small rudder wherever the will of the pilot directs"* (Jam 3:4). In its passage the picture belongs to the tongue: *"we guide their whole bodies"* (3:3), *"So also the tongue is a small member"* (3:5). The will directs a whole ship; the tongue sets on fire *"the entire course of life"* (3:6).
- **Wanting to know.** *"desiring to know the real reason"* (Act 22:30; 23:28); *"We wish to know therefore what these things mean"* (Act 17:20); *"I would like to hear the man myself"* (Act 25:22); Daniel, *"I desired to know the truth about the fourth beast"* (Dan 7:19).
- **Refusing a role.** *"I refuse to be a judge of these things"* (Act 18:15).
- **Paul's wishes for the churches** are mostly stated wishes in teaching. Their passages give each one a reason:
  - *"I desire then that in every place the men should pray, lifting holy hands without anger or quarreling"* (1Ti 2:8)
  - *"I would have younger widows marry … for some have already strayed after Satan"* (1Ti 5:14–15)
  - *"I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works"* (Tit 3:8)
  - *"I want you to know … what has happened to me has really served to advance the gospel"* (Phi 1:12)
  - *"I would rather not use paper and ink … talk face to face, so that our joy may be complete"* (2Jo 12)
  - *"I want to remind you"* (Jude 5)

### 1.7 The will turned toward harm

- **Toward riches:** *"those who desire to be rich fall into temptation, into a snare, into many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin"* (1Ti 6:9). Beside it: *"godliness with contentment is great gain"* (6:6), and *"flee these things. Pursue righteousness"* (6:11).
- **Toward the world:** *"whoever wishes to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God"* (Jam 4:4). The verses before: *"your passions are at war within you … You desire and do not have, so you murder"* (4:1–2).
- **Passions that draw:** *"when their passions draw them away from Christ, they desire to marry"* (1Ti 5:11). That verse uses another word, so it is noted here, not counted.
- **Imputing intent:** the council to the apostles, *"you intend to bring this man's blood upon us"* (Act 5:28). Peter's answer was *"We must obey God rather than men"* (5:29).

### 1.8 Absolute human will, and the Most High

- **Nebuchadnezzar:** *"Whom he would, he killed, and whom he would, he kept alive; whom he would, he raised up, and whom he would, he humbled"* (Dan 5:19). The next verse gives what happened in him: *"But when his heart was lifted up and his spirit was hardened so that he dealt proudly, he was brought down"* (5:20).
- **On the roof:** *"Is not this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power … for the glory of my majesty?"* (Dan 4:30). Daniel had offered counsel a year before: *"let my counsel be acceptable to you: break off your sins by practicing righteousness"* (4:27).
- **What he was to learn:** *"till you know that the Most High rules the kingdom of men and gives it to whom he will"* (4:25, 32; 4:17; 5:21).
- **The return:** *"my reason returned to me, and I blessed the Most High"* (4:34), and *"he does according to his will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay his hand"* (4:35); *"those who walk in pride he is able to humble"* (4:37).
- **Belshazzar:** *"you … have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this"* (5:22). Knowing was not enough.

### 1.9 God's will (route A, for 13.1)

**His "not willing", and the reasons the verses give:**
- **After intercession:** *"the Lord listened to me that time also. The Lord was unwilling to destroy you"* (Deu 10:10).
- **For a promise:** *"Yet the Lord was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of David his servant, since he promised to give a lamp to him"* (2Ki 8:19; 2Ch 21:7, *"because of the covenant"*).
- **In grace:** *"the Lord was gracious to them and had compassion on them, and he turned toward them, because of his covenant … and would not destroy them"* (2Ki 13:23).
- **Out of love, against a hired curse:** *"the Lord your God would not listen to Balaam; instead the Lord your God turned the curse into a blessing for you, because the Lord your God loved you"* (Deu 23:5; Jos 24:10).
- **Against the self-blessing stubborn heart:** of the one who *"blesses himself in his heart, saying, 'I shall be safe, though I walk in the stubbornness of my heart'"* (Deu 29:19), *"The Lord will not be willing to forgive him"* (29:20).
- **For innocent blood:** *"For he filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the Lord would not pardon"* (2Ki 24:4).

**His will, positively:**
- *"he has mercy on whomever he wills, and he hardens whomever he wills"* (Rom 9:18). *"So then it depends not on human will or exertion, but on God, who has mercy"* (9:16). Then the objection, *"who can resist his will?"* (9:19), and the potter: *"Will what is molded say to its molder, 'Why have you made me like this?'"* (9:20).
- *"Of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth"* (Jam 1:18). Just before: *"desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin"* (1:15), and the Father of lights *"with whom there is no variation"* (1:17).
- *"not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance"* (2Pe 3:9), said against those who *"count slowness"*.
- *"when God desired to show more convincingly … the unchangeable character of his purpose"* (Heb 6:17). This verse is in both the will set and the plan set.
- **The Son chooses:** *"yes, Father, for such was your gracious will. All things have been handed over to me by my Father … and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him"* (Mat 11:26–27; Luk 10:21–22). The Father's gracious will and the Son's choosing stand together. Luke adds *"he rejoiced in the Holy Spirit"* (10:21).
- **The Spirit wills:** *"one and the same Spirit, who apportions to each one individually as he wills"* (1Co 12:11).
- **Gethsemane (A and B):** *"Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me. Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done." And there appeared to him an angel from heaven, strengthening him. And being in agony he prayed more earnestly* (Luk 22:42–44). Before it: *"Pray that you may not enter into temptation"* (22:40).

**Nature (13.4):** *"Is the wild ox willing to serve you? Will he spend the night at your manger?"* (Job 39:9), among the creatures God sets beyond a man's control.

## Part 2. How the will reading works with the plan reading

These are notes for the narrative. Each point says where the verses themselves put the two together, or where the same thing shows in both readings. Nothing is joined that a passage does not join.

### 2.1 What each reading is mostly about

| | Plan verses | Will verses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the act holds | content: what is to be done, a scheme, a design, a purpose | a stance: yes or no to what is put before one (Hebrew); wanting, intending or resolving (Greek) |
| Who starts it | mostly the planner | mostly someone else: a request, plea, command, enticement, gift or call |
| Its most frequent use in these verses | against another person (43 verses of plotting) | refusal (50 of 52 *ʾāḇâ* verses), of God and of people, in faith and in violence |
| Toward God | plotting against him (Hos 7:15; Nah 1:9) | refusing him: his land, rest, filling and counsel |
| God's own | his plans: announced, open to turning, also fixed (13.1 *Purposing*) | his will: not willing to destroy (intercession, promise, covenant, love); not willing to forgive (the self-blessing stubborn heart, innocent blood); wills mercy and hardening; does as he wills |

I read the two as two sides of setting direction. Planning reaches ahead from the person. Willing answers what comes to the person. Neither verse set states this.

### 2.2 Passages where Scripture itself puts them together

1. **Pro 1:29–31.** Did not choose the fear of the Lord, would have none of my counsel, are filled with their own devices: choosing, willing and devising in one sentence. Earlier in the same chapter, *"if sinners entice you, do not consent. If they say, 'Come with us, let us lie in wait for blood'"* (1:10–11) puts the will's "no" right against a group plot. That is plotting as written in 10.10.
2. **Psa 81:11–12.** Would not submit, given over to stubborn hearts, to follow their own counsels.
3. **Isa 30:1, 9, 15–16.** A plan *"but not mine"*; unwilling to hear; unwilling, and *"No! We will flee upon horses."* The refusal and the people's own plan are in their own mouth together.
4. **1Ch 28:19–21.** The plan from the hand of the Lord, and *"every willing man who has skill"*. This is the positive pairing: a plan given, and the willing who carry it out.
5. **Gen 24:5–8.** Planning ahead allows for another person's will (*"Perhaps the woman may not be willing"*).
6. **2Sa 13:24–28.** A planner presses another's will until it gives way, and the plan goes on to murder.
7. **Mat 1:19–20.** Unwilling, then resolved, while still considering, then redirected by God.
8. **Act 12:3–5.** Herod's intending is set beside the church's prayer.
9. **2Co 1:15–17.** Wanting, then plans, then the question of a divided word.
10. **Act 27:42–43.** One man's wish against a group's plan.
11. **Dan 4:27–35.** Counsel offered and not taken; the human will made absolute; the Most High *"does according to his will"*; reason returned.

### 2.3 The same thing showing in both readings

- **Imputing intent.** *"you and the Jews intend to rebel"* (Neh 6:6, plan set) and *"you intend to bring this man's blood upon us"* (Act 5:28, will set). In both, an intention is pinned on the other side by those set against them.
- **The potter.** *"like the clay in the potter's hand, so are you in my hand"* (Jer 18:6, God's plans, open to turning). *"Will what is molded say to its molder"* (Rom 9:20, God's will, not resisted). The same picture serves two different points. It is noted here, not joined.
- **Heb 6:17** is in both sets. God *"desired"* (will) to show the unchangeable character of *"his purpose"* (plan).
- **The heart and spirit named with the act.**
  - Planning: *"The heart of man plans his way"* (Pro 16:9); *"plan evil things in their heart"* (Psa 140:2).
  - Willing: the heart or spirit hardened, stubborn or lifted up (Exo 10:27; Deu 2:30; Eze 3:7; Psa 81:12; Deu 29:19; Dan 5:20); the willing heart and spirit (Exo 35:21–22; 2Ch 29:31; Psa 51:12).
  - So the will verses name the state of the heart and spirit more often than the plan verses do. Chapters 4 (the heart) and 6 (the spirit) should take this.
- **Others acting on the act.**
  - Planning: done together, and plotting.
  - Willing: pressing (Judg 19:7–9; 2Sa 13:25–27), enticing (Deu 13:6–8; Pro 1:10), the crowd (Mar 15:15), friends restraining or encouraging (Act 19:30–31; 18:27), objection (Act 28:19), consent sought so that the good is free (Phm 14).
- **Fear.** In the plan set, fear is mostly in the one plotted against (*"terror on every side"*, Psa 31:13). In the will set, fear stops the willing: the armor-bearer (1Sa 31:4); David and the ark (2Sa 6:9–10, changed by what he heard, 6:12); melted hearts (Deu 1:26–28). For 10.12.
- **Wanting and refusing.** David's longing for the water, then his refusal for the men's sake (2Sa 23:15–17). For 10.6 *Wanting*, beside choosing.
- **Grief** (2Sa 12:17) and **jealousy** (Pro 6:34–35) fix the will in place. For 10.1 and 10.13.
- **Knowing is not enough.** Belshazzar *"knew all this"* and did not humble his heart (Dan 5:22). In the plan set, God *"understands every plan"* (1Ch 28:9). The will set adds that the person's own knowing does not move the will.
- **The outcome is not held.** Plans fail or are overruled (Pro 16:9; Isa 8:10; Act 5:38). Wills are overruled as well: Herod's intention (Act 12), the Romans' wish to free Paul (Act 28:18–19), Paul's wish to enter the theater (Act 19:30). And human will is set under God's: *"it depends not on human will or exertion"* (Rom 9:16); *"not my will, but yours"* (Luk 22:42).

### 2.4 Change of character (#1919)

| Word | Faces in the will set | What differs, from the passages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *ʾāḇâ* | refusing God (Isa 30:15); refusing sin (Pro 1:10); refusing to harm the anointed (1Sa 26:23); refusing a gift for others' sake (2Sa 23:17); refusing a plea by force (2Sa 13:14); refusing in grief (2Sa 12:17); God not willing to destroy (2Ki 8:19); God not willing to forgive (Deu 29:20) | What is refused, and for whose sake. The passages give the reason each time: faith, fear, grief, honour, force, covenant, love, a stubborn heart |
