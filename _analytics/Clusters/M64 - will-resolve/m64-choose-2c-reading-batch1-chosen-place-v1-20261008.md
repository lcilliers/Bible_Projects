## Deuteronomy 12:18: "And you shall rejoice before the Lord your God in all that you undertake"

The chapter opens by clearing away the places of the nations: *"You shall surely destroy all the places where the nations whom you shall dispossess served their gods"* (v2). Then: *"You shall not worship the Lord your God in that way. But you shall seek the place that the Lord your God will choose"* (v4–5). Two measures are set against each other: *"You shall not do according to all that we are doing here today, everyone doing whatever is right in his own eyes"* (v8). The place God chooses replaces the places a person sees for himself: *"Take care that you do not offer your burnt offerings at any place that you see, but at the place that the Lord will choose"* (v13–14).

Rejoicing is commanded three times in the chapter, each time at the chosen place and *"before the Lord"*:
- *"there you shall eat before the Lord your God, and you shall rejoice, you and your households, in all that you undertake, in which the Lord your God has blessed you"* (v7)
- *"you shall rejoice before the Lord your God, you and your sons and your daughters, your male servants and your female servants, and the Levite that is within your towns, since he has no portion or inheritance with you"* (v12)
- *"you shall eat them before the Lord your God in the place that the Lord your God will choose, you and your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant, and the Levite … And you shall rejoice before the Lord your God in all that you undertake"* (v18)

The ground of the joy is named in v7: *"in which the Lord your God has blessed you"*. The place is where the joy is had. The blessing is what it is about.

The joy is not had alone. The household, the servants and the Levite are named in it each time. The verse after v18 turns the same thought into a warning: *"Take care that you do not neglect the Levite as long as you live in your land"* (v19).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- At the place God chooses, the people are commanded to rejoice. It is joy *"before the Lord"*.
- The joy rests on what the Lord has blessed (v7), not on the place.
- It is shared. The servants and the Levite, who has no inheritance, are named in it.
- The chosen place is set against *"whatever is right in his own eyes"* (v8). This sits with §2a, "whose measure".

## Deuteronomy 12:21: "you may eat within your towns whenever you desire"

The verse belongs to a passage on ordinary meat, set apart from the holy offerings. The holy things must be eaten at the chosen place (v17–18, v26). Ordinary meat need not be:
- *"you may slaughter and eat meat within any of your towns, as much as you desire, according to the blessing of the Lord your God"* (v15)
- *"and you say, 'I will eat meat,' because you crave meat, you may eat meat whenever you desire"* (v20)
- *"If the place that the Lord your God will choose to put his name there is too far from you, then you may kill any of your herd or your flock … and you may eat within your towns whenever you desire"* (v21)

"Crave" in v20 is the soul with *ʾāwāh* (*to desire*; H5315L with H0183). "Whenever you desire" in v20 and v21 is the soul with *ʾawwāh* (*desire*; H0185). The soul's desire for meat is spoken of plainly, and it is allowed.

The one limit is the blood: *"Only be sure that you do not eat the blood, for the blood is the life, and you shall not eat the life with the flesh"* (v23).

**A correction to the HIB/qualifier file.** That file gives this row the relation "setting: happens at the chosen place". The verse says the opposite. The desire is satisfied away from the place, because the place is *"too far from you"*. The row should read "away from the chosen place".

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- The soul's desire for food is given room, and the room is given by the distance from the chosen place.
- The chosen place governs the holy offerings. It does not govern ordinary appetite.
- Desire and the blessing are named together: *"as much as you desire, according to the blessing of the Lord your God"* (v15).

## Deuteronomy 14:23: "that you may learn to fear the Lord your God always"

The tithe is to be eaten at the chosen place: *"And before the Lord your God, in the place that he will choose, to make his name dwell there, you shall eat the tithe … that you may learn to fear the Lord your God always"* (v23).

The fear here is learned, and it is learned at a meal. The next verses describe that meal. If the place is too far, the tithe is turned into money and taken there, and then: *"spend the money for whatever you desire—oxen or sheep or wine or strong drink, whatever your appetite craves. And you shall eat there before the Lord your God and rejoice, you and your household"* (v26).

"Whatever you desire" is the soul with *ʾāwāh* (H5315I with H0183). "Whatever your appetite craves" is the soul that asks (H5315L with H7592). The same eating that teaches fear satisfies the soul's appetite and is a time of rejoicing. The passage does not put these against each other.

The chapter ends with those who have nothing: *"the Levite … and the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow … shall come and eat and be filled"* (v29).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- At the chosen place, fear of the Lord is learned (*yārēʾ*, *to fear: revere*; H3372H).
- It is learned at a feast, in the same eating where the soul's appetite is satisfied and the household rejoices (v26).
- The verse gives *"always"*: what is learned at the place is to last beyond it.

## Deuteronomy 16:11 and 16:15: "you shall rejoice before the Lord your God" and "you will be altogether joyful"

The chapter sets the three feasts at the chosen place (v2, v6, v11, v15, v16). They do not carry the same inner act.

**Passover** is a feast of remembering, with *"the bread of affliction—for you came out of the land of Egypt in haste—that all the days of your life you may remember the day when you came out of the land of Egypt"* (v3).

**The Feast of Weeks** is a feast of rejoicing: *"And you shall rejoice before the Lord your God, you and your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant, the Levite who is within your towns, the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow who are among you, at the place that the Lord your God will choose"* (v11). The next verse joins a remembering to it: *"You shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt; and you shall be careful to observe these statutes"* (v12). The list in v11 includes the servant and the sojourner. The verse after it reminds the one rejoicing that he was a slave.

**The Feast of Booths:** *"You shall rejoice in your feast"* (v14), with the same list. Then: *"For seven days you shall keep the feast to the Lord your God at the place that the Lord will choose, because the Lord your God will bless you in all your produce and in all the work of your hands, so that you will be altogether joyful"* (v15).

"Altogether joyful" is *śāmēaḥ* (*glad*; H8056) with *ʾak* (H0389), "only" or "surely". In v15 the joy is the outcome the blessing is given for: *"will bless you … so that you will be altogether joyful"*.

The section ends: *"They shall not appear before the Lord empty-handed. Every man shall give as he is able, according to the blessing of the Lord your God that he has given you"* (v16–17).

**What these verses show of others around the choosing:**
- At the chosen place there is a feast for remembering (Passover) and feasts for rejoicing (Weeks, Booths).
- The rejoicing is commanded (v11, v14), and it is also promised as the end of the blessing (v15).
- The joy takes in those with least: the servant, the Levite, the sojourner, the fatherless and the widow.
- Beside that joy stands the command to remember being a slave (v12).

## Deuteronomy 17:10: "And you shall be careful to do according to all that they direct you"

The passage is about a case *"too difficult for you"* (v8): *"then you shall arise and go up to the place that the Lord your God will choose"*. There the priests and the judge *"shall declare to you the decision"* (v9). "Difficult" is *pālāʾ* (H6381), to be beyond one.

The person's own judgement has reached its limit, and the decision comes from the chosen place. What is asked of him then:
- *"you shall be careful to do according to all that they direct you"* (v10). "Careful" is *šāmar* (*to keep: careful*; H8104J).
- *"You shall not turn aside from the verdict that they declare to you, either to the right hand or to the left"* (v11).

Set against carefulness is presumption: *"The man who acts presumptuously by not obeying the priest … or the judge, that man shall die"* (v12). "Presumptuously" is *zādôn* (H2087). The passage ends in those who hear of it: *"And all the people shall hear and fear and not act presumptuously again"* (v13).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- When a matter is beyond a person, the decision is sought at the place God chooses.
- What is asked is carefulness to do what is declared there, and not turning aside.
- Presumption is the opposite, and it is punished by death.
- The people's fear follows on hearing of it, and its stated end is that they *"not act presumptuously again"* (v13).

## Deuteronomy 18:6: "and he may come when he desires"

Two choosings stand in the passage. First the tribe: *"For the Lord your God has chosen him out of all your tribes to stand and minister in the name of the Lord, him and his sons for all time"* (v5). Then the place: a Levite living in any town *"may come when he desires—to the place that the Lord will choose, and ministers in the name of the Lord his God, like all his fellow Levites"* (v6–7).

"When he desires" is literally "with all the desire of his soul": *kol* (all; H3605) with *ʾawwāh* (desire; H0185) and *nepeš* (*soul: appetite*; H5315L). It is the same desire of the soul that is given room for meat in Deu 12:20–21. Here it is turned toward the chosen place.

The Levite has no land: *"They shall have no inheritance among their brothers; the Lord is their inheritance, as he promised them"* (v2). The law secures his share when he comes: *"then he may have equal portions to eat"* (v8).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- The soul's whole desire can move a man toward the place God chose.
- The man's coming is left to that desire: *"he may come when he desires"*.
- He is one of a tribe God chose to minister, and he is given an equal share when he comes.
- I read the verse as joining the two choosings in one man: he was chosen to serve, and he desires to go to the place chosen for the serving. The verse places both in the passage. It does not comment on them.

## 1 Kings 8:48 (and 2 Chronicles 6:38): "if they repent with all their heart and with all their soul … and pray to you toward … the city that you have chosen"

Solomon dedicates the house. Before the prayer he recalls two choosings: *"I chose no city out of all the tribes of Israel in which to build a house … But I chose David to be over my people Israel"* (v16). He also recalls David's heart: *"you did well that it was in your heart"* (v18). David was not allowed to build (v19).

The prayer that follows is a run of cases. In each, someone far from the house prays toward it, and Solomon asks God to hear in heaven. The heart is named throughout:
- *"each knowing the affliction of his own heart and stretching out his hands toward this house"* (v38)
- *"render to each whose heart you know, according to all his ways (for you, you only, know the hearts of all the children of mankind)"* (v39)
- *"that they may fear you all the days that they live in the land"* (v40)

The case of v48 is the hardest one, captivity: *"If they sin against you—for there is no one who does not sin—and you are angry with them and give them to an enemy"* (v46). Then the inner turning, in steps:
- *"yet if they turn their heart in the land to which they have been carried captive"* (v47). Literally, "bring it back to their heart" (*šûb*, H7725N, with the heart).
- *"and repent and plead with you in the land of their captors, saying, 'We have sinned and have acted perversely and wickedly'"* (v47)
- *"if they repent with all their heart and with all their soul in the land of their enemies … and pray to you toward their land, which you gave to their fathers, the city that you have chosen, and the house that I have built for your name"* (v48)

"Repent" in v47 and v48 is *šûb* (*to return: repent*; H7725O), the same verb as "turn their heart". The turning happens in the enemy's land, far from the city. The whole heart and the whole soul are in it. The prayer is directed toward the chosen city.

What Solomon asks of God in return: *"forgive your people … and grant them compassion in the sight of those who carried them captive"* (v50). At the end he prays for the heart once more: *"that he may incline our hearts to him"* (v58), and *"Let your heart therefore be wholly true to the Lord our God"* (v61). The day ends with the people going home *"joyful and glad of heart for all the goodness that the Lord had shown"* (v66).

**2 Chronicles 6:38** gives the same words, with *"the land of their captivity"*. Chronicles closes the prayer differently: *"let your saints rejoice in your goodness"* (6:41).

**What these verses show of others around the choosing:**
- The chosen city is the direction of prayer for those who are far from it, even in an enemy's land.
- The inner act is a turning: first *"turn their heart"*, then repentance in words, then repentance *"with all their heart and with all their soul"*.
- The prayer is made knowing *"the affliction of his own heart"* (v38), before the one who *"only"* knows the hearts (v39).
- Solomon asks God to incline the heart (v58) and calls for a heart *"wholly true"* (v61). Both are asked in the same prayer.

## Nehemiah 1:9: "but if you return to me … I will gather them and bring them to the place that I have chosen"

Nehemiah is in Susa when he hears of Jerusalem: *"The remnant there … is in great trouble and shame. The wall of Jerusalem is broken down"* (v3). His response is inward first: *"As soon as I heard these words I sat down and wept and mourned for days, and I continued fasting and praying before the God of heaven"* (v4).

His prayer confesses his own part: *"confessing the sins of the people of Israel, which we have sinned against you. Even I and my father's house have sinned"* (v6). Then he asks God to remember a word: *"Remember the word that you commanded your servant Moses, saying, 'If you are unfaithful, I will scatter you among the peoples, but if you return to me and keep my commandments and do them, though your outcasts are in the uttermost parts of heaven, from there I will gather them and bring them to the place that I have chosen, to make my name dwell there'"* (v8–9).

"Return" is *šûb* (H7725O), the same tag as "repent" in 1Ki 8:47–48. Here the return is *"to me"*, to God himself. The chosen place is where God brings those who return. The verse says nothing of the place before that.

The prayer ends with those who pray with him: *"your servants who delight to fear your name"* (v11). The narrative already cites this phrase in 10.9.

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- The return is to God. The chosen place is where he gathers those who return.
- The returning is joined to keeping: *"return to me and keep my commandments and do them"*.
- The one who prays mourns, fasts and confesses his own sin with the people's (v4, v6).
- Nehemiah asks God to remember his own word (v8). He prays from that promise.

## What batch 1 adds

This is the side of the people around a place God chose. The verses do not say what the people feel about the choosing itself. They say what the people are to do and be at the place, toward it, or away from it.

**At the place:**
- rejoicing before the Lord, shared with the household, the servants, the Levite, the sojourner, the fatherless and the widow (Deu 12:7, 12, 18; 16:11, 14, 15)
- learning to fear the Lord, at a meal where the soul's appetite is satisfied (Deu 14:23, 26)
- remembering, at Passover and beside the rejoicing (Deu 16:3, 12)
- carefulness to do what is declared there, when a matter is too hard, against presumption (Deu 17:8–13)

**Toward the place:**
- the whole desire of a Levite's soul, which may bring him there (Deu 18:6)
- the turning of the heart, and repentance with all the heart and soul, prayed toward the city from an enemy's land (1Ki 8:47–48; 2Ch 6:37–38)

**Away from the place:**
- the soul's desire for food, given room because the place is far (Deu 12:20–21)

**Back to the place:**
- those who return to God and keep his commandments are gathered to it (Neh 1:9)

**The ground named.** In Deu 12:7, 16:15 and 16:17 the rejoicing and the giving are tied to the Lord's blessing, not to the place.

**Whose measure.** The chosen place stands against *"whatever is right in his own eyes"* (Deu 12:8) and *"any place that you see"* (12:13). This is the §2a theme of the measure by which a choice is judged. Here it is the people's measure set against God's choice.
