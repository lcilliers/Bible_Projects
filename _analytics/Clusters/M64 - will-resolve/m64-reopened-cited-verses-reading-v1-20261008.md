# M64: the 27 verses read in full, and their impact (v1, 2026-10-08)

**Escalation:** #1987 (cross-referenced to #1979; separated from #1986) · **Status:** for the researcher's review. Nothing is woven.

**Asked (researcher, verbatim):** *"add all the verses in 1,2 and 3 to a csv, then read them all in full as you suppose to do."*

**Scope (#1987):** the impact of these 27 verses, read in full, on the study as a whole: the narrative, the M64 accounts and faces, the open threads, #1986 (will) and #1979 (M47). How the narrative cites them is one part of that, not the whole.

**Why these verses:** they were set aside, or left unread, because the narrative already cites them. Researcher, 2026-10-08: *"assuming the verse is already in the narrative and therefore should be ignored, is wrong."*
- **Set 1:** the 9 cited choose verses not read in §2a to §2c. They are Deu 30:19, Job 7:15, Pro 1:29, Isa 42:1, Isa 66:4, Jer 8:3, Luk 10:42, Act 10:41 and Phili 1:22.
- **Set 2:** Act 10:41, whose OT-93 park note assumed its citation covers it. It is already in set 1.
- **Set 3:** the 21 cited verses shared by M64 and M47. Job 7:15, Isa 42:1 and Phili 1:22 are also in set 1.
- **Total:** 27 verses, listed in [`m64-reopened-cited-verses-v1-20261008.csv`](m64-reopened-cited-verses-v1-20261008.csv).

**How it was done:**
- Each verse was read in its whole passage, using the ESV text in iba.db `verse`. Each was read for its M64 word, in the passage's own genre and setting, with no frame.
- The narrative's use of each verse was then checked against the current chapter files.
- Word links were checked against the Strong's tags on the verse records.
- Readings beyond what the text says are marked "I read".

## Set 1: the cited choose verses

### Deuteronomy 30:19: "Therefore choose life"

This is Moses' last charge. The choice is set out twice: *"I have set before you today life and good, death and evil"* (v15), and *"life and death, blessing and curse"* (v19).

The passage says what choosing life is: *"loving the Lord your God, obeying his voice and holding fast to him, for he is your life and length of days"* (v20). Choosing life is a bond to a person. The person is called "your life".

The other way is described from inside: *"if your heart turns away, and you will not hear, but are drawn away to worship other gods"* (v17). The heart turns, the hearing stops, and the person is drawn.

Before the choice is offered, the passage says it can be done: *"the word is very near you. It is in your mouth and in your heart, so that you can do it"* (v14).

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Life set before a person*) and the 10.7 opening quote v19 for the choice itself. Here the narrative does use the choosing.

**Not yet carried:**
- v14, that the word is already in the mouth and heart, "so that you can do it"
- v17, the heart turning away and being "drawn away"
- v20, choosing life spelled out as loving, obeying and holding fast

### Job 7:15: "so that I would choose strangling and death rather than my bones"

Job speaks *"in the anguish of my spirit … in the bitterness of my soul"* (v11). The choice follows from what is done to him at night: *"When I say, 'My bed will comfort me' … then you scare me with dreams and terrify me with visions, so that I would choose strangling and death"* (vv13–15). He goes on: *"I loathe my life; I would not live forever. Leave me alone"* (v16).

The ESV "I" in v15 is *nepheš* (H5315I): in the Hebrew it is the soul that would choose. It is a choice the soul would make. Job does not act on it.

It comes out of terror, and it is spoken to God: *"Why have you made me your mark?"* (v20).

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Wanting to die*) quotes vv15–16 with the choosing word.

**Not yet carried:**
- that the soul is the chooser
- that the choice is set off by terror sent in dreams (v14)
- that it is spoken as complaint to God, not acted on

### Proverbs 1:29: "and did not choose the fear of the Lord"

Wisdom calls in the street (vv20–21). Not choosing the fear of the Lord stands among the other refusals:
- *"you refused to listen"* (v24)
- *"would have none of my reproof"* (v25)
- *"they hated knowledge"* (v29)
- *"would have none of my counsel and despised all my reproof"* (v30; "would" there is H0014, in the #1986 will set)

**What is refused is the fear of the Lord itself.** This fear is something a person can choose or leave.

The refusal has a history. It is chosen over time: *"How long, O simple ones, will you love being simple?"* (v22).

It has an offer on the other side: *"If you turn at my reproof, behold, I will pour out my spirit to you"* (v23).

Its outcome is their own choice returned to them: *"they shall eat the fruit of their way, and have their fill of their own devices"* (v31).

The fear they refused comes back as a different fear: *"I will mock when terror strikes you"* (v26). The verse does not join the two. I read the passage as setting the fear of the Lord, refused, beside terror that comes.

**Narrative:** 10.2 (*Knowing and hearing*) quotes v29, with the choosing word, for knowledge refused.

**Not yet carried:**
- the fear of the Lord as an object of choice (for 10.7 and 10.12)
- the refusals that stand beside it in the passage
- the outcome "have their fill of their own devices"

### Isaiah 42:1: "my chosen, in whom my soul delights"

God speaks of his servant. In one verse he chooses, delights with his soul, and gives his Spirit: *"my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him"*.

The passage then describes the chosen one's bearing:
- *"He will not cry aloud or lift up his voice"* (v2)
- *"a bruised reed he will not break, and a faintly burning wick he will not quench"* (v3)
- *"He will not grow faint or be discouraged till he has established justice in the earth"* (v4)

God calls, holds and keeps him: *"I have called you in righteousness; I will take you by the hand and keep you"* (v6).

**Narrative:** Ch 9 and Ch 11 §8 quote only "my soul delights", for God having a soul. The choosing is not used.

**Not yet carried:**
- For 13.1: God's choosing joined with his soul's delight and the giving of the Spirit (route A).
- For 10.7 *Being chosen*: the chosen one's own bearing. He is quiet, he is gentle with the bruised and the faint, and he does not grow faint or discouraged. I read this as the inner bearing of the one chosen. The verse states it of this servant only.

### Isaiah 66:4: "I also will choose harsh treatment for them"

Choosing runs both ways in vv3–4:
- *"These have chosen their own ways, and their soul delights in their abominations"* (v3)
- *"I also will choose harsh treatment for them and bring their fears upon them"* (v4)
- God's reason: *"when I called, no one answered, when I spoke, they did not listen; but they did what was evil in my eyes and chose that in which I did not delight"* (v4)

The passage opens with the one God looks to: *"he who is humble and contrite in spirit and trembles at my word"* (v2).

So the passage holds two kinds of fear:
- trembling at his word, in the one he looks to (v2)
- "their fears" brought on those who chose their own ways (v4)

**Narrative:**
- 10.12 (*What changes it*) quotes "bring their fears upon them".
- 10.7 points to Isa 66:4 in *Whose measure* (the delight word).
- 10.7 v13 and 13.1 v4 already carry God's choosing set against theirs, from the §2a reading of 66:3.

**Not yet carried:** most of the verse is in place. The pairing of trembling at his word (v2) with their fears (v4) is not drawn anywhere. I read it as the passage's own pairing; it is not stated as one in either verse.

### Jeremiah 8:3: "Death shall be preferred to life"

This follows the Topheth judgment (7:30–34). The bones of kings, priests, prophets and people are brought out and spread *"before the sun and the moon and all the host of heaven, which they have loved and served, which they have gone after, and which they have sought and worshiped"* (8:2).

Then: *"Death shall be preferred to life by all the remnant that remains of this evil family in all the places where I have driven them"* (8:3).

"Preferred" is H0977 in the Niphal: death *shall be chosen* (literal sense of the Niphal) rather than life. The remnant prefer death, and the Lord declares it as part of the judgment.

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Wanting to die*) quotes the verse with the word "preferred". The use is in its own context.

**Not yet carried:** that this preference is stated as part of a judgment the Lord declares, after the people had loved, served and gone after other things (v2). The verse does not say the one leads to the other.

### Luke 10:42: "Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her"

Mary *"sat at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching"* (v39). Martha was *"distracted with much serving"*. She asks him to intervene: *"Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her then to help me"* (v40).

Jesus answers:
- *"you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary"* (vv41–42)
- *"Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her"* (v42)

The choosing is seen in what Mary is doing: sitting and listening. Jesus is the one who calls it a choice. He then protects it against another person's demand: it "will not be taken away from her".

The verb is in the middle voice (G1586, V-AMI): she chose it for herself.

**Narrative:** 10.1 (*What anxiety does*, *Care that is good*) and Ch 11 §4 quote vv41–42 up to "one thing is necessary". These are about anxiety. The choosing half of v42 is not quoted.

**Not yet carried:** for 10.7, this choice:
- is seen in a posture (sitting and listening)
- is named a choice by Jesus
- stands against being distracted by many things
- is guarded against another's demand

### Acts 10:41: "to us who had been chosen by God as witnesses"

Peter speaks in Cornelius's house. The risen Christ appeared *"not to all the people but to us who had been chosen by God as witnesses, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead"* (v41). *"And he commanded us to preach to the people and to testify"* (v42).

G4401 means chosen beforehand. God chose the witnesses beforehand. They were chosen to see, to share meals with the risen Christ, and to testify.

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Christ first*) quotes "ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead", for the risen body. The choosing is not used.

**Reading:** the verse fits the face *chosen for a task* already in 10.7 *Being chosen*. I find no new face in it. It says little about the inner life of those chosen.

### Philippians 1:22: "Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell"

Paul writes from prison: *"to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain"* (v21). Then:
- *"If I am to live in the flesh, that means fruitful labor for me. Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell"* (v22)
- *"I am hard pressed between the two. My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better"* (v23)
- *"But to remain in the flesh is more necessary on your account"* (v24)
- *"Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with you all"* (v25)

The choice is held open between two goods. His own desire leans one way, and others' need leans the other. He is "hard pressed".

He does not settle it by choosing. He settles it by being "convinced" and coming to "know".

**Narrative:** Ch 7 (*Flesh*) quotes only "If I am to live in the flesh, that means fruitful labor for me". The choosing is not used.

**Not yet carried:** for 10.7:
- a choice that cannot be told, between two goods
- desire set against the need of others
- the matter resolved by conviction, not by an act of choosing

(It is in the choose group "A person chooses between death and life" with Job 7:15 and Jer 8:3. That is a sorting group, not a link the verses state.)

## Set 3: the cited verses shared by M64 and M47

The M64 words here are mostly plan, devise, count, intention and willing, not "choose".

### Genesis 6:5 and 8:21: "every intention of the thoughts of his heart"

**Gen 6:5:** the Lord sees that *"every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually"*. He *"regretted that he had made man … and it grieved him to his heart"* (6:6).

**Gen 8:21:** after Noah's offering, *"the Lord said in his heart, 'I will never again curse the ground because of man, for the intention of man's heart is evil from his youth'"*. The same intention that came before the flood is now named, and the Lord answers it by holding back the curse.

**Narrative:** 10.4 *The inclination: a shaped thing*, Ch 4, Ch 11 and Ch 12 use the M64 word itself (*yēṣer*, H3336) as their subject, in these verses' own setting.

**Reading:** both verses are carried for their M64 word. I find nothing further in the passages for M64.

### Exodus 35:35: "a designer … or skilled designer"

God fills Bezalel *"with the Spirit of God, with skill, with intelligence, with knowledge, and with all craftsmanship, to devise artistic designs"* (35:31–32). He *"inspired him to teach"* (v34), and *"filled them with skill"* (v35). "Devise" and "designer" are H2803G.

Then *"every craftsman in whose mind the Lord had put skill, everyone whose heart stirred him up to come to do the work"* (36:2).

**Narrative:** 10.4 and Ch 11 quote "God fills craftsmen with skill to devise": the M64 word, in its setting.

**Not yet carried:** 36:2 sets God putting skill in the mind beside the heart that "stirred him up to come". This is a willing to come. It belongs to the will reading (#1986), where Exo 35:5 and 35:22 (H5081G, "willing") are in the set.

### Deuteronomy 2:30: "But Sihon the king of Heshbon would not let us pass"

Moses sent *"words of peace"* (v26) and asked to pass by the road only (vv27–29). *"But Sihon … would not let us pass by him, for the Lord your God hardened his spirit and made his heart obstinate, that he might give him into your hand"* (v30).

"Would" is H0014, *be willing*. Sihon's refusal is the act. The verse gives its cause as God's act on his spirit and heart, and gives God's purpose.

**Narrative:**
- Ch 6 (*The human spirit*), Ch 9 and Ch 11 quote only the hardening.
- The refusal, which is the M64 word, is not quoted.

**Not yet carried:** the unwillingness and its stated cause. This verse is in the #1986 will set. Exo 10:27 in the same set has the same two things in one verse: *"the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he would not let them go"*.

### 2 Samuel 14:14: "he devises means so that the banished one will not remain an outcast"

The setting is a staged plea:
- Joab knew *"the king's heart went out to Absalom"* (v1).
- He told the woman of Tekoa to *"Pretend to be a mourner"* (v2), and *"put the words in her mouth"* (v3).
- Inside the story she tells, she asks: *"Why then have you planned such a thing against the people of God?"* (v13).
- Then she says v14: *"We must all die … But God will not take away life, and he devises means so that the banished one will not remain an outcast"*. "Devises" is H2803G.
- David sees through it: *"Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?"* (v19). *"In order to change the course of things your servant Joab did this"* (v20).

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*What death is*) and 10.4 (*Small, passing, and wholly seen*) quote v14, for God devising means and for death. They do not mention that the saying sits inside Joab's staged appeal.

**Reading:** in this passage, devising goes on in three places:
- Joab's plan to move the king
- the king's own decision, as the woman puts it (v13, "planned")
- God's devising, which she declares (v14)

I read v14 as a true saying put to work in a human scheme. The narrative's use of v14 is not wrong. The setting adds the human devising around it.

### 2 Samuel 19:19: "Let not my lord hold me guilty"

Shimei comes down to meet David: *"Let not my lord hold me guilty or remember how your servant did wrong … Do not let the king take it to heart. For your servant knows that I have sinned"* (vv19–20). Abishai wants him put to death (v21). David answers: *"You shall not die"*, and *"gave him his oath"* (v23).

"Hold" is H2803H, *count*: Shimei asks the king not to count his guilt against him, and not to take it to heart.

**Narrative:** 10.10 (*Relating to others*, opening) quotes v19 in its own setting, the wrongdoer asking not to be charged.

**Reading:** carried for its M64 word. Psa 32:2 uses the same word of God (below).

### 1 Chronicles 28:9: "understands every plan and thought"

David charges Solomon: *"know the God of your father and serve him with a whole heart and with a willing mind, for the Lord searches all hearts and understands every plan and thought. If you seek him, he will be found by you, but if you forsake him, he will cast you off forever"*.

"Plan" is H3336, the same word as "intention" in Gen 6:5.

The passage around it is God's choosing: *"the Lord God of Israel chose me"*, *"he has chosen Solomon"* (vv4–6), and *"the Lord has chosen you to build"* (v10). Those verses are in the choose set.

**Narrative:** 10.4 (*Small, passing, and wholly seen*) and 10.9 (opening) quote "searches all hearts and understands every plan and thought" and the condition. This is the M64 word, in its setting.

**Not yet carried:** "with a willing mind". "Willing" there is H2655, *delighting*, which is tagged M18, not M64. It is a will word outside this cluster. It is noted for #1986 alongside *thelō* and *thelēma*.

### 1 Chronicles 29:18: "keep forever such purposes and thoughts in the hearts of your people"

David prays after the people's gifts for the temple:
- *"who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able thus to offer willingly?"* (v14)
- *"In the uprightness of my heart I have freely offered all these things, and now I have seen your people … offering freely and joyously to you"* (v17)
- Then: *"keep forever such purposes and thoughts in the hearts of your people, and direct their hearts toward you"* (v18)

"Purposes" is H3336.

**Narrative:** Ch 14 (*Thoughts taken captive*) quotes v18 under "The leaning is kept".

**Not yet carried:** what "such purposes" refers to in the passage, namely the free and joyful giving just seen (vv14, 17). "Freely offered" and "offering freely" are H5068, *be willing*, which is tagged M18, not M64. It is noted for #1986 as a will word outside this cluster.

### Psalm 31:13: "as they plot to take my life"

The psalmist is in distress (vv9–12): *"I hear the whispering of many — terror on every side!— as they scheme together against me, as they plot to take my life"* (v13). The answer: *"But I trust in you, O Lord … My times are in your hand"* (vv14–15).

"Plot" is H2161. The plotting belongs to others. The psalmist's inner answer is trust.

**Narrative:** 10.12 (*Dread laid on others*) quotes vv13–14, the whisper and the answer.

**Reading:** carried in its setting.

### Psalm 32:2: "the man against whom the Lord counts no iniquity"

*"Blessed is the man against whom the Lord counts no iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no deceit"* (v2). Then:
- *"when I kept silent, my bones wasted away"* (v3)
- *"I acknowledged my sin to you, and I did not cover my iniquity … and you forgave the iniquity of my sin"* (v5)

"Counts" is H2803H, God's reckoning.

**Narrative:** Ch 6 and Ch 9 quote only "in whose spirit there is no deceit". The M64 word is not quoted.

**Not yet carried:** that the Lord does not count iniquity against this man, and that in the psalm this follows the man's confession after keeping silent (vv3–5). Shimei asks the same of David with the same word (2Sa 19:19).

### Psalm 51:12: "uphold me with a willing spirit"

David prays:
- *"Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me"* (v10)
- *"take not your Holy Spirit from me"* (v11)
- *"Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with a willing spirit"* (v12)
- *"Then I will teach transgressors your ways"* (v13)

"Willing" is H5081G. The willing spirit is asked for from God, and God is asked to uphold it. Teaching others follows from it.

**Narrative:** Ch 6 (*It is willing*) quotes v12 for the spirit.

**Not yet carried:** for the will reading (#1986), where this verse is in the set: the willingness is asked of God, comes with a renewed heart and spirit, and leads outward.

### Psalm 140:2: "who plan evil things in their heart"

The psalmist asks: *"Deliver me … from violent men, who plan evil things in their heart and stir up wars continually"* (vv1–2). Their tongue is *"sharp as a serpent's"* (v3). They *"have planned to trip up my feet"* (v4), and they hide a trap (v5).

**Narrative:** Ch 12 (*The heart deceives its owner*) quotes vv2 and 5: the trap set in the path was first planned in the heart.

**Reading:** carried, the M64 word in its setting.

### Proverbs 16:9: "The heart of man plans his way"

*"The heart of man plans his way, but the Lord establishes his steps"*. Around it:
- *"The plans of the heart belong to man, but the answer of the tongue is from the Lord"* (v1)
- *"the Lord weighs the spirit"* (v2)
- *"Commit your work to the Lord, and your plans will be established"* (v3)

**Narrative:** Ch 4 and 10.4 (*Where thoughts come from*) quote v9.

**Not yet carried:** v3, that plans committed to the Lord are established. The word for "plans" there is not the M64 word.

### Isaiah 10:7: "But he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think"

Assyria is *"the rod of my anger"* (v5), sent against a godless nation (v6). *"But he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy"* (v7).

He boasts: *"By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom"* (v13). The Lord *"will punish the speech of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria"* (v12). *"Shall the axe boast over him who hews with it?"* (v15).

"Think" is H2803I.

**Narrative:** 10.13 *Anger* gives only a pointer: "the one who carries it out, and their own heart".

**Not yet carried:**
- The one God uses has his own intention, and it is not God's: "it is in his heart to destroy".
- He is punished for "the arrogant heart" that boasts.

For 10.7 and 10.4, this is a heart's own purpose inside God's use of it.

### Zechariah 7:10: "let none of you devise evil against another in your heart"

The command: *"Render true judgments, show kindness and mercy to one another, do not oppress the widow … and let none of you devise evil against another in your heart"* (vv9–10). Then:
- *"But they refused to pay attention and turned a stubborn shoulder and stopped their ears"* (v11)
- *"They made their hearts diamond-hard lest they should hear … Therefore great anger came from the Lord of hosts"* (v12)

**Narrative:** Ch 10 (*Where I am answerable*) quotes the command only.

**Not yet carried:** what follows the command in the passage: the refusal, the ears stopped, the hearts made "diamond-hard", and the anger. 10.13 does not cite this verse.

### Romans 8:6 and 8:7: "to set the mind on the flesh is death"

Paul writes:
- *"those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh"* (v5)
- *"For to set the mind on the flesh is death, but to set the mind on the Spirit is life and peace"* (v6)
- *"For the mind that is set on the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to God's law; indeed, it cannot"* (v7)

"Mind" is G5427, the set mind.

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Death in the living*), Ch 7 (*Flesh*) and Ch 12 (*Flesh as an orientation*) quote vv6–7 for the set mind.

**Reading:** carried for the M64 word. One point is not drawn out: "indeed, it cannot" (v7). The mind set on the flesh does not submit, and is not able to.

### Romans 8:27: "knows what is the mind of the Spirit"

*"the Spirit helps us in our weakness. For we do not know what to pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us"* (v26). *"And he who searches hearts knows what is the mind of the Spirit, because the Spirit intercedes for the saints according to the will of God"* (v27).

"Mind" is G5427, here the Spirit's mind. "The will of God" in the ESV has no will word in the Greek tags of this verse.

**Narrative:** 10.9 (*Relating to God*, opening) quotes vv26–27 for intercession.

**Reading:** the M64 word here belongs to the Holy Spirit, and the Holy Spirit study is separate (CLAUDE.md). For the human inner being: "we do not know what to pray for". This is carried.
