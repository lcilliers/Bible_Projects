## Acts 6:5: "Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit"

The choosing starts from a complaint: *"the Hellenists arose against the Hebrews because their widows were being neglected"* (v1). The twelve set the terms of the choice before the gathering chooses: *"pick out from among you seven men of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom"* (v3). The men are to be chosen by what can already be seen in them, and by what others say of them ("good repute" is literally "being witnessed to", G3140).

The gathering chooses Stephen, *"a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit"* (v5). "Full" is one word through the whole account (G4134):
- *"full of the Spirit and of wisdom"*, the terms of the choice (v3)
- *"full of faith and of the Holy Spirit"*, the man chosen (v5)
- *"full of grace and power"*, as he works (v8)
- *"full of the Holy Spirit"*, as he dies (7:55)

What was seen in him when he was chosen is what is seen in him to the end.

Being chosen does not shelter him. Men *"rose up and disputed with Stephen. But they could not withstand the wisdom and the Spirit with which he was speaking"* (6:9–10). The wisdom named in the terms of the choice (v3) is what they cannot withstand. They turn to false witnesses (v11–13). The council sees *"that his face was like the face of an angel"* (v15).

His accusers are named in inner terms at the end: *"You stiff-necked people, uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit"* (7:51); *"they were enraged, and they ground their teeth at him"* (7:54); *"they cried out with a loud voice and stopped their ears"* (7:57). The chosen man's last words are for them: *"Lord, do not hold this sin against them"* (7:60).

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- Here people choose, and they choose by what is already seen in a man: the Spirit, wisdom, faith, and what others say of him.
- What was seen in him when chosen is what is seen in him through the account, to his death.
- Being chosen brings opposition. He meets it with the same wisdom and Spirit, and he dies praying for those who kill him.

## Ephesians 1:4: "that we should be holy and blameless before him"

The chapter is one long blessing of God: *"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing"* (v3). The choosing is part of that blessing: *"even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him"* (v4).

The choosing comes before anything the chosen did. It is *"before the foundation of the world"*. Being holy and blameless is its purpose, not its ground. The chosen are chosen *in order to* be holy, and *"before him"*, in his sight.

The love in the passage is God's: *"In love he predestined us for adoption to himself as sons through Jesus Christ, according to the purpose of his will"* (v4–5). The chapter goes on with what is given: *"redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses"* (v7).

The inner acts of the chosen come later in the chapter, in the order the chapter gives them:
- hearing and believing: *"when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and believed in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit"* (v13)
- hope: *"we who were the first to hope in Christ"* (v12)
- faith and love: *"because I have heard of your faith in the Lord Jesus and your love toward all the saints"* (v15)

Paul then prays for the chosen to know: *"having the eyes of your hearts enlightened, that you may know what is the hope to which he has called you"* (v18). The chosen still need their hearts' eyes opened to see what they were chosen for.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- The choosing comes before the world, and before anything in the chosen.
- Holiness and blamelessness before him are its purpose.
- The love is God's, as the ESV reads it.
- The chosen hear, believe, hope and love. They still need the eyes of their hearts enlightened to know the hope of their calling.

## Colossians 3:12: "Put on then, as God's chosen ones … compassionate hearts"

The chapter begins with where the chosen now stand: *"If then you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above"* (v1); *"Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth"* (v2); *"your life is hidden with Christ in God"* (v3).

Then it tells them what to put away:
- *"Put to death therefore what is earthly in you: sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry"* (v5)
- *"But now you must put them all away: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and obscene talk from your mouth"* (v8)

They have *"put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator"* (v10).

The verse comes next: *"Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience"* (v12). "Compassionate hearts" is literally the inward parts (G4698) of compassion. The verse gives being chosen as the ground ("as God's chosen ones"), and then gives a command. The qualities are not described as already there. They are to be put on, as clothing is.

The chosen are called *"holy and beloved"*. The holiness that is the purpose of the choosing in Ephesians 1:4 is here a name the chosen already carry, and the inner qualities are still to be put on. I set the two letters side by side; neither refers to the other.

What the chosen put on is turned toward one another:
- *"bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive"* (v13)
- *"And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony"* (v14)
- *"And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful"* (v15)

The forgiving is measured by being forgiven. The peace is to rule in the heart.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- Being chosen is the ground of a command: put on these qualities.
- The qualities are inner ones: the inward parts of compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, patience and love. They are put on, not assumed to be there already.
- They come after putting away anger, wrath and malice, and after setting the mind on things above.
- They are turned toward others: bearing with, forgiving as forgiven, peace ruling in the heart.

## 2 Thessalonians 2:13: "God chose you … through sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth"

The chapter is about deception. Paul asks them *"not to be quickly shaken in mind or alarmed"* (v2), and warns *"Let no one deceive you in any way"* (v3). The lawless one comes *"with all wicked deception for those who are perishing"* (v10).

Before the choosing verse, the passage names the inner state of those who perish:
- *"because they refused to love the truth and so be saved"* (v10)
- *"Therefore God sends them a strong delusion, so that they may believe what is false"* (v11)
- *"in order that all may be condemned who did not believe the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness"* (v12)

Then: *"But we ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers beloved by the Lord, because God chose you as the firstfruits to be saved, through sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth"* (v13). "Belief" in v13 and "believe" in v12 are the same root (G4102, G4100), and "truth" is the same word in v10, v12 and v13 (G0225). The chosen are set against the perishing in the passage's own words. The perishing did not believe the truth. The chosen are saved *"through … belief in the truth"*. The perishing had pleasure in unrighteousness. The chosen are sanctified *"by the Spirit"*.

In the verse, God's choosing and the person's belief stand in one sentence. The verse does not say which comes first, or how they fit.

The passage then gives the chosen two things to do, and asks for one thing to be done in them:
- *"To this he called you through our gospel, so that you may obtain the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ"* (v14)
- *"So then, brothers, stand firm and hold to the traditions that you were taught by us"* (v15)
- *"comfort your hearts and establish them in every good work and word"* (v17), asked of the Lord Jesus Christ and of God the Father, *"who loved us and gave us eternal comfort and good hope through grace"* (v16)

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- The chosen are set against those who refused to love the truth and had pleasure in unrighteousness, in the same words.
- Being chosen goes with belief in the truth and sanctification by the Spirit, in one sentence.
- The chosen are told to stand firm and hold on. Their hearts are to be comforted and established by the Lord.
- Those who perish are given over: God sends them *"a strong delusion"*.

## Titus 1:1: "for the sake of the faith of God's elect and their knowledge of the truth, which accords with godliness"

Paul opens by saying what his own service is for: *"for the sake of the faith of God's elect and their knowledge of the truth, which accords with godliness, in hope of eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began"* (v1–2). Faith, knowledge of the truth, godliness and hope are named as belonging to the elect, and his work serves them.

The rest of the chapter is about who may lead them. The overseer's inner life is set out in full:
- *"He must not be arrogant or quick-tempered or a drunkard or violent or greedy for gain"* (v7)
- *"but hospitable, a lover of good, self-controlled, upright, holy, and disciplined"* (v8)
- *"He must hold firm to the trustworthy word as taught"* (v9)

Against the elect's knowledge of the truth, the chapter sets those who teach otherwise:
- *"not devoting themselves to Jewish myths and the commands of people who turn away from the truth"* (v14)
- *"to the defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure; but both their minds and their consciences are defiled"* (v15)
- *"They profess to know God, but they deny him by their works"* (v16)

The elect's knowledge of the truth *"accords with godliness"*. The others say they know God and deny him by what they do. The chapter sets the two side by side.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- Faith, knowledge of the truth and godliness are named as the elect's own, with the hope of eternal life.
- Their knowledge "accords with godliness". In the same chapter, knowing God in word while denying him in works is the mark of those who turn from the truth.
- Their faith is something another person serves: Paul's work is *"for the sake of"* it.

## James 2:5: "has not God chosen those who are poor in the world to be rich in faith"

The setting is an assembly that chooses where people sit: *"if you pay attention to the one who wears the fine clothing and say, 'You sit here in a good place,' while you say to the poor man, 'You stand over there'"* (v3). James names what that shows of them inside: *"have you not then made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts?"* (v4).

He answers their way of choosing with God's: *"Listen, my beloved brothers, has not God chosen those who are poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom, which he has promised to those who love him?"* (v5). The poor man they sent to stand is the kind of man God chose. The assembly went by clothing. God's choice goes by something else, and its aim is that the poor be *"rich in faith"*.

The promise is to *"those who love him"*. The love here is the chosen people's love for God.

Then: *"But you have dishonored the poor man"* (v6). Choosing against God's choice is a sin: *"if you show partiality, you are committing sin"* (v9).

The faith of the chosen poor is then tested by what it does:
- *"What good is it, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works?"* (v14)
- *"So also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead"* (v17)
- Abraham: *"faith was active along with his works, and faith was completed by his works … and he was called a friend of God"* (v22–23)

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- God's choosing is set against people's choosing by appearance. The people's choosing comes from *"evil thoughts"*.
- The chosen are chosen to be rich in faith. The promise is to those who love him.
- To dishonour the one God chose is a sin.
- The faith of the chosen is shown in what it does. Faith without works is dead.

## 2 Peter 1:10: "be all the more diligent to confirm your calling and election"

The chapter begins with what has been given: *"His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness, through the knowledge of him who called us"* (v3). It then gives the reason for effort: *"For this very reason, make every effort to supplement your faith with virtue, and virtue with knowledge, and knowledge with self-control, and self-control with steadfastness, and steadfastness with godliness, and godliness with brotherly affection, and brotherly affection with love"* (v5–7).

The list is given as an order of adding, one to the next. That is the verse's own wording.

Whoever lacks these is described in inner terms: *"so nearsighted that he is blind, having forgotten that he was cleansed from his former sins"* (v9).

Then: *"Therefore, brothers, be all the more diligent to confirm your calling and election, for if you practice these qualities you will never fall"* (v10). Being chosen is something the chosen are told to make firm. "Diligent" here (G4704) is the verb behind *"make every effort"* in v15, and it is the same root as *"make every effort"* in v5 (G4710). The effort the chapter asks for at the start is the diligence that confirms the election.

Peter takes on the reminding himself: *"I intend always to remind you of these qualities"* (v12); *"I will make every effort so that after my departure you may be able at any time to recall these things"* (v15). Forgetting is what the blind man in v9 did. Remembering is what Peter works for.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- God's power has given everything first (v3). The effort follows *"for this very reason"*.
- The chosen are told to confirm their election by diligence, in a set of qualities added one to another: faith, virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection and love.
- Lacking them is blindness, and forgetting that one was cleansed.
- The promise is *"you will never fall"*.
- Another person works to keep the chosen remembering.

## Revelation 17:14: "those with him are called and chosen and faithful"

The chapter is a vision of judgment on *"the great prostitute"*, with whom *"the dwellers on earth have become drunk"* (v2). The seer himself is moved: *"When I saw her, I marveled greatly"* (v6). He is asked *"Why do you marvel?"* (v7). Those who marvel at the beast are *"the dwellers on earth whose names have not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world"* (v8). The vision *"calls for a mind with wisdom"* (v9).

The kings who serve the beast are of one mind: *"These are of one mind, and they hand over their power and authority to the beast"* (v13). Then: *"They will make war on the Lamb, and the Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings, and those with him are called and chosen and faithful"* (v14).

The chosen are named by three words, and the third is about them: *"faithful"*. They are *"with him"*. The verse says nothing more of their inner life.

The chapter says this of the kings: *"for God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose by being of one mind and handing over their royal power to the beast, until the words of God are fulfilled"* (v17). "Mind" in v13 and "purpose" in v17 are the same word (G1106): the kings' one mind is God's purpose carried out. This is God acting on the hearts of others, not on the chosen. It belongs with 13.1 more than here.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- The chosen are named "faithful" and are "with him".
- They stand on the conquering side in a war, against kings of one mind with the beast.
- Against them stand those who marvel at the beast, whose names are not written in the book of life.

## What batch 2 adds

This is set beside batch 1. Nothing in batch 1 has to be withdrawn.

**The order seen in Isaiah 43:10 holds, and is widened.** God chooses first, and the inner life of the chosen is the purpose:
- holy and blameless before him (Eph 1:4)
- rich in faith (Jam 2:5)
- saved through sanctification and belief in the truth (2Th 2:13)

Ephesians puts the choosing *"before the foundation of the world"*. The one verse where people choose (Act 6:5) has the other order. The gathering chooses by what is already seen in the man.

**The chosen are told to act.** Being chosen is given as the ground of commands:
- put on compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, patience and love (Col 3:12–14)
- stand firm and hold on (2Th 2:15)
- be diligent to confirm your calling and election, adding quality to quality (2Pe 1:5–10)

This sits with batch 1: *"be strong and do it"* (1Ch 28:10).

**What they put on is turned toward others.** Bearing with, forgiving as they were forgiven, and love that binds (Col 3:13–14). The assembly in James is told not to dishonour the poor God chose (Jam 2:6).

**The chosen are set against others in the passage's own words:**
- those who refused to love the truth and had pleasure in unrighteousness (2Th 2:10–12)
- those who profess to know God and deny him by works (Tit 1:16)
- an assembly that judges *"with evil thoughts"* (Jam 2:4)
- kings of one mind with the beast, and those whose names are not written (Rev 17:8, 13)
- Stephen's accusers, *"uncircumcised in heart and ears"* (Act 7:51)

**Being chosen is not a guarantee: the New Testament says it as a call.** Batch 1 had the condition put on Solomon and the chosen people's failures. Here the chosen are told to make their election firm, and *"if you practice these qualities you will never fall"* (2Pe 1:10). Faith without works is dead (Jam 2:17).

**Knowing and remembering.**
- The chosen are prayed for, that the eyes of their hearts be enlightened to know their hope (Eph 1:18).
- Their knowledge of the truth accords with godliness (Tit 1:1).
- Forgetting one's cleansing is blindness, and another works to keep them remembering (2Pe 1:9, 12–15).

Batch 1 had the chosen called to remember (Psa 105:5; Isa 44:21).

**Kept open:**
- **God giving over the perishing** (2Th 2:11, *"a strong delusion"*), and **God putting a purpose into the kings' hearts** (Rev 17:17). These are God acting on the hearts of others. They are for 13.1, not §2b.
- **Ephesians 1:4 and Colossians 3:12.** Holiness is the purpose of the choosing in one, and a name the chosen carry in the other. I set them side by side. Neither refers to the other.
- **How God's choosing and a person's belief fit** (2Th 2:13). The verse names both and does not say.
