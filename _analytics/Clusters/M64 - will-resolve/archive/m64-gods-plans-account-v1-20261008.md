# M64 plan set: how God's plans operate (account, v1, 2026-10-08)

**Escalation:** #1986 · **Destination:** 13.1 Divine, section *Purposing* (13.1 v5)

Researcher, verbatim: *"the 31 God ward verses need to find their way to 13.1 with an analysis of how Gods plans operate."*

- **Source:** the 31 verses grouped "God's own purposing" in [`m64-plan-scan-v1-20261008.csv`](m64-plan-scan-v1-20261008.csv).
- **How they were read:** each verse in its passage (about four verses either side), in the ESV text from iba.db.
- **Rules followed:**
  - Rule 72: these verses are about God in his own right (route A). Where a verse also bears on a person's inner life, that bearing is named.
  - The account says what each verse says in its setting. It does not join verses into a scheme that no verse states. Readings beyond the words are marked "I read".

## 1. What the verses say

### 1.1 He forms a plan and makes it known

- *"Therefore hear the plan that the Lord has made against Edom and the purposes that he has formed against the inhabitants of Teman"* (Jer 49:20). The same words are used against Babylon in Jer 50:45.
  - In both passages the plan follows his question *"For who is like me? Who will summon me?"* (49:19; 50:44).
- *"the Lord has both planned and done what he spoke concerning the inhabitants of Babylon"* (Jer 51:12).
  - The verse before gives the purpose and how it moves: *"The Lord has stirred up the spirit of the kings of the Medes, because his purpose concerning Babylon is to destroy it"* (51:11).
  - His purpose goes forward through the stirred spirit of other people.
- *"for I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole counsel of God"* (Act 20:27). His counsel is something to be declared in full, and Paul counts it a matter of his own innocence (20:26).

### 1.2 His devising answers human devising

- Micah 2 opens with those *"who devise wickedness and work evil on their beds"* (2:1), who *"covet fields and seize them"* (2:2). Then: *"Therefore thus says the Lord: behold, against this family I am devising disaster"* (2:3).
  - The same verb is used for both. The "therefore" ties his devising to theirs.
- In Jeremiah 18 he says *"I am shaping disaster against you and devising a plan against you. Return"* (18:11). They answer: *"We will follow our own plans, and will every one act according to the stubbornness of his evil heart"* (18:12).
  - His plan and their plans stand in one exchange.

### 1.3 He states a plan so that people may turn, and he relents

- Jer 18:7–10 sets out both directions.
  - If a nation he has spoken against *"turns from its evil, I will relent of the disaster that I intended to do to it"* (18:8).
  - If a nation he has promised to build *"does evil in my sight, not listening to my voice, then I will relent of the good that I had intended to do to it"* (18:10).
  - Both are set in the potter's house: *"like the clay in the potter's hand, so are you in my hand"* (18:6).
- *"It may be they will listen, and every one turn from his evil way, that I may relent of the disaster that I intend to do to them"* (Jer 26:3).
- *"It may be that the house of Judah will hear all the disaster that I intend to do to them, so that every one may turn from his evil way, and that I may forgive their iniquity and their sin"* (Jer 36:3). Here the plan is written in a scroll so that it can be heard (36:2, 6).

I read these verses as saying that the plan is announced in order to be heard, and that the hearing and turning of the people are part of what happens to the plan. The verses themselves say "it may be", and leave the outcome open.

### 1.4 He also purposes and does not turn back

- *"for I have spoken; I have purposed; I have not relented, nor will I turn back"* (Jer 4:28). The verse before sets a limit within it: *"yet I will not make a full end"* (4:27).
- *"As I purposed to bring disaster to you when your fathers provoked me to wrath, and I did not relent … so again have I purposed in these days to bring good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah; fear not"* (Zec 8:14–15).
  - The fixed purpose is set beside a fixed purpose for good.
- *"the unchangeable character of his purpose"* (Heb 6:17). He guaranteed it with an oath, *"so that … we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold fast to the hope set before us. We have this as a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul"* (6:18–19).

**Both are stated.** The verses say he relents (Jer 18:8, 10; 26:3; 36:3) and that he does not relent or turn back (Jer 4:28; Zec 8:14; Heb 6:17). No verse in this set explains how the two stand together, and this account does not.

### 1.5 It is carried out, and people see afterward that it was his

- *"The Lord has done what he purposed; he has carried out his word, which he commanded long ago"* (Lam 2:17).
- *"The Lord determined to lay in ruins the wall of the daughter of Zion; he stretched out the measuring line; he did not restrain his hand"* (Lam 2:8).
  - Straight after 2:17: *"Their heart cried to the Lord … Pour out your heart like water before the presence of the Lord!"* (2:18–19). The fulfilled purpose is met with the heart poured out to the one who purposed it.
- *"So they repented and said, 'As the Lord of hosts purposed to deal with us for our ways and deeds, so has he dealt with us'"* (Zec 1:6).
  - The fathers recognised his purpose in what had happened to them, and they repented.

### 1.6 Plans for welfare, and thought for the one in need

- *"For I know the plans I have for you, declares the Lord, plans for welfare and not for evil, to give you a future and a hope"* (Jer 29:11).
  - The verse looks ahead to *"When seventy years are completed"* (29:10).
  - It is followed by *"You will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart"* (29:13).
- *"As for me, I am poor and needy, but the Lord takes thought for me"* (Psa 40:17).
- *"But God will not take away life, and he devises means so that the banished one will not remain an outcast"* (2Sa 14:14). The wise woman says it against the fact that *"We must all die"*.

### 1.7 It takes in what people do against him, without excusing them

- *"this Jesus, delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God, you crucified and killed by the hands of lawless men"* (Act 2:23). The plan and the guilt are stated in one sentence.
- The church prays Psalm 2, *"Why did the Gentiles rage, and the peoples plot in vain?"* (Act 4:25). They say those gathered against Jesus came *"to do whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place"* (4:28).
  - Their next words are a request for themselves: *"grant to your servants to continue to speak your word with all boldness"* (4:29).
- Pointer, from outside the 31: *"you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good"* (Gen 50:20). The same verb is used for both.

### 1.8 Before the ages, not from works

- *"because of his own purpose and grace, which he gave us in Christ Jesus before the ages began"* (2Ti 1:9).
- *"though they were not yet born and had done nothing either good or bad—in order that God's purpose of election might continue, not because of works but because of him who calls"* (Rom 9:11).
- *"according to the purpose of him who works all things according to the counsel of his will"* (Eph 1:11).
- *"the mystery of his will, according to his purpose, which he set forth in Christ as a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in Christ"* (Eph 1:9–10).
- *"This was according to the eternal purpose that he has realized in Christ Jesus our Lord"* (Eph 3:11).
- *"whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood … This was to show God's righteousness"* (Rom 3:25).

### 1.9 It is turned toward those who love him

- *"And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good, for those who are called according to his purpose"* (Rom 8:28).
- In the same passage, *"we do not know what to pray for as we ought"* (8:26).

### 1.10 A person can refuse it, or serve it

- *"but the Pharisees and the lawyers rejected the purpose of God for themselves, not having been baptized by him"* (Luk 7:30). Over against them, the people and the tax collectors *"declared God just"* (7:29).
- *"For David, after he had served the purpose of God in his own generation, fell asleep"* (Act 13:36).

## 2. Where these verses touch the human inner being (route A and B)

These are named here and not developed. They belong with the human side of planning, which is not yet woven.

- **Turning:** in Jer 18:8, 11; 26:3 and 36:3, the plan is spoken so that a person may hear and turn.
- **Hope and holding fast:** in Heb 6:18–19 the unchangeable purpose is given as encouragement to hold fast, *"a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul"*.
- **Seeking:** after his plans for welfare, *"when you seek me with all your heart"* (Jer 29:13).
- **The heart poured out** after his purpose is carried out (Lam 2:18–19).
- **Recognising and repenting:** the fathers in Zec 1:6.
- **Refusing and serving:** rejecting it *"for themselves"* (Luk 7:30); David serving it (Act 13:36).
- **Fear not:** *"so again have I purposed … to bring good … fear not"* (Zec 8:15), with commands that follow (8:16–17), including *"do not devise evil in your hearts against one another"*. The verb is the one used for his own purposing in 8:14–15.
