Scripture does not stop to define choosing. In these verses it shows choosing as an act with two sides. The clearest sentence is about a child: *"when he knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good"* (Isa 7:15). Refusing and choosing are one act, seen from either side. The man the book of Job calls blameless is described by his refusing: he *"feared God and turned away from evil"* (Job 1:1). The father in Proverbs puts the choice entirely in the negative: *"do not choose any of his ways"* (Pro 3:31).

## The words

- **H0977, "choose".** This is the ordinary verb, and it carries most of these verses. The ESV renders it in several ways:
  - *"choose"*
  - *"desire"*: *"all that you desire of me I will do for you"* (2Sa 19:38)
  - *"to be chosen"* in the sense of preferred: *"To get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver"* (Pro 16:16)

  The same verb is used of the Lord choosing the place for his name (Deu 12:5, 14), and of the Lord's answering choice: *"I also will choose harsh treatment for them"* (Isa 66:4).
- **H3988A, "refuse, reject, despise".** This is the other side of the act:
  - *"refuse the evil"* (Isa 7:15)
  - *"this people has refused the waters of Shiloah"* (Isa 8:6)
  - *"because you reject it"* (Job 34:33)
  - Job's last word: *"I despise myself, and repent"* (Job 42:6)
- **G1586, "choose" (Greek).** Used for the gathering choosing the seven (Act 6:5), for the church choosing men to send (Act 15:22, 25), and for God's own choice before them: *"God made a choice among you"* (Act 15:7).
- **G0830, "of one's own accord".** One word, literally "self-chosen" (2Cor 8:3, 17).

## Choosing has two sides

Refusing is itself choosing. The child *"knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good"* (Isa 7:15). The oracle around that verse shows refusing going both ways. Ahaz refuses the Lord's offer of a sign with pious words: *"I will not ask, and I will not put the Lord to the test"* (Isa 7:12). The people *"refused the waters of Shiloah that flow gently"* (8:6). The right refusing and the wrong refusing are set in one oracle, each with its own consequence.

Not answering is already choosing. *"when I called, you did not answer; when I spoke, you did not listen, but you did what was evil in my eyes and chose what I did not delight in"* (Isa 65:12; repeated in 66:4). The choosing of what the Lord does not delight in is where the not-listening ends up.

The choice is laid out with both its sides and both its ends: *"cease to do evil, learn to do good"*; *"If you are willing and obedient, you shall eat the good of the land"*; *"but if you refuse and rebel, you shall be eaten by the sword"* (Isa 1:16–20).

## What choosing rests on

**Knowing how.** The child chooses *"when he knows how"* (Isa 7:15). The oracle of Isaiah 1 opens with the absence of that knowing: *"Israel does not know, my people do not understand"* (1:3).

**Being taught.** *"Who is the man who fears the Lord? Him will he instruct in the way that he should choose"* (Psa 25:12). The teaching comes from who the Lord is: *"Good and upright is the Lord; therefore he instructs sinners in the way"* (25:8). The psalmist asks for it: *"Make me to know your ways, O Lord; teach me your paths"* (25:4). In Psalm 119 he asks *"graciously teach me your law"*, and asks God to do the refusing with him: *"Put false ways far from me"* (119:29).

**The fear of the Lord.** In Psalm 25 the fear comes first, then the teaching, then the choice (25:12). Joshua puts the same order before the people: *"fear the Lord and serve him in sincerity and in faithfulness"* (Jos 24:14), and then *"choose this day"*.

**What has been seen and remembered.** The people at Shechem choose from memory: *"it is the Lord our God who brought us … up from the land of Egypt … and who did those great signs in our sight"* (Jos 24:17). Job's choice is made when *"now my eye sees you"* (Job 42:5). The psalmist keeps his eyes on what he chose: *"My eyes are ever toward the Lord"* (Psa 25:15).

**What is good to the chooser.** Two verses make the chooser's own good the ground, and honour it: *"wherever it suits him"* (Deu 23:16) and *"whatever seems good to you"* (2Sa 19:38). In Hebrew both say "the good" to him, or in his eyes.

## What draws it

**Desire and delight.** Choosing comes paired with desire: *"the oaks that you desired … the gardens that you have chosen"* (Isa 1:29). It comes with the soul's delight: *"These have chosen their own ways, and their soul delights in their abominations"* (Isa 66:3).

**Envy.** The pull can be named before the choice, and the command reaches it: *"Do not envy a man of violence and do not choose any of his ways"* (Pro 3:31).

**Being pleased, and earnestness.** In the church at Jerusalem the choosing comes out of a shared inward response: *"what they said pleased the whole gathering, and they chose"* (Act 6:5). Titus goes *"being himself very earnest … of his own accord"* (2Cor 8:17).

**Preferring.** To choose can be to weigh one good against another and set it above: *"How much better to get wisdom than gold! To get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver"* (Pro 16:16). Proverbs 3 sets the weighing against all desire: *"nothing you desire can compare with her"* (3:15).

## Whose measure

The verses ask in whose eyes, and by whose delight, a choice is good.
- **The chooser's eyes.** *"if it is evil in your eyes to serve the Lord, choose this day"* (Jos 24:15). Here the chooser's own sight is where turning from the Lord could begin.
- **The Lord's eyes.** *"you did what was evil in my eyes"* (Isa 65:12). The measure is the Lord's sight.
- **The Lord's delight, failed.** *"chose what I did not delight in"* (Isa 65:12); *"chose that in which I did not delight"* (66:4).
- **The Lord's delight, met.** *"who choose the things that please me"* (Isa 56:4). It is the same word, so the three verses are the two sides of one measure.
- **The soul's own delight, set against the Lord's.** *"their soul delights in their abominations"* (Isa 66:3). The word is the same one again.

Yet "good in your eyes" is not always the wrong measure. When the king says *"whatever seems good to you"* to the old man who served him (2Sa 19:38), the chooser's own eyes are honoured. The setting changes what the same expression means. (See "What changes the character" below.)

## Choosing before a God who has already acted

In these verses the human choice is never the first move.
- At Shechem it comes after eleven verses of what the Lord did, beginning with *"I took your father Abraham from beyond the River"* (Jos 24:3).
- In Isaiah 65 the wrong choice is made in front of a God who says *"Here I am, here I am"* and *"I spread out my hands all the day to a rebellious people"* (65:1–2).
- At the Jerusalem council the church's choosing follows Peter's word that *"God made a choice among you"* (Act 15:7), in the same verb.

## Own accord, and God at work

Two things are named side by side and not set against each other:
- *"thanks be to God, who put into the heart of Titus the same earnest care I have for you"*
- *"being himself very earnest he is going to you of his own accord"* (2Cor 8:16–17)

The Macedonians gave *"of their own accord"*, and the order of their giving is set out: *"they gave themselves first to the Lord and then by the will of God to us"* (8:3, 5). The church's choice is joined to the Spirit's in the same verb: *"it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us"* (Act 15:28). The psalmist chooses, and asks God to give the room to carry it out: *"I will run in the way of your commandments when you enlarge my heart!"* (Psa 119:32). I read these verses as saying that what God puts into the heart becomes the person's own. The verses name both and do not explain how they fit.

## Choosing together

A people can choose as one.
- Israel at Shechem: *"we will serve the Lord"*, and they become *"witnesses against yourselves"* (Jos 24:21–22).
- The gathering in Jerusalem, out of being pleased (Act 6:5).
- The church, *"having come to one accord"* (Act 15:25).

Weighing a case together is also called choosing: *"Let us choose what is right; let us know among ourselves what is good"* (Job 34:4).

## A choice that is honoured

Choosing is a freedom a person can lose, and Scripture gives it back. The escaped slave *"shall dwell with you … in the place that he shall choose within one of your towns, wherever it suits him. You shall not wrong him"* (Deu 23:16). The king hands the choice to the man who fed him in the wilderness: *"all that you desire of me I will do for you"* (2Sa 19:38).

The phrase *"the place that he shall choose within one of your towns"* has the same wording as *"the place that the Lord your God will choose"* (Deu 12:5, 14). I read this as the law giving the slave a small share of the kind of choosing the Lord keeps for his own dwelling. The verse does not draw this comparison (open item).

## Making the choice, and holding it

A choice spoken is not yet a choice carried out.
- **It binds.** *"You are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen the Lord"* (Jos 24:22).
- **It is not the same as being able.** *"You are not able to serve the Lord, for he is a holy God"* (Jos 24:19).
- **It exposes what is still there, and calls for the heart.** *"Then put away the foreign gods that are among you, and incline your heart to the Lord"* (Jos 24:23).
- **It is held by keeping in view and clinging.** *"I have chosen the way of faithfulness; I set your rules before me. I cling to your testimonies"* (Psa 119:30–31). The verb for "cling" is the one used two verses earlier: *"My soul clings to the dust"* (119:25). What the soul clings to has turned.
- **It is held by keeping and holding fast.** *"who keep my Sabbaths, who choose the things that please me and hold fast my covenant"* (Isa 56:4).
- **It is held while waiting.** *"May integrity and uprightness preserve me, for I wait for you"* (Psa 25:21).
- **Readiness has to be completed.** *"so that your readiness in desiring it may be matched by your completing it"* (2Cor 8:11).
- **It held as long as there were people who remembered what it was made from.** *"Israel served the Lord all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua and had known all the work that the Lord did for Israel"* (Jos 24:31). The verse gives the timing; the link to remembering is mine (I read).

## Choosing from weakness

The chooser in these verses is often low:
- **sorrow:** *"My soul melts away for sorrow"* (Psa 119:28)
- **guilt and trouble:** *"pardon my guilt, for it is great"*; *"I am lonely and afflicted"* (Psa 25:11, 16)
- **one's own verdict on oneself:** *"Behold, I am a dry tree"* (Isa 56:3). The Lord answers it: *"an everlasting name that shall not be cut off"* (56:5).
- **age:** *"Can I discern what is pleasant and what is not?"* (2Sa 19:35)
- **affliction, as Elihu reads it:** *"this you have chosen rather than affliction"* (Job 36:21)

## What follows the choice

**When the choice is right, the outcome reaches the inner being:**
- *"His soul shall abide in well-being"* (Psa 25:13). "Well-being" is the same word as *"the good"* in *"choose the good"* (Isa 7:15). I read this as the good that is chosen becoming the place the soul lives.
- *"The friendship of the Lord is for those who fear him"* (Psa 25:14). The upright are *"in his confidence"* (Pro 3:32), which is the same word.
- *"I will … make them joyful in my house of prayer"* (Isa 56:7).
- *"they rejoiced because of its encouragement"*, and Judas and Silas were *"sent off in peace"* (Act 15:31, 33).
- Barzillai: *"the king kissed Barzillai and blessed him"* (2Sa 19:39).
- The child who refuses the evil and chooses the good eats *"curds and honey"*, the food of *"everyone who is left in the land"* (Isa 7:15, 22). In the other oracle, *"If you are willing and obedient, you shall eat the good of the land"* (Isa 1:19).

**When the choice is wrong, the outcome reaches the inner being:**
- **Shame at the thing chosen:** *"they shall be ashamed of the oaks that you desired; and you shall blush for the gardens that you have chosen"* (Isa 1:29); *"you shall be put to shame"* (Isa 65:13); *"it is they who shall be put to shame"* (Isa 66:5).
- **The heart and spirit:** *"you shall cry out for pain of heart and shall wail for breaking of spirit"* (Isa 65:14).
- **Their own fears:** *"I also will choose harsh treatment for them and bring their fears upon them"* (Isa 66:4).

**What is chosen comes back on the chooser.** Each of these is stated in its own verse:
- They set *"cups of mixed wine for Destiny"*, and the Lord says *"I will destine you to the sword"*. "Destiny" and "destine" share a root (Isa 65:11–12).
- They chose gardens and desired oaks, and become *"like an oak whose leaf withers, and like a garden without water"* (Isa 1:30).
- *"the strong shall become tinder, and his work a spark"* (Isa 1:31).
- The Lord's choice answers theirs: *"I also will choose"* (Isa 66:4).

I read these as the same thing said in different figures.

**The way back stays open.** In the same oracles the offer stands: *"Come now, let us reason together"* (Isa 1:18); *"those in her who repent"* (Isa 1:27).

## Choices weighed by another

Another person can weigh someone's choices, and these verses show how, and how far that reaches.
- **They read the choice from the words.** Eliphaz: *"your iniquity teaches your mouth, and you choose the tongue of the crafty. Your own mouth condemns you"* (Job 15:5–6). He goes from the mouth to the heart: *"Why does your heart carry you away, and why do your eyes flash"* (15:12).
- **They weigh it as tasting.** Elihu: *"the ear tests words as the palate tastes food"*; *"Let us choose what is right"* (Job 34:3–4).
- **They read it in suffering.** Elihu: *"this you have chosen rather than affliction"*, seen in wrath and scoffing (Job 36:18, 21).
- **They cannot make the choice for the other.** *"For you must choose, and not I"* (Job 34:33).

The book does not let these evaluations have the last word. Job is the one the Lord calls *"a blameless and upright man, who fears God and turns away from evil"* (1:8). Of Eliphaz and his friends the Lord says *"you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has"* (42:7); Elihu is not named.

The choice Elihu pressed is made, in his own verb, but only when Job sees the Lord: *"now my eye sees you; therefore I despise myself, and repent"* (42:5–6).

## What changes the character

The same words change face with the setting (#1919):
- **"Good / evil in your eyes."**
  - It is the chooser's sight, from which turning from the Lord could begin (Jos 24:15).
  - It is the chooser's own good, honoured by the king (2Sa 19:38).
  - It gives way to *"evil in my eyes"*, the Lord's sight (Isa 65:12).
  - *Why:* in Joshua and Isaiah the choice is between the Lord and other gods. With Barzillai it is a matter of where an old man, who served, will live.
- **"To know between good and evil."**
  - It is the knowing that grounds the moral choice (Isa 7:15–16).
  - It is the palate's knowing of pleasant and unpleasant, which fades at eighty (2Sa 19:35).
  - It is the weighing of words in a dispute (Job 34:3–4).
  - *Why:* the same knowing is turned to right and wrong, to taste, and to judgement.
- **The refusing verb (H3988A).**
  - It is refusing the evil (Isa 7:15).
  - It is refusing the gentle waters (Isa 8:6).
  - It is refusing God's terms, as Elihu charges (Job 34:33).
  - It is Job turning it on himself in repentance (Job 42:6).
  - *Why:* the act is the same; its object decides whether it is right or wrong.
- **The choosing verb (H0977).** It is used for choosing a master (Jos 24:15), a place (Deu 23:16), a way (Psa 119:30; Isa 66:3), a preference (Pro 16:16), and a verdict (Job 34:4). *Why:* it is turned to whatever is set before a person.
- **Delight (H2654A).** It is the soul's own delight in what it chose (Isa 66:3). It is the Lord's delight as the measure, either failed (Isa 65:12; 66:4) or met (Isa 56:4). *Why:* whose delight it is decides.

## Still open

- **Deu 23:16 and Deu 12.** Whether the shared phrase is more than a shared form of words (marked "I read").
- **Elihu.** Whether his evaluations stand; the book does not rule on him.
- **Job's own voice.** Job's own words about choosing (7:15, 9:14, 29:25) are not yet read.
- **Waiting.** Psalm 25 ends still waiting and in trouble. The right choice does not end the trouble in that psalm.
- **The rest of the pull.** The remaining parts of the "choose" pull are still to be read: God's choosing, the chosen, and choosing between death and life. The account is built to take them.
