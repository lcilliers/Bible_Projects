## John 15:19: "but I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you"

Jesus speaks to his disciples on the night before his death. The passage begins with love among the chosen: *"This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you"* (v12). The choosing is said twice:
- *"You did not choose me, but I chose you and appointed you that you should go and bear fruit"* (v16)
- *"If you were of the world, the world would love you as its own; but because you are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you"* (v19)

The world's two inner acts are set side by side. "Love" is *phileō* (G5368): the world loves *"its own"*. "Hates" is *miseō* (G3404). The verse says the hatred is *because* of the choosing: *"I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you"*.

The passage says the hatred was first aimed at Jesus: *"If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before it hated you"* (v18). It gives a reason in the haters: *"because they do not know him who sent me"* (v21). It says they have *"seen and hated both me and my Father"* (v24), and *"They hated me without a cause"* (v25).

Beside the world's hatred stands the command given twice to the chosen: *"love one another"* (v12, v17).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- Being chosen out of the world brings the world's hatred. The verse states the cause.
- The world's love goes to *"its own"*, and stops where the choosing begins.
- The hatred is first aimed at the one who chose (v18).
- The passage names not knowing the Father as underneath it (v21).
- The chosen are told to love one another, with the world's hate in view (v17–18).

## Acts 15:7: "you know that in the early days God made a choice among you, that by my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel and believe"

The chapter is the dispute over circumcision: *"no small dissension and debate"* (v2), then *"much debate"* among the apostles and elders (v7). Peter stands up and appeals to what they already know: *"Brothers, you know that in the early days God made a choice among you, that by my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel and believe"* (v7).

"Know" is *epistamai* (G1987). Their knowing of a past choice by God is the ground Peter argues from. The purpose of that choice lies in others: *"that … the Gentiles should hear … and believe"* (*pisteuō*, G4100).

Peter goes on to the Gentiles' hearts:
- *"God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us"* (v8)
- *"he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith"* (v9)
- *"why are you putting God to the test by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear?"* (v10)

The debate ends: *"And all the assembly fell silent, and they listened"* (v12). The assembly's own choosing follows later in the chapter: *"it has seemed good to us, having come to one accord, to choose men"* (v25). That was read for §2a.

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- God's choosing of one man has its purpose in others: that the Gentiles hear and believe.
- Knowing that God has chosen is the ground on which a dispute is settled.
- The Gentiles' hearts are known by God and cleansed by faith (v8–9). God's knowing is rule 72, A. The cleansing of their hearts is in the human inner being.
- The debate gives way to silence and listening (v12).

## Romans 11:7: "Israel failed to obtain what it was seeking. The elect obtained it, but the rest were hardened"

Paul asks whether God has rejected his people and answers: *"By no means!"* (v1). *"God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew"* (v2). He gives Elijah, who thought he was alone, and God's reply: *"I have kept for myself seven thousand men who have not bowed the knee to Baal"* (v4). *"So too at the present time there is a remnant, chosen by grace. But if it is by grace, it is no longer on the basis of works"* (v5–6).

Then v7: *"What then? Israel failed to obtain what it was seeking. The elect obtained it, but the rest were hardened"*.

The others are *"the rest"*. Two things are said of them:
- They were seeking (*epizēteō*, G1934) and did not obtain.
- They were hardened (*pōroō*, G4456).

The hardening is described from Scripture: *"God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes that would not see and ears that would not hear, down to this very day"* (v8). The giving is God's act (rule 72, A). The eyes that do not see and ears that do not hear are in the human inner being.

Paul does not leave it there: *"So I ask, did they stumble in order that they might fall? By no means! Rather, through their trespass salvation has come to the Gentiles, so as to make Israel jealous"* (v11). He goes on: *"how much more will their full inclusion mean!"* (v12).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- Those who are not among the chosen are described as seeking and not obtaining.
- They were hardened: eyes that do not see, ears that do not hear (v8).
- The hardening is not the end. Their stumbling is not *"in order that they might fall"* (v11).
- Jealousy is named as an aim: *"so as to make Israel jealous"* (v11).
- The remnant is chosen *"by grace"*, not by works (v5–6). The verse says how the elect were chosen. It does not say why the rest were not.

## 1 Thessalonians 1:4: "For we know, brothers loved by God, that he has chosen you"

Paul writes of what he remembers of them: *"your work of faith and labor of love and steadfastness of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ"* (v3). Then: *"For we know, brothers loved by God, that he has chosen you, because our gospel came to you not only in word, but also in power and in the Holy Spirit and with full conviction"* (v4–5).

"Know" is *oida* (G6063). Here another person knows that someone has been chosen. The ESV joins v4 to v5 with "because": the knowing rests on how the gospel came to them. The passage goes on to what was seen in them:
- *"you received the word in much affliction, with the joy of the Holy Spirit"* (v6)
- *"you became an example to all the believers"* (v7)
- *"your faith in God has gone forth everywhere"* (v8)
- *"you turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God, and to wait for his Son from heaven"* (v9–10)

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- Another person can know that someone is chosen.
- In this verse the knowing rests on what was seen when the gospel came: power, the Holy Spirit and full conviction.
- What is seen in the chosen is faith, love and hope (v3), joy in affliction (v6), and turning from idols (v9).
- The one who knows it gives thanks and prays for them (v2).

## 2 Timothy 2:10: "Therefore I endure everything for the sake of the elect"

Paul writes from chains: *"my gospel, for which I am suffering, bound with chains as a criminal. But the word of God is not bound!"* (v8–9). Then: *"Therefore I endure everything for the sake of the elect, that they also may obtain the salvation that is in Christ Jesus with eternal glory"* (v10).

"Endure" is *hupomenō* (G5278). The same word comes in the saying that follows: *"if we endure, we will also reign with him"* (v12). The endurance is Paul's. It is for others, the elect, and for their salvation.

The same passage calls Timothy to it: *"Share in suffering as a good soldier of Christ Jesus"* (v3). It gives the ground: *"if we are faithless, he remains faithful—for he cannot deny himself"* (v13).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- A person endures suffering for the sake of the chosen.
- The aim is their salvation: *"that they also may obtain"*.
- The same endurance is promised its outcome: *"if we endure, we will also reign with him"* (v12).

## 1 Peter 2:4: "a living stone rejected by men but in the sight of God chosen and precious"

The chapter opens with what the readers are to put away and to long for: *"So put away all malice and all deceit and hypocrisy and envy and all slander. Like newborn infants, long for the pure spiritual milk"* (v1–2).

Then: *"As you come to him, a living stone rejected by men but in the sight of God chosen and precious"* (v4).

Two measures stand in one verse: *"rejected by men"* and *"in the sight of God chosen"*. "Rejected" is *apodokimazō* (G0593), to reject after testing. The quotation in v7 uses the same word: *"The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone"*.

The readers share the same name: *"you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation"* (v9). They will be spoken against in turn: *"when they speak against you as evildoers"* (v12).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- People reject the one God chose. The rejection is after testing, by their own judgement.
- God's sight is set against theirs in the same verse.
- Those who come to the rejected stone share his name and his treatment (v9, v12).

## 1 Peter 2:6: "a cornerstone chosen and precious, and whoever believes in him will not be put to shame"

*"For it stands in Scripture: 'Behold, I am laying in Zion a stone, a cornerstone chosen and precious, and whoever believes in him will not be put to shame'"* (v6).

The other's inner act here is believing (*pisteuō*, G4100), toward the chosen stone. The outcome is stated: *"will not be put to shame"*.

The next verses divide people by it: *"So the honor is for you who believe, but for those who do not believe, 'The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone,' and 'A stone of stumbling, and a rock of offense.' They stumble because they disobey the word, as they were destined to do"* (v7–8).

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- Believing in the one God chose brings honour, and *"will not be put to shame"*.
- For those who do not believe, the same stone is a stone of stumbling.
- The stumbling is given its reason in the verse: *"because they disobey the word"*. The verse adds *"as they were destined to do"*, and does not explain it.

## 2 John 1: "The elder to the elect lady and her children, whom I love in truth"

*"The elder to the elect lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all who know the truth, because of the truth that abides in us and will be with us forever"* (v1–2).

The love is the elder's, and it is shared by *"all who know the truth"*. "Love" is *agapaō* (G0025). "Know" is *ginōskō* (G1097). Both are toward the one called elect. The verse grounds the love *"in truth"* and *"because of the truth that abides in us"*. It does not ground it in the choosing.

The letter goes on: *"I rejoiced greatly to find some of your children walking in the truth"* (v4), and *"this is love, that we walk according to his commandments"* (v6).

The HIB/qualifier file has the relation as "none". The reading agrees that the love is not bound to the choosing. "Elect" names the reader.

**What this verse shows of others around the choosing:**
- Love and knowing are directed toward one called elect.
- The verse grounds them in the truth that abides, not in her being chosen.
- I read this as a verse to note, not to build on, for the choosing account.

## What batch 3 adds

**What others do around a choice, in these verses:**
- hate the chosen, because they are chosen out of the world (Joh 15:19)
- know that God chose, and settle a dispute on it (Act 15:7)
- believe, as the purpose of God's choosing one man (Act 15:7)
- seek and not obtain, and be hardened, with eyes that do not see (Rom 11:7–8)
- know that others are chosen, from what was seen when the gospel came (1Th 1:4–5)
- endure suffering for the sake of the chosen (2Ti 2:10)
- reject the chosen one after testing (1Pe 2:4, 7)
- believe in the chosen one, and not be put to shame (1Pe 2:6)
- love the chosen in truth (2Jo 1)

**Set against the chosen.** The world's hatred (Joh 15:19) and men's rejection (1Pe 2:4) are each set against God's choosing in the same verse. Each passage says the same treatment fell first on the one who chose, or the one chosen first (Joh 15:18; 1Pe 2:4, 7).

**Whose measure.** *"rejected by men but in the sight of God chosen"* (1Pe 2:4). This sits with batch 1 (Deu 12:8), batch 2 (2Sa 6:20; Jer 33:24) and §2a.

**Others knowing a choice.** In Act 15:7 the assembly knows God's past choice and argues from it. In 1Th 1:4 Paul knows that the readers are chosen, from what he saw. In Joh 15:21 the haters *"do not know him who sent me"*.

**Toward the chosen, for their good.** Paul's endurance (2Ti 2:10) and the elder's love (2Jo 1) are aimed at the chosen. Both are tied in their passages to something other than the choosing: salvation (2Ti 2:10) and the truth (2Jo 1–2).

**Kept open:**
- **Rom 11:8 and 1Pe 2:8.** God giving a spirit of stupor, and *"as they were destined to do"*, are acts of God on others. They go to 13.1 under rule 72 and are not read further here.
