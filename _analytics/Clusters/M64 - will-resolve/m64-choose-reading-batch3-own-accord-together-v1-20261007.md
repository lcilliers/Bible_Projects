## Deuteronomy 23:16 — "in the place that he shall choose … wherever it suits him"

The law concerns *"a slave who has escaped from his master to you"*. Israel is told *"You shall not give up to his master"* (v15). Then the choice is handed to the slave: *"He shall dwell with you, in your midst, in the place that he shall choose within one of your towns, wherever it suits him. You shall not wrong him"* (v16).

The man who escaped had no say over where he lived. The law gives it back to him. The choice is his, and its ground is his own good: *"wherever it suits him"* is literally "in what is good to him" (H2896A). Israel's part is to honour it: not to hand him back, and not to *"wrong him"* (H3238, to oppress).

The phrase is a weighty one in Deuteronomy. *"the place that he shall choose within one of your towns"* has the same shape as the phrase Deuteronomy uses for the Lord’s own choosing: *"the place that the Lord your God will choose out of all your tribes"* (12:5); *"the place that the Lord will choose in one of your tribes"* (12:14). The words (place, that, choose, in one of your …) are the same (H4725, H0977). I read this as the law giving the escaped slave, in his own small measure, the kind of choosing the Lord keeps for his own dwelling. The verse does not draw the comparison.

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- Choosing is a freedom that can be taken from a person, and the law gives it back.
- Its ground is the chooser's own good: *"wherever it suits him"*.
- Others are bound to honour it, and not to oppress the one who chose.

## 2 Samuel 19:38 — "whatever seems good to you, and all that you desire of me"

Barzillai had fed David in his flight. He came with others bringing *"honey and curds and sheep and cheese from the herd, for David and the people with him to eat"*, saying *"The people are hungry and weary and thirsty in the wilderness"* (17:27, 29). When David returns, the king offers him a place at court: *"Come over with me, and I will provide for you with me in Jerusalem"* (19:33).

Barzillai declines, and his reason is about his own inner capacity: *"I am this day eighty years old. Can I discern what is pleasant and what is not? Can your servant taste what he eats or what he drinks? Can I still listen to the voice of singing men and singing women?"* (v35). The Hebrew is "know between good and evil" (H3045 with H0996, H2896B, H7451A). It is the same pairing as Isaiah 7:15–16, *"knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good"*. Here it is the knowing of taste and pleasure, and Barzillai says that at eighty it has gone. The same words, put to the palate rather than to right and wrong, mark a change of face (#1919).

He makes his choice, and it is for someone else: *"Please let your servant return, that I may die in my own city … But here is your servant Chimham. Let him go over with my lord the king, and do for him whatever seems good to you"* (v37).

The king hands the choosing back to Barzillai: *"Chimham shall go over with me, and I will do for him whatever seems good to you, and all that you desire of me I will do for you"* (v38). *"Whatever seems good to you"* is literally "the good in your eyes" (H2896A, H5869). *"all that you desire of me"* is the choosing verb (H0977): "all that you choose of me". So the old man who says he can no longer tell good from bad on his palate is given the king's word that what is good in his eyes will be done.

The outcome is told: *"the king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and he returned to his own home"* (v39); *"Chimham went on with him"* (v40).

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- Choosing can be handed over by one who has power to one who has served.
- The ground is "the good in your eyes", which the king honours.
- The chooser chooses home and death in his own city for himself, and the king's favour for another.
- The knowing of good and bad appears here as taste, and it fades with age, yet the choosing remains his.
- The outcome is blessing, and each goes his way.

## 2 Corinthians 8:17 — "being himself very earnest he is going to you of his own accord"

The chapter is about giving to the poor saints. It opens with the Macedonians: *"in a severe test of affliction, their abundance of joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity"* (v2). They gave *"according to their means … and beyond their means, of their own accord"* (v3). That is the same word as in v17 (G0830, *authairetos*, "self-chosen"). The order of their giving is set out: *"they gave themselves first to the Lord and then by the will of God to us"* (v5). Their own accord and the will of God stand side by side in one sentence.

Paul will not command the Corinthians: *"I say this not as a command, but to prove by the earnestness of others that your love also is genuine"* (v8). He presses them on the gap between wanting and doing: *"finish doing it as well, so that your readiness in desiring it may be matched by your completing it"* (v11); *"if the readiness is there, it is acceptable according to what a person has"* (v12). The readiness (G4288) and the desiring (G2309) have to be matched by completing.

Titus is described the same way: *"thanks be to God, who put into the heart of Titus the same earnest care I have for you. For he not only accepted our appeal, but being himself very earnest he is going to you of his own accord"* (v16–17). The verse holds three things together:
- God put the care into his heart (G1325, G2588)
- he accepted the appeal
- he goes of his own accord, out of his own earnestness (G4705)

The text does not set them against each other or explain how they fit. I read it as saying that what God put into the heart became Titus's own, so that he goes because he wants to.

The outcome is stated only as his going. Another brother is sent who is *"more earnest than ever because of his great confidence in you"* (v22). The aim is that the giving be honourable *"not only in the Lord’s sight but also in the sight of man"* (v21).

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- A choice of one's own accord stands alongside God putting the care into the heart, and alongside his will (v5).
- Both are named; neither cancels the other.
- Its inner ground is earnestness.
- It is not commanded, and it is meant to prove that love is genuine.
- Desire and readiness have to be completed in the doing.

## Acts 6:5 — "what they said pleased the whole gathering, and they chose Stephen"

The choice is made at a point of division: *"a complaint by the Hellenists arose against the Hebrews because their widows were being neglected in the daily distribution"* (v1). The twelve do not choose. They give the gathering the choice and the measure for it: *"pick out from among you seven men of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom"* (v3).

The gathering's response is inward before it is an act: *"what they said pleased the whole gathering"* (G0700). Then *"they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit"*, and six others (v5). The choosing comes from the whole gathering being pleased, and it follows the measure they were given: the men are known for what is in them (Spirit, wisdom, faith), not for rank.

The outcome: *"These they set before the apostles, and they prayed and laid their hands on them"* (v6). The narrative goes straight on: *"the word of God continued to increase, and the number of the disciples multiplied greatly"* (v7). It places the increase next to the choosing; it does not say one caused the other.

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- Choosing together starts in a shared inward response, the whole gathering being pleased.
- It answers a division.
- It is measured by what is inside the people chosen.
- It is handed to the people by those with authority, and then confirmed with prayer and the laying on of hands.

## Acts 15:22, 25 — "it seemed good … having come to one accord, to choose men"

The choosing ends a dispute that went to the heart of the faith. Some taught *"Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved"* (v1), and there was *"no small dissension and debate"* (v2).

At the council the first word about choosing is God's. Peter says *"Brothers, you know that in the early days God made a choice among you, that by my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel and believe"* (v7). It is the same verb as the church's later choosing (G1586). He goes on: *"God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit … having cleansed their hearts by faith"* (v8–9). The assembly *"fell silent"* and *"listened"* (v12). James gives his judgement from the prophets (v13–21).

Then the church chooses: *"it seemed good to the apostles and the elders, with the whole church, to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch"* (v22). The letter says how they came to it: *"it has seemed good to us, having come to one accord, to choose men and send them to you"* (v25). "One accord" (G3661) is a single shared mind. The same verb for "seemed good" (G1380) then joins them to the Spirit: *"it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us"* (v28).

So the church's choosing:
- follows God's choosing, in the same verb
- follows listening in silence
- comes from one accord
- is joined to what seemed good to the Holy Spirit

The outcome is in the hearers' hearts: *"when they had read it, they rejoiced because of its encouragement"* (v31). Judas and Silas *"encouraged and strengthened the brothers"* (v32), and were *"sent off in peace"* (v33). The letter answers the *"unsettling"* of their *"minds"* (v24).

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- The church's choosing comes after God's own choosing and after listening.
- It is reached in one accord and joined to what seemed good to the Holy Spirit.
- Its outcome is joy, encouragement, strength and peace, where minds had been unsettled.

## Proverbs 3:31 — "Do not envy a man of violence and do not choose any of his ways"

The chapter is a father's teaching to a son, and it is about the heart:
- *"let your heart keep my commandments"* (v1)
- *"Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding"* (v5)
- *"Be not wise in your own eyes; fear the Lord, and turn away from evil"* (v7)

The father's ground is love: *"the Lord reproves him whom he loves, as a father the son in whom he delights"* (v12).

The verse forbids two things, and they are in order:
- *"Do not envy a man of violence"*: the pull, inside. Envy (H7065) is wanting what he has.
- *"and do not choose any of his ways"*: the choice that would follow from it.

The command reaches back past the choice to the envy that would draw it. Proverbs describes the same man elsewhere, in a separate saying: *"A man of violence entices his neighbor and leads him in a way that is not good"* (16:29). The violent man draws others into his way.

The reason given is the Lord’s own stance: *"for the devious person is an abomination to the Lord, but the upright are in his confidence"* (v32). "Confidence" is H5475, the same word as *"The friendship of the Lord is for those who fear him"* (Psa 25:14). The one who does not choose the violent man's ways is brought into the Lord’s confidence. The sayings that follow set the outcomes side by side: *"The Lord’s curse is on the house of the wicked, but he blesses the dwelling of the righteous"* (v33); *"The wise will inherit honor, but fools get disgrace"* (v35).

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- Before the choice is the pull, here envy, and the command reaches the pull as well as the choice.
- Not choosing a way is itself a choice.
- Its ground is the Lord’s own verdict on what is devious and what is upright.
- Its outcome is the Lord’s confidence, the same word as his friendship in Psalm 25.

## Proverbs 16:16 — "To get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver"

The saying weighs one good against another: *"How much better to get wisdom than gold! To get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver."* "Better" is H2896A, and "to be chosen" is the choosing verb (H0977). To choose here is to prefer: to set one thing above another and take it. What is preferred is inward, wisdom and understanding, and what is passed over is wealth.

The same weighing is made at more length in Proverbs 3: *"the gain from her is better than gain from silver and her profit better than gold. She is more precious than jewels, and nothing you desire can compare with her"* (3:14–15). "Nothing you desire" uses the word for delight (H2656). Choosing wisdom means valuing her above everything a person delights in.

The saying stands among other sayings on how a person's ways are chosen and weighed. Each is its own saying, not an outcome of 16:16:
- *"All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but the Lord weighs the spirit"* (16:2)
- *"The heart of man plans his way, but the Lord establishes his steps"* (16:9)
- *"There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way to death"* (16:25)
- *"The lot is cast into the lap, but its every decision is from the Lord"* (16:33)

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- Choosing is preferring: one good weighed against another.
- What is to be preferred is inward (wisdom, understanding) over wealth.
- The weighing goes against what a person desires; nothing desired compares with wisdom.

## What batch 3 adds to the account of choosing

These are set beside the test readings and batches 1–2. Nothing earlier has to be withdrawn.

**New:**
- **Choosing as a person's own freedom, honoured.** In Deu 23:16 and 2Sa 19:38 the ground is the chooser's own good: *"wherever it suits him"*, *"whatever seems good to you"*. Others are bound to honour it. This is a face not seen in batches 1–2, where the choosing was between the Lord and something else. Here "good in your eyes" is honoured. In Jos 24:15, "evil in your eyes" was where the choice against the Lord could start. The same expression changes character with the setting (#1919).
- **Own accord alongside God's working.** Titus goes *"of his own accord"* after God *"put into the heart"* the care (2Cor 8:16–17). The Macedonians give *"of their own accord"*, having given themselves to the Lord *"by the will of God"* (8:3, 5). Both are named and neither cancels the other. This sits with batch 2 (the way to choose is taught) and the test (Psa 119:32, the heart enlarged by God).
- **Choosing together.** The church chooses out of a shared inward response (*"pleased the whole gathering"*) and in *"one accord"*. Its choosing follows God's own choosing, uses the same verb, and is joined to what *"seemed good to the Holy Spirit"* (Act 6:5; 15:7, 22, 25, 28). The outcome is in hearts: joy, encouragement, peace.
- **The pull before the choice.** *"Do not envy … and do not choose"* (Pro 3:31): the command reaches the envy as well as the choice. This stands beside batch 1, where *"desired"* is paired with *"chosen"* (Isa 1:29) and the soul's delight with choosing one's own ways (Isa 66:3).
- **Choosing as preferring.** *"to be chosen rather than silver"* (Pro 16:16): to choose is to weigh and set above. What is to be preferred is inward, and it is valued above *"nothing you desire"* (Pro 3:15).
- **The knowing of good and evil has more than one face.** In Isa 7:15 it is the knowing that grounds choosing the good. In 2Sa 19:35 the same words are the palate's knowing of pleasant and unpleasant, and they fade with age.
- **The Lord’s confidence as the outcome.** The upright are *"in his confidence"* (Pro 3:32), the same word as *"The friendship of the Lord"* for those who fear him (Psa 25:14).

**Kept open:**
- Deu 23:16 echoes the phrase used for the place the Lord chooses (Deu 12). I have marked this "I read". Whether it is to be read as more than a shared form of words is for the researcher.

**For the next batch:** batch 4 is the Job verses (Job 15:5, 34:4, 34:33, 36:21). These are read as choices weighed by another party, per the researcher's F1 ruling, and kept in their own section.
