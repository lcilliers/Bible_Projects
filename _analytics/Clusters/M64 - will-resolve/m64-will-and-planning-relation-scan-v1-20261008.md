# M64: how the will verses relate to choosing, planning and setting direction (scan, v1, 2026-10-08)

**Escalation:** #1986 · **Status:** a scan for your review. Nothing is woven.

Researcher, verbatim: *"how does the will verses relate to planning or setting direction, these will verses specifically appears to be in support of the choose, will, plan, devise continuim"*.

- **Input:** [`m64-will-verses-v1-20261008.csv`](m64-will-verses-v1-20261008.csv) (99 verses).
- **How they were read:** this is a scan. Each verse was read on its own, and the passage only where noted. No verse has had a full passage reading.
- **What the counts come from:** they are taken from the ESV text and the Strong's in iba.db.

## 1. In brief

The verses support your picture, but each word joins it at a different point. **The Greek gives the clearest evidence.** The Greek "will" word belongs to the same family of words as the Greek "plan" words. **The Hebrew is different.** The main "will" word, *ʾāḇâ*, is almost always a "no": a refusal of something another person puts forward. It is the yes or no to someone else's request, counsel or command. It is not the forming of one's own plan.

So the will verses do not repeat choosing or planning. They add the point where a person consents or refuses: to a request, to counsel, to God's call. **No verse sets out an order** such as choose, then will, then plan, then devise. The links below are only the ones a word or a passage itself makes.

## 2. The Greek: will and plan are one family

| Strong's | Word | Step gloss | ESV renderings in M64 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1014 | *boulomai* | to plan | want, wish, desire, will, would, intend, resolved, **chooses** |
| G1013 | *boulēma* | plan | **plan** (Act 27:43), **will** (Rom 9:19) |
| G1012 | *boulē* | plan | plan, purpose, counsel, decision |
| G1011 | *bouleuomai* | to plan | made plans, planned, deliberate, wanted |

All four have the step gloss "plan". One word, G1013, is rendered "plan" in one verse and "will" in another. And G1014 is rendered "chooses": *"anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him"* (Mat 11:27; Luk 10:22). In Greek, then, willing, choosing and planning are spoken of with one family of words. This is a fact about the words. What it means for the inner being is still to be read verse by verse.

These verses join willing to planning or setting direction directly:
- **The will steers.** *"they are guided by a very small rudder wherever the will of the pilot directs"* (Jam 3:4). The will is pictured as what sets a ship's direction. In its passage (3:3–5) the picture is used of the tongue.
- **A will set against a plan.** *"But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, kept them from carrying out their plan"* (Act 27:43). His wishing (G1014) stopped the soldiers' plan (G1013).
- **A resolve reached after weighing, then turned.** *"her husband Joseph, being a just man and unwilling to put her to shame, resolved to divorce her quietly"* (Mat 1:19). The next verse: *"But as he considered these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream"* (1:20). The resolve was still being weighed, and God redirected it.
- **An intention carried ahead.** Herod put Peter in prison, *"intending after the Passover to bring him out to the people"* (Act 12:4).
- **A wish that becomes a plan.** *"I wanted to come to you first"* (2Co 1:15), and two verses on, *"Do I make my plans according to the flesh"* (1:17). The passage moves from wanting (G1014) to plans (G1011). This is already in 10.7 *Planning*.
- **A wish stopped or helped by others.** *"when Paul wished to go in among the crowd, the disciples would not let him"* (Act 19:30). *"when he wished to cross to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him"* (Act 18:27).
- **A will turned toward ruin.** *"those who desire to be rich fall into temptation, into a snare"* (1Ti 6:9). *"whoever wishes to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God"* (Jam 4:4).
- **Wishing to please.** *"Pilate, wishing to satisfy the crowd, released for them Barabbas"* (Mar 15:15).

**God's will** (route A, for 13.1):
- *"Of his own will he brought us forth"* (Jam 1:18)
- *"not wishing that any should perish"* (2Pe 3:9)
- *"when God desired to show more convincingly … the unchangeable character of his purpose"* (Heb 6:17), where will and purpose stand in one sentence
- *"who can resist his will?"* (Rom 9:19)
- the Spirit *"apportions to each one individually as he wills"* (1Co 12:11)

**And at Gethsemane:** *"Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me. Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done"* (Luk 22:42). This is the one verse in the set where two wills are named side by side and one is given up to the other.

**The epistle openings and requests** (*"I want you to know"*, Phi 1:12; Tit 3:8; Jude 5; 1Ti 2:8; 5:14; 2Jo 12; Phm 13; 3Jo 10) mostly use the word as a stated wish in teaching. Their bearing on setting direction is slight, but each still needs to be read.

## 3. The Hebrew: the will is the yes or no to another

*ʾāḇâ* (H0014) has 52 verses. **50 of them are negated**: "would not", "not willing", "unwilling", "refuse", "would have none". The other two are a question that expects "no" (*"Is the wild ox willing to serve you?"*, Job 39:9) and a condition (*"If you are willing and obedient, you shall eat the good of the land"*, Isa 1:19).

In almost every case, the "no" answers something put before the person:
- **A request:** *"he would not let them go"* (Exo 10:27); *"the king of Edom would not listen … he would not consent"* (Judg 11:17); *"Joab would not come to him"* (2Sa 14:29).
- **A command:** the king's servants *"would not put out their hand to strike the priests of the Lord"* (1Sa 22:17). The armor-bearer *"would not, for he feared greatly"* (1Sa 31:4).
- **Counsel and reproof:** *"you have ignored all my counsel and would have none of my reproof"* (Pro 1:25; 1:30). The word for counsel, *ʿēṣāh* (H6098, M16), is one the ESV elsewhere renders "plan".
- **God's call:** *"In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength." But you were unwilling* (Isa 30:15); *"Israel would not submit to me"* (Psa 81:11); *"not willing to listen to me: because all the house of Israel have a hard forehead and a stubborn heart"* (Eze 3:7).
- **Something offered at a cost to others:** David *"would not drink it"* and gives his reason: *"Shall I drink the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?"* (2Sa 23:16–17).

**Isaiah 30 joins the refusing will to planning in one passage.** It opens: *"Ah, stubborn children," declares the Lord, "who carry out a plan, but not mine"* (Isa 30:1). It goes on to the people *"unwilling to hear the instruction of the Lord"* (30:9) and ends the oracle with *"But you were unwilling"* (30:15). Here refusing God's word goes with carrying out a plan of one's own. This is the closest the Hebrew verses come to your continuum, and it is a single passage, not a pattern.

**The will is tied to the state of the heart and spirit:**
- *"the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he would not let them go"* (Exo 10:27)
- *"the Lord your God hardened his spirit and made his heart obstinate"* (Deu 2:30)
- *"a hard forehead and a stubborn heart"* (Eze 3:7)

**God's "not willing"** (route A):
- *"The Lord was unwilling to destroy you"* (Deu 10:10)
- *"the Lord was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of David his servant"* (2Ki 8:19)
- *"the Lord would not pardon"* (2Ki 24:4)
- *"The Lord will not be willing to forgive him"* (Deu 29:20)

His unwillingness goes both ways, and the verses give his reasons: covenant, promise and the blood shed.

**The willing heart (*nāḏîḇ*, H5081G, 5 verses)** is the will freely given:
- *"Whoever is of a generous heart, let him bring the Lord's contribution"* (Exo 35:5)
- *"All who were of a willing heart brought"* (Exo 35:22; also 2Ch 29:31; 1Ch 28:21)
- It is asked of God: *"uphold me with a willing spirit"* (Psa 51:12).

10.7 *Setting the heart* already cites Exo 35:21, *"everyone whose heart stirred him, and everyone whose spirit moved him"*, the verse just before 35:22.

**The Aramaic *ṣᵊḇāʾ* (H6634, Daniel, 7 verses)** sets an absolute human will beside God's.
- Of Nebuchadnezzar: *"Whom he would, he killed, and whom he would, he kept alive"* (Dan 5:19).
- He was driven out *"until he knew that the Most High God rules the kingdom of mankind and sets over it whom he will"* (5:21; 4:17, 25, 32).
- *"he does according to his will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay his hand"* (4:35).

## 4. Where the will verses sit beside choosing and planning

This is what the words and passages above show. It is not a scheme laid over them.

| Act | What the verses show it doing | Word link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choosing (10.7) | Taking one thing over another | G1014 is rendered "chooses" (Mat 11:27) |
| Willing | Consenting or refusing what is put before one (Hebrew); wanting, intending, resolving, steering (Greek) | G1014 and G1013 share a family with the plan words; Jam 3:4 is the rudder |
| Planning and devising (10.7, 10.4, 10.10) | Shaping ahead, weighing, plotting | G1011 and G1012 are in the same family; Isa 30:1 ties the refusal to a plan "not mine" |

I read the will verses as standing at the point where a choice meets what someone else puts forward: another's request, counsel, plan or call. The Hebrew sees it almost always as refusal. The Greek sees it as wanting and intending, which can become a plan or be set against one. This reading is mine; no single verse states it.
