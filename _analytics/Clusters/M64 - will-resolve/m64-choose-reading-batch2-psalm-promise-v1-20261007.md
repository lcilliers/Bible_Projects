## Psalm 25:12 — "Him will he instruct in the way that he should choose"

The psalm is a prayer from someone in trouble. It begins *"To you, O Lord, I lift up my soul"*; *"O my God, in you I trust; let me not be put to shame"* (v1–2). It ends with that same person *"lonely and afflicted"*. Among the trouble:
- *"The troubles of my heart are enlarged"* (v17)
- *"how many are my foes, and with what violent hatred they hate me"* (v19)
- *"guard my soul … Let me not be put to shame"* (v20)

Running through the whole psalm is the person's own guilt:
- *"Remember not the sins of my youth or my transgressions"* (v7)
- *"pardon my guilt, for it is great"* (v11)
- *"forgive all my sins"* (v18)

The person who asks about the way is a sinner, and says so.

Before the verse about choosing, the psalmist asks for the way three times over:
- *"Make me to know your ways, O Lord; teach me your paths"* (v4)
- *"Lead me in your truth and teach me"* (v5)

Then he says why he can expect it, from who the Lord is: *"Good and upright is the Lord; therefore he instructs sinners in the way. He leads the humble in what is right, and teaches the humble his way"* (v8–9). The Lord's instructing comes out of his goodness. It is given to sinners and to the humble.

Then comes the verse: *"Who is the man who fears the Lord? Him will he instruct in the way that he should choose"* (v12). The word "instruct" is the same in v8 and v12 (H3384B). The person who chooses here does not make up the way. The way to choose is taught, and the one who is taught it is the one who fears the Lord. The fear comes first, then the teaching, then the choice. The verse does not say the man chooses well on his own; it says the Lord instructs him in what he should choose.

What follows the choice is stated:
- *"His soul shall abide in well-being, and his offspring shall inherit the land"* (v13). "Well-being" here (H2896B) is the same word as *"the good"* in Isaiah 7:15, *"refuse the evil and choose the good"*. The man taught the way he should choose has his soul *"abide"*, lodge or stay, in the good.
- *"The friendship of the Lord is for those who fear him, and he makes known to them his covenant"* (v14). The teaching goes further than a direction to take. The one who fears the Lord is brought into his friendship, and the covenant is made known (H3045, "know", as in v4).

The psalmist then turns his eyes: *"My eyes are ever toward the Lord, for he will pluck my feet out of the net"* (v15). Where the eyes are turned decides where the feet are kept from.

The heart's trouble comes back after the choosing verse, not before it. *"The troubles of my heart are enlarged"* (v17) uses the same verb as Psalm 119:32, *"when you enlarge my heart"* (H7337). In Psalm 25 it is the troubles that widen in the heart. In Psalm 119 it is the heart that the Lord widens so the chooser can run. I note the shared word; neither psalm refers to the other.

The psalm does not end with a person who has chosen and is now safe. It ends with waiting: *"May integrity and uprightness preserve me, for I wait for you"* (v21). The way chosen is held while waiting on the Lord, with the troubles still there.

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- The way to choose is taught by the Lord, out of his goodness, to sinners and the humble.
- The one taught is the one who fears the Lord. Fear comes before the choosing.
- The outcome reaches the soul: it abides in the good, and is brought into the Lord's friendship and the knowing of his covenant.
- Choosing is held with the eyes turned to the Lord, and while waiting on him, in the middle of trouble and guilt.

## Isaiah 56:4 — "who choose the things that please me and hold fast my covenant"

The oracle opens with a command and a reason: *"Keep justice, and do righteousness, for soon my salvation will come, and my righteousness be revealed"* (v1). Then a blessing on the one who *"holds it fast, who keeps the Sabbath, not profaning it, and keeps his hand from doing any evil"* (v2). The holding fast and the keeping from evil are already there before the choosing verse.

The verse is addressed to two people who speak about themselves:
- *"Let not the foreigner who has joined himself to the Lord say"*, *"The Lord will surely separate me from his people"*
- *"let not the eunuch say"*, *"Behold, I am a dry tree"* (v3)

Each one has a judgement about himself: shut out, and fruitless. The foreigner expects to be separated. The eunuch sees himself as a tree with no sap and no fruit, with no sons and daughters to carry his name. The Lord tells them both not to say it.

Then he describes the eunuchs he means: *"who keep my Sabbaths, who choose the things that please me and hold fast my covenant"* (v4). The word "please" is H2654A, the same word as *"delight"* in Isaiah 65:12 and 66:4 (*"chose what I did not delight in"*, *"chose that in which I did not delight"*). Here the choosing goes the other way. These choose what the Lord delights in. The measure is the same as in those two oracles, the Lord's delight, and here it is met.

The choosing stands between keeping and holding fast:
- *"keep my Sabbaths"*
- *"choose the things that please me"*
- *"hold fast my covenant"* (H2388H, the same word as *"holds it fast"* in v2)

The choice is not one moment. It sits inside a keeping and a holding on.

The Lord's answer goes straight to what the eunuch said about himself: *"I will give in my house and within my walls a monument and a name better than sons and daughters; I will give them an everlasting name that shall not be cut off"* (v5). The man who called himself a dry tree is given a name that outlasts children. The foreigner who feared being separated is brought in: *"these I will bring to my holy mountain, and make them joyful in my house of prayer; their burnt offerings and their sacrifices will be accepted"* (v7).

The foreigners are described in inner terms as well: they *"join themselves to the Lord, to minister to him, to love the name of the Lord, and to be his servants"* (v6). Love of the name stands with the keeping and the holding fast.

What follows in the chapter is the opposite. Israel's own watchmen *"are all without knowledge"*. They are *"shepherds who have no understanding; they have all turned to their own way, each to his own gain"* (v10–11). "Their own way" is the same phrase as in Isaiah 66:3, *"These have chosen their own ways"* (H1870G). The outsiders who choose what the Lord delights in are brought in. The insiders turn to their own way.

**What this verse shows of choosing:**
- Choosing what the Lord delights in is the positive counterpart to choosing what he does not delight in (65:12, 66:4). The measure is the same.
- The choice is held inside keeping and holding fast. It is not a single act.
- It is made by people whose own judgement of themselves is "shut out" and "a dry tree", and the Lord answers that judgement directly.
- The outcome is a name that is not cut off, a place in his house, joy in prayer, and acceptance.
- The love of his name goes with it.
- Its opposite in the same chapter is turning to one's own way, by those who should know and do not.

## What batch 2 adds to the account of choosing

These are set beside the test readings and batch 1. Nothing earlier has to be withdrawn.

**New:**
- **The way to choose is taught (Psa 25:8, 12).** The Lord instructs sinners and the humble out of his goodness, and the one who fears him is taught *"the way that he should choose"*. This stands beside Psalm 119:29, where the psalmist asks God to put the false way far off. In both psalms the chooser does not stand alone.
- **Fear of the Lord comes before the choosing (Psa 25:12, 14).** The test reading of Joshua 24 had *"fear the Lord and serve him"* (v14) before the choice. Psalm 25 puts the fear first explicitly: it is the man who fears whom the Lord instructs.
- **The good chosen is the good the soul lives in.** Isaiah 7:15 has *"choose the good"*, and Psalm 25:13 has *"His soul shall abide in well-being"*. It is the same word (H2896B). I read this as the good that is chosen becoming the place where the soul lives. Each verse says its own half; neither refers to the other.
- **Choosing what the Lord delights in (Isa 56:4).** This is the counterpart to Isaiah 65:12 and 66:4. With the three verses together, "the things that please me" and "what I did not delight in" are the two sides of one measure.
- **The choice is held, not just made.** It is set between keeping and holding fast (Isa 56:2, 4, 6), and between eyes turned to the Lord and waiting (Psa 25:15, 21). This sits with the test readings: the heart inclined after the choice (Jos 24:23), and clinging to the testimonies (Psa 119:31).
- **The chooser's own judgement of himself (Isa 56:3).** "Separated" and "a dry tree" are answered by the Lord, not by the chooser. The chooser's verdict on himself is not the last word.
- **Outcomes reach the inner being on the right side too.** Batch 1 recorded shame and fears for the wrong choice. Here:
  - the soul abides in the good, and is given friendship with the Lord and the knowing of his covenant (Psa 25:13–14)
  - joy in his house, and a name not cut off (Isa 56:5, 7)

**Kept open:**
- Psalm 25 ends still waiting and still in trouble (v16–22). The right choosing does not remove the trouble in the psalm. It is held while waiting.

**For the next batches:** batch 3 is people choosing of their own accord, and choosing together (Deu 23:16, 2Sa 19:38, 2Cor 8:17, Act 6:5, Act 15:22 and 25), with the wisdom sayings Pro 3:31 and 16:16. These are mostly not choices between God and other gods. Whether they show the same tie to what the eyes see, to delight and to knowing is to be read from them.
