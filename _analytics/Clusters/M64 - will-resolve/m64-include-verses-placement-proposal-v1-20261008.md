## 3. Proposed placements

### 3.1 Deu 30:14, 17, 20, in 10.7 *Life and death set before a person*

After the Deu 30:19 sentence, add:

> The passage says what choosing life is: *"loving the Lord your God, obeying his voice and holding fast to him, for he is your life and length of days"* (30:20). It describes the other way from inside: *"if your heart turns away, and you will not hear, but are drawn away to worship other gods and serve them"* (30:17). Before the choice is set out, it says the choice can be made: *"the word is very near you. It is in your mouth and in your heart, so that you can do it"* (30:14).

### 3.2 Job 7:15, in Ch 2 *Wanting to die*

Extend the Job bullet:

> … (7:11). The wish comes out of his nights: *"When I say, 'My bed will comfort me' … then you scare me with dreams and terrify me with visions"* (7:13–14). He says it to God, *"Leave me alone"* (7:16), and he does not act on it.

(The section's opening already says that Job's "I" is *nephesh*.)

### 3.3 Jer 8:3, in Ch 2 *Wanting to die*

After the Jer 8:3 sentence (fixed under #1991), add:

> It is said as part of a judgment the Lord declares. Before it, the bones of the dead are spread *"before the sun and the moon and all the host of heaven, which they have loved and served, which they have gone after"* (8:2). The verse does not say that the one leads to the other.

### 3.4 Luk 10:42, in 10.7 *Holding a choice*, as a new bullet

> - **It can be guarded by another.** Mary *"sat at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching"* (Luke 10:39). Martha asked Jesus to send her to help (10:40). He named what Mary was doing a choice, and kept it for her: *"Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her"* (10:42).

10.1 already carries "many things against one" for anxiety. This bullet adds the choosing half of v42.

### 3.5 Phili 1:22–25, in 10.7 *Life and death set before a person*, as a new bullet

> - **A choice between two goods can stay open.** *"Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell. I am hard pressed between the two. My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better. But to remain in the flesh is more necessary on your account"* (Philippians 1:22–24). Paul does not settle it by choosing. He goes on: *"Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with you all"* (1:25).

### 3.6 Exo 36:2, in 10.7 *Willing*, *The willing heart*

Add a bullet after the Exo 35:21–22 bullet:

> - Of the craftsmen, the skill given and the heart that moves are named together: *"every craftsman in whose mind the Lord had put skill, everyone whose heart stirred him up to come to do the work"* (Exodus 36:2).

### 3.7 Psa 51:13, in 10.7 *Willing*, *The willing heart*

Extend the Psa 51 bullet:

> … (Psalm 51:10, 12). The next verse goes on: *"Then I will teach transgressors your ways, and sinners will return to you"* (51:13).

### 3.8 Isa 10:12–15, in 10.13 *Those who carry it out*

Extend the Assyria bullet:

> … (Isaiah 10:5, 7). He boasts, *"By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom"* (10:13), and the Lord *"will punish the speech of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria and the boastful look in his eyes"* (10:12). *"Shall the axe boast over him who hews with it …?"* (10:15).

### 3.9 Rom 8:7, in Ch 12 *Flesh as an orientation*

Lengthen the quote:

> It has a mind *"hostile to God, for it does not submit to God's law; indeed, it cannot"* (Romans 8:7).

### 3.10 Isa 66:2 beside 66:4, in 10.12 *What changes it*, *One's own conduct*

After the Isa 66:4 quote, add:

> The same passage opens with the one God looks to, who *"trembles at my word"* (66:2). I read the passage as setting that trembling beside "their fears".

### 3.11 Pro 1:29, in 10.12, the *It strikes* bullet

After the Pro 1:26–27 quote, add:

> The same speech says they *"did not choose the fear of the Lord"* (1:29). I read the passage as setting the fear of the Lord, not chosen, beside the terror that comes.

(Pro 1:22, *"How long, O simple ones, will you love being simple?"*, is not proposed. 10.7 *Willing* already carries the refusals of this passage.)

### 3.12 2Sa 19:23 in 10.10, and 2Sa 14:13–14 in 10.7 *Planning*

**2Sa 19:19, in 10.10 *Love does not keep the count*.** After Shimei's plea, add:

> David answered, *"You shall not die"*, and gave him his oath (19:23).

(1Ki 2:8–9, David's later charge to Solomon, uses another word. It is not proposed.)

**2Sa 14:13–14, in 10.7 *Planning*.** This is a citation fix of the #1990 kind: the same Joab issue in a place the #1987 reading did not list. Replace the paragraph that begins *"A plan can also be held against someone's good"* with:

> A plan can also be held against someone's good without anyone calling it a plot. Joab knew *"that the king's heart went out to Absalom"* (2 Samuel 14:1). He sent a wise woman of Tekoa with a staged plea, and *"put the words in her mouth"* (14:3). In it she told David that by leaving Absalom in exile he had *"planned such a thing against the people of God"* (14:13). Beside it she set God, who *"devises means so that the banished one will not remain an outcast"* (14:14). David saw whose plan it was: *"Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?"* (14:19). Three are at work in the one passage: Joab's scheme, the king's planning, and God's devising as she declares it. The king's planning is measured against God's (13.1, *Purposing*).
