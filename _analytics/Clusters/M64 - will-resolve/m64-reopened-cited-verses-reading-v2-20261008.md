## Set 1: the cited choose verses

### Deuteronomy 30:19: "Therefore choose life"

> **Q1 citation: ✅ Accurate.** Ch 2 *Life set before a person* and 10.7 opening use the choosing in its setting. Ch 2 also quotes v20 "he is your life" (*Scripture names life as a person*).
>
> **Q2 M64: Include: corroborates and adds.** Corroborates Ch 2 and 10.7 (life set before a person as a choice). Adds: v20 states what choosing life is (loving, obeying, holding fast); Ch 2 makes that point from other verses. Adds v17, the heart turning and being "drawn away", and v14, "so that you can do it".
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M25 life/death: carried in Ch 2; M25 placement is held (OT-08), so no revisit now. M80 blessing, M07 curse, M82 witness: set before the person, not inner acts; data only.

This is Moses' last charge. The choice is set out twice: *"I have set before you today life and good, death and evil"* (v15), and *"life and death, blessing and curse"* (v19).

The passage says what choosing life is: *"loving the Lord your God, obeying his voice and holding fast to him, for he is your life and length of days"* (v20). Choosing life is a bond to a person. The person is called "your life".

The other way is described from inside: *"if your heart turns away, and you will not hear, but are drawn away to worship other gods"* (v17). The heart turns, the hearing stops, and the person is drawn.

Before the choice is offered, the passage says it can be done: *"the word is very near you. It is in your mouth and in your heart, so that you can do it"* (v14).

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Life set before a person*) and the 10.7 opening quote v19 for the choice itself. Here the narrative does use the choosing.

**Not yet carried:**
- v14, that the word is already in the mouth and heart, "so that you can do it"
- v17, the heart turning away and being "drawn away"
- v20, choosing life spelled out as loving, obeying and holding fast *(v2: Ch 2 quotes "he is your life" from v20, but not as what choosing life is)*

### Job 7:15: "so that I would choose strangling and death rather than my bones"

> **Q1 citation: ❗ Needs attention.** Ch 2 *Wanting to die* is accurate. The 10.7 working note (*Expected additions*) puts "Job's own words about choosing (Job 7:15 …)" under *Choices weighed by another*. No one weighs this choice: it is Job's own would-be choice, spoken to God in complaint. It belongs with the next bullet, *Choosing between death and life*. This is a working note, not narrative text.
>
> **Q2 M64: Include: new.** Corroborates Ch 2 *Wanting to die*. New: the soul (*nepheš*) is the chooser; the choice is set off by terror sent in dreams (v14); it is spoken to God, not acted on.
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M47 soul (H5315I): yes, for Ch 5 *The soul*: the soul as the one that would choose (#1979). M25 death: carried in Ch 2.

Job speaks *"in the anguish of my spirit … in the bitterness of my soul"* (v11). The choice follows from what is done to him at night: *"When I say, 'My bed will comfort me' … then you scare me with dreams and terrify me with visions, so that I would choose strangling and death"* (vv13–15). He goes on: *"I loathe my life; I would not live forever. Leave me alone"* (v16).

The ESV "I" in v15 is *nepheš* (H5315I): in the Hebrew it is the soul that would choose. It is a choice the soul would make. Job does not act on it.

It comes out of terror, and it is spoken to God: *"Why have you made me your mark?"* (v20).

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Wanting to die*) quotes vv15–16 with the choosing word.

**Not yet carried:**
- that the soul is the chooser
- that the choice is set off by terror sent in dreams (v14)
- that it is spoken as complaint to God, not acted on

### Proverbs 1:29: "and did not choose the fear of the Lord"

> **Q1 citation: ✅ Accurate.** 10.2 *Knowing and hearing* quotes it for knowing refused. 10.2, 10.12 and 10.13 also carry vv26–27 and v33.
>
> **Q2 M64: Include: new.** Corroborates 10.2 (knowledge refused) and 10.12/10.13 (terror that strikes, vv26–27). New: the fear of the Lord as something chosen or not chosen; the refusal loved over time (v22); the outcome "have their fill of their own devices" (v31).
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M01 fear: yes. 10.12 does not carry the fear of the Lord as an object of choice. M06 hate ("hated knowledge"): yes, when hatred is taken up; here hating is a form of refusing. M15 knowledge: carried in 10.2.

Wisdom calls in the street (vv20–21). Not choosing the fear of the Lord stands among the other refusals:
- *"you refused to listen"* (v24)
- *"would have none of my reproof"* (v25)
- *"they hated knowledge"* (v29)
- *"would have none of my counsel and despised all my reproof"* (v30; "would" there is H0014, in the #1986 will set)

**What is refused is the fear of the Lord itself.** This fear is something a person can choose or leave.

The refusal has a history. It is chosen over time: *"How long, O simple ones, will you love being simple?"* (v22).

It has an offer on the other side: *"If you turn at my reproof, behold, I will pour out my spirit to you"* (v23).

Its outcome is their own choice returned to them: *"they shall eat the fruit of their way, and have their fill of their own devices"* (v31).

The fear they refused comes back as a different fear: *"I will mock when terror strikes you"* (v26). The verse does not join the two. I read the passage as setting the fear of the Lord, refused, beside terror that comes.

**Narrative:** 10.2 (*Knowing and hearing*) quotes v29, with the choosing word, for knowledge refused.

**Not yet carried:**
- the fear of the Lord as an object of choice (for 10.7 and 10.12)
- the refusals that stand beside it in the passage
- the outcome "have their fill of their own devices" *(v2: vv26–27 and v33 are quoted in 10.2, 10.12 and 10.13; v31 is not)*

### Isaiah 42:1: "my chosen, in whom my soul delights"

>
> **Q2 M64: Include: new.** New for 13.1 (route A): God's choosing joined with his soul's delight and the giving of his Spirit. New for 10.7 *Being chosen*: the bearing of the chosen one (vv2–4: quiet, gentle with the bruised, not faint or discouraged). Stated of this servant only.
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M39 delight (H7521): yes, for 13.1 (God's delight in the one he chose). M47 soul/Spirit: God's soul is in Ch 11 §8; the Spirit belongs to the separate Holy Spirit study. M12 justice: the task, not an inner act. In the passage, v4 "grow faint or be discouraged" (M24) is said of the chosen one: a pointer for M24 when it is taken up.

God speaks of his servant. In one verse he chooses, delights with his soul, and gives his Spirit: *"my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him"*.

The passage then describes the chosen one's bearing:

### Isaiah 66:4: "I also will choose harsh treatment for them"

> **Q1 citation: ✅ Accurate.** Accurate in all six places: 10.12 *What changes it*, 10.7 *Whose measure* and *When the choice is wrong*, Ch 11 §4 *Delight*, 13.1 (twice).
>
> **Q2 M64: Include: corroborates.** Corroborates 10.7 v13, 13.1 v4, 10.12 and Ch 11 §4. The only thing not drawn is the passage's own setting of trembling at his word (v2) beside "their fears" (v4). Both verses are in 10.12, in different sections. That pairing is my reading ("I read").
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M01 fear: only the v2/v4 pairing, as above; hold it for the researcher. M41 hearing, M42 calling, M65 speaking, M18 delight, M58 evil: carried in 13.1, 10.6, 10.7 and Ch 11. No other revisit.

Choosing runs both ways in vv3–4:
- *"These have chosen their own ways, and their soul delights in their abominations"* (v3)
- *"I also will choose harsh treatment for them and bring their fears upon them"* (v4)
- God's reason: *"when I called, no one answered, when I spoke, they did not listen; but they did what was evil in my eyes and chose that in which I did not delight"* (v4)

The passage opens with the one God looks to: *"he who is humble and contrite in spirit and trembles at my word"* (v2).

So the passage holds two kinds of fear:
- trembling at his word, in the one he looks to (v2)
- "their fears" brought on those who chose their own ways (v4)

**Narrative:**
- 10.12 (*What changes it*) quotes "bring their fears upon them".
- 10.7 points to Isa 66:4 in *Whose measure* (the delight word).
- 10.7 v13 and 13.1 v4 already carry God's choosing set against theirs, from the §2a reading of 66:3.

**Not yet carried:** most of the verse is in place. The pairing of trembling at his word (v2) with their fears (v4) is not drawn anywhere. I read it as the passage's own pairing; it is not stated as one in either verse.

### Jeremiah 8:3: "Death shall be preferred to life"

> **Q1 citation: ⚠ Check.** Ch 2 *Wanting to die*: "Under judgment, death is wished for and withheld". The verse says death "shall be preferred" (H0977, Niphal: *shall be chosen*). "Wished for" softens the choosing word, and "withheld" belongs to Rev 9:6 only, not to Jer 8:3.
>
> **Q2 M64: Include: corroborates and adds.** Corroborates Ch 2 *Wanting to die*. New: the preference is a choosing word, and the Lord declares it as part of a judgment, after the people had loved, served and gone after other things (v2).
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M25 life/death: carried in Ch 2. M58 evil, M43 declares, M72 hosts: no.

This follows the Topheth judgment (7:30–34). The bones of kings, priests, prophets and people are brought out and spread *"before the sun and the moon and all the host of heaven, which they have loved and served, which they have gone after, and which they have sought and worshiped"* (8:2).

Then: *"Death shall be preferred to life by all the remnant that remains of this evil family in all the places where I have driven them"* (8:3).

"Preferred" is H0977 in the Niphal: death *shall be chosen* (literal sense of the Niphal) rather than life. The remnant prefer death, and the Lord declares it as part of the judgment.

**Narrative:** Ch 2 (*Wanting to die*) quotes the verse with the word "preferred". The use is in its own context.

**Not yet carried:** that this preference is stated as part of a judgment the Lord declares, after the people had loved, served and gone after other things (v2). The verse does not say the one leads to the other.

### Luke 10:42: "Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her"

> **Q1 citation: ⚠ Check.** 10.1 *What anxiety does* and Ch 11 §4 quote vv41–42 accurately for anxiety. The headings "It divides" and "Whether it divides the person" take the word "divided" from 1 Cor 7:34. Luke's own words are "distracted" (v40) and "many things" against "one thing". The second half of v42, the choosing, is not quoted.
>
> **Q2 M64: Include: new.** New for 10.7: a choice seen in a posture (sitting, listening); named a choice by Jesus; set against distraction by many things; guarded against another's demand ("will not be taken away from her"). Corroborates 10.1 on anxiety.
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M05 good ("the good portion"): describes what was chosen; no. No other M-code in the verse.

Mary *"sat at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching"* (v39). Martha was *"distracted with much serving"*. She asks him to intervene: *"Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her then to help me"* (v40).

Jesus answers:
- *"you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary"* (vv41–42)
- *"Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her"* (v42)

The choosing is seen in what Mary is doing: sitting and listening. Jesus is the one who calls it a choice. He then protects it against another person's demand: it "will not be taken away from her".

The verb is in the middle voice (G1586, V-AMI): she chose it for herself.

**Narrative:** 10.1 (*What anxiety does*, *Care that is good*) and Ch 11 §4 quote vv41–42 up to "one thing is necessary". These are about anxiety. The choosing half of v42 is not quoted.

**Not yet carried:** for 10.7, this choice:

- is named a choice by Jesus
- stands against being distracted by many things
- is guarded against another's demand

### Philippians 1:22: "Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell"

> **Q1 citation: ✅ Accurate.** Ch 7 *Flesh* quotes "If I am to live in the flesh …" for flesh as bodily life. Ch 2 quotes vv23–24 for Paul weighing life and death. Both accurate.
>
> **Q2 M64: Include: new.** New for 10.7: a choice between two goods that "I cannot tell"; his desire set against others' need; settled by being "convinced" and coming to "know" (v25), not by an act of choosing. Corroborates Ch 2.
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** M25 live and M47 flesh: carried in Ch 2 and Ch 7. In the passage, v23 "My desire is to depart": yes, for 10.6 *Wanting* (desire weighed against others' need).

Paul writes from prison: *"to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain"* (v21). Then:
- *"If I am to live in the flesh, that means fruitful labor for me. Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell"* (v22)
- *"I am hard pressed between the two. My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better"* (v23)
- *"But to remain in the flesh is more necessary on your account"* (v24)
- *"Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with you all"* (v25)

The choice is held open between two goods. His own desire leans one way, and others' need leans the other. He is "hard pressed".

He does not settle it by choosing. He settles it by being "convinced" and coming to "know".

**Narrative:** Ch 7 (*Flesh*) quotes only "If I am to live in the flesh, that means fruitful labor for me". The choosing is not used.

**Not yet carried:** for 10.7:
- a choice that cannot be told, between two goods
- desire set against the need of others
- the matter resolved by conviction, not by an act of choosing

### Genesis 6:5 and 8:21: "every intention of the thoughts of his heart"

(2) §8 says "the same evil intention had grieved him 'to his heart' (6:5–6)". Gen 6:6 says the Lord regretted making man, "and it grieved him to his heart". It does not name the intention as what grieved him. **Gen 8:21: ✅ accurate** in Ch 4, Ch 11 §8 and Ch 12.
>
> **Q2 M64: Carried: corroborates.** Gen 6:5 and 8:21 are carried for the M64 word (*yēṣer*) in 10.4, Ch 11 and Ch 12. Nothing new for M64.
>
> **Q3 other M-codes:** Gen 6:5: M58 evil and M06 "thoughts" (H4284) are carried in Ch 12 and 10.4; no. Gen 8:21: M04 "pleasing" aroma (God's, route A) and M25 "living creature"; no.

**Narrative:** 10.4 *The inclination: a shaped thing*, Ch 4, Ch 11 and Ch 12 use the M64 word itself (*yēṣer*, H3336) as their subject, in these verses' own setting.
