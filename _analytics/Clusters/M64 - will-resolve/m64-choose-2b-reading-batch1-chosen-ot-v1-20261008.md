## 2 Samuel 6:21: "and I will celebrate before the Lord"

The chapter is the bringing up of the ark. It opens in celebration: *"David and all the house of Israel were celebrating before the Lord, with songs and lyres"* (v5). Then Uzzah takes hold of the ark and *"the anger of the Lord was kindled against Uzzah"* (v7). David's inner state turns three times in three verses:
- *"David was angry because the Lord had broken out against Uzzah"* (v8)
- *"David was afraid of the Lord that day, and he said, "How can the ark of the Lord come to me?""* (v9)
- *"So David was not willing to take the ark of the Lord into the city of David"* (v10)

What brings him back is hearing that *"The Lord has blessed the household of Obed-edom"* (v12). He brings the ark up *"with rejoicing"*, and *"David danced before the Lord with all his might"* (v14).

Michal sees this from the window and *"she despised him in her heart"* (v16). The contempt is inward first. Her words come out only when he arrives (v20). Her measure is the eyes of others: *"uncovering himself today before the eyes of his servants' female servants"* (v20).

David's answer: *"It was before the Lord, who chose me above your father and above all his house, to appoint me as prince over Israel, the people of the Lord—and I will celebrate before the Lord"* (v21). "Celebrate" is the same word as *"celebrating"* in v5 (H7832). The celebration that stopped at Uzzah's death has come back, and David names the ground: he was chosen. He also names before whom he celebrates. Michal spoke of the servants' eyes. David speaks of the Lord.

He does not defend his dignity. He goes further: *"I will make myself yet more contemptible than this, and I will be abased in your eyes"* (v22). He accepts being lowered in her eyes. Her contempt does not move him, because he answers to a different measure.

The choosing is set against Michal's own house: *"above your father and above all his house"*. I read the reply as answering her contempt with the fact of the choosing. The verse names the house of Saul directly. The chapter closes on Michal: *"Michal the daughter of Saul had no child to the day of her death"* (v23). The text does not give a cause.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- The one chosen names his being chosen as the ground of his celebrating.
- The celebrating is *"before the Lord"*. It is measured by him, not by the eyes of others.
- The chosen man accepts being made contemptible in another's eyes, and her contempt does not stop him.
- The celebration came back after anger, fear and unwillingness. Being chosen did not keep David from those. The joy returned when he heard of the Lord's blessing.

## 1 Chronicles 28:10: "the Lord has chosen you … be strong and do it"

David speaks to the whole assembly, then to Solomon. He begins with his own heart and its refusal: *"I had it in my heart to build a house of rest for the ark"* (v2), *"But God said to me, 'You may not build a house for my name'"* (v3). The man who says *"the Lord God of Israel chose me"* (v4) was refused what was in his heart. Being chosen did not give David everything he intended.

He speaks of his own choosing in inner terms: *"among my father's sons he took pleasure in me to make me king"* (v4, H7521). Then Solomon: *"I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father"* (v6).

The promise to Solomon is conditional: *"I will establish his kingdom forever if he continues strong in keeping my commandments and my rules"* (v7). The words to Solomon follow from that condition:
- *"know the God of your father and serve him with a whole heart and with a willing mind, for the Lord searches all hearts and understands every plan and thought"* (v9)
- *"If you seek him, he will be found by you, but if you forsake him, he will cast you off forever"* (v9)
- *"Be careful now, for the Lord has chosen you to build a house for the sanctuary; be strong and do it"* (v10)

"Strong" in v10 is the same word as *"continues strong in keeping"* in v7 (H2388G). The strength asked of the chosen man is first a strength in keeping. "Be careful" in v10 is the verb "see" (H7200H).

The chosen man's inner being is named on every side:
- the heart, whole
- the mind, willing (H5315G, the soul)
- every plan and thought, searched by the Lord

Being chosen does not end the searching. The same verse that calls him chosen holds out *"cast you off forever"*.

David repeats the charge at the end with its reason: *"Be strong and courageous and do it. Do not be afraid and do not be dismayed, for the Lord God, even my God, is with you. He will not leave you or forsake you"* (v20). In v9, Solomon is told the Lord will forsake him if he forsakes the Lord. In v20 he is told the Lord will not forsake him until the work is done. Both are said to the one man.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- Being chosen calls for strength, and the strength is first in keeping (v7, v10).
- It is given a task, and the inner being is engaged in it: a whole heart, a willing soul, plans and thoughts that the Lord searches.
- It carries a condition. The one chosen can still forsake, and can be cast off.
- Fear and dismay are answered by *"my God, is with you"*.
- The chooser's own heart can be refused. David was chosen, and not allowed to build.

## Psalm 65:4: "We shall be satisfied with the goodness of your house"

The psalm opens with prayer and with sin:
- *"O you who hear prayer, to you shall all flesh come"* (v2)
- *"When iniquities prevail against me, you atone for our transgressions"* (v3)

The verse comes straight after the atoning: *"Blessed is the one you choose and bring near, to dwell in your courts! We shall be satisfied with the goodness of your house, the holiness of your temple!"* (v4). Being chosen is set with being brought near and dwelling. The response is satisfaction (H7646), and what satisfies is *"the goodness of your house"*. The satisfaction is not in the chosen person; it is in what is in God's house.

The speaker moves from *"me"* (v3) to *"we"* (v4). The one chosen is spoken of in the singular, and the satisfaction is spoken in the plural.

The psalm then widens beyond the chosen. God is *"the hope of all the ends of the earth"* (v5), and *"those who dwell at the ends of the earth are in awe at your signs"* (v8). The land itself is given voice at the end: *"they shout and sing together for joy"* (v13). The joy of the chosen in v4 is one part of a psalm that ends with all of creation rejoicing. The verse does not say the chosen have the joy alone.

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- Being chosen comes after iniquity is atoned for. The starting point is *"iniquities prevail against me"* (v3).
- To be chosen is to be brought near and to dwell.
- The response is satisfaction, and what satisfies is the goodness and holiness of God's house.
- The singular "the one you choose" becomes "we": the satisfaction is shared.

## Psalm 105:43: "his chosen ones with singing"

Psalm 105 is a call to remember. It is addressed to the chosen: *"Remember the wondrous works that he has done … O offspring of Abraham, his servant, children of Jacob, his chosen ones!"* (v5–6). It starts with the heart: *"let the hearts of those who seek the Lord rejoice!"* (v3).

The psalm's reason is God's own remembering:
- *"He remembers his covenant forever"* (v8)
- *"For he remembered his holy promise, and Abraham, his servant"* (v42)

The verse follows straight on: *"So he brought his people out with joy, his chosen ones with singing"* (v43). The joy and singing are set on the people brought out. They come with being brought out, and the bringing out comes from God remembering his promise. The chosen are told to remember (v5) because he remembered (v8, v42).

On the way there the psalm tells one man's inward testing: *"His feet were hurt with fetters … until what he had said came to pass, the word of the Lord tested him"* (v18–19). That man was Joseph, sent ahead of the chosen.

Psalm 105 tells nothing of the people's failures. It has *"none among his tribes who stumbled"* (v37). Its last verse states the purpose of the whole: *"that they might keep his statutes and observe his laws"* (v45).

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- The chosen are called to remember what God has done, and to let their hearts rejoice.
- The joy and singing of the chosen go with God's act of bringing them out. That act comes from his remembering his promise.
- The purpose given at the end is keeping his statutes.

## Psalm 106:5: "that I may rejoice in the gladness of your nation"

Psalm 106 follows 105 and tells the same history from the other side. I read the two side by side because they stand together; neither refers to the other.

The speaker asks to be included: *"Remember me, O Lord, when you show favor to your people; help me when you save them, that I may look upon the prosperity of your chosen ones, that I may rejoice in the gladness of your nation, that I may glory with your inheritance"* (v4–5). He does not claim the chosen ones' gladness. He asks to look upon it and to share it. "Gladness" here (H8057) is the same word as David's *"rejoicing"* in 2 Samuel 6:12.

He then confesses at once: *"Both we and our fathers have sinned; we have committed iniquity; we have done wickedness"* (v6). The rest of the psalm is the chosen people's inner failure, named act by act:
- *"did not consider your wondrous works; they did not remember the abundance of your steadfast love"* (v7)
- *"Then they believed his words; they sang his praise. But they soon forgot his works; they did not wait for his counsel"* (v12–13)
- *"they had a wanton craving in the wilderness"* (v14)
- *"men in the camp were jealous of Moses and Aaron"* (v16)
- *"They forgot God, their Savior"* (v21)
- *"Then they despised the pleasant land, having no faith in his promise"* (v24)
- *"they were rebellious in their purposes"* (v43)

"Despised" in v24 is H3988A, the same word as *"refuse the evil"* in Isaiah 7:15. The chosen people refused what they were given. "Faith" in v24 is H0539, the same word as *"believed"* in v12. The psalm has them believing, then forgetting, then without faith.

One of the chosen acts for the rest: *"had not Moses, his chosen one, stood in the breach before him, to turn away his wrath"* (v23).

The psalm's turn is God's remembering, as in Psalm 105: *"he looked upon their distress, when he heard their cry. For their sake he remembered his covenant"* (v44–45). It ends with the chosen asking again for the joy of v5: *"gather us from among the nations, that we may give thanks to your holy name and glory in your praise"* (v47).

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- The gladness of the chosen is something another person asks to share. He asks; he does not assume it.
- He asks while confessing that he and the chosen have sinned.
- Being chosen did not keep the chosen from forgetting, craving, jealousy, despising and unbelief. The psalm names each one.
- The way back is the Lord's remembering his covenant at their cry.

## Isaiah 43:10: "my servant whom I have chosen, that you may know and believe me and understand"

The setting is a court. The nations are summoned: *"All the nations gather together … Let them bring their witnesses to prove them right"* (v9). Israel is brought out as the Lord's witness, and described as unfit for it: *"Bring out the people who are blind, yet have eyes, who are deaf, yet have ears!"* (v8).

Then: *"You are my witnesses," declares the Lord, "and my servant whom I have chosen, that you may know and believe me and understand that I am he"* (v10). The knowing, believing and understanding are the purpose of the choosing. They are not its ground. The chosen were blind and deaf when chosen. They were chosen in order to come to know.

This is the reverse of the order in §2a, where the child chooses *"when he knows how"* (Isa 7:15). There the knowing comes before the person's own choosing. Here, the knowing follows God's choosing of the person. Each verse states its own order.

What the chosen are to know is one thing: *"that I am he. Before me no god was formed"* (v10). The Lord's own witness follows: *"I declared and saved and proclaimed, when there was no strange god among you"* (v12).

The oracles that follow set the chosen beside those who make idols:
- The chosen people do not perceive what God is doing: *"Behold, I am doing a new thing; now it springs forth, do you not perceive it?"* (43:19). "Perceive" is the same word as "know" in v10 (H3045).
- The idol-makers' witnesses *"neither see nor know"* (44:9).
- Of the idol-maker himself: *"They know not, nor do they discern, for he has shut their eyes, so that they cannot see, and their hearts, so that they cannot understand"* (44:18). "Know" and "discern" are the same words as "know" and "understand" in 43:10 (H3045, H0995).

The chosen people are also accused: *"Yet you did not call upon me, O Jacob; but you have been weary of me, O Israel!"* (43:22); *"you have burdened me with your sins; you have wearied me with your iniquities"* (43:24). Then comes the answer: *"I, I am he who blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and I will not remember your sins"* (43:25).

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- Being chosen comes before knowing, believing and understanding. Those are its purpose.
- The chosen are blind and deaf when chosen. They are made witnesses all the same.
- What they are chosen to know is that the Lord is God and there is no other.
- In the same oracles, those who make idols cannot know or discern. Their hearts are shut. The chosen are not described as different by nature: they too fail to perceive, do not call, and weary him. What differs is that he blots out their sins.

## Isaiah 44:2: "Fear not, O Jacob my servant, Jeshurun whom I have chosen"

The chapter opens directly after a threat: *"deliver Jacob to utter destruction and Israel to reviling"* (43:28). Then: *"But now hear, O Jacob my servant, Israel whom I have chosen!"* (44:1). The "fear not" comes right after the words of destruction.

The ground of not fearing is who the Lord is to the one chosen: *"the Lord who made you, who formed you from the womb and will help you: Fear not, O Jacob my servant, Jeshurun whom I have chosen"* (v2). He made, he formed, he will help, and he has chosen. The chosen person is not told to be brave. He is told who has chosen him.

What follows is given to the chosen one's offspring, and it reaches the inner being:
- *"I will pour my Spirit upon your offspring, and my blessing on your descendants"* (v3)
- *"This one will say, 'I am the Lord's,' another will call on the name of Jacob, and another will write on his hand, 'The Lord's'"* (v5)

Those who are chosen come to name themselves as belonging to him.

The "fear not" comes again with a second ground: *"Fear not, nor be afraid; have I not told you from of old and declared it? And you are my witnesses!"* (v8). The second ground is having been told.

The idol-maker in the same chapter has no such ground. He cuts the wood himself and then prays to it: *"Deliver me, for you are my god!"* (v17). The text says *"he cannot deliver himself"* (v20). The chosen one is told: *"Remember these things, O Jacob … you will not be forgotten by me"* (v21), and *"return to me, for I have redeemed you"* (v22).

**What this verse shows of being chosen:**
- The chosen person's fear is answered with who has chosen him: maker, former, helper.
- It is answered again with what he has been told from of old.
- Being chosen reaches the next generation: the Spirit poured out, and people naming themselves the Lord's.
- The chosen one is called to remember, and is told he will not be forgotten.
- Against the chosen stands the idol-maker, who picks his own wood and cannot deliver himself. The ESV has *"he chooses a cypress tree or an oak"* (v14), but the Hebrew there is "take" (H3947G), not the choose word.

## What batch 1 adds

This is the chosen person's side. It is set beside the §2a account, which is the chooser's side. Nothing in the §2a account has to be withdrawn.

**What the chosen are moved to:**
- celebrating (2Sa 6:21)
- being strong in keeping (1Ch 28:7, 10)
- satisfaction in the goodness of God's house (Psa 65:4)
- joy and singing (Psa 105:43)
- gladness, which another asks to share (Psa 106:5)
- knowing, believing and understanding (Isa 43:10)
- not fearing (Isa 44:2)

In 2Sa 6:21, 1Ch 28:10, Isa 43:10 and Isa 44:2 the verse names the being chosen as the ground or the reason. In Psa 65:4 the response follows the choosing in the same sentence. In Psa 105:43 and 106:5, "chosen ones" names the people whose joy it is; the verse does not make the choosing the cause.

**Being chosen comes before the inner act, not after it.** In §2a, knowing, fear and teaching came before a person's own choosing (Isa 7:15; Psa 25:12). In Isaiah 43:10, God chooses first, and knowing is the purpose. The chosen were blind and deaf when chosen (43:8). In Psalm 65 they were chosen after their iniquity was atoned for (v3–4).

**Being chosen is not a guarantee.** This is stated in the verses, not inferred:
- the condition put on Solomon, and the *"cast you off forever"* (1Ch 28:7, 9)
- the chosen people's forgetting, craving, jealousy and unbelief, and their despising the land (Psa 106:7–43)
- the chosen people weary of God, and not calling on him (Isa 43:22–24)

In Psalm 106:24 the chosen *"despised"* (H3988A) what was given. This is the same word as the refusing in §2a.

**The return is the Lord's remembering.** He remembered his covenant at their cry (Psa 106:44–45). He remembered his promise and brought them out with joy (Psa 105:42–43). He blots out their sins and will not remember them (Isa 43:25). The chosen are called to remember in turn (Psa 105:5; Isa 44:21).

**Before whom.** David celebrates *"before the Lord"* against Michal's measure of the servants' eyes (2Sa 6:20–22). This sits with §2a, "whose measure": the eyes that judge the choice.

**The chosen still go through anger, fear and refusal.** David was angry, afraid and unwilling between the two celebrations (2Sa 6:8–10). His own heart's wish to build was refused, though he was chosen (1Ch 28:2–4).

**Kept open:**
- **Moses in the breach (Psa 106:23).** One chosen man stands before God for the rest. This is already cited in the narrative. Whether it belongs with "together" in the §2a account, or with 2c ("in others, around the choosing"), is left until 2c is read.
- **Michal's contempt (2Sa 6:16).** This is an inner act of another person, aimed at the chosen. It may belong with 2c ("response of others") rather than here.
- **The pair Psalm 105 and 106.** They are read side by side because they stand side by side. The text does not join them.

**For batch 2:** the 8 New Testament verses. In five of them (Eph 1:4, 2Th 2:13, Tit 1:1, Jam 2:5, 2Pe 1:10) the inner act is given as the purpose of the choosing, or as the call that follows it. Whether they keep the order seen in Isaiah 43:10, with the choosing first and the knowing after, is to be read from them.
