## Cross-strong synergy

Read together, the seven strongs are not describing guilt as a *feeling* — none of the cited verses
frame it as an emotion word. What the verse set actually depicts is guilt as an **objective,
transactable status** that moves through a consistent sequence:

1. **An act occurs** — intentional or unintentional (Lev 4:13/4:22/4:27/5:2-5/5:17 repeatedly frame
   this as "sins... doing any of the things... ought not to be done," explicitly covering
   inadvertent breach, not only deliberate wrongdoing).
2. **The guilt is *realized*** — H0816's Leviticus 4-6 verses use "and he realizes his guilt" as
   what reads like a near-technical hinge phrase (repeated near-verbatim across Lev 4:13, 4:22,
   4:27, 5:2, 5:3, 5:4, 5:5, 5:17, 6:4, 6:5): guilt is not complete as a status until it is
   *recognized*, not merely incurred.
3. **It accrues as a load** — H0819's "guilt is already great," "incurred guilt more and more"
   (2 Chr 28:13, 33:23) and H5375J's "bear your iniquity/guilt/punishment" idiom treat guilt as
   cumulative weight, not a binary switch.
4. **The load is carried — by the guilty party or a substitute.** H5375J's verses split cleanly
   into two carriers of the same idiom: the guilty party bears it themselves (Lev 5:17, 17:16,
   Num 5:31, Num 14:34 — the wilderness generation), *or* it is transferred onto a substitute: the
   scapegoat "shall bear all their iniquities" (Lev 16:22), and — the same verb — the Servant "bore
   the sin of many" (Isa 53:12). Ezekiel 18:19-20 sits directly against the transfer reading in the
   other direction: explicitly denying that a son bears (nasa) his father's iniquity — the same verb
   used to assert strict non-transferability of *personal* guilt in one direction, while other texts
   in the same set use it for *sacrificial* transferability in the other. The input data does not
   resolve this tension; it simply contains both uses side by side.
5. **A remedy exists at the institutional level.** H0817 is the pivot strong that is *both* the
   abstract guilt-word and the name of the sacrifice that removes it (Lev 5:7, 7:5, 14:21, 19:21,
   1 Sam 6:17, and — notably — Isa 53:10, where the Servant's soul itself "makes an offering for
   guilt"). The guilt-offering is not incidental vocabulary; it is the same lexical item as the
   guilt it answers.
6. **A separate, forensic register runs in parallel (G1777).** The Greek liability sense doesn't
   describe a process of realization or a burden carried — it names a fixed legal exposure to a
   specific penalty ("liable to judgment," "liable to the council," "guilty of an eternal sin,"
   "guilty of all of it" for failing at *one point* of the law — Jas 2:10). This reads as a
   different grammatical shape of the same concept: guilt as *standing exposed to a verdict*,
   rather than guilt as *something building up inside a person*.
7. **One verse frames guilt relationally rather than ritually or forensically** — H0818's single
   citation (Gen 42:21) has Joseph's brothers indict themselves for a failure of compassion
   ("we saw the distress of his soul... and did not listen"), with no offering, no court, no
   accrual language — guilt as retrospective conscience alone.

## What this suggests for #1680 (observation only, not a decision)

The verse-and-meaning-block evidence for this subgroup does not read as one flat "guilt" idea —
it reads as (at least) three distinguishable movements sharing the family label: **(a) a
recognition-driven accrual process** (H0816/H0819), **(b) a bear/transfer mechanic** (H5375J,
pivoting on the same verb for both self-bearing and substitutionary-bearing), and **(c) a fixed
forensic-liability register** (G1777) that doesn't obviously belong to the same movement as (a)/(b)
at all. If the meaning-distillation method treats "guilt/guiltiness" as one lexical family with one
synthesized meaning, this data suggests that collapse may lose a real distinction the verses
themselves are making — worth weighing before the method finalizes how subgroups like this get
distilled to a single statement.
