## The thirty-one strongs — overwhelmingly one plain concept

Unlike every other family in this exercise, this family shows almost no internal ambiguity: 27 of
31 strongs are straightforward "to destroy" (verb) or "destruction" (noun) across both languages,
applied to nations, cities, idols, and individuals as an act of judgment (divine or military). This
is, in effect, the *least* interesting family taken as a whole — its size comes from Strong's-number
proliferation across near-synonymous roots (Greek apollymi/apōleia-family verbs, Hebrew abad/shamad/
charam-family verbs), not from genuine polysemy or metaphor-splitting the way most other families
showed.

## The two genuinely distinct sub-themes

- **H2763A (cherem, "to devote/destroy," 29 verses) is not ordinary destruction — it is a formal,
  legally-defined institution: the herem/ban.** Its own meaning block distinguishes "to prohibit
  (for common use), ban" from "to consecrate, devote, dedicate for destruction" from "to exterminate" —
  three legally distinct actions, not one act loosely described three ways. The verses are entirely
  covenant-law and conquest-narrative material (Deut 7:2, 20:17; Josh 6, 10, 11; 1 Sam 15's Amalek
  command). **This connects directly to family J's finding**: Joshua 7:1's Achan narrative — the
  same event already analysed there as *ma'al* ("broke faith... took some of the devoted things") —
  is the paradigm violation of *this* institution: taking cherem-devoted property for oneself. Two
  separately-grouped families (J's unfaithfulness, A's destruction) converge on the identical
  narrative event from two different lexical angles (the betrayal side vs. the violated-institution
  side).
- **H8074G (shamem, "be desolate: destroyed," many verses) carries a distinct apocalyptic register
  not found elsewhere in the M10 corpus**: Daniel's repeated "abomination that makes desolate"
  formula (Dan 8:13, 9:27, 11:31, 12:11), tied specifically to sanctuary desecration and an
  eschatological timeline (the 1,290-days count in 12:11). This is a different genre and referent
  (end-times sanctuary desecration) from the same root's more ordinary prophetic usage elsewhere
  (Ezekiel/Isaiah's desolated cities and altars as historical judgment).

## Note for #1680

This family is the clearest counter-example to the pattern seen everywhere else in this exercise:
most families needed careful reading to surface a real internal split or a converging metaphor;
this one is genuinely closer to what a "distilled single meaning" family looks like on the surface
— except for the two carved-out sub-themes above, both of which are institutional/generic rather
than about an inner disposition, and neither of which should be flattened into the family's
otherwise-uniform "destroy" sense. The Achan cross-reference with family J is worth recording
alongside the earlier D/E/F/G judicial-corruption convergence and the five-family Isaiah 53
convergence as a cross-family pattern, not a family-internal one.

## Cross-family patterns surfacing across the full M10 exercise (summary, not new analysis)

   families D (H5771G), F (H2555, H1792), and G (H6588) — five separate gloss-families converge on
   different verses of the same Servant Song.
2. **A recurring three-way sense split** ("as state / as guilt-consequence / as punishment-
   consequence", or "against man / against God") appearing independently in H5771 (avon, family D),
   H6588 (pesha, family G), H2399/H2403 (chattah, family B), and the guilt subgroup's own asham
   material — worth checking whether this is a real textual pattern or a lexicon-authoring
   convention before treating it as a finding about the Bible text itself.
3. **A judicial/legal-fairness sub-theme** recurring across families D (avel/avlah — injustice
   "in court"), E (salaph/avat — bribery, perverted judgment), and G (riv, family I — formal lawsuit
   vocabulary) — corruption of *justice specifically* reads as a distinguishable strand from
   wrongdoing in general.
4. **At least three cluster-membership / gloss-homonym false positives found along the way**,
   independent of any meaning-distillation question: family R (G21497, H5512B — "Sin" as letter-name
   and place-name), family I's G0073 (agōn — an athletic-perseverance metaphor, not a vice), and
   family E's H2342J/K (chul — dancing/waiting) and H7806 (shazar — literal tabernacle-linen

5. **The guilt/violence "bear or transfer" and "destructive vs. redemptive" ambiguities** (guilt
   subgroup's nasa; family F's dakha/dakhah) show the same word used for opposite moral valences
   depending only on referent, with no lexical marker distinguishing them.
