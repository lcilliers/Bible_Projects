## Finding: this is the most heterogeneous family in the whole exercise — four unrelated concepts sharing an English gloss

Unlike every other family reviewed so far, the verse evidence here does **not** converge on one
concept even loosely. It splits into four genuinely separate things:

1. **G0073 (agōn) is not a vice at all — it is a positive athletic/military metaphor for
   perseverance.** Every one of its 6 verses is Paul (or Hebrews) describing his own or the
   believer's faithful effort: "Fight the good fight of the faith" (1 Tim 6:12), "I have fought the
   good fight, I have finished the race" (2 Tim 4:7), "run with endurance the race that is set
   before us" (Heb 12:1). Nothing in this strong's verse set describes wrongdoing, discord, or a
   moral failure of any kind — its inclusion in a "sin/inner-being-failure" cluster looks like a
   gloss-text accident ("fight" the English word) rather than a thematic match. This is a candidate
   for the same kind of `cluster_strong` review already flagged for family R.
2. **The Hebrew "strife" nouns (H4066, H4079, H4090, H4683, H4695) are genuine domestic/social
   discord — the family's only real match to the cluster's apparent theme.** Overwhelmingly
   Proverbs material: hot temper, wrath, and greed as strife's stated causes (Prov 15:18, 29:22,
   28:25); gossip/whispering as its mechanism (Prov 16:28, 26:20); and a repeated, near-formulaic
   complaint about "a quarrelsome wife" (H4079: Prov 19:13, 21:9, 21:19, 25:24, 27:15 — five
   separate citations of essentially the same domestic-conflict image).
3. **H4421 (milchamah, 91 verses — the single largest strong found across all 14 families) is
   almost entirely literal armed warfare between nations and armies** — "man of war," troop counts,
   battle-line narrative (1 Chr, 2 Chr, Judges, Samuel). A handful of verses apply it to God
   metaphorically ("the LORD is a man of war," Exod 15:3; "mighty in battle," Ps 24:8), but the
   overwhelming majority is straightforward military-historical narration, not moral vocabulary
   about an inner disposition at all.
4. **H7379 (riv) is a distinct, formal legal/covenant-lawsuit term**, not domestic quarrelling —
   "case at law," "dispute... in court," and repeatedly God's own formal indictment against Israel
   or the nations (Mic 6:2 "the LORD has an indictment against his people"; Jer 25:31; Isa 41:21).
   This is forensic vocabulary, structurally closer to family D (crime/injustice) than to the
   Proverbs strife-nouns it was grouped with here.

## Note for #1680 — the clearest single case against gloss-text grouping in this exercise

Every other family reviewed retained at least a loose shared concept even where it split into
sub-registers (guilt, transgression, unfaithfulness). This family does not: "fight" (positive
athletic perseverance), "strife" (domestic Proverbs discord), "battle" (literal warfare narrative),
and "contention/dispute" (formal legal indictment) are four different things that happen to share
English glosses. If the meaning-distillation method needs a worked example of gloss-text grouping
failing outright, this is it.
