## 0. How this was done

- **All 275 M20 verses were read in canonical order.** That includes the 30 that name a seat, because the unit is the phenomenon, not the seat. The context was read for about 70 passages.
- **The phenomena were not chosen in advance.** Each verse was read for the inner-being movement it actually shows, and verses showing the same movement were grouped. The groups are the phenomena below.
  - A group was split where the verses showed a genuinely different movement. For example, hiding *from God's presence* reads differently from hiding *from a man with a sword*.
  - The twelve "methods" table in `../M47 - inner-seat/wa-cluster-M47-ib-operation-modes-consideration-v1-20260928.md` was **not** used as a checklist.
- **Every verse is accounted for.** `wa-cluster-M20-phenomena-ledger-v1-20260928.csv` (this folder) gives each verse's phenomenon code. 26 verses carry no inner phenomenon, and the reason is given in §17.
- **Each phenomenon answers the researcher's questions in the same order:**
  - **What it is**
  - **What it does** (the movement it makes in the person)
  - **Where it comes from**
  - **How it co-exists** (with other inner movements, and in which order)
  - **What it leads to**

  *I have read the second "what does it do" as "what it produces or leads to". Please correct this if you meant something else.*
- **Rules applied:** the subject is the human inner being (prose section 1090; `hib.set`). Where God is the one who hides or conceals, the phenomenon described is **the human side** of it.

### 0.1 The seat-sense mapping (new in v3)

Each phenomenon now ends with a **Seat** block. It records three things.

1. **Whose seat it is:**
   - **the bearer's own:** the person who undergoes the phenomenon
   - **the other party's:** someone else in the scene (God, a second person, an interpreter)
   - **the offender's:** the one whose wrong produces the phenomenon (e.g. Isa 57:17)
   - **none**
2. **The seat-sense check:** whether the seat word is used in a genuine inner-being sense. It fails where the word means something else: wind, meat, flesh as food, bodily life only, or the divine Spirit speaking. The spurious links were first set out in v1 §0.
3. **Where the seat arrives outside the verse:** in the next verse, or at the head of the passage or discourse.

**Citations, not re-reading.** Where M47 Batches A–H or the v1 pilot already read a seated verse, the block cites that section. New reading is added only where the phenomenon view shows something the batch did not, and it is marked **New in v3**. The section numbers cited are those of the current batch files: A v3, B v3, C v3, D v2, E v2, F v4, G v3, H v1.

## 1. Hiding from God's presence (HG) — Gen 3:8, 3:10; Exo 3:6; Job 13:20; Isa 2:10; Rev 6:15, 6:16

- **What it is.** A person removes himself, or his face, from God's presence at the moment that presence is perceived.
- **What it does.** It puts a covering between the self and God:
  - "among the trees of the garden" (Gen 3:8)
  - "Moses hid his face" (Exo 3:6)
  - "enter into the rock and hide in the dust" (Isa 2:10)
  - "in the caves and among the rocks … Fall on us and hide us from the face of him who is seated on the throne" (Rev 6:15–16)
- **Where it comes from.** In every case **a perception of God comes first**:
  - "I heard the sound of you in the garden" (Gen 3:10)
  - God names himself: "I am the God of your father" (Exo 3:6)
  - "the terror of the Lord, and … the splendor of his majesty" (Isa 2:10)
  - "the face of him who is seated on the throne, and … the wrath of the Lamb" (Rev 6:16)

  Inside the person, what meets that perception differs:
  - **Adam:** knowledge of exposure. "The eyes of both were opened, and they knew that they were naked" (Gen 3:7), and so "I was afraid, **because I was naked**" (3:10).
  - **Moses:** "he was afraid to look at God" (Exo 3:6), with no transgression stated.
  - **The kings, the great, "slave and free":** "who can stand?" (Rev 6:17).
- **How it co-exists.** It is always with **fear** (Gen 3:10; Exo 3:6), terror (Isa 2:10), or wrath felt as unbearable (Rev 6:16–17). In Adam, self-knowledge ("naked") comes between perception and fear. **Job 13:20–22 turns the phenomenon inside out.** Job names the conditions under which he will *not* hide: "withdraw your hand far from me, and let not dread of you terrify me. Then call, and I will answer". Job shows that hiding is what **dread** produces. Remove the dread and the person can face God and speak.
- **What it leads to.** The same act has three outcomes:
  - **Being sought and questioned:** "Where are you?" (Gen 3:9), then Adam's answer and his blame of the woman (3:12)
  - **Being addressed further:** God goes on to speak to the hidden-faced Moses, "I have surely seen the affliction of my people" (Exo 3:7)
  - **Despair of standing at all** (Rev 6:17)
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** Gen 3:8 "cool" is *ruach* as the day's wind. **Spurious** (v1 §0). No other verse in the seven names a seat.
  - **Whose seat:** none. The movement is carried by the fear word together with the bodily act. v1 §1.5 and §3 item 2 already read this: "Hiding is the body's answer to fear."
  - **Outside the verse:** no seat arrives in the next verse in any of the seven passages read.

## 2. Hiding oneself from human danger (HS) — 40 verses

- **What it is.** Taking oneself out of sight of a person or force that threatens harm. The verses fall into **seven strands**, and they are kept apart because they work differently.
- **(a) The fugitive hunted to death.**
  - Verses: 1Sa 19:2; 20:5, 19, 24; 23:19, 23; 26:1; Psa 54:1 (David); Judg 9:5 (Jotham); 2Sa 17:9 (David, as Hushai pictures him); 1Ki 17:3 (Elijah); Jer 36:19 (Baruch and Jeremiah); Jos 2:16 (the spies); Jos 10:16, 17, 27 (five kings); 2Ch 22:9 (Ahaziah); Joh 8:59; 12:36 (Jesus).
  - **Where it comes from:** a stated threat to life. "Saul my father seeks to kill you" (1Sa 19:2); Abimelech "killed his brothers … But Jotham … hid himself" (Judg 9:5); "they picked up stones to throw at him" (Joh 8:59).
  - **The feeling of the one who hides is almost never stated.** What is stated is **someone else's counsel to hide**: Jonathan (1Sa 19:2), Rahab (Jos 2:16), the officials (Jer 36:19), and God (1Ki 17:3). In this strand the inner operation that shows is the **counsellor's**: foresight and concern for the other.
  - **What it leads to:** survival (Jotham; David), or capture and death (Ahaziah, "captured while hiding", 2Ch 22:9; the five kings, Jos 10:17, 27).
- **(b) A people hiding under pressure.**
  - Verses: 1Sa 13:6; 14:11, 22; Pro 28:28; Job 24:4; Deu 7:20; Jer 49:10; 2Ki 7:12.
  - **Where it comes from:** "when the men of Israel saw that they were in trouble (for the people were hard pressed), the people hid themselves in caves and in holes and in rocks and in tombs and in cisterns" (1Sa 13:6). The next verse gives the inner state: "all the people followed him trembling" (13:7). Also "When the wicked rise, people hide themselves" (Pro 28:28), and "the poor of the earth all hide themselves" (Job 24:4). **Hiding is the posture of the weaker party.**
  - **What it leads to:** the enemy's contempt, "Look, Hebrews are coming out of the holes" (1Sa 14:11). **Reversal when news changes**: "when all the men … who had hidden themselves … heard that the Philistines were fleeing, they too followed hard after them" (1Sa 14:22). Hiding ends when fear is lifted by **hearing**.
  - Jer 49:10 is the end of this strand: "he is not able to conceal himself".
  - 2Ki 7:12 is a **suspicion** of hiding. The king reads the Syrians' empty camp as an ambush ("they have gone out of the camp to hide themselves"). His fear projects hiding onto the enemy, and he is wrong.
- **(c) Hiding with the fear named.**
  - Gen 31:27: Jacob "fled secretly". The motive is stated four verses later: "Because I was afraid, for I thought that you would take your daughters from me by force" (31:31).
  - Dan 10:7: "a great trembling fell upon them, and they fled to hide themselves", though they did not see the vision.
  - 1Ch 21:20: "He turned and saw the angel, and his four sons … hid themselves."
  - Joh 19:38: Joseph of Arimathea, "a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Jews". Here the hiding is of **allegiance**, not of the body, and it ends in a public act: he "asked Pilate … took away his body".
  - 1Ki 22:25 = 2Ch 18:24: Micaiah foretells a false prophet's future panic, "you shall see on that day when you go into an inner chamber to hide yourself".
- **(d) Withdrawal with the motive unstated, or inferred by the text only later.**
  - 1Sa 10:22: Saul, chosen by lot, "could not be found" (10:21) and "has hidden himself among the baggage". **The verse gives no motive, and none is supplied here.**
  - Luk 1:24: Elizabeth "kept herself hidden" five months. The following verse gives her words, "to take away my reproach among people" (1:25). The hiding stands between the reproach and its removal. The text does not say *why* she hid.
- **(e) Withdrawal as honour.** Job 29:8, "the young men saw me and withdrew, and the aged rose and stood". With 29:9, "the princes refrained from talking and laid their hand on their mouth", **withdrawal is deference**, the respect Job once received.
- **(f) Hiding as prudence.** Pro 22:3 = 27:12, "The prudent sees danger and hides himself, but the simple go on and suffer for it." The sequence is **seeing leads to judging leads to hiding**. The failure is the simple one's: seeing without acting.
- **(g) Where hiding is refused or impossible.**
  - Isa 50:6: "I hid not my face from disgrace and spitting". The servant **chooses not to hide**, and the body is offered: "I gave my back … my cheeks".
  - Psa 55:12–13: "it is not an adversary … then I could hide from him. But it is you, a man, my equal, my companion, my familiar friend". **Hiding works against an enemy, not against a friend.** Intimacy has already removed the cover, so betrayal cannot be hidden from.
- **How it co-exists across the strands.** Trembling (1Sa 13:7; Dan 10:7), fear (Gen 31:31; Joh 19:38), and seeing danger (Pro 22:3; 1Ch 21:20). Its opposite is the prudent/simple pair (f).
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** 2Ch 22:9 "heart". The word has a genuine inner-being sense, but the heart is **Jehoshaphat's**, the dead grandfather's: "who sought the Lord with all his heart". Ahaziah, the one hiding, has no seat. **Spurious for this phenomenon** (v1 §0). Batch C v3 §7.1 read it as seeking the Lord with the heart. Batch G v3 §16.11 read it as "a dead man honoured for his grandfather's heart". Both readings stand. Neither concerns hiding.
  - **Whose seat:** none of the 40 hiders has one. In strand (a) the inner operation belongs to **the counsellor**, and it is shown by what the counsellor says, not by a seat word (v2, kept above).
  - **Outside the verse — new in v3.** In strand (a), the nearest seat word is *nephesh* in its **life sense**: the life being hunted. David, in the psalm headed by the Ziphites' report that he is "hiding among us" (Psa 54:1), says "the Lord is the upholder of my life" (54:4). The seat word names **what is at stake in the hiding, not where the hider feels it**. This fits Batch G v3 §5 ("Life spared, life hunted — the *nephesh* as a prize"), which it does not change.
  - **Strand (f), prudence — new in v3:** Pro 22:3 ("The prudent sees danger and hides himself") → 22:5 "Thorns and snares are in the way of the crooked; whoever guards his soul will keep far from them". **The prudent one's hiding is a guarding of the soul.** *Nephesh* here is the self that is kept from harm, and it passes the check in that sense. Strand (f) is the only strand of §2 where the one who hides has a seat, and the seat is **the thing the hiding protects**.

## 3. Being hidden by another: shelter (SH) — 23 verses

- **What it is.** One person, or God, hides another from harm.
- **The human who hides another:**
  - **Rahab** (Jos 2:6; 6:17, 25). The reason she gives: "as soon as we heard it, our hearts melted … for the Lord your God, he is God" (2:11). The hiding comes out of a **confession**.
  - **Obadiah** hid a hundred prophets "by fifties in a cave and fed them with bread and water" (1Ki 18:4, 13). He recalls it himself when he fears for his life (18:13).
  - **Jehosheba** hid Joash (2Ki 11:2–3; 2Ch 22:11–12). The text's reason: "because she was a sister of Ahaziah" (2Ch 22:11).
  - **Moses's parents** (Heb 11:23): "By faith … hidden … by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful, and **they were not afraid** of the king's edict". The one who hides another **acts without fear**, the reverse of §2.
  - **The mother in the famine** (2Ki 6:29): "she has hidden her son". This is the one protective act in a scene where two women agreed to eat their children (6:28). Her inner movement is not stated; the act alone shows it.
  - **The command:** "shelter the outcasts; do not reveal the fugitive" (Isa 16:3).
- **God hiding the person:**
  - "hide me in the shadow of your wings" (Psa 17:8)
  - "he will hide me in his shelter in the day of trouble" (Psa 27:5)
  - "you hide them from the plots of men; you store them … from the strife of tongues" (Psa 31:20)
  - "Hide me from the secret plots of the wicked" (Psa 64:2)
  - "You shall be hidden from the lash of the tongue" (Job 5:21)
  - "the Lord hid them" (Jer 36:26)
  - "in the shadow of his hand he hid me … in his quiver he hid me away" (Isa 49:2): hidden **for later use**, not only for safety
  - "enter your chambers … hide yourselves for a little while until the fury has passed by" (Isa 26:20): God's own people hidden from **God's own fury**
  - "perhaps you may be hidden on the day of the anger of the Lord" (Zep 2:3): conditional, after "seek humility"
  - "your life is hidden with Christ in God" (Col 3:3), after "Set your minds on things that are above" (3:2)
- **What it does.** For the sheltered it answers a **petition** (Psa 17:8; 64:2) and grounds **trust**. In the hider it shows **confession** (Rahab), **fearlessness** (Heb 11:23), or **kinship** (2Ch 22:11).
- **Where it comes from.** A threat to life. Notably, **in three verses the threat is speech**: "the strife of tongues" (Psa 31:20), "the lash of the tongue" (Job 5:21), "secret plots" (Psa 64:2).
- **False shelter.** Isa 28:15: "we have made lies our refuge, and in falsehood we have taken shelter". The scoffers (28:14) hide in their own lies from the "overwhelming whip". It is the same movement with a false object.
- **What it leads to.** Life: Rahab "has lived in Israel to this day" (Jos 6:25); Joash survived "six years" to reign (2Ki 11:3). Col 3:3 names the self's life as **located in another**.
- **Co-existence in one psalm, Psa 27:** "he will hide me in his shelter" (27:5) and "Hide not your face from me" (27:9). Being hidden **by** God and God hiding **from** me are asked for side by side (see §5).
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** none of the 23 names a seat.
  - **Outside the verse — new in v3.** For Rahab, the one human hider who explains herself, **the seat arrives in her own confession**: "our hearts melted, and there was no spirit left in any man because of you, for the Lord your God, he is God" (Jos 2:11). The seat is **the hider's own**, and genuine. It is the collective heart of Jericho that melted. [Claude reading] What turns Rahab to shelter the spies is not the melting itself, which all Jericho shares, but the confession she draws from it. The seat is where the fear sits. The act of hiding comes from the confession.
  - Isa 26:20 → 26:18 "wind" ("we have given birth to wind") is *ruach* as wind. **Spurious.**
  - Psa 17:8 → 17:9 "my deadly enemies who surround me". The seat word is inside the English "deadly": *nephesh*, the life the enemies are against. **Life-sense only.** It names what is at stake in the petition "hide me", as in §2 (Psa 54:4).
  - **Whose seat, God as hider:** none is named. The sheltered person's inner state is carried by the petition ("hide me") and the trust, not by a seat word.

## 4. Longing to be hidden (HW) — Job 3:10, 3:16; 14:13

- **What it is.** The wish to have been, or to be, hidden from life's trouble.
- **What it does.**
  - Job 3:10 curses the night of his birth because it "did not shut the doors of my mother's womb, nor hide trouble from my eyes". The wish is **not to have seen**.
  - Job 3:16: "why was I not as a hidden stillborn child, as infants who never see the light?" The earlier verse 3:11 makes it explicit: "Why did I not die at birth".
- **How it shifts.** Job 14:13 moves the same wish into hope: "Oh that you would hide me in Sheol, that you would conceal me **until your wrath be past**, that you would appoint me a set time, and **remember me**!" Being hidden is no longer non-existence. It is **being kept** by God through the wrath, and then remembered.
- **Where it comes from.** Suffering. The same movement belongs with Batch G v3 §16 (the death side). **Pointer only.**
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** none of the three names a seat.
  - **Outside the verse — new in v3.** Four verses after 3:16, Job generalises his own case: "Why is light given to him who is in misery, and life to the bitter in soul" (Job 3:20). The seat is **the bearer's own**, and genuine: the longing to have been hidden is the longing of **the bitter soul**. This is the same passage that Batch G v3 §16 reads as the death side, so it is recorded here as a pointer and not read further.

## 5. God's face hidden, as the human bears it (FH) — 31 verses

- **What it is.** God turns his face away, and this section is about what that does **in the human**: withdrawal of God's attention, hearing and favour, as experienced, feared, complained of, or asked for.
- **Where it comes from, as the verses themselves state:**
  - **The human's turning or sin:**
    - "because of all the evil that they have done, because they have turned to other gods" (Deu 31:18)
    - "a perverse generation, children in whom is no faithfulness" (Deu 32:20)
    - "your iniquities have made a separation … your sins have hidden his face from you" (Isa 59:2)
    - "he went on backsliding in the way of his own heart" (Isa 57:17): the seat named is **the offender's**
    - "because they have made their deeds evil" (Mic 3:4)
    - "they dealt so treacherously with me" (Eze 39:23); "their uncleanness and their transgressions" (39:24)
    - "all their evil" (Jer 33:5)
  - **Momentary anger:** "In overflowing anger for a moment I hid my face from you, but with everlasting love I will have compassion" (Isa 54:8).
  - **No reason given, only "why?" and "how long?":** Psa 13:1; 44:24; 88:14; 89:46; Job 13:24.
- **What it does in the human:**
  - **Dismay:** "you hid your face; I was dismayed" (Psa 30:7); "When you hide your face, they are dismayed; when you take away their breath, they die" (Psa 104:29)
  - **The spirit failing:** "My spirit fails! Hide not your face from me, lest I be like those who go down to the pit" (Psa 143:7)
  - **Being cast away:** "why do you cast my soul away? Why do you hide your face" (Psa 88:14)
  - **Feeling forgotten:** "Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?" (Psa 13:1). The seat follows at once: "How long must I take counsel in my soul and have sorrow in my heart all the day?" (13:2)
  - **The body pressed down:** "Why do you hide your face? … For our soul is bowed down to the dust; our belly clings to the ground" (Psa 44:24–25)
  - **Melting:** "you have hidden your face from us, and have made us melt in the hand of our iniquities" (Isa 64:7). The previous verse: "We all fade like a leaf" (64:6)
  - **Being counted an enemy:** "Why do you hide your face and count me as your enemy?" (Job 13:24); "Will you frighten a driven leaf?" (13:25)
  - **A collective conclusion:** "Have not these evils come upon us because our God is not among us?" (Deu 31:17)
  - **Complaint:** "My way is hidden from the Lord, and my right is disregarded by my God" (Isa 40:27). The answer: "He does not faint or grow weary … He gives power to the faint" (40:28–29)
  - **Banishment and the fear that follows it:** Cain, "from your face I shall be hidden. I shall be a fugitive … whoever finds me will kill me" (Gen 4:14), after "My punishment is greater than I can bear" (4:13). Being hidden from God's face is followed at once by **fear of man**.
- **How it co-exists.**
  - **Petition** is the most common companion: "Hide not your face from me" (Psa 27:9; 69:17 "for I am in distress"; 102:2 "in the day of my distress"; 143:7).
  - **Petition reversed:** "Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities" (Psa 51:9), then "Create in me a clean heart" (51:10). The penitent asks God to hide his face **from the sin**, not from the self: the same act, wished in the opposite direction.
  - **A false inference:** the wicked "says in his heart, 'God has forgotten, he has hidden his face, he will never see it'" (Psa 10:11). He reads hiddenness as **licence**.
  - **Its denial as resolution:** "he has not hidden his face from him, but has heard, when he cried to him" (Psa 22:24).
  - **Waiting and hope through it:** "I will wait for the Lord, who is hiding his face … and I will hope in him" (Isa 8:17). "Truly, you are a God who hides himself, O God of Israel, the Savior" (Isa 45:15) holds hiddenness and saving together. Job 34:29 (Elihu): "When he hides his face, who can behold him".
- **Before and after in one psalm, Psa 30:6–8.**
  - Security in prosperity: "I said in my prosperity, 'I shall never be moved'"
  - The hidden face and dismay: "you hid your face; I was dismayed"
  - Crying out: "To you, O Lord, I cry … I plead for mercy"

  **The hidden face breaks a false security and produces prayer.**
- **What it leads to at the end.** "I will not hide my face anymore from them, when I pour out my Spirit" (Eze 39:29). "With everlasting love I will have compassion" (Isa 54:8).
- **Seat.** This is the phenomenon where the seat is densest. There are six links in the verse itself, and in nine more passages a seat arrives in the next verse or close by: Psa 13, 22, 27, 44, 51, 69, 89 and 102, and Isa 54.
  - **In the verse, the bearer's own (genuine):**
    - Psa 143:7 "My spirit fails! Hide not your face". Batch A v3 §6 ◆ (and §7 Faintness).
    - Psa 88:14 "why do you cast my soul away? Why do you hide your face". Batch C v3 §9.5 ("Cast off"). **New in v3:** the two questions are parallel, so **the hidden face and the rejected soul are one experience seen from two sides**, God's act and the human's undergoing of it.
    - Psa 104:29 "they are dismayed … you take away their breath". Batch A v3 §6 ◆. **Seat-sense:** *ruach* here is **life-breath** (H7307H "spirit: breath"), the breath that keeps the creature alive, not an inner faculty. The inner state is carried by "dismayed". The pairing A §6 drew still holds: hidden face with breath withdrawn, dismay with death.
  - **In the verse, the offender's (genuine):**
    - Psa 10:11 "He says in his heart, 'God has forgotten, he has hidden his face'". Batch A v3 §6 ◆. The heart is **where the false inference is spoken**: the wicked tells himself that the hidden face means no one sees.
    - Isa 57:17 "I hid my face and was angry, but he went on backsliding in the way of his own heart". Batch H v1 §4.1 ("the heart … go[es] its own way"). **New in v3:** the verse sets two movements against each other. God hides his face; the offender does not turn but keeps to **his own heart's way**. The hidden face here is **not felt at all**. That is the opposite of Psa 30:7 and 143:7, where it is felt at once.
  - **In the verse, the other party's (divine):**
    - Eze 39:29 "I will not hide my face anymore … when I pour out my Spirit upon the house of Israel". Batch A v3 §6 ◆ [D1]. The Spirit is God's. **The human side is the receiving**: the end of hiddenness lands on the house of Israel as a Spirit poured out on them.
  - **In the next verse or the passage (bearer's own, genuine):**
    - Psa 13:1 → 13:2 "counsel in my soul … sorrow in my heart all the day" (v1 §2, *satar* row).
    - Psa 44:24 → 44:25 "our soul is bowed down to the dust" (v2).
    - Psa 51:9 → 51:10 "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me". **New in v3:** when God is asked to hide his face *from the sin*, the next request places the remedy in **the heart and spirit**. The seat is where the cleansing is to happen. The requests go on: "Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your Holy Spirit from me" (51:11), then "uphold me with a willing spirit" (51:12). **The face hidden from the sin (51:9) is set against the presence not withdrawn from the person (51:11).** Between them stand two spirits: God's, which is not to be taken away, and the human's, which is to be renewed and made willing.
    - **New in v3:** Psa 102:2 ("Do not hide your face from me in the day of my distress") → 102:4 "My heart is struck down like grass and has withered; I forget to eat my bread". **The distress of 102:2 is a withered heart, and it reaches the body:** the sufferer forgets to eat. It is the same movement as Psa 44:25 ("our belly clings to the ground").
    - **New in v3:** Psa 27:9 ← 27:8 "You have said, 'Seek my face.' My heart says to you, 'Your face, Lord, do I seek.'" **The heart seeks the face one verse before it asks that the face not be hidden.** The petition "Hide not your face" (27:9) is the voice of a heart already turned toward the face. What hiddenness would cut off is the heart's own seeking.
    - **New in v3:** Psa 69:17 → 69:20 "Reproaches have broken my heart, so that I am in despair. I looked for pity, but there was none". The "distress" of 69:17 is named three verses later as **a broken heart that finds no comforter**. The hidden face of 69:17 and the absent human pity of 69:20 are **the same lack, felt from God and from people**. In between, the petition names the soul: "Draw near to my soul, redeem me" (69:18). **The opposite of the hidden face is God drawing near to the soul.**
    - **New in v3, the resolution in the heart:** Psa 22:24 ("he has not hidden his face from him, but has heard") → 22:26 "The afflicted shall eat and be satisfied; those who seek him shall praise the Lord! May your hearts live forever!" **When the face is not hidden, the outcome lands in hearts that live.** It is the reverse of the withered heart that forgets to eat (Psa 102:4): here the afflicted eat, and the hearts live.
    - **Life-sense, in the passage:** Psa 89:46 ("How long, O Lord? Will you hide yourself forever?") → 89:48 "What man can live and never see death? Who can deliver his soul from the power of Sheol?" *Nephesh* here is **the life facing death**. The hidden face is set against human mortality. This is a pointer to Batch G v3 §16.4 ("The soul delivered from death"), not read further here.
    - **New in v3:** Isa 54:8 ← 54:6 "the Lord has called you like a wife deserted and grieved in spirit, like a wife of youth when she is cast off". **The momentary hiding of 54:8 is borne in a grieved spirit** (the bearer's own, Zion's). The image is marital: the hidden face is felt as desertion.
  - **Spurious context links:** Isa 64:7 ← 64:6 "our iniquities, like the wind, take us away" (*ruach* as wind); Mic 3:4 ← 3:2–3 "flesh" (the flesh torn from the people). Neither is an inner seat.
  - **What the map shows.** Every genuine seat in this phenomenon falls **on the human side**. Three forms:
    1. **The sufferer's spirit, soul or heart is where the hiddenness is felt:** failing, cast away, sorrowing, bowed down, broken, grieved.
    2. **The offender's heart is where it is misread or ignored:** "God has forgotten" (Psa 10:11); "the way of his own heart" (Isa 57:17).
    3. **God's Spirit is where it ends:** poured out (Eze 39:29).

## 6. Nothing is hidden: exposure before God (EX) — 11 verses

- **What it is.** Being fully seen: the failure of every hiding place before God, and in one case before a king.
- **What it does.** The same fact works **in opposite directions**, depending on who is seen:
  - **Comfort for the sufferer:** "all my longing is before you; my sighing is not hidden from you" (Psa 38:9), between "the tumult of my heart" (38:8) and "My heart throbs" (38:10)
  - **Confession for the penitent:** "you know my folly; the wrongs I have done are not hidden from you" (Psa 69:5)
  - **Wonder at being known before birth:** "My frame was not hidden from you, when I was being made in secret" (Psa 139:15)
  - **Judgement for the evildoer:** "They are not hidden from me, nor is their iniquity concealed" (Jer 16:17); "Israel is not hidden from me; for now, O Ephraim, you have played the whore" (Hos 5:3); "There is no gloom or deep darkness where evildoers may hide themselves" (Job 34:22); "Can a man hide himself in secret places so that I cannot see him? … Do I not fill heaven and earth?" (Jer 23:24); "If they hide themselves on the top of Carmel … at the bottom of the sea …" (Amo 9:3)
- **Where it comes from: the attempt to hide.** "Ah, you who hide deep from the Lord your counsel … and who say, 'Who sees us? Who knows us?'" (Isa 29:15). The next verse names what that attempt does: "You turn things upside down! Shall the potter be regarded as the clay …?" (29:16). **Hiding from God inverts creature and creator.**
- **As a restraint between people.**
  - Gen 31:49–50, Mizpah: "The Lord watch between you and me, when we are out of one another's sight … God is witness." Mistrust between Laban and Jacob is handed to the One who sees what they cannot.
  - 2Sa 18:13: a soldier would not strike Absalom because "there is nothing hidden from the king". Knowing he will be seen restrains the act.
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** 2Sa 18:13 "if I had dealt treacherously against his life". *Nephesh* here is **Absalom's life**, the other party's, in its **life sense**. It is the life that would have been taken, not an inner faculty. **New in v3:** the inner movement of the verse (restraint by knowing one will be seen) belongs to the soldier, and it has no seat word. This verse was not read in the M47 batches.
  - **In the passage, the bearer's own (genuine):** Psa 38:9 stands between "the tumult of my heart" (38:8) and "My heart throbs; my strength fails me" (38:10). **The sighing that is not hidden from God is a heart's sighing.** Exposure here is received as comfort, because what is seen is the heart's pain.
  - **In the passage, the offender's (genuine):** Isa 29:15, "you who hide deep from the Lord your counsel", follows 29:13, "their hearts are far from me". **The attempt to hide counsel comes from a heart already far off.** This is the same heart that §8 (Isa 29:14) reads as losing its discernment. Batch G v3 §12.3 ("Honour with lips, heart far") read 29:13 itself.
  - **New in v3, within one psalm:** Psa 139 holds §6 and §11 together. "My frame was not hidden from you" (139:15, EX) and "Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts [anxious thoughts]" (139:23, AX; Batch A v3 §6 "God searching the anxious heart"). **Being wholly seen, accepted as a fact at 139:15, becomes a search the psalmist asks for at 139:23.** The seat named in the request is the heart, and the anxieties in it. The verse before 139:15 gives the knowing from the human side: "I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made. Wonderful are your works; **my soul knows it very well**" (139:14). **God sees the frame (139:15), and the soul knows it has been made (139:14).** Two knowings meet: God's knowledge of the person, and the soul's knowledge of being known and made. Exposure here produces praise.
  - **In the next verse, the offender's (genuine) — new in v3:** Hos 5:3 ("Israel is not hidden from me") → 5:4 "Their deeds do not permit them to return to their God. For the spirit of whoredom is within them, and they know not the Lord". *Ruach* here is an **inner disposition**, "within them", and it passes the check. **The two knowings do not match: God knows Ephraim completely (5:3), and Ephraim does not know God (5:4).** The spirit within is what blocks the return. This is the judgement side of the same fact that is praise in Psa 139:14–15.

## 7. Concealing and disclosing what one holds (CD) — 29 verses

- **What it is.** Keeping inside, or bringing out, something one holds: a deed, a sin, a word, a truth, love, a treasure. Six strands emerged.
- **(a) Concealing one's own wrong.** Each case has its own sequence:
  - **Achan:**
    - "when I saw … then I coveted them and took them. And see, they are hidden in the earth inside my tent" (Jos 7:21)
    - Joshua: "tell me now what you have done; do not hide it from me" (7:19)
    - Achan: "Truly I have sinned" (7:20)
    - "behold, it was hidden in his tent" (7:22)

    **Seeing, coveting, taking, hiding, being demanded to disclose, confessing, being found.**
  - **Moses:** "He looked this way and that, and seeing no one, he struck down the Egyptian and hid him in the sand" (Exo 2:12). When the deed surfaces: "Moses was afraid, and thought, 'Surely the thing is known'" (2:14). **Concealment fails, and fear follows.**
  - **The hidden adultery:** "it is hidden from the eyes of her husband" (Num 5:13). The next verse: "the spirit of jealousy comes over him" (5:14). **Where a deed is hidden, suspicion grows in the other person.**
  - **Job's denial:** "if I have concealed my transgressions as others do by hiding my iniquity in my heart [*chob*, bosom], because I stood in great fear of the multitude, and the contempt of families terrified me, so that I kept silence" (Job 31:33–34). The verse names the **motive for concealing sin: fear of social contempt**. Job denies he acted on it.
  - **The lepers:** they hid the plunder (2Ki 7:8), then "said to one another, 'We are not doing right … If we are silent … punishment will overtake us'" (7:9). **Conscience reverses concealment into telling.**
  - **Concealment as savouring:** "Though evil is sweet in his mouth, though he hides it under his tongue, though he is loath to let it go and holds it in his mouth" (Job 20:12–13). The hidden sin is **kept because it is enjoyed.**
  - **Not hiding at all:** "the look on their faces bears witness against them; they proclaim their sin like Sodom; they do not hide it" (Isa 3:9). **The face discloses**: shamelessness.
- **(b) Demanding or giving full disclosure between persons.**
  - Samuel "was afraid to tell the vision to Eli" (1Sa 3:15). Eli binds him with an oath, "Do not hide it from me" (3:17). "Samuel told him everything and hid nothing from him. And he said, 'It is the Lord. Let him do what seems good to him'" (3:18). **Fear of telling is overcome, and the disclosure is met by submission.**
  - Jonathan: "my father does nothing either great or small without disclosing it to me. And why should my father hide this from me?" (1Sa 20:2). This is **trust in a father's openness**, and chapter 20 proves it misplaced.
  - Kings demanding it: "Do not hide from me anything I ask you" (2Sa 14:18); "I will ask you a question; hide nothing from me" (Jer 38:14).
  - Under threat: "hide nothing from us and we will not put you to death" (Jer 38:25).
  - Disclosure of utter need: "We will not hide from my lord that our money is all spent … nothing left … but our bodies and our land" (Gen 47:18).
- **(c) Not withholding God's word or works.**
  - "I have not hidden your deliverance within my heart; I have spoken" (Psa 40:10). The seat is **where a word would be held back**.
  - "We will not hide them from their children, but tell to the coming generation" (Psa 78:4).
  - "what is with the Almighty I will not conceal" (Job 27:11); "what wise men have told, without hiding it from their fathers" (Job 15:18).
  - "This would be my comfort … for I have not denied [concealed] the words of the Holy One" (Job 6:10): **having not concealed** is Job's comfort in pain.
  - "proclaim, conceal it not" (Jer 50:2).
- **(d) What cannot stay hidden.**
  - "A city set on a hill cannot be hidden" (Mat 5:14).
  - "even those [good works] that are not [conspicuous] cannot remain hidden" (1Ti 5:25).
  - "Better is open rebuke than hidden love" (Pro 27:5), then "Faithful are the wounds of a friend" (27:6). **Love that stays hidden is worth less than rebuke that shows.**
- **(e) Hiding a treasure.**
  - "treasure hidden in a field, which a man found and covered up. Then **in his joy** he goes and sells all that he has" (Mat 13:44). **Concealment serves joy and resolve.**
  - Against it, the servant's burial of the talent (Mat 25:18). He explains: "I knew you to be a hard man … so I was afraid, and I went and hid your talent" (25:24–25). **A perception of the master produces fear, and fear produces burial.** The master names it "wicked and slothful" (25:26).
- **(f) Silence as respect.** "the voice of the nobles was hushed, and their tongue stuck to the roof of their mouth" (Job 29:10), with 29:9. The voice is withheld in honour of another.
- **How it co-exists.** Fear is the most frequent companion, on both sides:
  - fear drives concealment (Job 31:34; Mat 25:25), or follows its failure (Exo 2:14)
  - fear blocks disclosure until it is overcome (1Sa 3:15–18)

  Joy (Mat 13:44), enjoyment of sin (Job 20:12–13) and conscience (2Ki 7:9) are the other companions.
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse, the bearer's own (genuine):** Psa 40:10 "I have not hidden your deliverance within my heart". Batch A v3 §6 ("The heart can conceal or not"). The heart is **the place where a word could be kept back**, and the verse says it was not.
  - **In the verse, the offender's own self:** Isa 3:9 "they have brought evil on themselves [*their nephesh*]". Batch D v2 §2.3 ("Evil against one's own soul"). **Seat-sense:** *nephesh* is here the **self**, reflexively, and it passes the check in that sense. **New in v3:** the verse holds the phenomenon and its outcome together. The face does *not* conceal ("they do not hide it"), and the evil disclosed comes back on **the self that disclosed it**. Shamelessness is not a safe disclosure.
  - **In the next verse, the other party's (genuine):** Num 5:13 → 5:14 "the spirit of jealousy comes over him". The deed is hidden in the wife; **the seat that registers it is the husband's spirit** (v2).
  - **A seat outside M47:** Job 31:33 *chob*, "bosom", where the iniquity would be hidden (v1 §3 item 6; not an M47 word).
  - **In the passage, the bearer's own — new in v3:** Job 6:10 ("This would be my comfort … for I have not denied the words of the Holy One") is flanked by two uses of *nephesh*:
    - "My appetite refuses to touch them; they are as food that is loathsome to me" (6:7). *Nephesh* is **appetite** here. The sense passes the check only as bodily desire, and it has turned to loathing.
    - "What is my strength, that I should wait? And what is my end, that I should be patient?" (6:11). *Nephesh* is inside the English "be patient", literally "lengthen my soul". It is genuine, and the soul **cannot endure**.

    **Job's one comfort, that he has not concealed God's words, stands between an appetite that loathes and a soul that can no longer wait.** Not concealing is the one thing left intact.
  - **Spurious context link:** 2Sa 14:18 → 14:19 "As surely as you live [*nephesh*]" is an oath formula. Batch G v3 §4 read the formula as "the soul as what one swears by". It says nothing of the disclosure.
  - **Whose seat, strand (b) and (e):** none. The disclosures of Samuel, the kings' demands, the treasure-finder's joy and the servant's fear are all carried by speech and act, not by a seat word.

## 8. Understanding closed from outside (UC) — 13 verses

- **What it is.** Not-knowing that the verse attributes to **concealment by another** (God, or the nature of things), not only to the person's failure.
- **What it does.**
  - Luk 9:45: "they did not understand this saying, and it was concealed from them, so that they might not perceive it. And they were afraid to ask him". The next verse: "An argument arose among them as to which of them was the greatest" (9:46). **Blocked perception is followed by fear of asking, and then by rivalry.**
  - "This saying was hidden from them, and they did not grasp what was said" (Luk 18:34).
  - "Would that you, even you, had known on this day the things that make for peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes" (Luk 19:42): the not-knowing has become a judgement.
- **Where it comes from.**
  - **God's own act:** "you have hidden these things from the wise and understanding and revealed them to little children" (Mat 11:25), said in thanks; "I will utter what has been hidden since the foundation of the world" (Mat 13:35).
  - **Judgement:** "the discernment of their discerning men shall be hidden" (Isa 29:14), on a people who "honor me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me" (29:13). **Discernment is hidden from those whose heart is absent from their worship.**
  - **The nature of things:** "It is hidden from the eyes of all living" (Job 28:21, of wisdom); "he reveals deep and hidden things" (Dan 2:22).
  - **Hidden from oneself:** "Who can discern his errors? Declare me innocent from hidden faults" (Psa 19:12). The next verse asks: "Keep back your servant also from presumptuous sins" (19:13).
- **What it leads to, in the person.** Fear to ask (Luk 9:45); a plea, "I am a sojourner on the earth; hide not your commandments from me!" (Psa 119:19); searching, "the glory of kings is to search things out" (Pro 25:2); obedience to what is revealed, "the things that are revealed belong to us … that we may do all the words of this law" (Deu 29:29). Job 3:23, "a man whose way is hidden, whom God has hedged in", is the not-knowing that **hems in**.
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** none of the 13 names a seat.
  - **In the passage — new in v3:** in six passages the seat arrives close by. Each time it is **genuine**, but it belongs to a different party or plays a different role:
    - **The other party names it.** Luk 9:45–46 → 9:47 "But Jesus, knowing the reasoning of their hearts, took a child". The disciples' blocked perception, their fear of asking and their rivalry have no seat word. **The seat appears only when Jesus reads it**: their hearts, known by another. This matches what v1 §3 item 1 found for doubt (Luk 24:38): the seat is named **when someone diagnoses**.
    - **The offender's heart as the cause.** Isa 29:14 ← 29:13 "their hearts are far from me" (v2; Batch G v3 §12.3). Hidden discernment is the consequence of a heart that has gone elsewhere.
    - **The bearer's own heart as the prayer's end.** Psa 19:12 (hidden faults) → 19:14 "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in your sight". The one who cannot see his own hidden faults **hands the heart's meditation over to God's sight**. What is hidden from the self is entrusted to the one from whom nothing is hidden (§6).
    - **The bearer's own soul as the source of the plea.** Psa 119:19 ("hide not your commandments from me!") → 119:20 "My soul is consumed with longing for your rules at all times". **The plea not to have the commandments hidden comes from a soul consumed with longing for them.** Here the not-knowing is **feared, and the fear comes from desire**, the reverse of Luk 9:45, where the disciples are afraid to ask.
    - **Heart and soul in the response to what is revealed.** Deu 29:29 → 30:2 "return to the Lord your God … and obey his voice … with all your heart and with all your soul". The revealed things (29:29) are to be done, and the next verses place that doing **in the whole heart and soul**.
    - **The sufferer's soul.** Job 3:23 ("a man whose way is hidden") sits in the passage opened by "life to the bitter in soul" (3:20; see §4). The hemmed-in man is the bitter soul.

## 9. Perplexity (PX) — 11 verses

- **What it is.** Knowing that cannot close: the mind held open before something it cannot interpret.
- **Who bears it:** Herod (Mar 6:20; Luk 9:7); the disciples (Joh 13:22); the women at the tomb (Luk 24:4); the Pentecost crowd (Act 2:12); the temple captain and chief priests (Act 5:24); Peter (Act 10:17); Festus (Act 25:20); Paul (2Cor 4:8; Gal 4:20); Belshazzar's lords (Dan 5:9).
- **Where it comes from.** Something **heard or seen that exceeds the categories at hand**:
  - "it was said by some that John had been raised from the dead" (Luk 9:7)
  - "we hear them telling in our own tongues" (Act 2:11–12)
  - "what the vision … might mean" (Act 10:17)
  - an empty tomb (Luk 24:3–4)
  - Jesus's "one of you will betray me" (Joh 13:21–22)
  - writing on a wall no one could read (Dan 5:8–9)
- **What it does.** It **asks aloud**: "What does this mean?" (Act 2:12); "who is this about whom I hear such things?" (Luk 9:9). It **looks at others**: "The disciples looked at one another" (Joh 13:22). It **turns inward**: "Peter was inwardly [*en heautō*, in himself] perplexed" (Act 10:17). Festus **hands the question on**: "Being at a loss how to investigate … I asked whether he wanted to go to Jerusalem" (Act 25:20).
- **How it co-exists.**
  - Herod: "Herod feared John, knowing that he was a righteous and holy man … When he heard him, he was greatly perplexed, and yet he heard him gladly" (Mar 6:20). **Fear, knowledge, perplexity and gladness are all present in one man towards one prophet**, and the perplexity keeps him listening.
  - Amazement: "all were amazed and perplexed" (Act 2:12).
  - The body of another man: Belshazzar's "color changed … his limbs gave way, and his knees knocked together" (Dan 5:6) while his lords were perplexed (5:9).
  - Anguish of love: "I am perplexed about you" (Gal 4:20), after "my little children, for whom I am again in the anguish of childbirth" (4:19).
  - A limit held: "perplexed, but not driven to despair" (2Cor 4:8). The perplexity is **bounded**, and the neighbouring verses set it inside "God … has shone in our hearts" (4:6) and "this treasure in jars of clay" (4:7).
- **What it leads to.** Mostly, **something from outside ends it**:
  - "While they were perplexed about this, behold, two men stood by them" (Luk 24:4)
  - "the men … stood at the gate" and "the Spirit said to him" (Act 10:17, 19)
  - "Peter, standing with the eleven, lifted up his voice" (Act 2:14)
  - the queen names Daniel (Dan 5:10–12)
  - Herod "sought to see him" (Luk 9:9)

  **Perplexity ends when an interpreter or a meeting arrives.**
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** none of the 11 names a seat. The operation word names itself (v1 §1.1, §3 item 1). Act 10:17 locates it with a reflexive pronoun, *en heautō*, "in himself", which is not an M47 word (v1 §3 item 6).
  - **In the passage, the other party's (genuine):** Joh 13:22 ← 13:21 "Jesus was troubled in his spirit". **The seat is Jesus's, and the perplexity is the disciples'** (v1 §1.1).
  - **In the passage, the bearer's own (genuine):** 2Cor 4:8 ← 4:6 "God … has shone in our hearts" (v1 §1.1). The bounded perplexity sits inside hearts already lit.
  - **In the passage, the interpreter's — new in v3:** Dan 5:9 → 5:12 "an excellent spirit, knowledge, and understanding to interpret dreams, explain riddles, and solve problems were found in this Daniel". The lords are perplexed and have no seat. **The seat that arrives belongs to the interpreter, and it is named as the very capacity the perplexed lack.** This gives v2's finding a seat. Perplexity ends when an interpreter arrives, and in Daniel's case the text locates the interpreter's gift in his spirit.
  - **Divine Spirit:** Act 10:17 → 10:19 "the Spirit said to him". That is the other party, and divine. It ends the perplexity from outside.

## 10. Doubt: the divided soul (DB) — Mat 14:31; 28:17; Jam 1:8; 4:8

- **What it is.** Trust that is split in two.
- **What it does.** In Peter it **acts through the body**. "When he saw the wind, he was afraid, and beginning to sink he cried out, 'Lord, save me'" (Mat 14:30). Then: "O you of little faith, why did you doubt?" (14:31). **Seeing leads to fear, which leads to sinking.** The failing trust is visible as the sinking.
- **How it co-exists.**
  - With worship, in the same company: "they worshiped him, but some doubted" (Mat 28:17). The text leaves both standing and goes straight on to the commission (28:18–19).
  - With instability: "a double-minded man, unstable in all his ways" (Jam 1:8), after "the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea that is driven and tossed by the wind" (1:6).
  - *Dipsuchos* is "two-souled". **The division is in the word itself.**
- **What it leads to.** Rescue by a hand (Mat 14:31), then worship (14:33). The call to be undivided: "Draw near to God … purify your hearts, you double-minded" (Jam 4:8). **What is divided is the heart, and the remedy is cleansing.**
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse, the bearer's own (genuine):** Jam 4:8 "purify your hearts, you double-minded". Batch A v3 §6 ("Double-mindedness"); v1 §1.3.
  - **Inside the word:** *dipsuchos* (Jam 1:8; 4:8) carries *psychē* in its own form (v1 §1.3). The seat is present in both Jam verses, once by compound and once by a separate word.
  - **No seat:** Mat 14:31 and 28:17. The doubt is carried by the sinking body and the divided company (v1 §1.2). Mat 28:17 → 28:19 "the Holy Spirit" is divine and does not bear on the doubt.
  - **Outside M20, for comparison only:** "why do doubts arise in your hearts?" (Luk 24:38) and "does not doubt in his heart" (Mar 11:23) are Batch A v3 §6 ("Doubt in the heart"). They are other words and other clusters. They agree with v1 §1.2: doubt gets a seat when someone names it from outside (Jesus, twice), and has none when the narrator records it.
  - **New in v3, what the map shows:** in the four M20 doubt verses, **the seat appears only in the remedy**, "purify your hearts" (Jam 4:8). The doubt itself is shown in a wave, a sinking and a divided company, and the heart is named as the place to be cleansed.

## 11. Anxiety and care (AX) — 40 verses

- **What it is.** The mind taken up with something not yet secured: provision, the future, speech under trial, other people, one's sin.
- **Where the strands differ.** Eight strands emerged; they are kept apart because what the anxiety **attaches to** changes how it works.
- **(a) Anxiety over provision** (Mat 6:25, 27, 28, 31, 34; Luk 12:22, 25, 26, 29).
  - **Where from:** "what you will eat or what you will drink … what you will put on" (Mat 6:25).
  - **What it does:** nothing. "which of you by being anxious can add a single hour to his span of life?" (6:27 = Luk 12:25). It **multiplies itself forward**: "tomorrow will be anxious for itself" (6:34). It **suspends the person**: *meteōrizō*, "be held in suspense" (Luk 12:29).
  - **What it co-exists with:** "little faith" (Luk 12:28), and the Gentiles' seeking (Mat 6:32).
  - **What displaces it:** looking at birds and lilies (6:26, 28); knowing "your heavenly Father knows that you need them" (6:32); "seek first the kingdom" (6:33).
  - The seat is stated **once**, at the head: "anxious about your life [*psychē*]" (Mat 6:25; Luk 12:22). The rest of the discourse inherits it.
- **(b) Anxiety about speaking under trial** (Mat 10:19; Luk 12:11). It is answered by gift: "what you are to say will be given to you in that hour. For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you" (Mat 10:19–20).
- **(c) Divided care.**
  - "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things" (Luk 10:41), against "one thing is necessary" (10:42).
  - "The unmarried man is anxious about the things of the Lord … the married man is anxious about worldly things … and his interests are divided" (1Cor 7:32–34). The aim is "undivided devotion" (7:35).
  - Here anxiety is **not condemned as such**. What matters is **where it is directed**, and whether it splits the person.
- **(d) Care for others, valued as good.**
  - "I have no one like him, who will be genuinely concerned for your welfare" (Phili 2:20), against "they all seek their own interests" (2:21).
  - "the daily pressure on me of my anxiety for all the churches" (2Cor 11:28). The next verse shows what that anxiety is: "Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to fall, and I am not indignant?" (11:29). **Anxiety for others is sharing in their condition.**
  - "the members may have the same care for one another" (1Cor 12:25).
  - "that I may be less anxious" (Phili 2:28), in a chain of feeling between Epaphroditus, Paul and the Philippians (2:26–28).
  - A father: Kish "anxious about you, saying, 'What shall I do about my son?'" (1Sa 10:2). His care shifts **from the donkeys to the son** (9:5; 10:2).
  - **The same word *merimnaō* carries (a) and (d).** Its value depends on its object: self-provision against others' welfare.
- **(e) Cares that choke.** The sower parable: "the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches choke the word" (Mat 13:22); "the cares of the world … **enter in** and choke the word" (Mar 4:19); "as they go on their way they are choked by the cares and riches and pleasures of life" (Luk 8:14); "lest your hearts be weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness and cares of this life" (Luk 21:34).
  - **Where from:** outside ("enter in").
  - **What it does:** it acts on the word already received. It chokes it and weighs down the heart.
  - **Co-exists with** riches, pleasures and drunkenness.
  - **Leads to:** "it proves unfruitful"; "that day come upon you suddenly like a trap".
- **(f) Anxiety handed over or answered.**
  - "do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God" (Phili 4:6) → "the peace of God … will guard your hearts and your minds" (4:7)
  - "casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you" (1Pe 5:7)
  - "When the cares of my heart are many, your consolations cheer my soul" (Psa 94:19)
  - "Anxiety in a man's heart weighs him down, but a good word makes him glad" (Pro 12:25)
  - "Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts [*sar'appim*, anxious thoughts]" (Psa 139:23)

  **The movement: anxiety is given to another, or answered by a word, and becomes peace or gladness.** The seat is named where the anxiety sits (heart) and where the answer lands (soul, hearts and minds).
- **(g) Dread of an anticipated harm** (*da'ag*, *de'agah*).
  - The Transjordan tribes built an altar "from fear that in time to come your children might say to our children, 'What have you to do with the Lord'" (Jos 22:24), "so your children might make our children cease to worship the Lord" (22:25). Anxiety about **a future break in worship** leads to a **preventive act**.
  - "I am afraid of the Judeans who have deserted … lest I be handed over to them" (Jer 38:19). Jeremiah answers: "Obey now the voice of the Lord" (38:20). **Dread of people stands against obedience.**
  - "the famine of which you are afraid shall follow close after you to Egypt" (Jer 42:16), from those who said "we will go to the land of Egypt, where we shall not … be hungry" (42:14). **Fleeing the feared thing brings it along.**
  - "Whom did you dread and fear, so that you lied, and did not remember me, did not lay it to heart?" (Isa 57:11). **Dread leads to lying, and lying to forgetting God.**
  - "they melt in fear, they are troubled like the sea that cannot be quiet" (Jer 49:23), after hearing bad news.
  - The same word turned to sin: "I confess my iniquity; I am sorry [*da'ag*] for my sin" (Psa 38:18). **Anxiety becomes contrition.**
- **(h) Anxiety eaten and drunk.** "eat your bread with quaking, and drink water with trembling and with anxiety" (Eze 12:18); "They shall eat their bread with anxiety, and drink water in dismay" (12:19; 4:16). The prophet's own body enacts the coming siege. **The inner state is carried into eating and drinking.**
- **The one who is not anxious.** "He is like a tree planted by water … does not fear when heat comes … is not anxious in the year of drought" (Jer 17:8). He is "the man who trusts in the Lord" (17:7), and the passage then turns to "The heart is deceitful … I the Lord search the heart" (17:9–10).
- **Seat.** Anxiety holds the most in-verse seats of any M20 phenomenon (8 of 30). The seat word plays **three different roles**, and v3 separates them.
  1. **The seat as where the anxiety sits** (bearer's own, genuine):
     - Pro 12:25 "Anxiety in a man's heart weighs him down" (strand f). Batch A v3 §6 ("Anxiety in the heart").
     - Luk 21:34 "lest your hearts be weighed down with … cares of this life" (strand e). Batch A v3 §6.
     - Psa 139:23 "know my heart! … know my thoughts [anxious thoughts]" (strand f). Batch A v3 §6 ("God searching the anxious heart").
  2. **The seat as where the answer lands** (bearer's own, genuine):
     - Psa 94:19 "your consolations cheer my soul" (strand f). Batch A v3 §6 notes that "heart" in this verse is English only. **New in v3** [lexical]: the Hebrew locates the cares "within me" (*beqirbi*) and names the *nephesh* only at the receiving end. **In Hebrew, the seat is named where the consolation arrives, not where the anxiety sits.**
     - Phili 4:6 → 4:7 "will guard your hearts and your minds" (v1 §2 *merimnaō* row).
  3. **The seat as the object the anxiety is about** (bearer's own, but not its location):
     - Mat 6:25; Luk 12:22 "do not be anxious about your life [*psychē*] … nor about your body". Batch A v3 §6 ("Anxiety about life (psychē) and body"). **New in v3, seat-sense:** *psychē* here is the **life that needs food**. The next verse puts it so: "For life is more than food, and the body more than clothing" (Luk 12:23). The seat word is **what the anxiety is about**, not where it happens. The whole discourse inherits this frame (v1 §1.4: "discourse-level seat").
     - 1Cor 7:34 "anxious about the things of the Lord, how to be holy in body and spirit" (strand c). Batch A v3 §6 ("Divided interests") [H]. **New in v3:** here too the spirit is **the object of the care**, what she wants to be holy, and the care has a good aim. Within strand (c), the seat is on the undivided side of the divided care.
  4. **The seat as what dread displaces** (the dreader's own, genuine):
     - Isa 57:11 "did not lay it to heart". Batch D v2 §4.6 ("fear → lying → not laying to heart"). **New in v3:** the heart is not where the dread sits. It is the place **God should have been set, and dread took that place instead.**
  - **Outside the verse, other seats in the strands:**
    - **Strand (b), a divine Spirit replaces the anxious speaker's own seat.** Mat 10:19 → 10:20 "it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you". Batch E v2 §1.6 ("The Spirit speaks through the person"). The anxiety about what to say is answered **by another's Spirit in the speaker's place**. The Luke parallel runs the same way: Luk 12:11 → 12:12 "for the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say". Luke gives the answer as **teaching**: the Spirit supplies the knowing that the anxiety lacked.
    - **Strand (e), the choked hearer is set between two hearts** — new in v3. Luk 8:14 (the thorns) stands between "the devil comes and takes away the word from their hearts" (8:12) and "hold it fast in an honest and good heart, and bear fruit with patience" (8:15). **The cares act on a heart where the word has been sown, and the contrast the passage draws is with a good heart that holds the word fast.** Mat 13:22 ← 13:19 "snatches away what has been sown in his heart" is the same frame (v1 §2 *merimna* row).
    - **The man who is not anxious.** Jer 17:8 is framed by "whose heart turns away from the Lord" (17:5), and by "The heart is deceitful … I the Lord search the heart and test the mind" (17:9–10) (v1 §2 *de'agah* row). The unanxious tree stands between two statements about the heart.
    - **Strand (g), a neighbouring oracle's heart.** Jer 49:23 (Hamath and Arpad "melt in fear, they are troubled like the sea") follows 49:22, the end of the oracle against Edom: "the heart of the warriors of Edom shall be in that day like the heart of a woman in her birth pains". **The party is different**: Edom in 49:22, the cities of Damascus in 49:23. So the seat does not belong to the anxious ones of 49:23. What the two oracles share is **the same collapse under bad news**. It is stated as a heart in labour for Edom's warriors, and as melting and a troubled sea for Hamath. It is recorded as a parallel, not as the seat of 49:23.
    - **Spurious context link:** Jer 38:19 → 38:17 "your life shall be spared". *Nephesh* is bodily life here: the life Zedekiah fears to lose. It names what the dread is about, not a seat.

## 12. Losing heart (LH) — Col 3:21; Eze 13:22; Psa 10:10; 109:16; Dan 11:30

- **What it is.** The heart's strength taken away.
- **Where it comes from.** **In all five verses, from another party**:
  - a father's provoking: "Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged" (Col 3:21)
  - false prophets' lies: "you have disheartened the righteous falsely, although I have not grieved him" (Eze 13:22). God did not do the grieving; the lie did.
  - an oppressor: "The helpless are crushed, sink down, and fall by his might" (Psa 10:10), while "He says in his heart, 'God has forgotten'" (10:11)
  - a persecutor who "pursued the poor and needy and the brokenhearted, to put them to death" (Psa 109:16), who "loved to curse" (109:17)
  - military defeat: "ships of Kittim shall come against him" (Dan 11:30)
- **What it does and leads to.**
  - The crushed "sink down, and fall" (Psa 10:10).
  - In Dan 11:30 losing heart **turns into rage and action**: "he shall be afraid [disheartened] and withdraw, and shall turn back and be enraged and take action against the holy covenant". **Disheartenment feeds aggression against a third party.**
  - God's answer to the lie: "I will deliver my people out of your hand" (Eze 13:23).
- **Seat.**
  - **Inside the word or phrase, the victim's own (genuine):**
    - Eze 13:22 "you have disheartened the righteous". The Hebrew includes *lev*, the heart, in the phrase. Batch A v3 §6 ("Disheartened by others").
    - Psa 109:16 "the brokenhearted" (*nikh'eh levav*). Batch G v3 §16.1 ("A broken heart is the prey of the pursuer"); Batch D v2 §10.3 and R6 (poverty and lowliness entangled).
    - Col 3:21 *athumeō*, "without *thumos*" (v1 §1.3). Not an M47 word, but the inner place is inside the verb.
  - **New in v3, what the map shows:** **in all three cases the seat named is the victim's, and the cause is another person's act.** Losing heart is the one M20 phenomenon where the seat word always sits with the one acted upon.
  - **In the passage, the aggressor's heart (genuine):**
    - Psa 10:10 → 10:11 "He says in his heart" (v1 §1.3; the same verse is FH §5, Batch A v3 §6 ◆). **The oppressor's heart speaks while the victim is seen only from outside.**
    - **New in v3:** Dan 11:30 ← 11:27 "as for the two kings, their hearts shall be bent on doing evil. They shall speak lies at the same table". The king who is disheartened in 11:30 is **one of the two whose heart was already bent on evil three verses earlier**. The losing of heart feeds an aggression that was already in the heart. The rage of 11:30 has a prior seat. The next step names that seat's object: "he shall return to his land with great wealth, but **his heart shall be set against the holy covenant**" (11:28). **The act of 11:30, "take action against the holy covenant", is the act of a heart set on it two verses earlier.** Losing heart does not create the target. It releases a hostility the heart already held.
  - **Different party, next verse:** Col 3:21 → 3:22 "with sincerity of heart, fearing the Lord" is addressed to bondservants, the next household group. It does not bear on the children's discouragement (v1 §1.3).

## 13. Grumbling (GR) — 17 verses

- **What it is.** A **collective, spoken complaint** against leaders, which is re-addressed as being **against God**.
- **Where it comes from.**
  - **Bodily need:** "What shall we drink?" (Exo 15:24); hunger (16:2–3); "the people thirsted there for water" (17:3).
  - **A report:** the spies "made all the congregation grumble … by bringing up a bad report about the land" (Num 14:36). Before that: "we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers" (13:33) and "the people wept that night" (14:1).
  - **A death:** after Korah, "You have killed the people of the Lord" (Num 16:41).
  - **A restraint:** "the people of Israel did not attack them, because the leaders … had sworn … Then all the congregation murmured against the leaders" (Jos 9:18). The leaders reply, "we may not touch them" (9:19).
- **What it does: it reinterprets the past as malice.**
  - "Would that we had died by the hand of the Lord in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the meat pots" (Exo 16:3)
  - "Why did you bring us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our livestock with thirst?" (17:3)
  - "Would that we had died in this wilderness!" (Num 14:2)
  - "Because the Lord hated us he has brought us out of the land of Egypt … to destroy us" (Deu 1:27)

  **Need becomes an accusation, and the exodus is recast as murder.** The quoted speech **exposes a death-wish and a verdict on God's heart**.
- **How it co-exists.**
  - with weeping (Num 14:1)
  - with rebellion: "you would not go up, but rebelled" (Deu 1:26)
  - with unbelief: "they despised the pleasant land, having no faith in his promise. They murmured in their tents" (Psa 106:24–25)
  - with violence: "They are almost ready to stone me" (Exo 17:4)
  - **The private form:** "in your tents" (Deu 1:27; Psa 106:25).
- **The re-addressing.** "he has heard your grumbling against the Lord. For what are we, that you grumble against us?" (Exo 16:7); "Your grumbling is not against us but against the Lord" (16:8); "it is against the Lord that you and all your company have gathered together" (Num 16:11). **An outside voice re-aims the complaint at its real object.**
- **What it leads to.** It varies:
  - **Provision:** Moses "cried to the Lord", and "the water became sweet … there he tested them" (Exo 15:25)
  - **Judgement:** "your dead bodies shall fall in this wilderness" (Num 14:29)
  - **A sign to end it:** Aaron's staff, "that you may make an end of their grumblings against me, lest they die" (Num 17:5, 10)
  - **Future healing:** "those who murmur will accept instruction" (Isa 29:24), beside "those who go astray in spirit will come to understanding"
- ◆ Psa 59:15 ("they … growl if they do not get their fill") is the same word **as an image** of enemies as dogs. Grumbling as the sound of an unfilled appetite fits the Exodus cases, where the grumbling starts from thirst and hunger.
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse, spurious:** Exo 16:8 "meat" (*basar* as food) (v1 §0). The same holds for Exo 16:3 "meat pots", the verse before 16:2.
  - **In the verse, the bearer's own (genuine):** Isa 29:24 "those who go astray in spirit will come to understanding, and those who murmur will accept instruction". Batch B v3 §1.6 ("The human spirit in knowing"); Batch H v1 §4.2 ("The spirit, with a way back"). **New in v3:** the two halves are parallel, so **the murmurers and the strayers-in-spirit are the same people**. Grumbling's only in-verse seat comes in its **healing**, not its act. The spirit that strayed is where understanding will come.
  - **In the next verse, the bearer's own heart, melted by others (genuine):** Deu 1:27 → 1:28 "Our brothers have made our hearts melt" (v1 §1.6). The grumbling mouth speaks for a melted heart one verse away, and the melting was done by the spies' report. This matches §13's "Where it comes from: a report".
  - **In the passage, the one who did not grumble — new in v3:** Num 14:27 ("How long shall this wicked congregation grumble against me?") comes three verses after "But my servant Caleb, because he has **a different spirit** and has followed me fully, I will bring into the land" (14:24). **The only seat in the Numbers 14 grumbling passage belongs to the one who did not grumble**, and it is named as what made him different. The seat is the other party's, and genuine.
  - **What the map shows.** The grumbling itself is carried by **quoted speech**, never by a seat. The seat appears on either side of it: **before it**, as the melted heart that the report produced (Deu 1:28), and **after it**, as the spirit that will come to understanding (Isa 29:24).

## 14. Whispering (WH) — Pro 16:28; 18:8; 26:20, 22

- **What it is.** Private, damaging speech. It is the same root as the murmuring of Deu 1:27 and Psa 106:25 (*ragan*), but **addressed to a hearer, not voiced as a complaint**.
- **What it does.**
  - It **separates**: "a whisperer separates close friends" (Pro 16:28).
  - It **feeds strife**: "where there is no whisperer, quarreling ceases" (26:20), beside "a quarrelsome man for kindling strife" (26:21).
- **Where it lands.** "The words of a whisperer are like delicious morsels; they go down into the inner parts of the body" (18:8 = 26:22). The whisperer's words enter the **hearer's** inner place, *chadrei-veten*, "the chambers of the belly", and they are **wanted**: "delicious morsels". **The inner phenomenon is appetite in the one who listens.**
- **Where it comes from.** "A worthless man plots evil, and his speech is like a scorching fire" (16:27) comes just before 16:28.
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse:** no M47 word. The inner place is *chadrei-veten*, "the chambers of the belly", **the hearer's** (v1 §2 *ragan* row; v1 §3 item 6, a seat outside the M47 list).
  - **In the previous verse, the fool's own soul — new in v3:** Pro 18:7 → 18:8 "A fool's mouth is his ruin, and his lips are a snare to his soul". The proverb pair sets **two inner places side by side**. The speaker's lips trap **his own soul** (18:7); the whisperer's words go down into **the hearer's belly** (18:8). **Speech endangers the speaker's soul and feeds the listener's inner parts.** Whispering is harmful at both ends.
  - **In the next verses, the speaker's heart — new in v3:** Pro 26:22 (the whisperer's "delicious morsels") is followed by "Like the glaze covering an earthen vessel are fervent lips with an evil heart" (26:23), and "when he speaks graciously, believe him not, for there are seven abominations in his heart" (26:25). **The whisperer's words are sweet because they are glazed. The heart behind them is evil.** The seat is the offender's, and genuine.
  - **What the map shows.** Whispering involves **three inner places**:
    1. the speaker's **evil heart**, hidden under glazed lips (26:23, 25)
    2. the speaker's **soul**, snared by his own lips (18:7)
    3. the hearer's **inner parts**, which take the words in as wanted food (18:8 = 26:22)

    It is a concealment phenomenon, like §7 and §15. **The heart is hidden behind the speech.**

## 15. The hidden trap (TR) — 9 verses

- **What it is.** A hostile purpose, **concealed** so that it can take another person.
- **The one who lays it.**
  - "They hold fast to their evil purpose; they talk of laying snares secretly, thinking, 'Who can see them?'" (Psa 64:5). The next verse gives the depth: "For the inward mind and heart of a man are deep" (64:6). **The concealment is of intent, and the heart is its hiding place.**
  - Also: "The arrogant have hidden a trap for me" (Psa 140:5); "without cause they hid their net for me" (35:7); "they have dug a pit to take me and laid snares for my feet" (Jer 18:22).
- **What it does to the one it is set for.** "When my spirit faints within me, you know my way! In the path where I walk they have hidden a trap for me" (Psa 142:3). The next verse: "no one cares for my soul" (142:4). The trap is aimed at "my life" (35:7).
- **How it co-exists: with a petition, and with a prayer of reversal.**
  - "you take me out of the net they have hidden for me, for you are my refuge" (Psa 31:4)
  - "let the net that he hid ensnare him" (35:8)
  - "The nations have sunk in the pit that they made; in the net that they hid, their own foot has been caught" (9:15)
  - "A rope is hidden for him in the ground" (Job 18:10), of the wicked
- **What it leads to.** The trap turns on its maker (Psa 9:15; 35:8), or God is the refuge from it (31:4). **The seat appears on both sides:** the trapper's deep heart (64:6), and the victim's fainting spirit and threatened life (142:3; 35:7).
- **Seat.**
  - **In the verse, the victim's own (genuine):** Psa 142:3 "When my spirit faints within me … they have hidden a trap for me". Batch A v3 §7 (Faintness & Despair, M24). → 142:4 "no one cares for my soul" (v2).
  - **In the verse, the victim's life (life-sense):** Psa 35:7 "without cause they dug a pit for my life". *Nephesh* here is the **life aimed at**, in its life sense. It passes the check as life, not as an inner faculty. **New in v3:** this verse was not read in the M47 batches. It is the same use as Batch G v3 §5.3 ("Hunting souls").
  - **In the next verse, the trapper's (genuine):** Psa 64:5 → 64:6 "the inward mind and heart of a man are deep" (v2). **The heart is where the trap is first hidden**, before the net is hidden in the path. **New in v3**, a second instance: Psa 140:5 ("The arrogant have hidden a trap for me") follows "who plan evil things in their heart and stir up wars continually" (140:2). The trappers' heart is again where the plan starts.
  - **Life-sense, in the passage:** Jer 18:22 ("they have dug a pit to take me") comes two verses after "Yet they have dug a pit for my life. Remember how I stood before you to speak good for them, to turn away your wrath from them" (18:20). *Nephesh* is the life aimed at, as in Psa 35:7. **New in v3:** the verse adds what makes the hidden trap bitter. **It is set by those the victim once interceded for.** Good repaid with evil, as in Psa 55:12–13 (§2 g), where the betrayer is the familiar friend.
  - **In the next verse, the victim's soul after the reversal — new in v3:** Psa 35:8 ("let the net that he hid ensnare him") → 35:9 "Then my soul will rejoice in the Lord, exulting in his salvation". **The soul the pit was dug for (35:7) is the soul that rejoices when the trap turns on its maker (35:9).** The seat is the same in the threat and in the rescue.
  - **In the next verses, the victim's own — new in v3:** Psa 31:4 → 31:5 "Into your hand I commit my spirit; you have redeemed me, O Lord, faithful God", and → 31:7 "you have seen my affliction; you have known the distress of my soul". **The one taken out of the hidden net commits his spirit into God's hand.** The next verses then say that the soul's distress has been **known** by God. Being known (§6) answers the concealment of the trap. Batch E v2 §1.7 reads Psa 31:5 as the spirit committed at death. Here the committal comes in the middle of danger, not at death.
  - **What the map shows.** This phenomenon has **three seats in three hands**: the trapper's heart (the hiding place of the intent), the victim's spirit (fainting, then committed), and God's knowing of the victim's soul.

## 16. The sluggard's buried hand (SL) — Pro 19:24; 26:15

"The sluggard buries his hand in the dish and will not even bring it back to his mouth" (19:24); "it wears him out to bring it back to his mouth" (26:15). **The inner inertia is shown only in the hand.** The weariness is described at the smallest effort, eating. It belongs with M67 (Sloth), under the same word *taman*, "hide". The hand is hidden in the dish.

- **Seat.** None, in the verse or in the passage as read (v1 §2 *taman* row; §3 item 2, "the act carries it").

## 17. No inner phenomenon (NO) — 26 verses

| Reason | Verses |
|---|---|
| "Cut off / destroy" (*kachad*) | Exo 9:15; 23:23; 1Ki 13:34; 2Ch 32:21; Job 4:7; 15:28; 22:20; Psa 83:4; Zec 11:8, 9, 16 |
| Objects hidden or laid up | Gen 35:4; Deu 33:19; Job 20:26; Jer 43:9, 10 |
| Prophetic sign-act (the loincloth) | Jer 13:4, 5, 6, 7 |

| A plight, with no inner state stated | Isa 42:22 ("hidden in prisons") |
| A gift | Rev 2:17 (hidden manna) |

◆ Two of these verses have inner content that **does not belong to the M20 word**:
- Zec 11:8, "I became impatient with them, and they also detested me"
- Gen 35:4: Jacob buries the foreign gods; the renunciation is set up in 35:2

Both are recorded, not read further here.

- **Seat.**
  - **Spurious:**
    - Zec 11:9 and 11:16 "flesh": flesh as food, "devour the flesh of one another"; "devours the flesh of the fat ones" (v1 §2 *kachad* row; Batch A v3 R6).
    - Rev 2:17 "Spirit": the divine Spirit speaking to the churches (v1 §0). Batch E v2 §11 read the verse for its "new name", not the seat.
  - **Genuine, but not the M20 word's:** Zec 11:8 "I" and "they" are *nephesh* twice: "my soul was short with them, and their soul loathed me". Batch F v4 §4 ("Mutual detestation between shepherd and flock"). The inner-being content is real and belongs to both parties. The M20 word in the verse (*kachad*, "destroyed") is the cut-off sense and carries none of it. **The verse is kept in NO.** The seat is recorded against the verse, not the phenomenon.
  - **New in v3, a seat arriving in the interpretation of a sign-act:** the loincloth verses (Jer 13:4–7) are a sign-act with no inner state. Its interpretation names one: "This evil people, who refuse to hear my words, who stubbornly follow their own heart … shall be like this loincloth, which is good for nothing" (Jer 13:10). **The hidden, spoiled loincloth stands for a people whose heart goes its own way.** The seat is the people's, and genuine, but it belongs to the sign's meaning, not to the hiding. **No ledger change.** The four verses stay NO, because the M20 word describes the prophet's act with the cloth. The interpretation is recorded here as a pointer to M30 (stubbornness of heart; Batch C v3 §9.1).

## 18. How the phenomena co-exist: what the verses themselves put together

These sequences are all **inside single passages**. None is built across the cluster.

| Passage | Sequence the text gives |
|---|---|
| Gen 3:7–12 | eyes opened, knowing nakedness, → hearing God → fear → hiding → being called → blame |
| Jos 7:19–22 | seeing → coveting → taking → hiding → demand to disclose → confession → found |

## 19. On the researcher's question: is this too ambitious?

- **For M20 it was possible** to read at phenomenon level verse by verse. 16 phenomena emerged, plus 26 verses with none.
- **The risk the researcher named, brushing over detail, was greatest in the two large phenomena** (hiding from danger, 40 verses; anxiety, 40 verses). I handled it by letting **strands** emerge within them, with verses listed per strand, rather than writing a summary sentence over the whole group.
- **M02 is 2.5 times the size** (681 verses). Most of it is one divine-subject series, "the anger of the Lord was kindled", whose human side (§5 here is the parallel) needs reading as its own phenomenon.

  **Proposal:** take M02 in two units, human anger's phenomena first and divine anger as borne by the human second, with a researcher review between them. The alternative, one pass at this depth, is where the brushing-over would start.
- **Clusters are loose, as expected.** M20's own description covers 60 of its 275 verses. The concealment family (§1–§8, §15) would repay reading alongside whatever other clusters hold the same verbs.
