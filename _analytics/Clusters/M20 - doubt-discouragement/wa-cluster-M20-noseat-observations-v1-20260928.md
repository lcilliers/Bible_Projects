**Question (researcher, verbatim):** "are there inner being related operations that takes place without a explicit, or implicit through cross - correlation, context that is related to the inner being."
**Data:** `wa-cluster-M20-noseat-verses-v1-20260928.csv` (this folder). One row per verse, with the cluster Strong's, other codes, parties, any seat word within ±3 verses, and the implicit-by-strong flag.

## 0. What was read

- **275** verses carry an M20 word. **30** of them also carry an M47 seat word, and those are the M47 cross-cluster set, read in Batches A–H. The other **245** verses have no seat word. All of them were read, and the context of the key passages was read as well.
- **Implicit seat (researcher, 2026-09-28):** the seat is implicit when *"another verse for the same strong established a seat"*.
- **Refinement applied:** the link counts only where the other verse's seat word is used **in an inner-being sense**. Three M20 Strong's are "seated" only through links that are not:

  | Strong's | Its only "seat" link | Why it does not count |
  |---|---|---|
  | *lun* H3885B, grumble | Exo 16:8, "meat" (*basar*) | flesh as food |
  | *chava* H2244, hide | Gen 3:8, "cool" (*ruach* = the day's wind) | wind |
  | | 2Ch 22:9, "heart" | the heart is Jehoshaphat's, not the one hiding |
  | *kruptō* G2928, hide | Rev 2:17, "Spirit" | the divine Spirit speaking, not the hider |

  These three are treated as **never seated** below.
- **What counts as inner being:** this file uses the programme's own definition (prose section 5, "Defining Inner Being", which `cfg_prose_concept.inner_being_definition` points to). Inner-being characteristics are the *non-physical, internal states, capacities and expressions* of a person: thinking, feeling, choosing, relating, and orienting toward meaning, others and God. **The non-physical test:** a bodily or outward act counts only when it expresses an inner state. **The subject is the human inner being** (prose section 1090, and the `hib.set` non-human-scope rule): where God is the one who acts, the inner-being question moves to the human party. **`phenomenon.set` hidden-behind-act:** an inner state may sit behind a stated act, with only the act written.
- **One tag note:** most of M20's no-seat vocabulary is **concealment** (*satar, chava, taman, kachad, kruptō*: about 180 of the 245 verses). In most of those verses the word names an outward act (hiding a person, an object or a trap) or, for *kachad*, "cut off / destroy". The tag "Doubt & Discouragement" does not describe them, and that should be kept in mind when reading §2.

## 1. Strong's with no seat anywhere — observations

### 1.1 Perplexity: *aporeō* G0639, *diaporeō* G1280, *she·vash* H7672 (11 verses)

- **What it is.** Knowing that cannot close: a mind held open before something it cannot interpret. The bearers are all human: Herod (Mar 6:20; Luk 9:7), the disciples (Joh 13:22), the women at the tomb (Luk 24:4), the Pentecost crowd (Act 2:12), the temple captain and chief priests (Act 5:24), Peter (Act 10:17), Paul (2Cor 4:8; Gal 4:20), Festus (Act 25:20), and Belshazzar's lords (Dan 5:9).
- **What carries it instead of a seat:**
  - **the question spoken aloud:** "What does this mean?" (Act 2:12); "who is this about whom I hear such things?" (Luk 9:9, context)
  - **the gaze:** "The disciples looked at one another" (Joh 13:22)
  - **a reflexive pronoun:** Act 10:17 "inwardly perplexed" is *en heautō*, "in himself". The inner location is there, but through a pronoun, not a seat word.
  - **the body of the other party:** in Dan 5:6 the king's "color changed … his limbs gave way, and his knees knocked together". The body tells the alarm while his lords are perplexed.
- **Before and after.** Perplexity is a **waiting state that something from outside ends**:
  - Luk 24:4, "While they were perplexed … behold, two men stood by them"
  - Act 10:17–19: the men arrive at the gate, and the Spirit speaks
  - Act 2:12–14: "But Peter, standing … lifted up his voice"
  - Dan 5:10–12: the queen names Daniel

  The operation rarely resolves from within. An interpreter arrives.
- **Implicit context.** Some of these verses stand inside passages that name the seat of someone else, not the perplexed person. In Joh 13:21, "Jesus was troubled in his spirit" in the verse before the disciples' uncertainty: **the seat is Jesus's, and the perplexity is theirs.** In 2Cor 4:6, "God … has shone in our hearts", two verses before "perplexed, but not driven to despair". The inner frame there is set by the passage (see also 2Cor 4:16).
- **How the sense shifts.** In Mar 6:20, Herod "feared John, knowing … greatly perplexed, and yet he heard him gladly". Four inner operations sit on one bearer with no seat word, each named by a word from a different cluster (fear M01, knowing M15, gladness M04). **The operations name themselves.** In Gal 4:20 the perplexity is **love's**: it comes straight after "my little children, for whom I am again in the anguish of childbirth" (4:19).
- ◆ **Fringe.** Act 25:20 "being at a loss how to investigate": Festus's perplexity is procedural, close to the outward edge.

### 1.2 Doubt: *distazō* G1365 (2 verses)

- **Mat 14:31.** "O you of little faith, why did you doubt?" In the context (14:30), "when he saw the wind, he was afraid, and beginning to sink". **The body enacts the doubt.** Peter sinks as the trust fails. Seeing leads to fear, which leads to sinking. After the rescue comes worship (14:33).
- **Mat 28:17.** "they worshiped him, but some doubted". Worship and doubt are set side by side in the same company, with no seat and no resolution given; the Great Commission follows directly.
- **Compare the one seated resurrection doubt:** in Luk 24:38 (an M63 word, so outside this set), "why do doubts arise in your hearts?". Doubt gets its seat **when Jesus names it from outside**. When the narrator records it, it has none.

### 1.3 Words that carry the seat inside them: *dipsuchos* G1374, *athumeō* G0120, *alupos* G0253, *ka·eh* H3512B

- ***Dipsuchos*, "double-minded"** (Jam 1:8), is literally *two-souled*: the *psychē* is inside the compound. Its one seated occurrence, Jam 4:8 ("purify your hearts, you double-minded"), is a genuine link. In 1:6 (context) doubt is "like a wave of the sea … driven and tossed by the wind", and 1:8 ends "unstable in all his ways". The divided soul shows up as an unsteady walk.
- ***Athumeō*, "be discouraged"** (Col 3:21), is literally *without thumos*, "dis-spirited". It is paired with its cause: "Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged." This is **relational causation**: one person's provoking produces the other's loss of heart. Col 3:22 (context) turns to "sincerity of heart".
- ***Alupos*** (Phili 2:28, "less anxious") is literally *without lupē*, grief. Philippians 2:26–28 chains inner states across three people with no seat word:
  - Epaphroditus "longing for you all and … distressed because you heard that he was ill"
  - Paul spared "sorrow upon sorrow"
  - the Philippians who will "rejoice"
  - Paul who will be "less anxious"

  **The relationships carry the feeling.**
- ***Ka·eh*** (Psa 10:10, "the helpless") comes from the root that is seated in Psa 109:16, "brokenhearted". Here it names the victim class. The next verse (10:11) has the oppressor "says in his heart". The seat in this passage is the **oppressor's**, and the victim is seen only from outside.
- **Observation.** Hebrew and Greek can **build the seat into the word itself**. A search for a separate seat word misses these.

### 1.4 Anxiety, once: *meteōrizō* G3349 (Luk 12:29)

"Nor be worried" (literally "held in suspense, up in the air"). The seat was set at the head of the discourse: Luk 12:22 (seated) reads "do not be anxious about your life (*psychē*)". **Every later anxiety in the discourse inherits it.** This is the clearest case of a **discourse-level seat**: stated once, then assumed. The same happens in Mat 6:25 → 6:27–34 (§2).

### 1.5 Hiding: *chavah* H2247, *se·tar* H5642A, *perikruptō* G4032, *parakaluptō* G3871 — and *chava* H2244, *kruptō* G2928, whose links are spurious (together about 55 verses)

- **Mostly outward.** People and things are hidden: spies, kings in a cave, Joash in a bedroom, an idol-cache, a talent in the ground. There is no inner movement stated. These verses **fail the non-physical test**.
- **Where inner being is present, it sits behind the act** (`phenomenon.set` hidden-behind-act), and the motive is named by *another cluster's* word:
  - **fear:** Gen 3:10 ("I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself"); Mat 25:25 ("I was afraid, and I went and hid your talent"); Joh 19:38 ("secretly for fear of the Jews"); Dan 10:7 ("a great trembling fell upon them, and they fled to hide themselves"); 1Ch 21:20 (Ornan's sons hid on seeing the angel)
  - **shame or withdrawal:** Luk 1:24, Elizabeth "kept herself hidden". The motive follows in 1:25: "to take away my reproach among people".

  **Hiding is the body's answer to fear.** The seat is replaced by the act together with the fear word.
- **Hiding acting on the knowing faculty.** Luk 9:45: "it was concealed from them, so that they might not perceive it. And they were afraid to ask him". It is followed at once by 9:46, "An argument arose among them as to which of them was the greatest". The sequence runs: **perception blocked from outside, then fear of asking, then rivalry.** Three inner operations follow in turn, with no seat. Mat 11:25 and Luk 19:42 ("hidden from your eyes") are the same operation; there the eye stands in for the knowing faculty.
- **Divine hiding.** Dan 2:22 ("he reveals deep and hidden things") has God as the bearer. The inner-being question moves to the human side, and the verse does not state it.

### 1.6 Grumbling: *lun* H3885B, 14 verses (spurious link), with *ragan* H7279 treated in §2

- **What it is.** A **collective** bearer: the congregation, the people, "all the congregation". The operation is carried almost entirely by **quoted speech**, and the speech exposes what is inside:
  - a death-wish: "Would that we had died in the land of Egypt!" (Num 14:2)
  - fear for dependants: "Our wives and our little ones will become a prey" (14:3)
  - a verdict on God's heart: "Because the Lord hated us he has brought us out" (Deu 1:27, *ragan*)
- **Before and after.**
  - Num 13:33 → 14:1–4: "we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers" (a **self-perception**, an inner act), then "the people wept that night", then grumbling, then "Let us choose a leader and go back to Egypt".
  - Deu 1:27 → 1:28: the seat arrives **as the cause**, in the next clause: "Our brothers have made our hearts melt."

  **The grumbling mouth speaks for a melted heart one verse away.**
- **How the target shifts.** The grumbling is aimed at Moses and Aaron, and it is **re-addressed** by them: "What are we? Your grumbling is not against us but against the Lord" (Exo 16:7–8; Num 16:11). The inner operation is re-aimed by an outside interpreter.
- ◆ **Fringe.** Psa 59:15, enemies "growl if they do not get their fill". The enemies are depicted as dogs; it is an animal image.

## 3. What carries the inner being when there is no seat (M20)

1. **The operation word names itself.** *Perplexed, doubted, anxious, discouraged* need no seat. The seat is added **when someone diagnoses** (Luk 24:38) or **sets the frame of a discourse** (Mat 6:25; Luk 12:22).
2. **The act carries it.** Hiding for fear; the sluggard's buried hand (§1.5; §2 *taman*).
3. **The body carries it.** Peter sinking; Belshazzar's knees; bread eaten with trembling (§1.2; §1.1; §2).
4. **Speech carries it.** The grumblers' quoted words; "What does this mean?"; the father's "What shall I do about my son?" (§1.6; §1.1; §2).
5. **The seat is inside the word.** *Dipsuchos*, *athumeō*, *alupos*, and brokenhearted *ka'ah* (§1.3).
6. **Inner places named outside M47:**
   - "bosom" H2243 T2 (Job 31:33: ESV "heart" is *chob*)
   - "chambers of the belly" (Pro 18:8)
   - "in himself" (Act 10:17)

   These are **seats the M47 list does not hold.**
7. **The passage carries it.** The seat is stated once and inherited (Luk 12; Mat 6), stated as the cause in the next clause (Deu 1:28), or given as the passage's frame (Jer 17:5–10; 2Cor 4:6, 16).
8. **Another party's seat.** Jesus troubled in spirit while the disciples are perplexed (Joh 13:21–22); the oppressor's heart and the victim's crushing (Psa 10:10–11); God hides his face and **the human is dismayed** (Psa 30:7).
9. **No inner operation at all.** Physical concealment of objects and people, and "cut off". This is the largest single group, and the tag does not describe it.

**Answer to the key question (M20):**
- **Yes, inner-being operations occur with no explicit seat.** They are carried by forms 1–5 above, and they are most secure in the perplexity, doubt and discouragement words, which are M20's own core. Among the Strong's that never carry a seat in the corpus (§1), the bearer is always human, and the operation is shown by **what the person says, does, or undergoes in the body**. It is never placed in an organ.
- **The implicit (cross-correlated) seat is real, but it needs the sense to match.** It holds for "God hides his face", "hidden in my heart", "anxious about your life", and "anxiety in a man's heart". It does not hold for the same words used of objects, places or destruction.
