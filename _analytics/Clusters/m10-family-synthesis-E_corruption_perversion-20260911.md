## Finding: two members are pure gloss-homonym noise, not a shared concept

- **H2342J/H2342K (chul, 9 verses combined)** — glossed "to twist: dance" / "to twist: anticipate."
  Every verse is literal: a woman dancing (2 Sam 3:29's negated wish), waiting anxiously (Ps 37:7,
  Mic 1:12, Judg 3:25), a dove sent out and waited for (Gen 8:10). None describe moral perversion.
  The root's full range (dance, whirl, writhe in labor, wait) has nothing to do with corruption —
  it was pulled into this family purely because one of its many senses translates as "twist."
- **H7806 (shazar, 19 verses — the family's single largest strong)** — glossed "to twist," but
  every one of its 19 citations is literal tabernacle-construction material: "fine twined linen,"
  repeated almost verbatim through Exodus 26-39 (curtains, the ephod, the breastpiece, the veil).
  This is technical textile vocabulary, not moral language at all.

Together these two homonym clusters account for 28 of the family's ~98 total verses (≈29%) —
a substantial share of the family's raw volume carries zero thematic content. This is the same
kind of gloss-text-grouping failure already flagged for family R (Sin/Shin) and G0073 in family I
(agōn/"fight") — a `cluster_strong` membership question, separate from meaning-distillation.

## The genuine core: a literal crooked/bent-body metaphor for moral perversion, textually explicit

Excluding the homonym noise, this family shows the clearest, most consistently *literal* metaphor
of any family surveyed: the physical image of bending, twisting, or making crooked is applied to
moral deviation directly and repeatedly, in both languages:

- **H5753A/B (avah) makes the metaphor's two halves visible in one root split into two codes**:
  the *physical* sense (bent, bowed down in pain/anguish — Isa 21:3, Ps 38:6) and the *moral* sense
  (confession formula "we have sinned and acted perversely," 1 Kgs 8:47, 2 Chr 6:37, Dan 9:5) are
  literally the same Hebrew word, split here only for cataloguing purposes.
- **The justice/judiciary sub-theme recurs strongly**: H5557 (salaph) and H5791 (avat) are both
  explicitly about bribery and perverting legal judgment — "a bribe... subverts the cause of the
  righteous" (Exod 23:8, Deut 16:19), "does God pervert justice?" (Job 8:3, 34:12) — and H6127/
  H6140 apply the same "crooked" language directly to justice itself ("justice goes forth
  perverted," Hab 1:4; "make crooked all that is straight," Mic 3:9). **This is the third family
  (after D and G) to independently converge on judicial/legal-fairness vocabulary** — corruption
  of justice specifically, not just wrongdoing in general.
- **G1294 (diastrephō)** carries the identical figure into Greek, applied collectively: "faithless
  and twisted generation" (Matt 17:17, Luke 9:41), "crooked and twisted generation" (Phil 2:15) —
  the moral-deviation-as-physical-distortion figure applied to a whole people, not just an
  individual, and its own LSJ entry confirms the base sense is genuinely physical (distorted eyes,
  limbs, warped wood) before any moral extension.
- **Prov 10:9/28:18's "walks in integrity... crooked in his ways"** and Eccl 1:15's "what is
  crooked cannot be made straight" state the straight/crooked = righteous/wicked mapping almost as
  a proverb-form axiom, the most direct statement of the metaphor found in this exercise.
