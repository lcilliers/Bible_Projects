## Alternative / candidate meanings

The English surface shifts the register from an internal state ('guilty') to a forensic outcome ('condemned'), and the verse pairing itself frames guilt/condemnation as the fate specifically avoided by refuge in God, not merely an abstract state.

## Differences and inferences

- Across enochos's 7 verses (9 span occurrences), the word spans from a fully literal 'held/bound' sense (Heb 2:15, bondage to fear) to a fully forensic 'liable to penalty X' sense (Matt 5:21-22, 26:66, Mark 3:29, Jas 2:10) to a cultic-propriety sense (1Cor 11:27). This is not one meaning applied uniformly — it is one root image (being held/bound) realised in three distinct registers depending on what is doing the holding: a legal system, a psychological state, or a sacred practice.
  *[strong=G1777]*

- asham's surface rendering in Lev 5:7 is 'sin' (not 'guilt' or 'guilt offering'), in a verse listing sin-offering and burnt-offering alongside it — showing the same Hebrew guilt-offering vocabulary sits directly beside, and is translated interchangeably with, the separate chattah ('sin offering') system documented in family B — the two offering-systems' vocabulary is not kept lexically distinct in the English rendering even where the underlying Hebrew terms differ.
  *[strong=H0817 · verse=Lev.5.7 · related_family=B_sin]*

- Ezekiel 18:19-20 uses the identical verb (nasa, 'suffer/bear') to state the opposite direction of transfer from Lev 16:22 and Isa 53:12: 'the son shall not [bear/suffer for] the iniquity of the father' — explicitly denying transferability of guilt between generations, in the same lexical family that elsewhere affirms substitutionary transfer (scapegoat, Servant). The data does not resolve this tension; both uses are equally attested by the same word.
