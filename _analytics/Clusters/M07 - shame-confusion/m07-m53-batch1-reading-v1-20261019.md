## 1. Sennacherib: 2Ki 19:1–37; Isa 37 (the same account); 2Ch 32:9–22

**The passage.** The Rabshakeh's speech has been heard (2Ki 18). Hezekiah "tore his clothes and covered himself with sackcloth and went into the house of the Lord" (19:1). His message: "This day is a day of distress, of rebuke, and of disgrace" (19:3). Isaiah answers: "Do not be afraid because of the words that you have heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have reviled me" (19:6). A letter comes; Hezekiah "spread it before the Lord" and prays (19:14–19). The Lord answers with an oracle against Sennacherib (19:21–28), and the camp is struck (19:35). 2Ch 32 tells the same campaign with the purpose of the shouting stated (32:18) and the end: "he returned with shame of face to his own land" (32:21).

**Earlier readings:** 2Ch 32:10–13 in 10.9 *Knowing who rules* (C-298); 32:18 in 10.5 *Fear and the mouth*; 2Ki 19:28 in Ch 11 §4; 19:30–31 and Isa 37:31–32 in 13.1 *Jealousy*. 2Ki 19:22–23 and Isa 37:23–24 were decided in the M08 strand (the boast of the heights). None of the set verses is cited.

**The original.** "Mock" in 19:4, 16, 22, 23 is *ḥārap̄* (M53, Piel). "Reviled" in 19:6 and 19:22 is *gādap̄* (M58, "blaspheme"). "Disgrace" in 19:3 is *nᵊʾāṣāh* (M06, "contempt"). "Cast contempt" in 2Ch 32:17 is *ḥārap̄*. "Your raging against me" (19:27–28) is *rāgaz* (M01), the verb that elsewhere is "tremble" and "quake" (Hithpael here). "Shame of face" (2Ch 32:21) is *bōšet* (M07 group B, not in this set).

### 2Ki 19:4 / Isa 37:4: "... whom his master the king of Assyria has sent to mock the living God ..."

- **What the verse carries (§4.0):** a mocking sent by one king through a servant; God named as "the living God" and as the one who "heard"; the hope that God "will rebuke the words"; and the call to "lift up your prayer for the remnant".
- **Main IB operation:** Hezekiah's reading of the speech: it was sent to mock the living God. His response is prayer, asked of the prophet.
- **Roles:** "mock" (M53) is the act named; "living" (M25) qualifies God; "heard", "words", "prayer" (M41, M65, M42) carry the response: God hears the words, the people pray.
- **Face:** mocking aimed at God through his people, sent by a third party.
- **Parties:** Sennacherib (sender), the Rabshakeh (mouth), God (target and hearer), Hezekiah and the remnant. Routes A and B.

### 2Ki 19:16 / Isa 37:17: "Incline your ear, O Lord, and hear; open your eyes, O Lord, and see; and hear the words of Sennacherib, which he has sent to mock the living God."

- **Main IB operation:** laying the mockery before God (19:14: the letter "spread ... before the Lord"). Hezekiah asks God to hear and see it.
- **Roles:** "mock" (M53) is what is laid before God; "hear" (M41) is God's act asked for. Ear and eyes (T14) are asked of God.
- **Relational observation:** the answer to mockery here is not a reply to the mocker. It is a prayer that God hear the mockery. **I read** this beside Psa 69:19 ("You know my reproach"): in both, the one under it brings it to God. The two verses state their own acts; the comparison is mine.
- **Purpose stated:** "that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you, O Lord, are God alone" (19:19).
- **Parties:** Hezekiah, God, Sennacherib. Routes A and B.

### 2Ki 19:22–23 / Isa 37:23–24: "Whom have you mocked and reviled? ... Against the Holy One of Israel! By your messengers you have mocked the Lord, and you have said, 'With my many chariots I have gone up the heights ...'"

- **Earlier reading:** decided in M08 (the heights; pride over God). This reading builds on it.
- **Main IB operation:** God names the mockery as directed at himself, and quotes the mocker's own boast back.
- **Roles:** "mocked" (M53) and "reviled" (M58) are the acts; "raised your voice and lifted your eyes to the heights" (M22, M08) qualifies them: the mocking is done from a lifted-up stance. The boast that follows (19:23–24) is what the mocking says.
- **Relational observation:** in this oracle the mocking of God and the boast of the self are one speech. **I read** the mocking as carried by the boast: to say "I have gone up the heights" is to set oneself above the one mocked. The verse sets them side by side; the link is my reading.
- **What the passage adds:** the reversal begins before the judgment: "She despises you, she scorns you— the virgin daughter of Zion; she wags her head behind you" (19:21). The one mocked is given the mocker's own gestures. And God says, "I know your sitting down and your going out and coming in, and your raging against me" (19:27). **I read** "raging" (*rāgaz*, a trembling word) as the inner side of the mockery, known to God.
- **Pointer:** 2Ki 19:26 / Isa 37:27, "dismayed and confounded" (M07 group A), is one of the 8 M72 forwards to M07, two verses on. It is said of the inhabitants of the cities Assyria destroyed.
- **Parties:** God (speaking), Sennacherib (mocker), the daughter of Zion. Routes A and B.

### 2Ch 32:17: "And he wrote letters to cast contempt on the Lord, the God of Israel, and to speak against him ..."

- **Main IB operation:** contempt of God written down and sent. The next verse gives the purpose of the shouting: "to frighten and terrify them, in order that they might take the city" (32:18).
- **Roles:** "cast contempt" (M53 *ḥārap̄*) is the act; "deliver" (M45) and "hand" (M72) are its content: the claim that God cannot deliver.
- **Relational observation:** the passage states that the words against God were aimed at the hearers' fear (32:18). **I read** the mocking of God as a means here, with the people's fear as its target. 32:18 is told in 10.5 *Fear and the mouth*; the mocking that carries it is not.
- **The end:** "he returned with shame of face to his own land" (32:21). The mocker of 32:17 goes home ashamed, and is killed in the house of his own god.
- **Parties:** Sennacherib, his servants, God, the people on the wall, Hezekiah. Routes A and B.

**Sennacherib as a passage.** Mockery of God sent through servants, letters and shouting; its stated aim is to make the hearers afraid (2Ch 32:18). Hezekiah's response: sackcloth, the house of the Lord, the letter spread before God, prayer (2Ki 19:1, 14–19). God's word to the people: "Do not be afraid" (19:6). God names himself as the one mocked (19:22), knows the mocker's raging (19:27), turns the scorn back (19:21), and the mocker goes home with shame of face (2Ch 32:21). Hezekiah calls the day one "of disgrace" (2Ki 19:3, a contempt word, M06).

## 2. The giants and the mighty men

### 2Sa 21:21 / 1Ch 20:7: "And when he taunted Israel, Jonathan the son of Shimei, David's brother, struck him down."

- **The passage:** a list of four giants of Gath and the men who struck them (2Sa 21:18–22; 1Ch 20:4–8). This one had six fingers and six toes (21:20).
- **Main IB operation:** a taunt, answered by a strike. There is no other M-code.
- **Face:** the taunt as the giant's act of war, as with Goliath (1Sa 17:10). The verse records the taunt and the answer and nothing of the inner state.
- **Route:** B, thinly. **I read** these two as data for the account beside 1Sa 17, which carries the inner side. They are kept as the same face, not a new one.

### 2Sa 23:9: "He was with David when they defied the Philistines who were gathered there for battle, and the men of Israel withdrew."

- **The original:** the subject of *ḥārap̄* here is David's side: "when they defied the Philistines". It is Israel's taunt against the enemy.
- **What the passage carries:** the men of Israel "withdrew", and Eleazar "rose and struck down the Philistines until his hand was weary, and his hand clung to the sword. And the Lord brought about a great victory that day" (23:10).
- **Face:** the taunt turned outward by God's people, before a battle in which most of them withdrew. **I read** the defiance and the withdrawal as standing in the same verse without comment.
- **Pointer:** "his hand was weary, and his hand clung to the sword" (23:10) is the body at its limit, with the victory credited to the Lord (signpost #1893, hands; flesh-and-body).
- **Parties:** David, Eleazar, the men of Israel, the Philistines, the Lord. Routes A and B.

## 3. Nehemiah

**The passage set.** Nehemiah hears of Jerusalem (Neh 1), rebuilds the wall against Sanballat and Tobiah (Neh 2–6), and deals with the nobles' exaction (Neh 5).

**Earlier readings:** Neh 1:5, 9, 11 in 10.9, 10.7, 13.1 and Ch 11; Neh 2:17–18 in 10.10 and 10.12 (C-249, resolve given by another); Neh 4:1–2 in 10.15 *The mock*; 4:6 in Ch 15; Neh 5:6–7 in Ch 4, 10.0, 10.1, 10.13, 10.2 and 10.8; Neh 6:13 in 10.12, 10.8 and Ch 12.

### Neh 1:3: "The remnant there in the province who had survived the exile is in great trouble and shame. The wall of Jerusalem is broken down ..."

- **Main IB operation:** a people's condition reported to one far away: "great trouble and shame" (*ḥerpāh*). The broken wall and burned gates are named in the same breath.
- **What it does (1:4):** "As soon as I heard these words I sat down and wept and mourned for days, and I continued fasting and praying before the God of heaven." Then a confession: "we have sinned against you. Even I and my father's house have sinned" (1:6–7).
- **Roles:** "shame" (M07) is the characteristic of the remnant's condition; "trouble" (M24) stands beside it.
- **Face:** reproach as a people's condition, reported, and taken on by one who is not in it. **I read** Nehemiah's weeping and confession as his taking the reproach of his people as his own ("we have sinned"). The verses state the acts; the "taking on" is my reading.
- **Parties:** the remnant, Nehemiah, God, the messengers. Routes A and B.

### Neh 2:17: "You see the trouble we are in ... Come, let us build the wall of Jerusalem, that we may no longer suffer derision."

- **Earlier reading:** told in 10.10 (C-249: what one tells of God's dealing gives others resolve).
- **The original:** "that we may no longer suffer derision" is "that we may no longer be a reproach" (*ḥerpāh*).
- **Main IB operation:** a resolve to act, so that a reproach will end. The reproach is the stated reason for building.
- **Face:** reproach as something to be ended by work. What follows: "they jeered at us and despised us" (2:19), and Nehemiah's answer: "The God of heaven will make us prosper ... but you have no portion or right or claim in Jerusalem" (2:20).
- **What 10.10 does not carry:** the reproach as the reason for the building. **I read** it as part of the resolve C-249 describes.
- **Parties:** Nehemiah, the people, Sanballat and his allies. Route B.

### Neh 4:4: "Hear, O our God, for we are despised. Turn back their taunt on their own heads and give them up to be plundered in a land where they are captives."

- **The passage:** Sanballat "was angry and greatly enraged, and he jeered at the Jews" (4:1); "What are these feeble Jews doing?" (4:2; told in 10.15 *The mock*); Tobiah's fox (4:3). Then this prayer, and: "So we built the wall ... for the people had a mind to work" (4:6). The Hebrew of "had a mind" is "the people had a heart" (*lēḇ*, M47) to work.
- **Main IB operation:** under taunt, a prayer that the taunt return on the taunters. "Despised" is *bûzāh* (M06); "taunt" is *ḥerpāh* (M07).
- **Roles:** "taunt" (M07) is what is to be turned back; "despised" (M06) names the state of those praying; "hear" (M41) is asked of God.
- **Relational observation:** the prayer asks for a return: "Turn back ... on their own heads". **I read** this beside 10.4 *What is devised comes back* (the same "on his own head" in Psa 7:16 and Est 9:25), as a prayer that what was aimed at them come back. The verses state their own returns; the comparison is mine.
- **What the passage adds:** the work went on: "So we built the wall" (4:6). **I read** the prayer and the work as standing together in the passage: the taunt was handed to God, and the heart went to the work.
- **Parties:** Nehemiah and the builders, God, Sanballat and Tobiah. Routes A and B.

### Neh 5:9: "The thing that you are doing is not good. Ought you not to walk in the fear of our God to prevent the taunts of the nations our enemies?"

- **Earlier reading:** Neh 5:6–7 (Nehemiah's anger, and "I took counsel with myself", literally "my heart took counsel within me") is told in Ch 4, 10.13 and other places. 5:9 is not cited.
- **Main IB operation:** a reproof: walking in the fear of God is set against giving the enemies cause to taunt.
- **Roles:** "fear" (M01) is the characteristic: the fear of God as the way to walk. "Taunts" (M07) is what that walk prevents. "Good" (M18) is the measure of the act ("not good").
- **Relational observation:** the verse joins the fear of God to the taunts of the nations: how God's people treat one another (5:7–8, exacting interest from brothers, selling them) becomes the enemies' cause to reproach. **I read** the reproach here as earned by conduct, unlike the reproach of Neh 1:3 and 2:17, which comes from ruin. The verse states the link between the walk and the taunts; the contrast with Neh 1–2 is mine.
- **What the passage adds:** "They were silent and could not find a word to say" (5:8), and then, "We will restore these and require nothing from them" (5:12).
- **Face:** reproach that can be prevented by the fear of God.
- **Parties:** Nehemiah, the nobles and officials, the poor brothers, the nations, God. Routes A and B.

### Neh 6:13: "For this purpose he was hired, that I should be afraid and act in this way and sin, and so they could give me a bad name in order to taunt me."

- **Earlier reading:** told in 10.12 (*Fear used as a tool*, *Fear between people*), 10.8 (opening) and Ch 12 (*Fear that goes wrong*). All three quote the verse only to "and sin". 10.12 says: "The fear was the means, and the sin was the aim."
- **The verse in full:** the purpose runs on: fear, then the act, then sin, then a bad name (*šēm rāʿ*), then the taunt (*ḥārap̄*, M53). The verse states the chain with its own purpose words ("for this purpose", "that", "in order to").
- **Main IB operation:** a plan to bring a man to reproach through his own fear. The fear is planted; the sin would follow from it; the bad name from the sin; and the taunt is the end.
- **Roles:** "afraid" (M01) is the means; "sin" (M56) the step; "bad name" (M58 with *šēm*) and "taunt" (M53) the end.
- **Correction (rule 77; `feedback_role_label_never_licenses_dropping_what_verse_joins`):** 10.12 says the sin was the aim. The verse says the aim was a bad name, to taunt him. The three tellings drop the end of the verse. When 10.12, 10.8 and Ch 12 are touched, the quotation should run to its end and the statement should follow it.
- **What the passage adds:** Nehemiah's answer, "Should such a man as I run away? ... I will not go in" (6:11), and "I understood and saw that God had not sent him" (6:12). He did not fear, so the chain did not start.
- **Face:** reproach as the goal of a plot, reached through the target's own fear and sin.
- **Parties:** Tobiah and Sanballat (who hired), Shemaiah (hired), Nehemiah, God (who had not sent him). Routes A and B.

## 4. Gen 34:14: "We cannot do this thing, to give our sister to one who is uncircumcised, for that would be a disgrace to us."

- **The passage:** Shechem "seized her and lay with her and humiliated her" (34:2), then "his soul was drawn to Dinah" (34:3). Jacob's sons "were indignant and very angry, because he had done an outrageous thing in Israel" (34:7). "The sons of Jacob answered Shechem and his father Hamor **deceitfully**" (34:13). Then 34:14. The city was circumcised, and on the third day Simeon and Levi killed all the males (34:25). "Should he treat our sister like a prostitute?" (34:31).
- **Earlier readings:** 34:2 and 34:8 in 10.6; 34:7 in 10.1, 10.13 and 10.8; 34:13 and 34:19 in 10.1. 34:14 is not cited.
- **Main IB operation:** reproach named as a reason, inside an answer the narrator calls deceitful (34:13). The disgrace they name (an uncircumcised husband) is not the wrong they are angry about (what was done to their sister, 34:7, 31).
- **Roles:** "disgrace" (M07 *ḥerpāh*) is the stated reason; "uncircumcised" (M32) is its ground.
- **Face:** reproach used as a pretext. **I read** the brothers' real reproach as the one in 34:31: the sister treated "like a prostitute". The narrator's "deceitfully" is the ground for reading 34:14 as a pretext.
- **Parties:** the sons of Jacob, Shechem, Hamor, Dinah (who is not heard), Jacob. Route B.

## 5. 2Sa 13:13: "As for me, where could I carry my shame? And as for you, you would be as one of the outrageous fools in Israel."

- **The passage:** Amnon seizes Tamar (13:11). She pleads: "do not violate me, for such a thing is not done in Israel; do not do this outrageous thing" (13:12), then 13:13, and "please speak to the king, for he will not withhold me from you". "But he would not listen to her, and being stronger than she, he violated her" (13:14). "Then Amnon hated her with very great hatred" (13:15). She "put ashes on her head and tore the long robe ... and went away, crying aloud as she went" (13:19). Absalom: "hold your peace, my sister ... do not take this to heart" (13:20). "So Tamar lived, a desolate woman, in her brother Absalom's house" (13:20).
- **Earlier readings:** 13:12 and 13:14 in 10.7 *Willing* and Ch 11 §4. 13:13 and 13:15–20 are not cited.
- **The original:** "my shame" is *ḥerpāh*. "Carry" is *hālak* (Hiphil, "cause to go"): "where would I make my reproach go?" "Do not take this to heart" is literally "do not set your heart to this thing" (*lēḇ*, M47). "Desolate" is *šāmēm* (T3), the word for a ruined land.
- **Main IB operation:** a plea in which Tamar sets before Amnon what the act would do to each of them: reproach she would have to carry, folly he would become.
- **Roles:** "shame" (M07) is the characteristic of her side; "outrageous fools" (M16 *nāḇāl*) the characteristic of his. The verse sets the two side by side as the cost.
- **Face:** reproach as a burden with nowhere to go, foreseen by the one about to bear it, and then borne (13:19–20).
- **What the passage adds:** after the act, hatred greater than the love (13:15); her grief shown in body and clothing (13:19); a brother telling her not to set her heart on it (13:20); and her life after, "desolate". **I read** "desolate" as the reproach carried for life: the verse states only where and how she lived.
- **Pointer:** 13:15 (love turned to hatred) and 13:19 (ashes, torn robe, crying aloud) are not told anywhere; they bear on 10.10 and on grief in the body (10.12 and 10.13 have fear and anger in the body).
- **Parties:** Tamar, Amnon, Absalom, the king (named in the plea). Route B.

## 6. 1Sa 11:2: "On this condition I will make a treaty with you, that I gouge out all your right eyes, and thus bring disgrace on all Israel."

- **The original:** "and thus set it as a reproach upon all Israel" (*śîm ... ḥerpāh*).
- **Main IB operation:** a reproach designed and imposed: a mark in the body (the right eye) on one town, meant to be a reproach on the whole people.
- **What the passage carries:** the people of Gibeah "wept aloud" at the news (11:4). "The Spirit of God rushed upon Saul when he heard these words, and his anger was greatly kindled" (11:6). "The dread of the Lord fell upon the people, and they came out as one man" (11:7). Jabesh is told, "you shall have salvation", and "they were glad" (11:9).
- **Earlier readings:** 11:6 in Ch 6, 10.13 and 10.9; 11:7 in 10.7; 11:9–11 in 10.11 and 10.1. 11:2 and 11:4 are not cited.
- **Roles:** "disgrace" (M07) is the aim of the act; the eye (T14) is where it would be put; "make [a treaty]" (M32) is the frame.
- **Face:** reproach as an aim, to be put on a whole people through the body of some.
- **Relational observation:** the reproach planned in 11:2 is what set off the anger of 11:6 (already told in 10.13). **I read** the people's weeping (11:4) and Saul's anger (11:6) as two responses to the same threatened reproach. The passage states both; the link is in the passage order.
- **Parties:** Nahash, the men of Jabesh, all Israel, Saul, the Spirit of God. Routes A and B.

## 7. 1Sa 25:39: "Blessed be the Lord who has avenged the insult I received at the hand of Nabal, and has kept back his servant from wrongdoing."

- **The original:** "who has pleaded the case of my reproach (*rîḇ ḥerpātî*) from the hand of Nabal". *Rîḇ* (M26, M10) is the word for contending at law. The ESV's "avenged" is its rendering.
- **The passage:** Nabal had insulted David's men (25:10–11, 14). David set out to kill (25:13, 21–22) and was stopped by Abigail (25:32–34). Nabal's "heart died within him, and he became as a stone" (25:37), and "the Lord struck Nabal, and he died" (25:38).
- **Earlier readings:** 25:36–38 in Ch 2 and Ch 11 §2 (claims 3-7, C-103, C-88). 25:39 is neither cited nor a claim source.
- **Main IB operation:** David blesses God for taking up his reproach as a case and for keeping him from taking it up himself.
- **Roles:** "reproach" (M07) is what was suffered; "pleaded the case" (M26) is God's act on it; "kept back ... from wrongdoing" is God's act on David; "returned the evil of Nabal on his own head" names the outcome.
- **Relational observation:** the verse puts two acts of God side by side: he took up the reproach, and he kept David from avenging it. **I read** David's relief as being as much about the second as the first: he had meant to do it himself (25:22, 34). The verse states both acts; the weight is my reading.
- **Face:** reproach handed to God as a case, and the one reproached kept from answering it himself.
- **Pointer:** 1Sa 25:39 is one of the M72 group D forwards to M07; it is read here.
- **Parties:** David, God, Nabal, Abigail. Routes A and B.

## 8. Judg 8:15: "Behold Zebah and Zalmunna, about whom you taunted me, saying, 'Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your hand, that we should give bread to your men who are exhausted?'"

- **The passage:** Gideon and his 300, "exhausted yet pursuing" (8:4), ask Succoth for bread (8:5). The officials answer with the question of 8:6. Gideon promises to "flail your flesh with the thorns" (8:7). After the victory he "took the elders of the city ... and with them taught the men of Succoth a lesson" (8:16), literally "made the men of Succoth know" (*yāḏaʿ*, Hiphil).
- **Earlier readings:** none in the passage.
- **Main IB operation:** a taunt that is a refusal of help. The officials' question mocks the chance of victory and withholds bread from exhausted men.
- **Roles:** "taunted" (M53) is the act; "exhausted" (M24) qualifies those refused; "hand" (M72) is the content of the taunt (who holds whom).
- **Face:** taunt as a cover for refusing help to the exhausted. **I read** the officials as hedging against Midian: to feed Gideon before the kings were taken would be to take a side. The verse does not say why they refused.
- **What the passage adds:** the answer is punishment, and it is named "making them know". **I read** it as a lesson meant for the taunters themselves. The passage states the act; the purpose is in the verb.
- **Parties:** Gideon and his men, the officials of Succoth, Zebah and Zalmunna. Route B.

## 9. Judg 5:18: "Zebulun is a people who risked their lives to the death; Naphtali, too, on the heights of the field."

- **The original:** "Zebulun is a people who reproached (*ḥērēp̄*, M53, Piel) its soul (*nep̄eš*, M47) to death". The ESV's "risked" renders the idiom. The verb is the taunt verb; its object is the people's own life.
- **The passage:** the Song of Deborah sets the tribes who came against those who stayed: "Among the clans of Reuben there were great searchings of heart. Why did you sit still among the sheepfolds?" (5:15–16).
- **Earlier readings:** none in the passage; the verse was decided in M08 (the heights).
- **Main IB operation:** holding one's own life cheap to the point of death, for the people's cause.
- **Roles:** "reproached" (M53) is the act, turned on the self; "soul / life" (M47) is its object; "death" (M25) is its measure; "heights" (M08) is the place.
- **Face:** the taunt verb turned inward and given honour: Zebulun scorned its own life. **I read** this as the opposite face of the word: elsewhere *ḥārap̄* belittles another; here a people belittles its own life, and the song praises it. The literal sense is in the Hebrew; the reading is mine.
- **Pointer:** "great searchings of heart" (5:15–16) among those who stayed is not told anywhere (Ch 4; 10.4; 10.7).
- **Parties:** Zebulun, Naphtali, the tribes who stayed. Route B.

## 10. Jos 5:9: "And the Lord said to Joshua, 'Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you.' And so the name of that place is called Gilgal to this day."

- **The passage:** the generation born in the wilderness is circumcised (5:2–7), after "the men of war who came out of Egypt, perished, because they did not obey the voice of the Lord" (5:6). They stay in camp "until they were healed" (5:8).
- **Earlier readings:** none in the passage.
- **The original:** "rolled away" is *gālal*, the root of the name Gilgal. The name keeps the act.
- **Main IB operation:** God removes a reproach from a people, and names a place for it.
- **What "the reproach of Egypt" is:** the verse does not say. The passage gives two things beside it: the uncircumcised state of the new generation (5:5, 7) and the fathers who died in the wilderness for disobedience (5:6). **I read** it as left open by the verse; I do not choose between them.
- **Face:** reproach removed by God, at a point of new beginning, with a name to remember it.
- **Pointer:** Gen 30:23 (trial): God takes away a reproach, and a name is given (Joseph). **I read** the two as alike in that a name keeps the removal; each verse states its own.
- **Parties:** God, Joshua, the people. Routes A and B.

## 11. Lev 20:17: "If a man takes his sister ... and sees her nakedness, and she sees his nakedness, it is a disgrace, and they shall be cut off in the sight of the children of their people. He has uncovered his sister's nakedness, and he shall bear his iniquity."

- **The original:** "it is a disgrace" uses *ḥeseḏ* (H2617B), the homonym of the word for steadfast love. The lexicon gives this one sense, "shame", in two verses (here and Pro 14:34).
- **Main IB operation:** a law naming an act between brother and sister as a disgrace, with the consequence set "in the sight of" the people, and the iniquity borne by the man.
- **Roles:** "disgrace" (M07) is the law's verdict on the act; "bear ... iniquity" (M56) is the consequence; "sees" (twice) and "in the sight of" carry the seeing that runs through the verse.
- **Face:** disgrace as a verdict on an act, bound to seeing: what is seen between them, and their being cut off in the sight of others.
- **Pointer:** the nakedness words are T2 (M07 overview §5). Gen 2:25, "naked and were not ashamed", is an M07 verse. **I read** seeing nakedness as one of the circumstances in which shame is named; this verse and Gen 2:25 state their own.
- **Parties:** the man, his sister, the people. Route B.

## 12. What batch 1 adds

**Faces of taunt and reproach (to be added to the trial's; nothing is joined into a pattern yet):**
- mocking of God sent through servants, letters and shouting, aimed at the hearers' fear (Sennacherib; 2Ch 32:18)
- mocking carried by a boast from a lifted-up stance (2Ki 19:22–23)
