## 1. Psalm 69

**The passage.** A prayer of David from deep water (69:1–2), weary with crying and waiting (69:3), hated "without cause" (69:4), and confessing "you know my folly" (69:5). The middle (69:6–12) is about reproach; the plea follows (69:13–18); then reproach again (69:19–21); and the psalm ends in praise and in "the Lord hears the needy" (69:33).

**What the passage carries (§4.0), before the words of the set:**
- the one who prays is under threat from outside (water, enemies, lies) and from his own wrongs (69:5)
- his concern is for others who hope in God: "Let not those who hope in you be put to shame through me" (69:6)
- he is cut off from his family (69:8) and made a song in the gate and among drunkards (69:11–12)
- his heart is broken, he looks for pity and comfort and finds none (69:20), and he is given poison and sour wine (69:21; John 19:28–30 is in 10.6)
- the end: "you who seek God, let your hearts revive" (69:32)

### Psa 69:7: "For it is for your sake that I have borne reproach, that dishonor has covered my face."

- **Main IB operation:** bearing reproach for God's sake. The verb is *nāśāʾ* (T3, "lift, carry"): reproach is something carried.
- **Roles:** *ḥerpāh* "reproach" (M07) is the characteristic here: what he carries. *kᵊlimmāh* "dishonor" (M07) is a second characteristic in parallel, said of the face. "covered" (*kāsāh*, M45) is a qualifier: it says how dishonor sits, over the face. The face (T14) is where it shows.
- **Face of the word:** reproach borne, with a cause named ("for your sake").
- **Parties:** the one who prays bears it; God is the one for whose sake; the reproachers are behind it (69:9). Route B.
- **I read** shame here as having a place in the body, the face, as fear has the hands (10.12 *Fear in the body*). The verse states the face; the comparison is mine.

### Psa 69:9: "For zeal for your house has consumed me, and the reproaches of those who reproach you have fallen on me."

- **Earlier reading:** read in M18 for zeal and told in 10.6 *Zeal that consumes the one who has it* and Ch 11 §4. 10.6 already says the zeal "draws onto him the reproach that was meant for God". This reading builds on it, with one correction below.
- **The original:** both M53 and M07 are here. "Those who reproach you" is the participle of *ḥārap̄* (M53, Piel); "the reproaches" is *ḥerpāh* (M07). One root, as verb and noun, in one clause.
- **Main IB operation:** two in parallel. Zeal consumes him (M18, characteristic, already read). The reproach aimed at God falls on him (characteristic for this set).
- **Relational observation:** the verse joins them with "and". It does not say that the zeal causes the reproach. **I read** the two lines as one situation seen twice: what he has for God's house, and what comes to him for it.
- **Correction candidate (rule 77):** 10.6 says the zeal "draws onto him" the reproach, which states a cause the verse does not state. When 10.6 is touched, the sentence should say that the verse sets the two side by side.
- **Face of the word:** reproach that changes target. It is aimed at God ("those who reproach you") and lands on God's servant ("fallen on me").
- **Pointer:** Rom 15:3 quotes this line of Christ (below). John 2:17 quotes the first line ("Zeal for your house will consume me") at the cleansing of the temple; it is M18, not in this set, and is told in 10.3.
- **Parties:** God (the target of the reproach), the reproachers, the servant (where it lands). Routes A and B.

### Psa 69:10: "When I wept and humbled my soul with fasting, it became my reproach."

- **The original (§4.6a):** the Hebrew is "and I wept, with fasting my soul". There is no humbling verb in the verse's word rows; "humbled" is the ESV's rendering of the construction. The soul (*nep̄eš*, M47) is the object of the weeping and fasting.
- **Main IB operation:** grief before God (weeping, fasting) turned by others into a reproach against him. The verb is "became" (*hāyāh*): his act of devotion became the occasion of reproach.
- **Roles:** "wept" (M03) is the characteristic of his act; "soul" (M47) is where it happens; "reproach" (M07) is the characteristic of what others make of it.
- **Face of the word:** reproach made out of a person's own devotion.
- **Parties:** the one who prays; the onlookers who make it a reproach (69:11–12: a byword, the talk of the gate, the songs of drunkards). Route B.

### Psa 69:19: "You know my reproach, and my shame and my dishonor; my foes are all known to you."

- **Main IB operation:** laying reproach, shame and dishonor before God as known to him. The verb is *yāḏaʿ* "know" (M15), said of God.
- **Roles:** three M07 words stacked: *ḥerpāh*, *bōšet*, *kᵊlimmāh*. They are the object of God's knowing. "Know" (M15) is God's act here, not the person's.
- **Face:** reproach known by God. **I read** this as the opposite of hiding (10.11): it is not concealed and not explained; it is stated as already known to God.
- **Parties:** God (who knows), the one who prays, the foes. Routes A and B.

### Psa 69:20: "Reproaches have broken my heart, so that I am in despair. I looked for pity, but there was none, and for comforters, but I found none."

- **Earlier reading:** told in Ch 4 (*The heart can be broken*), 10.9 (*No one else comforts either*), 10.14 (*By what others do*) and 10.6 (thirst, with 69:21); claim C-189.
- **Main IB operation:** the heart broken by reproach. *ḥerpāh* (M07) is the subject of the verb *šāḇar* "break" (M24), and the heart (M47) is the object. The reproach is the **cause**.
- **The original:** "despair" is *nûš* "be sick" (M73). **I read** "I am sick" as the plainer sense beside the ESV's "despair".
- **Roles:** reproach is the agent; "broken" (M24) the characteristic of what happens in the heart; "despair / be sick" (M73) its result; "looked" (*qāwāh*, M68, the hope word) and "pity" and "comforters" (M45) the want that went unmet.
- **Relational observation:** this verse states what reproach does inside a person: it breaks the heart and makes it sick, and the loneliness follows (no pity, no comforters). The verses already told name the heart broken and the comfort missing. **What they have not told** is that reproach is the thing that breaks it.
- **Parties:** the reproachers (behind "reproaches"), the one who prays, the absent comforters. Route B.

**Psalm 69 as a passage.** Reproach appears five times (69:7, 9, 10, 19, 20), with shame and dishonor beside it. In this one prayer, reproach is:
- borne for God's sake (69:7)
- aimed at God and fallen on his servant (69:9)
- made out of his own weeping and fasting (69:10)
- known to God (69:19)
- what broke his heart (69:20)

The psalm also asks that others **not** be put to shame "through me" (69:6). **I read** the one who prays as fearing that his reproach will spread to those who hope in God. The verse states the request; the fear is my reading.

## 2. 1 Samuel 17: Goliath

**The passage.** Goliath challenges the ranks of Israel (17:8–10), and Saul and Israel are "dismayed and greatly afraid" (17:11). Every morning and evening for forty days (17:16) the same words, and "All the men of Israel, when they saw the man, fled from him and were much afraid" (17:24). David hears (17:23), asks (17:26), is rebuked by Eliab (17:28; told in 10.15), tells Saul "Let no man's heart fail because of him" (17:32), and goes out "in the name of the Lord of hosts" (17:45).

**The original:** the verb throughout is *ḥārap̄* (M53, Piel), rendered "defy" (17:10, 25, 26, 36, 45). Its range (`strong_meaning_tree`) is "to reproach, taunt, blaspheme, defy". The noun *ḥerpāh* (M07) is in 17:26 alone.

### 1Sa 17:10: "And the Philistine said, 'I defy the ranks of Israel this day. Give me a man, that we may fight together.'"

- **Main IB operation:** a taunt spoken as a challenge. There is no other M-code in the verse.
- **What it does (17:11):** "they were dismayed and greatly afraid". The taunt is aimed at the hearers' heart, and it reaches it.
- **Face:** the taunt as a weapon, before any fighting.
- **Parties:** Goliath (the taunter), Israel and Saul (the taunted). Route B.

### 1Sa 17:25: "Have you seen this man who has come up? Surely he has come up to defy Israel. And the king will enrich the man who kills him ..."

- **Main IB operation:** the men of Israel name what Goliath is doing ("to defy Israel"). They answer it with a reward (riches, the king's daughter, freedom), not with action. The verse before has them fleeing (17:24).
- **Roles:** "defy" (M53) is what is named. "King", "enrich", "riches" (M72, M46) qualify the response: a reward held out.
- **Parties:** the men of Israel speaking, Goliath named, the king behind the offer. Route B.

### 1Sa 17:26: "What shall be done for the man who kills this Philistine and takes away the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"

- **The original:** verb and noun in one verse. *ḥerpāh* (M07) is the reproach as a condition: the Hebrew is literally "from upon Israel", a reproach that rests on a people and can be "taken away" (*sûr*, M11). *ḥārap̄* (M53) is the act of defying.
- **Main IB operation:** David re-reads the taunt. The others heard a defiance of Israel (17:10, 25). David asks who this is "that he should defy the armies of the living God". The same act gets a new target.
- **Roles:** "reproach" (M07) is the characteristic of Israel's condition; "defy" (M53) is the act that put it there; "living" (M25) qualifies God.
- **Relational observation:** the taunt (act) produces a reproach (a condition) that rests on a people until it is taken away.
- **Face:** reproach as a public condition, removable by an act. Psa 69:9 had reproach aimed at God that lands on a man; here a taunt aimed at Israel is read as aimed at God. **I read** the two as related, because both verses name God as the one defied or reproached, but I join them only as a pointer for the account.
- **Parties:** David, the men beside him, Goliath, Israel, the living God. Routes A and B.

### 1Sa 17:36: "... this uncircumcised Philistine shall be like one of them, for he has defied the armies of the living God."

- **Main IB operation:** David's confidence, stated from what God has done before (the lion and the bear, 17:34–37). The defiance is the reason he gives that this fight is like those.
- **Face:** the defiance named as the ground for confidence, not for fear. Israel's response to the same taunt was fear (17:11, 24).
- **Relational observation:** one taunt meets two hearts: fear in the ranks, confidence in David. **I read** the difference as lying in whom each sees as defied: Israel heard itself defied; David heard God defied. The verses state both readings (17:25, 26); the link to the different responses is my reading.
- **Parties:** David, Saul, Goliath, God ("The Lord who delivered me", 17:37). Routes A and B.

### 1Sa 17:45: "You come to me with a sword ... but I come to you in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied."

- **Main IB operation:** answering a taunt with a name. David sets the name of the Lord against the weapons. The verse just before has Goliath cursing David "by his gods" (17:43).
- **Face:** the one defied (God) named back to the taunter.
- **Parties:** David, Goliath, the Lord of hosts. The outcome is "that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel" (17:46). Routes A and B.

**1 Samuel 17 as a passage.** The taunt is an act on the hearts of the hearers before it is a battle. It leaves a reproach resting on a people until it is taken away, and David reframes it from a defiance of Israel to a defiance of the living God. The fear it produced is already told: 1Sa 17:11 is in 10.12 and 10.1. 17:24 ("fled from him and were much afraid") and David's "Let no man's heart fail because of him" (17:32) are not cited anywhere; 17:32 goes to the heart (Ch 4) and to 10.12.

## 3. Genesis 30:23: "She conceived and bore a son and said, 'God has taken away my reproach.'"

**The passage.** Rachel "saw that she bore Jacob no children, she envied her sister", and said, "Give me children, or I shall die!" (30:1). Jacob's anger is kindled: "Am I in the place of God, who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?" (30:2). Then "God remembered Rachel, and God listened to her and opened her womb" (30:22).

- **The original:** "taken away" is *ʾāsap̄* "gather, take away" (T3). The name Joseph in 30:24 is explained with *yāsap̄* "add": "May the Lord add to me another son!" Two verbs that sound alike: one takes away, one adds.
- **Main IB operation:** Rachel's own reading of what God has done: her barrenness was a reproach, and God has removed it.
- **Roles:** "reproach" (M07) is the characteristic: the condition she lived under. God (T7) is the subject of the removal.
- **What the passage carries:** the reproach had an inner side before it was removed: envy of her sister and "or I shall die" (30:1). It was felt as a matter of life and death. Jacob answers with anger and with "Am I in the place of God …?" (30:2). God's remembering and listening (30:22) come before the removal.
- **Face:** reproach as a condition that rests on a person through no act of her own, and that God removes. **I read** "my reproach" as how she understood childlessness among others; the verse states only that she named it so.
- **Pointer:** Luk 1:25 (Elizabeth): "to take away my reproach among people" (set 1, unit 1-073). Isa 4:1, Isa 54:4 and 1Sa 1:6–7 (Hannah, provoked by her rival, not in the set) are the same circumstance.
- **Earlier readings:** Rachel's envy and "or I shall die" (30:1) are told in 10.6 and Ch 2; Jacob's anger (30:2) in 10.13. Her reproach (30:23), and God's remembering and listening (30:22), are not cited.
- **Parties:** Rachel, God, Jacob, Leah behind the envy. Routes A and B.

## 4. Romans 1:24–27 (set 2) and Romans 15:3 (set 1)

### The Romans 1 passage

"For although they knew God, they did not honor him as God or give thanks to him, but they became futile in their thinking, and their foolish hearts were darkened" (1:21). They "exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images" (1:23). Then three times "God gave them up" (1:24, 26, 28), each after an exchange (1:23, 25, and "did not see fit to acknowledge God", 1:28).

**The original (§4.6a).** The English sets "honor" (1:21) against "dishonoring" and "dishonorable" (1:24, 26). The Greek does not: "honor" in 1:21 is *doxazō* "glorify". "Dishonoring" (1:24) is *atimazō* and "dishonorable passions" (1:26) is *pathē atimias*, literally "passions of dishonor". *Atimia* is the negative of *timē* "honour" (M71). "Shameless acts" (1:27) is *aschēmosynē*, "indecency", whose range runs to "nakedness, shame" (Rev 16:15).

### Rom 1:24: "Therefore God gave them up in the lusts of their hearts to impurity, to the dishonoring of their bodies among themselves"

- **Earlier reading:** told in 10.6 (*God gave them up in the lusts of their hearts*, beside Eze 23:21–22). Ch 12 holds "Romans 1:21–32 as a five-step downward progression" as **Held, not yet re-examined**.
- **Main IB operation:** God's giving over (T3 *paradidōmi*, God as subject), "in the lusts of their hearts". The lusts (M18) and the heart (M47) are where they already were; the giving over leaves them there.
- **Roles:** "dishonoring" (M07) is the **outcome**, and it is done to the body (*sōma*, now in M47 with flesh, #2163) "among themselves". "Impurity" (M10c) stands beside it.
- **Relational observation:** the verse names heart and body in one sentence: the lusts are in the heart, the dishonor is in the body. It does not say how one leads to the other beyond "gave them up ... to".
- **Face of dishonor:** dishonor done to one's own body, as the outcome of being given up.
- **Pointer:** the flesh-and-body reading (#2162): the body dishonored. Ch 12 *Held* item on Rom 1:21–32.
- **Parties:** God (who gives up), the people (who exchanged, and who dishonor their bodies among themselves). Routes A and B.

### Rom 1:26: "For this reason God gave them up to dishonorable passions ..."

- **Main IB operation:** the second giving over, "to passions of dishonor". *Pathos* (G3806, "passion") carries no M-code in iba.db, but it names the inner act (§4.6: read every row).
- **Role of the M07 word:** *atimia* qualifies the passions: they are passions **of dishonor**. It gives them their measure.
- **The second half:** "their women exchanged natural relations for those that are contrary to nature". The word "exchanged" (T3 *metallassō*) is the same as in 1:25. **I read** the passage as answering exchange with exchange. The verse does not state it; the repeated word does.
- **Parties:** God, the women. Routes A and B.

### Rom 1:27: "... men committing shameless acts with men and receiving in themselves the due penalty for their error."

- **Main IB operation:** being consumed with passion (*ekkaiō* "be inflamed", with *orexis* "desire", M28), acting it out, and "receiving in themselves" what is due.
- **Roles:** "shameless acts" (M53 *aschēmosynē*) is the act that comes from the burning. "error" (M55) names what it was. "Gave up" (M59 *aphiēmi*, "leave") is the men's own giving up of the natural.
- **Face:** indecency as the outworking of a consuming passion. "Receiving in themselves" places the consequence inside the person. **I read** "in themselves" as within the person and the body, not only as an outside penalty; the Greek *en heautois* allows both.
- **Pointer:** M53 E has one other verse with *aschēmosynē*, Rev 16:15 ("that he may not go about naked and be seen exposed"), which is about nakedness. The two faces of the word are indecency and exposure.
- **Parties:** the men, God (behind the giving up). Route B.

**Romans 1 as a passage.** Honour withheld from God (1:21, glorify) is followed by dishonor in the body (1:24) and passions of dishonor (1:26). The passage's own connectives say how they follow: "Therefore" (1:24), "For this reason" (1:26), "since" (1:28). That bears on the Ch 12 held item (a five-step progression). **I read** the passage as stating the connections itself, so the Ch 12 item can be checked against the passage's own connectives when that chapter is touched. I do not resolve it here.

### Rom 15:3: "For Christ did not please himself, but as it is written, 'The reproaches of those who reproached you fell on me.'"

- **The original:** the Greek has both words of the set: *oneidismoi* "reproaches" (M07) and *oneidizontōn* "those who reproached" (M53). It is the Septuagint of Psa 69:9b, so the root pair of the Hebrew is carried into the Greek.
- **The passage:** "We who are strong have an obligation to bear with the failings of the weak, and not to please ourselves. Let each of us please his neighbor for his good, to build him up" (15:1–2). Then 15:3. Then "whatever was written in former days was written for our instruction, that through endurance and through the encouragement of the Scriptures we might have hope" (15:4).
- **Main IB operation:** not pleasing oneself (*areskō*, M04) for the good of another. Christ is the example. The quotation names what that cost him: the reproach aimed at God fell on him.
- **Roles:** "please" (M04) is the characteristic (not pleasing himself); the reproach words are the **ground** Paul gives for it, from Scripture.
- **Face:** reproach borne by Christ, cited as the pattern for bearing with the weak. **I read** Paul as applying the psalm's "for your sake" (69:7) to Christ, and Christ's bearing it to the church's bearing with one another. The verse states the quotation and the "for"; the application is Paul's argument in 15:1–3.
- **Pointer:** Heb 11:26 ("the reproach of Christ"), Heb 13:13 ("bear the reproach he endured") and 1Pe 4:14 (insulted for the name of Christ) are in set 1.
- **Parties:** Christ (13.1 for his own act), the believers (the strong and the weak). Routes A and B.

## 5. What the trial shows, for your check

**On the method.**
- Reading outward gave each verse more than one significance. Psa 69:9 holds zeal (already told), reproach that changes target (not told), and a root pair (verb and noun). Psa 69:20 holds the broken heart (told) and **its cause, reproach** (not told). 1Sa 17:10 holds a taunt and the fear it causes (17:11).
- The verb–noun pairing that brought M53 and M07 together shows inside the verses: Psa 69:9, 1Sa 17:26 and Rom 15:3 each have both words. In 1Sa 17 the verb is the act (defy) and the noun is the condition left (a reproach resting on Israel). Reading the two clusters apart would have split these verses.
- One earlier reading needs correcting: 10.6 states a cause (zeal "draws onto him" the reproach) that Psa 69:9 does not state (§1). Earlier readings were otherwise checked, not assumed: 1Sa 17:11 is in 10.12 and 10.1, Gen 30:1 in 10.6 and Ch 2, Gen 30:2 in 10.13, and Psa 69:9 and 69:20 in five places. What the trial adds to them is named per verse.
- The Ch 12 *Held* item on Rom 1:21–32 bears on set 2. I noted it and did not resolve it.

**Faces of reproach seen in the trial** (the full set will add to these; nothing is joined into a pattern yet):
- an act (a taunt, a defiance) and a condition it leaves (a reproach resting on a person or a people)
- borne for God's sake; aimed at God and landing on his servant; read by one man as aimed at God when others heard it aimed at themselves
- made out of a person's own devotion (weeping, fasting)
- what breaks the heart
- known to God
- removed by God (Rachel), or by an act (killing Goliath)
- borne by Christ, and cited as the pattern for bearing with others

**Faces of dishonor in set 2 so far:** dishonor done to one's own body; passions "of dishonor"; indecency as the outworking of a consuming passion, with its consequence "in themselves".

**Where it would go (not decided).** There is no shame or reproach section in the narrative. When the whole set is read, the faces above point to one account of reproach and shame (a new section, its place yours to decide), with parts woven into Ch 4 (the heart broken by reproach), 10.12 (the taunt that makes afraid), 10.10 (taunting and reviling another), 10.9 (reproach laid before God), 13.1 (God reproached; Christ bearing it), and 10.6 / Ch 12 / Ch 7 for Rom 1.
