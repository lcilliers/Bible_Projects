## 2. The readings

### I. The heart lifted up (22)

**The heart rises out of what it has been given.** This is the clearest single pattern in the batch, and the verses state it each time:
- **Strength.** Uzziah *"was marvelously helped, till he was strong. But when he was strong, he grew proud, to his destruction"* (2Ch 26:15–16).
- **Healing and honour.** Hezekiah was healed, given a sign, and *"exalted in the sight of all nations"*. Then: *"But Hezekiah did not make return according to the benefit done to him, for his heart was proud"* (2Ch 32:23–25). Here pride is a failure to return what was received. It was humbled the next verse (32:26, carried from M09).
- **Greatness and majesty.** *"the Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar your father kingship and greatness and glory and majesty ... But when his heart was lifted up and his spirit was hardened so that he dealt proudly, he was brought down"* (Dan 5:18–20). It lasted *"until he knew that the Most High God rules the kingdom of mankind"* (5:21).
- **Wealth.** *"by your great wisdom in your trade you have increased your wealth, and your heart has become proud in your wealth"* (Eze 28:5).
- **Beauty.** *"You were blameless in your ways from the day you were created, till unrighteousness was found in you ... Your heart was proud because of your beauty; you corrupted your wisdom for the sake of your splendor"* (Eze 28:15–17).
- **Height.** The cedar God made beautiful (*"I made it beautiful"*, Eze 31:9): *"its heart was proud of its height"* (31:10).

**In its fullest form, pride claims God's place.** *"Because your heart is proud, and you have said, 'I am a god, I sit in the seat of the gods' ... yet you are but a man, and no god, though you make your heart like the heart of a god"* (Eze 28:2). The verse itself names the gap between what the heart says and what is so.

**Pride deceives the one who has it.**
- *"The pride of your heart has deceived you ... who say in your heart, 'Who will bring me down to the ground?'"* (Obd 3).
- *"Though you soar aloft like the eagle ... from there I will bring you down"* (Obd 4).
- Jer 49:16 adds a second deceiver: *"The horror you inspire has deceived you, and the pride of your heart"*. The fear others feel feeds the self-deception.

**It speaks of its own hand.**
- The king of Assyria: *"the speech of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria and the boastful look in his eyes. For he says: 'By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom, for I have understanding'"* (Isa 10:12–13).
- In the same passage he is the Lord's rod (10:5, 15). **I read:** pride credits itself with what it was used for.

**It has no room for God.**
- *"In the pride of his face the wicked does not seek him; all his thoughts are, 'There is no God'"* (Psa 10:4).
- *"his soul is puffed up; it is not upright within him, but the righteous shall live by his faith"* (Hab 2:4). Then: *"an arrogant man who is never at rest. His greed is as wide as Sheol"* (2:5). The swollen soul is set against faith, and joined to restlessness and greed.

**It is seen in the eyes, the walk and the house.**
- *"Haughty eyes and a proud heart, the lamp of the wicked, are sin"* (Pro 21:4). It follows *"the Lord weighs the heart"* (21:2).
- *"Whoever has a haughty look and an arrogant heart I will not endure"* (Psa 101:5).
- The daughters of Zion *"walk with outstretched necks, glancing wantonly with their eyes"* (Isa 3:16), in the same oracle as *"the spoil of the poor is in your houses"* (3:14).
- *"he who makes his door high seeks destruction"* (Pro 17:19).
- *"How sick is your heart ... the deeds of a brazen prostitute"* (Eze 16:30).

**Against patience.** *"the patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit. Be not quick in your spirit to become angry"* (Ecc 7:8–9). The proud spirit is set beside a quick temper.

*"Everyone who is arrogant in heart is an abomination to the Lord"* (Pro 16:5).

### II. Presumption: going beyond what was given (16)

*zîd* and *zādôn* name a pride that acts where it was not sent.
- **Speaking what God did not say.** *"the prophet who presumes to speak a word in my name that I have not commanded him"* (Deu 18:20). Such a word fails, and *"You need not be afraid of him"* (18:22). Presumption here borrows God's name for its own word.
- **Going up when told not to.** *"Do not go up ... So I spoke to you, and you would not listen; but you rebelled against the command of the Lord and presumptuously went up"* (Deu 1:42–43). It came straight after *"We have sinned ... We ourselves will go up"* (1:41). After the defeat, *"you returned and wept before the Lord"* (1:45). **I read:** presumption can wear the clothes of repentance. It goes up when the moment to go up has passed.
- **Not obeying.**
  - Not obeying the priest or judge (Deu 17:12). The rest *"shall hear and fear and not act presumptuously again"* (17:13).
  - *"they ... acted presumptuously and stiffened their neck and did not obey your commandments. They ... were not mindful of the wonders that you performed"* (Neh 9:16–17).
  - *"you warned them in order to turn them back ... Yet they acted presumptuously"* (9:29).
  - Joined to these are a stiff neck, a stubborn shoulder, and forgetting the wonders. Each time God's mercy answers: *"But you are a God ready to forgive"* (9:17).
- **Kept from it.** *"Keep back your servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me!"* (Psa 19:13). It follows *"Who can discern his errors?"* (19:12). Presumption is asked to be kept from, as something that would rule.
- **Not hearing.**
  - *"Hear and give ear; be not proud, for the Lord has spoken"* (Jer 13:15).
  - *"By insolence comes nothing but strife, but with those who take advice is wisdom"* (Pro 13:10).
  - *"When pride comes, then comes disgrace, but with the humble is wisdom"* (Pro 11:2, carried).
  - The opposite named for pride is hearing, taking advice, humility.
- **The fool.** *"a fool is reckless and careless"*, against the wise who *"is cautious and turns away from evil"* (Pro 14:16).
- **Scoffing.** *"'Scoffer' is the name of the arrogant, haughty man who acts with arrogant pride"* (Pro 21:24).
- **Intent.** *"if a man willfully attacks another to kill him by cunning"* (Exo 21:14). It is set against the one who *"did not lie in wait for him"* (21:13). Here the word names intent carried out against another.
- **Pride named wrongly.**
  - Eliab, angry: *"I know your presumption and the evil of your heart"*. David answers: *"What have I done now? Was it not but a word?"* (1Sa 17:28–29).
  - The passage shows David asking about the one who defies *"the armies of the living God"* (17:26).
  - **I read:** the charge of presumption here comes from an older brother's anger. The verses do not show it to be true.

### III. The insolent against others (17)

- **Psalm 119** gives one person's whole experience of the insolent:
  - *"The insolent utterly deride me, but I do not turn away from your law"* (119:51).
  - *"The insolent smear me with lies, but with my whole heart I keep your precepts; their heart is unfeeling like fat"* (119:69–70).
  - *"they have wronged me with falsehood; as for me, I will meditate on your precepts"* (119:78).
  - *"The insolent have dug pitfalls for me; they do not live according to your law"* (119:85).
  - *"let not the insolent oppress me"* (119:122).
  - The insolent wander from the commandments (119:21). Against them each time, the speaker holds the law, meditates, and asks for a pledge.
- **Rising up, with God not set before them.** *"insolent men have risen up against me; a band of ruthless men seeks my life, and they do not set you before them"* (Psa 86:14). The answer is *"But you, O Lord, are a God merciful and gracious"* (86:15).
- **Trampling, and the sufferer's fear.** *"many attack me proudly. When I am afraid, I put my trust in you"* (Psa 56:2–3).
- **Words.**
  - *"They pour out their arrogant words; all the evildoers boast"* (Psa 94:4).
  - *"They scoff and speak with malice; loftily they threaten oppression. They set their mouths against the heavens"* (Psa 73:8–9).
- **Not turning to them.** *"Blessed is the man who makes the Lord his trust, who does not turn to the proud"* (Psa 40:4).
- **Order inverted.** *"the youth will be insolent to the elder, and the despised to the honorable"* (Isa 3:5), when *"infants shall rule"* (3:4).
- **Refusing the word.** *"all the insolent men said to Jeremiah, 'You are telling a lie'"* (Jer 43:2).
- **Seen from below, and admired.**
  - *"now we call the arrogant blessed. Evildoers not only prosper but they put God to the test and they escape"* (Mal 3:15). It follows *"It is vain to serve God"* (3:14).
  - The next verse turns: *"Then those who feared the Lord spoke with one another. The Lord paid attention and heard them"* (3:16).
  - **I read:** the arrogant can look blessed to those who watch them prosper.
- **Egypt.**
  - Jethro: *"Now I know that the Lord is greater than all gods, because in this affair they dealt arrogantly with the people"* (Exo 18:11).
  - *"you knew that they acted arrogantly against our fathers. And you made a name for yourself"* (Neh 9:10).
  - God's greatness is known in his answer to arrogance against his people.
- Sodom: *"They were haughty and did an abomination before me"* (Eze 16:50, after 16:49 in batch 1).

### IV. Lifted against God, and brought down (17)

- **The eyes lifted against the Holy One.** *"Whom have you mocked and reviled? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes to the heights? Against the Holy One of Israel!"* (2Ki 19:22; Isa 37:23).
- **Defying.** *"she has proudly defied the Lord, the Holy One of Israel"* (Jer 50:29). *"Behold, I am against you, O proud one"* (50:31). *"The proud one shall stumble and fall, with none to raise him up"* (50:32).
- **Who lifts up.** *"do not lift up your horn on high, or speak with haughty neck. For not from the east or from the west and not from the wilderness comes lifting up, but it is God who executes judgment, putting down one and lifting up another"* (Psa 75:5–7). Lifting up is God's to give.
- **Talk no more proudly.** Hannah: *"Talk no more so very proudly, let not arrogance come from your mouth; for the Lord is a God of knowledge, and by him actions are weighed. The bows of the mighty are broken, but the feeble bind on strength"* (1Sa 2:3–4). The reason given against proud talk is that God knows and weighs.
- **Ripening and fall.**
  - *"the rod has blossomed; pride has budded"* (Eze 7:10). Pride is pictured as a plant that has come to bud.
  - *"all the arrogant and all evildoers will be stubble"*, while *"for you who fear my name, the sun of righteousness shall rise with healing"* (Mal 4:1–2).
  - The fallen cedar is made an example: *"in order that no trees by the waters may grow to towering height ... For they are all given over to death"* (Eze 31:14).
  - Shebna, who *"cut out a tomb on the height"*, is hurled away: *"you shame of your master's house"* (Isa 22:16–18).
- Carried from M09: Isa 2:11, 17; 5:15; 10:33; 26:5. In Isa 26:6 the lofty city is trampled by *"the feet of the poor"*.

### V. The other face (4)

- **A heart lifted up in the ways of the Lord.** Jehoshaphat *"sought the God of his father and walked in his commandments ... His heart was courageous in the ways of the Lord"* (2Ch 17:4–6). The word is *gābah*, the one used for Uzziah's and Hezekiah's proud hearts.
  - **I read:** the lifting is the same; the direction differs. Jehoshaphat's heart rose in God's ways and against the high places, not above God.
  - This is a change of character (#1919).
- **A heart not lifted up.** *"O Lord, my heart is not lifted up; my eyes are not raised too high; I do not occupy myself with things too great and too marvelous for me. But I have calmed and quieted my soul, like a weaned child with its mother"* (Psa 131:1–2). Not being lifted up is pictured as a soul at rest, against Hab 2:5's *"arrogant man who is never at rest"*.
- **Made bold.** *"On the day I called, you answered me; my strength of soul you increased"* (Psa 138:3). The word *rāhab* is glossed "be assertive". Elsewhere it is "insolent" (Isa 3:5). Here God increases it, in answer to a call.
- *"though the Lord is high, he regards the lowly, but the haughty he knows from afar"* (Psa 138:6, carried).

### VI–VII. Other beings and other senses (7)

- **Leviathan** *"sees everything that is high; he is king over all the sons of pride"* (Job 41:34). This is 13.4. It comes in God's speech after *"Who then is he who can stand before me?"* (41:10). Leviathan rules the proud beasts, and God rules Leviathan.
- *"As the heavens for height, and the earth for depth, so the heart of kings is unsearchable"* (Pro 25:3). Height here is the measure of a heart that cannot be searched. There is no pride in it.
- *"A greedy man stirs up strife, but the one who trusts in the Lord will be enriched"* (Pro 28:25). The word's gloss is "broad: arrogant"; ESV has "greedy". It bears on M18.
- Data: Gen 25:29 (cooking), Pro 6:3 (plead urgently), Song 6:5 (overwhelm), Job 28:8 (proud beasts).

## 3. For Ch 13: God's height and the lifted-up heart

This continues batch 1 §3, on the researcher's question about pride words and God's character.

1. **The verses say outright that pride takes a place that is God's.**
   - *"you have said, 'I am a god, I sit in the seat of the gods' ... yet you are but a man, and no god, though you make your heart like the heart of a god"* (Eze 28:2).
   - *"Who will bring me down to the ground?"* (Obd 3).
   - *"By the strength of my hand I have done it"* (Isa 10:13).
   - This is the researcher's "assumed position" in the verses' own words. Pride is a heart *made* like the heart of a god. It is not so.
2. **What was given is turned into pride.**
   - *"the Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar ... kingship and greatness and glory and majesty ... But when his heart was lifted up"* (Dan 5:18–20). Majesty is the same word family God wears (batch 1).
   - The pattern recurs: Uzziah's strength (2Ch 26:15–16), Hezekiah's healing (2Ch 32:24–25), the cherub's beauty (Eze 28:17), the cedar's height (Eze 31:9–10).
   - **I read:** the human standing in these verses is real, and it is given. Pride begins when the heart treats it as its own. Batch 1 showed the same in Eze 7:20 and 24:21.
3. **God's height does not keep him from the low.** *"though the Lord is high, he regards the lowly, but the haughty he knows from afar"* (Psa 138:6). The high God comes near the lowly. The proud, who make themselves high, are known *"from afar"*.
4. **Lifting up belongs to God.** *"not from the east or from the west ... comes lifting up, but it is God who executes judgment, putting down one and lifting up another"* (Psa 75:6–7). The one who has height by nature is the one who gives height.
5. **God is known as greater through his answer to arrogance.** *"Now I know that the Lord is greater than all gods, because ... they dealt arrogantly"* (Exo 18:11). *"you knew that they acted arrogantly ... And you made a name for yourself"* (Neh 9:10).
6. **God knows and weighs.** *"Talk no more so very proudly ... for the Lord is a God of knowledge, and by him actions are weighed"* (1Sa 2:3). This is joined to *"the Lord weighs the heart"* (Pro 21:2) beside *"a proud heart"* (21:4). **I read:** pride claims a height that cannot bear being weighed. God's knowing is part of what it runs into.
7. **His mercy answers presumption.** *"they acted presumptuously ... But you are a God ready to forgive, gracious and merciful"* (Neh 9:16–17). *"insolent men have risen up against me ... But you, O Lord, are a God merciful and gracious"* (Psa 86:14–15).

**So far, batches 1 and 2 support the researcher's suspicion.** God's majesty is his by nature, shown in acts and never overturned. Human pride, in its own words, makes the heart like a god's. It holds as its own what was given, and it does not survive being weighed. Two qualifications stand from the verses:
- God's majesty is challenged, but not overturned (batch 1 §3.3).
- The same lifting can be good: given by God (batch 1 §V), or a heart lifted *in his ways* (2Ch 17:6). In those cases it is not pride.

## 4. Points for the account

- **The giving-then-lifting pattern** (§I) is the strongest thread so far. It bears on Ch 12 claim C-209 ("Pride can grow from a beauty that was given"). The verses now widen it beyond beauty, to strength, healing, wealth, height and majesty. This is a reconciliation point under rule 77 when Ch 12 is touched.
- **Presumption** (§II) is a distinct face: acting where one was not sent, and speaking what was not said. It bears on 10.7 (choosing, planning), 10.2 (hearing) and Ch 11 §4.
- **Pride and rest:** Hab 2:5 (*"never at rest"*) against Psa 131:2 (*"calmed and quieted my soul"*). This bears on Ch 5 *Rest for the soul* (M09).
- **The sufferer's side** (Psalm 119; Psa 56; 86; 123 in batch 1) bears on 10.12 (fear) and 10.9.
- **The other face** (2Ch 17:6; Psa 138:3) goes to Ch 11 §4 (change of character).
