## 1. In short

Mocking is the pride words turned outward into speech and laughter at another. In Proverbs the scoffer is also the one who cannot be taught.

| Group | Verses | What the verses show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I. The scoffer | 23 | will not hear rebuke, hates the one who corrects, seeks wisdom in vain, breeds strife |
| II. Mocking another | 21 | the poor, a father, a prophet, builders, the sufferer; the mocker mocked |
| III. Mocking God's messengers and word | 3 | until there was no remedy; God is not mocked |
| IV. Jesus mocked and laughed at | 14 | foretold, then done: as king, as prophet, as saviour |
| V. God's laughter and derision | 3 | from the heavens, at those set against him and his Anointed |
| VI. Made a byword | 5 | the people become the object of the nations' mockery |
| VII. Other senses | 5 | interpreter, mediator, envoys, stammering; an angel mediator (13.2) |

## 2. The readings

### I. The scoffer: the one who will not be taught (23)

Proverbs paints the scoffer by what he will not receive.
- **Rebuke.**
  - *"A wise son hears his father's instruction, but a scoffer does not listen to rebuke"* (Pro 13:1).
  - *"A scoffer does not like to be reproved; he will not go to the wise"* (15:12). The verse before is *"Sheol and Abaddon lie open before the Lord; how much more the hearts of the children of man!"* (15:11).
- **Hatred of correction.** *"Whoever corrects a scoffer gets himself abuse ... Do not reprove a scoffer, or he will hate you; reprove a wise man, and he will love you"* (Pro 9:7–8). The same rebuke brings out hate in one and love in the other.
- **Wisdom in vain.** *"A scoffer seeks wisdom in vain, but knowledge is easy for a man of understanding"* (Pro 14:6). He seeks, and does not find.
- **Learning by his fall.**
  - *"Strike a scoffer, and the simple will learn prudence"* (19:25).
  - *"When a scoffer is punished, the simple becomes wise"* (21:11).
  - The scoffer is not the one who learns. Others learn from what happens to him.
- **Strife.** *"Drive out a scoffer, and strife will go out, and quarreling and abuse will cease"* (Pro 22:10). *"the scoffer is an abomination to mankind"* (24:9).
- **Justice and the guilt offering.**
  - *"A worthless witness mocks at justice"* (19:28). *"Condemnation is ready for scoffers"* (19:29).
  - *"Fools mock at the guilt offering, but the upright enjoy acceptance"* (14:9). They mock the very means of putting guilt right.
- **Delight, and the offer.** *"How long will scoffers delight in their scoffing ... If you turn at my reproof, behold, I will pour out my spirit to you"* (Pro 1:22–23). Scoffing is a delight held on to, and the offer still stands to the one who turns.
- **Alone.** *"If you are wise, you are wise for yourself; if you scoff, you alone will bear it"* (Pro 9:12).
- **The seat.** *"Blessed is the man who ... nor sits in the seat of scoffers; but his delight is in the law of the Lord"* (Psa 1:1–2). The scoffer's seat is set against delight in the law.
- **The warning.**
  - *"do not scoff, lest your bonds be made strong"*, then *"Give ear, and hear my voice"* (Isa 28:22–23). Again the opposite of scoffing is hearing.
  - *"the scoffer cease ... who by a word make a man out to be an offender, and lay a snare for him who reproves in the gate"* (Isa 29:20–21). The scoffer works against the one who reproves. In the same passage *"The meek shall obtain fresh joy in the Lord"* (29:19).
- **Wine and company.**
  - *"Wine is a mocker, strong drink a brawler, and whoever is led astray by it is not wise"* (Pro 20:1).
  - *"the princes became sick with the heat of wine; he stretched out his hand with mockers"*, and *"none of them calls upon me"* (Hos 7:5, 7).
- **In Job's debates.**
  - Zophar: *"when you mock, shall no one shame you?"* (Job 11:3).
  - Job: *"after I have spoken, mock on"* (21:3).
  - Elihu: *"Beware lest wrath entice you into scoffing"* (36:18).
  - **I read:** the charge of scoffing is thrown between the speakers. As with Eliab in batch 2, the verses do not settle that it is true of Job.
- *"Toward the scorners he is scornful, but to the humble he gives favor"* (Pro 3:34, carried). This is the source of Jam 4:6 and 1Pe 5:5.

### II. Mocking another (21)

- **The poor.** *"Whoever mocks the poor insults his Maker; he who is glad at calamity will not go unpunished"* (Pro 17:5). The mocking reaches past the poor man to the one who made him. This bears on OT-105, the poor as seen by another.
- **A parent.** *"The eye that mocks a father and scorns to obey a mother"* (Pro 30:17).
- **A prophet.** *"some small boys came out of the city and jeered at him, saying, 'Go up, you baldhead!'"* (2Ki 2:23).
- **Builders.**
  - Sanballat *"was angry and greatly enraged, and he jeered at the Jews. And he said ... 'What are these feeble Jews doing?'"* (Neh 4:1–2). Mocking comes with anger, and it belittles.
  - The answer: *"The God of heaven will make us prosper"* (Neh 2:20).
- **The sufferer.**
  - *"My friends scorn me; my eye pours out tears to God"*, after *"my witness is in heaven"* (Job 16:19–20). Scorned by friends, he turns to God.
  - *"All who see me mock me; they make mouths at me; they wag their heads; 'He trusts in the Lord; let him deliver him'"* (Psa 22:7–8). The mockery takes the sufferer's trust as its target. The psalm answers: *"Yet you are he who took me from the womb"* (22:9).
  - *"our enemies laugh among themselves. Restore us, O God of hosts"* (Psa 80:6–7).
  - Jeremiah: *"I have become a laughingstock all the day; everyone mocks me ... the word of the Lord has become for me a reproach and derision ... there is in my heart as it were a burning fire shut up in my bones"* (Jer 20:7–9). He is mocked for the word, and he cannot hold the word in.
- **The half-built tower.** *"all who see it begin to mock him, saying, 'This man began to build and was not able to finish'"* (Luk 14:29). The fear of being mocked is used to call for counting the cost of following Jesus (14:27–28).
- **Might as god.** The Chaldeans: *"At kings they scoff, and at rulers they laugh ... guilty men, whose own might is their god!"* (Hab 1:10–11). Here mocking sits with making one's own might a god.
- **The mocker mocked.** Of Sennacherib, who mocked the Lord: *"She despises you, she scorns you — the virgin daughter of Zion; she wags her head behind you"* (2Ki 19:21; Isa 37:22). Elijah mocks the prophets of Baal: *"Cry aloud, for he is a god ... perhaps he is asleep"*, and *"there was no voice. No one answered"* (1Ki 18:27, 29).
  - **I read:** mocking here exposes a false claim, of a king who rose against the Holy One, and of a god who does not answer.
- **Being mocked as a consequence.** *"Those who are near and those who are far from you will mock you; your name is defiled"* (Eze 22:5).
- **Fear of being taken for a mocker.** Jacob: *"Perhaps my father will feel me, and I shall seem to be mocking him and bring a curse upon myself"* (Gen 27:12). The deception is feared as a mockery of his father.
- **Being made a fool of.** Herod, *"when he saw that he had been tricked by the wise men, became furious"* (Mat 2:16). Being mocked turns to fury. This bears on M02.
- **Scorning payment.** *"you were not like a prostitute, because you scorned payment"*: Jerusalem gave gifts to her lovers instead (Eze 16:31–33).
- **Job's complaint.** *"When disaster brings sudden death, he mocks at the calamity of the innocent"* (Job 9:23). This is Job in anguish, speaking of God. It is held as his complaint, not taken as a statement of what God does.

### III. Mocking God's messengers and word (3)

- *"The Lord ... sent persistently to them by his messengers, because he had compassion on his people ... But they kept mocking the messengers of God, despising his words and scoffing at his prophets, until the wrath of the Lord rose against his people, until there was no remedy"* (2Ch 36:15–16).
  - The mocking answers God's compassion.
  - **I read:** "until there was no remedy" marks mocking the word as the end of the way back.
- Hezekiah's couriers called the people to return: *"they laughed them to scorn and mocked them. However, some men ... humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem"* (2Ch 30:10–11). Mocking and humbling are the two answers to the same call.
- *"Do not be deceived: God is not mocked, for whatever one sows, that will he also reap"* (Gal 6:7).

### IV. Jesus mocked and laughed at (14)

- **Foretold.** *"deliver him over to the Gentiles to be mocked and flogged and crucified, and he will be raised on the third day"* (Mat 20:19; Mar 10:34; Luk 18:32). The disciples *"understood none of these things"* (Luk 18:34).
- **Mocked as prophet.** *"mocking him as they beat him. They also blindfolded him and kept asking him, 'Prophesy! Who is it that struck you?'"* (Luk 22:63–64).
- **Mocked as king.**
  - *"kneeling before him, they mocked him, saying, 'Hail, King of the Jews!'"* (Mat 27:29).
  - Herod *"treated him with contempt and mocked him. Then, arraying him in splendid clothing"* (Luk 23:11). Before this, *"he made no answer"* (23:9).
  - *"when they had mocked him, they stripped him of the robe ... and led him away to crucify him"* (Mat 27:31; Mar 15:20).
- **Mocked as saviour, and in his trust.**
  - *"He saved others; he cannot save himself"* (Mar 15:31).
  - *"He trusts in God; let God deliver him now, if he desires him"* (Mat 27:43). This is the mockery of Psa 22:8 (§II).
  - *"The soldiers also mocked him ... 'If you are the King of the Jews, save yourself!'"* (Luk 23:36–37).
  - Just before: *"Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do"* (23:34).
- **Laughed at.** *"'the girl is not dead but sleeping.' And they laughed at him"* (Mat 9:24; Mar 5:40). *"they laughed at him, knowing that she was dead"* (Luk 8:53). Then the girl arose.
- **I read:**
  - Each mockery takes a true thing as its target: that he is king, that he is prophet, that he saves, that he trusts, that she will rise.
  - *"He saved others; he cannot save himself"* says what is so, but it reads the reason backwards. He does not come down because he is saving others.
  - This bears on 13.1 *Lowering himself*. The one whose majesty the disciples saw (batch 1: Luk 9:43; 2Pe 1:16) is mocked about that same standing, and he answers with *"Father, forgive them"*.

### V. God's laughter and derision (3)

- *"The kings of the earth set themselves ... against the Lord and against his Anointed ... He who sits in the heavens laughs; the Lord holds them in derision"* (Psa 2:2–4). Then: *"As for me, I have set my King on Zion"* (2:6).
- *"There they are, bellowing with their mouths ... for 'Who,' they think, 'will hear us?' But you, O Lord, laugh at them; you hold all the nations in derision"* (Psa 59:7–8).
- Wisdom: *"Because I have called and you refused to listen ... I also will laugh at your calamity; I will mock when terror strikes you"* (Pro 1:24–26).
- With Pro 3:34 (§I): *"Toward the scorners he is scornful"*.

### VI. Made a byword (5)

If they turn to other gods, *"Israel will become a proverb and a byword among all peoples"* (1Ki 9:7; 2Ch 7:20; Deu 28:37). *"a reproach, a byword, a taunt, and a curse"* (Jer 24:9). *"a reproach and a taunt, a warning and a horror"* (Eze 5:15).

The people become the object of mockery. In Jer 24 this sits beside *"I will give them a heart to know me ... for they shall return to me with their whole heart"* (24:7).

### VII. Other senses (5)

- Data: *"an interpreter between them"* (Gen 42:23); *"your mediators transgressed"* (Isa 43:27); *"the envoys of the princes of Babylon"* (2Ch 32:31); *"stammering in a tongue"* (Isa 33:19).
- 2Ch 32:31 is read for the heart elsewhere (10.11): *"God left him to himself, in order to test him and to know all that was in his heart"*. Its M08 word is only "envoys".
- *"If there be for him an angel, a mediator, one of the thousand, to declare to man what is right for him"* (Job 33:23). This is held for 13.2. The M08-tagged word means "mediator" here.

## 3. For Ch 13: the mocking words used of God, and of the Son

1. **A pride word is used of God here.** *lāʿag* "mock" and *lîṣ* "scorn" are said of God:
   - *"the Lord holds them in derision"* (Psa 2:4)
   - *"you hold all the nations in derision"* (Psa 59:8)
   - *"Toward the scorners he is scornful"* (Pro 3:34)
   - wisdom: *"I will mock when terror strikes you"* (Pro 1:26)
2. **The verses show how God's derision differs from human mocking.**
   - **Where it comes from.** It comes from the one who *"sits in the heavens"* (Psa 2:4), from the height that batches 1–3 found is his by nature.
   - **What it answers.** It always answers a rising against him: kings set *"against the Lord and against his Anointed"* (Psa 2:2), those of whom it says *"'Who,' they think, 'will hear us?'"* (Psa 59:7), the scorners (Pro 3:34), those who *"refused to listen"* (Pro 1:24).
   - **Whom it never targets.** In these verses it is never aimed at the weak, the poor or the sufferer. Human mocking is aimed at exactly those: the poor (Pro 17:5), a father (Pro 30:17), the sufferer (Psa 22:7), builders called *"feeble"* (Neh 4:2).
   - **I read:** God's derision is the laughter of the undisputed height at a dispute that cannot succeed. Human mockery is a self-made height looking down on someone made low.
3. **God is not mocked.** *"Do not be deceived: God is not mocked"* (Gal 6:7). The mocking of his messengers goes on *"until there was no remedy"* (2Ch 36:16). This is the batch 1 point again: God's standing is challenged, and is not overturned.
4. **The Son is mocked, and does not answer in kind.**
   - The one with the undisputed standing is mocked on that very standing: *"Hail, King of the Jews!"* (Mat 27:29); *"He saved others; he cannot save himself"* (Mar 15:31).
   - He *"made no answer"* (Luk 23:9), and he prays *"Father, forgive them"* (Luk 23:34).
   - **I read:** in the Son, the majesty of batch 1 does not defend itself against mockery. This is the clearest instance of 13.1 *Lowering himself* in these words. It joins the batch 1 point that his majesty and his being *"delivered into the hands of men"* stand side by side (Luk 9:43–44).
5. **Mocking exposes a false height.** Zion scorns Sennacherib (2Ki 19:21). Elijah mocks a god who does not answer (1Ki 18:27). Here the mocking does not come from pride. It names a claim that is empty.

## 4. Points for the account

- **The scoffer and hearing:** the scoffer's mark is refusing rebuke (Pro 13:1; 15:12; 9:7–8). With batch 1 (Jer 13:15) and batch 2 (Pro 13:10), the M08 words keep setting pride against hearing. This bears on 10.2 *Humility and hearing*.
- **Mocking the poor** (Pro 17:5) goes to OT-105.
- **The sufferer's side of mockery** (Psa 22; Job 16; Jer 20) bears on 10.9, 10.12 and Ch 6.
- **Jesus mocked** goes to 13.1 *Lowering himself*. **God's derision** goes to 13.1 with the batch 1–4 reflection.
- **Fury at being mocked** (Mat 2:16) and **anger with jeering** (Neh 4:1) bear on OT-103 (M02).
