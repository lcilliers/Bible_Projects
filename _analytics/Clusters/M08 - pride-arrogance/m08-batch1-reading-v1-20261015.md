## 1. In short

The same words are used of people and of God. ESV renders them "pride", "arrogance" and "pomp" for people, and "majesty", "triumphed gloriously" and "glorious" for God. The gloss of H1342 is "to rise up". The verses group by whose rising it is, and by where it comes from:

| Group | Verses | Whose rising | What the verses join to it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I. Pride held in the heart and said | 10 | a person or people | the heart, speech, a fall |
| II. Pride that does not listen or seek | 7 | a person or people, before God | not hearing, not returning, not seeking, an unanswered cry |
| III. Pride set over another | 15 | the proud against the poor, the righteous, the widow | contempt, pursuit, traps, words |
| IV. What a people takes pride in, brought down | 21 | a nation's might, splendour, sanctuary, ornament | broken, ended, brought to Sheol |

## 2. The readings

### I. Pride held in the heart and said (10)

**Pride is placed in the heart, and it speaks.**
- *"who say in pride and in arrogance of heart: 'The bricks have fallen, but we will build with dressed stones'"* (Isa 9:9–10).
- What pride says here is a reply to a blow. The Lord had *"sent a word against Jacob"* (9:8). Pride answers the fallen bricks with better stone.
- **I read:** pride here does not read the blow as a word from God. It reads it as a setback to be outbuilt.

**The words pile up when pride is named in full.** *"We have heard of the pride of Moab — he is very proud — of his loftiness, his pride, and his arrogance, and the haughtiness of his heart"* (Jer 48:29). The next verse joins it to speech: *"his boasts are false"* (48:30). Isa 16:6 gives the same list and ends *"in his idle boasting he is not right"*.

**Pride is shown as well as held.**
- *"Therefore pride is their necklace; violence covers them as a garment"* (Psa 73:6). It is worn where it can be seen.
- The "therefore" points back to ease: *"they have no pangs until death ... They are not in trouble as others are"* (73:4–5).
- The next line is the heart: *"their hearts overflow with follies"* (73:7).

**Pride carries its own outcome.**
- *"Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall"* (Pro 16:18).
- *"One's pride will bring him low"* (Pro 29:23, carried from M09). Here pride itself is the subject that brings low.
- *"It is better to be of a lowly spirit with the poor than to divide the spoil with the proud"* (Pro 16:19, carried). Pride is pictured sharing out spoil.

**Wisdom and God hate it.** *"The fear of the Lord is hatred of evil. Pride and arrogance and the way of evil and perverted speech I hate"* (Pro 8:13). Pride stands in a list with the way of evil and perverted speech.

**Its removal is a gift.** *"I will remove from your midst your proudly exultant ones, and you shall no longer be haughty in my holy mountain. But I will leave in your midst a people humble and lowly"* (Zep 3:11–12).

**A head that cannot be lifted.** Job: *"If I am in the right, I cannot lift up my head ... And were my head lifted up, you would hunt me like a lion"* (Job 10:15–16). The word is the same rising. Job feels that any rising of his own head before God would be hunted down.

### II. Pride that does not listen or seek (7)

These verses join pride to the turning away from God.
- **Pride is a witness against its owner.** *"The pride of Israel testifies to his face; yet they do not return to the Lord their God, nor seek him, for all this"* (Hos 7:10; also Hos 5:5).
- **It goes with not knowing.** The verse before says *"Strangers devour his strength, and he knows it not; gray hairs are sprinkled upon him, and he knows it not"* (Hos 7:9). In Hos 5:4–5, *"they know not the Lord"* stands next to *"The pride of Israel testifies to his face"*.
- **Pride does not listen.**
  - *"Hear and give ear; be not proud, for the Lord has spoken"* (Jer 13:15). The opposite of pride here is hearing.
  - Then: *"if you will not listen, my soul will weep in secret for your pride"* (13:17).
  - The pride God will "spoil" belongs to a people *"who refuse to hear my words, who stubbornly follow their own heart"* (13:9–10).
- **Pride leaves a cry unanswered.**
  - *"There they cry out, but he does not answer, because of the pride of evil men"* (Job 35:12).
  - The cry has no turning to God in it: *"none says, 'Where is God my Maker'"* (35:10).
  - **I read:** this cry is a cry of pain that does not seek God.
- **God works to keep pride from a man.** He speaks *"in a dream ... that he may turn man aside from his deed and conceal pride from a man; he keeps back his soul from the pit"* (Job 33:15–18). God's warning is aimed at pride before it ripens.
- **The wicked does not see.** *"If favor is shown to the wicked, he does not learn righteousness ... and does not see the majesty of the Lord"* (Isa 26:10). Not seeing God's majesty sits in the same verse as not learning from his favour.

### III. Pride set over another (15)

In these verses pride is not alone. It stands over someone weaker or someone righteous.
- **The poor:**
  - *"In arrogance the wicked hotly pursue the poor"* (Psa 10:2).
  - Sodom's guilt was *"pride, excess of food, and prosperous ease, but did not aid the poor and needy"* (Eze 16:49). Pride is listed with plenty and ease, and with what was not done for others.
- **The widow:** *"The Lord tears down the house of the proud but maintains the widow's boundaries"* (Pro 15:25). Pride's house is set against the boundary of the one without defence.
- **The righteous:**
  - Lips *"which speak insolently against the righteous in pride and contempt"* (Psa 31:18).
  - *"The arrogant have hidden a trap for me"* (Psa 140:5).
  - *"Let not the foot of arrogance come upon me"* (Psa 36:11). Pride is pictured as a foot that treads.
- **The heart closed to pity.** *"They close their hearts to pity; with their mouths they speak arrogantly"* (Psa 17:10). A closed heart and an arrogant mouth sit side by side.
- **The one who bears it.**
  - *"Our soul has had more than enough of the scorn of those who are at ease, of the contempt of the proud"* (Psa 123:4).
  - The psalm's answer is the eyes of a servant looking to the master's hand *"till he has mercy upon us"* (123:2).
  - This is pride as it is felt by the one under it.
- **Another's fall in one's mouth.** *"Was not your sister Sodom a byword in your mouth in the day of your pride, before your wickedness was uncovered?"* (Eze 16:56–57). In the day of its pride, Jerusalem talked of Sodom's fall while its own wickedness was still covered.
- **Taunting God's people.** Moab and Ammon's lot comes *"in return for their pride, because they taunted and boasted against the people of the Lord"* (Zep 2:10).
- **Repaid.** *"repay to the proud what they deserve"* (Psa 94:2). The proud *"pour out their arrogant words"* and *"crush your people"* (94:4–5). *"The Lord preserves the faithful but abundantly repays the one who acts in pride"* (Psa 31:23). *"let them be trapped in their pride"* (Psa 59:12).
- Carried from M09: Isa 13:11 ("the pomp of the arrogant") and Job 22:29.

### IV. What a people takes pride in, brought down (21)

Here the word names a thing more than a bearing: what a people stands on.
- **Might.**
  - *"the pride of your power"* (Lev 26:19).
  - Egypt's *"proud might"* (Eze 30:6, 18) and Israel's *"proud might"* (Eze 33:28), whose people *"rely on the sword"* (33:26).
  - *"the pride of the strong"* (Eze 7:24).
- **Splendour and pomp.**
  - Babylon, *"the splendor and pomp of the Chaldeans"* (Isa 13:19).
  - Tyre: *"to defile the pompous pride of all glory, to dishonor all the honored of the earth"* (Isa 23:9).
  - *"the proud crown of the drunkards of Ephraim ... the fading flower of its glorious beauty"* (Isa 28:1, 3).
- **Strongholds.** *"I abhor the pride of Jacob and hate his strongholds"* (Amo 6:8). In the passage they lie at ease and are *"not grieved over the ruin of Joseph"* (6:6).
- **What God gave, used for pride.**
  - *"His beautiful ornament they used for pride, and they made their abominable images and their detestable things of it"* (Eze 7:20).
  - *"Behold, I will profane my sanctuary, the pride of your power, the delight of your eyes, and the yearning of your soul"* (Eze 24:21). God's own sanctuary had become what they gloried in. It sits with delight and yearning: a pride, a delight and a longing all set on one thing.
- **Brought down.**
  - *"Your pomp is brought down to Sheol"* (Isa 14:11). The passage names the heart behind it: *"You said in your heart, 'I will ascend to heaven ... I will set my throne on high'"* (14:13).
  - The pride of Egypt (Eze 32:12), Assyria (Zec 10:11) and Philistia (Zec 9:6).
- Carried from M09 (the proud brought low): Isa 25:11, Isa 2:12, Dan 4:37, Job 40:11–12.

**I read:** in this group, "pride" is often the standing a people holds, such as might, wealth, strongholds or a sanctuary, as much as an attitude. The verses join the two. The thing stood on is broken because it is stood on against God or instead of him (Lev 26:18–19 "if ... you will not listen"; Eze 33:26 "You rely on the sword").

### V. Pride given: the honour God gives (7)

The same word names a standing that God gives his people.
- *"He chose our heritage for us, the pride of Jacob whom he loves"* (Psa 47:4).
- *"the fruit of the land shall be the pride and honor of the survivors of Israel"* (Isa 4:2).
- To a city *"forsaken and hated"*: *"I will make you majestic forever, a joy from age to age"* (Isa 60:15).
- *"the Lord is restoring the majesty of Jacob as the majesty of Israel"* (Nah 2:2).
- The Lord is *"the sword of your triumph"* (Deu 33:29): Israel's "pride" is the Lord himself.
- *"The Lord has sworn by the pride of Jacob: 'Surely I will never forget any of their deeds'"* (Amo 8:7). The oath follows the buying of *"the poor for silver"* (8:6). The verse does not say what "the pride of Jacob" refers to. **I read:** it may be Jacob's arrogance, sworn by in irony, or the honour God gave Jacob. The verse is held, not settled.
- God's *"proudly exulting ones"* are the warriors he summons to carry out his anger (Isa 13:3).

So the word is not bad in itself. Where it is chosen, given or restored by God, it is honour. Where it is held against him, or over others, it is pride. Batch 1 shows the turn in Eze 7:20 and 24:21: what was given becomes what is gloried in.

### VI. The majesty of God (22)

- **Shown in an act.**
  - *"I will sing to the Lord, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea"* (Exo 15:1, 21).
  - *"In the greatness of your majesty you overthrow your adversaries"* (15:7).
  - The enemy's own rising is quoted in the same song: *"I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil, my desire shall have its fill of them"* (15:9).
- **Worn.** *"The Lord reigns; he is robed in majesty ... you are from everlasting"* (Psa 93:1–2).
- **Turned toward help.** *"There is none like God ... who rides through the heavens to your help, through the skies in his majesty"* (Deu 33:26). *"whose majesty is over Israel ... he is the one who gives power and strength to his people"* (Psa 68:34–35).
- **Heard and praised.** *"he thunders with his majestic voice"* (Job 37:4). *"he has done gloriously"* (Isa 12:5). *"over the majesty of the Lord they shout"* (Isa 24:14).
- **Hidden from.**
  - *"Enter into the rock and hide in the dust from before the terror of the Lord, and from the splendor of his majesty"* (Isa 2:10, 19, 21).
  - In the same passage: *"The haughty looks of man shall be brought low, and the lofty pride of men shall be humbled, and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day"* (2:11, 17).
  - It closes with *"Stop regarding man in whose nostrils is breath, for of what account is he?"* (2:22).
- **A limit on what rises.** The sea: *"here shall your proud waves be stayed"* (Job 38:11). *"You rule the raging of the sea; when its waves rise, you still them"* (Psa 89:9).
- **The challenge to Job.**
  - *"Adorn yourself with majesty and dignity; clothe yourself with glory and splendor. Pour out the overflowings of your anger, and look on everyone who is proud and abase him ... Then will I also acknowledge to you that your own right hand can save you"* (Job 40:10–14).
  - Majesty is joined to what only God does: abasing every proud one, and saving by his own hand.
- **Lent to the one he sends.** The ruler from Bethlehem *"shall stand and shepherd his flock in the strength of the Lord, in the majesty of the name of the Lord his God"* (Mic 5:4). He stands in the majesty of God's name, not in a majesty of his own.
- **The Son.**
  - *"all were astonished at the majesty of God"* at the healing of the boy. At once Jesus says: *"The Son of Man is about to be delivered into the hands of men"* (Luk 9:43–44). The majesty and the handing over stand side by side.
  - *"we were eyewitnesses of his majesty. For when he received honor and glory from God the Father"* (2Pe 1:16–17). His majesty is described as received.
  - *"After making purification for sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high"* (Heb 1:3; also 8:1). "Majesty" is used here as a name for God.
  - *"to the only God, our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, dominion, and authority"* (Jude 25).
- **A majesty held up by people.** Artemis may *"be deposed from her magnificence, she whom all Asia and the world worship"* (Act 19:27). This verse is data for the inner being. It is kept only for the reflection in §3.

### VII. Other senses (10, data)

- the "thicket" or "jungle" of the Jordan (Jer 12:5; 49:19; 50:44; Zec 11:3)
- water risen (Eze 47:5)
- papyrus that grows (Job 8:11)
- the sea's "swelling" (Psa 46:3)
- a column of smoke (Isa 9:18)
- Leviathan's "back" (Job 41:15) and the fool's "rod for his back" (Pro 14:3). ESV renders "back" in both, with no pride in the English.

## 3. For Ch 13: are the pride words indicative of God's character?

The researcher's question, verbatim: *"are any of the pride words indicative of their characteristics? I suspect there is difference between the 'pride','most high' of God, which is not an assumed position but a undisputed state, verses IB pride that is mostly assumed."*

**What the batch 1 verses state:**
1. **The same words are used of God and of people.** *gāʾôn*, *gaʾăwāh*, *gēʾût* and *gāʾāh* name God's majesty (Exo 15:1, 7; Psa 93:1; Isa 2:10) and human pride (Isa 9:9; Jer 48:29; Pro 16:18). So yes: these words describe a characteristic of God. In ESV English the difference shows in the choice of word ("majesty" against "pride"), not in the Hebrew.
2. **God's majesty is his own and is not taken on.** He is *"robed in majesty"* while *"you are from everlasting"* (Psa 93:1–2). Nothing in these verses shows it being acquired. It is shown in acts: the sea, the overthrow, the thunder.
3. **It is challenged, but it is not overturned.** The researcher's word "undisputed" needs one qualification from the verses. God's majesty *is* disputed: by the enemy at the sea (Exo 15:9), by *"all that is proud and lofty"* (Isa 2:12), and by the one who says *"I will ascend to heaven"* (Isa 14:13). Each time the dispute ends with the one who rose brought down. So it is not uncontested; it is never overturned.
4. **Majesty goes with what only God can do.** Job 40:10–14 makes the test plain: whoever wears majesty must abase every proud one and save by his own hand. Only God does.
5. **Human pride in these verses is mostly a standing that is taken, or kept against God.**
   - It is said in the heart against the facts: *"the bricks have fallen, but we will build"* (Isa 9:10).
   - It is worn as a show (Psa 73:6).
   - It is set over the weak (Psa 10:2; Eze 16:49; Pro 15:25).
   - It refuses to hear (Jer 13:15–17).
   - It stands on might and strongholds (Lev 26:19; Amo 6:8; Eze 33:26).
   - This agrees with the researcher's "mostly assumed".
6. **But the same word for people is not always assumed.** God *gives* a "pride" to his people: chosen (Psa 47:4), given (Isa 4:2), made (Isa 60:15), restored (Nah 2:2). The Lord himself is their *"triumph"* (Deu 33:29). The verses also show a given honour turned into pride (Eze 7:20; 24:21).
   - **I read:** the line the verses draw is not between God having majesty and people having none. It runs between a standing that is one's own by nature (God), a standing given by God (his people), and a standing taken, or turned against the giver (pride).
7. **The Son's majesty is described as received**, and it stands beside his being handed over.
   - *"he received honor and glory from God the Father"* (2Pe 1:17).
   - *"after making purification for sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty"* (Heb 1:3).
   - At the very moment of *"the majesty of God"*, he speaks of being *"delivered into the hands of men"* (Luk 9:43–44).
   - This joins 13.1 *Lowering himself* (M09): the one with majesty did not hold onto it in the way pride holds.
   - **I read:** where Php 2:6–9 joins the two, it lies outside this batch's words (*hyperypsoō* is M22, §5 of the overview).
8. **A majesty that depends on people is the contrast.** Artemis's "magnificence" can be "deposed", because it rests on *"she whom all Asia and the world worship"* (Act 19:27). **I read:** this is the borrowed standing that pride is, set against God's majesty, which no one confers.

These points are for 13.1. They are not woven. They will be tested against batches 2–5. "Most High" (H5945B *ʿelyôn* as a title) is not an M08 word. The B-scan height verses that say God is "on high" were set aside as data, and are noted for 13.1 in the B-scan CSV.

## 4. Points for later batches and for the account

- **A pride/majesty account will need its home.** Ch 12 already holds *Pride and the lifted-up heart and spirit*, and 10.14 holds *The proud brought low*. Placement is for after batch 5, as in M09.
- **Change of character (#1919).** The same word moves between majesty (God), honour given (his people), the thing gloried in (might, sanctuary, ornament) and pride (a bearing over others or against God). What changes it, in these verses, is the source and the direction: from God or taken, toward God or against him.
- **Pride and not hearing** (Jer 13:15–17) bears on 10.2 *Humility and hearing* (M09).
- **Pride over the poor and the widow** (Psa 10:2; Eze 16:49; Pro 15:25) bears on OT-105 (the poor as seen by another).
- **The M72 forward mark** Psa 36:11, "the foot of arrogance", is read here (group III).
