## 2. The answer to the Ch 13 question

**Are any of the pride words indicative of God's characteristics?** Yes.
- The *gāʾôn* family is used of God's majesty: "majesty", "triumphed gloriously", "robed in majesty" (Exo 15:1, 7; Psa 93:1; Isa 2:10).
- "Majesty" is used as a name for God (Heb 1:3; 8:1).
- The mocking words are used of God's derision: *"He who sits in the heavens laughs"* (Psa 2:4); *"Toward the scorners he is scornful"* (Pro 3:34).
- God is never the subject of a boast in these verses. He is its right object: *"let him who boasts boast in this, that he understands and knows me"* (Jer 9:24).

**Does the researcher's distinction hold in the verses?** Largely, yes, with two refinements. What the verses state:

1. **God's height is his own, shown in acts, and not overturned.**
   - *"robed in majesty ... you are from everlasting"* (Psa 93:1–2). *"In the greatness of your majesty you overthrow your adversaries"* (Exo 15:7).
   - Majesty goes with what only God does: abasing every proud one and saving by his own hand (Job 40:10–14).
   - *"God is not mocked"* (Gal 6:7).
2. **Human pride, in the verses' own words, is a standing taken or assumed.**
   - *"you make your heart like the heart of a god"* (Eze 28:2).
   - *"takes his seat in the temple of God, proclaiming himself to be God"* (2Th 2:4).
   - *"By the strength of my hand I have done it"* (Isa 10:13).
   - *"if anyone thinks he is something, when he is nothing, he deceives himself"* (Gal 6:3).
3. **Mostly, pride holds as its own what was given.**
   - *"What do you have that you did not receive? If then you received it, why do you boast as if you did not receive it?"* (1Co 4:7).
   - In story: Uzziah's strength, Hezekiah's healing, Nebuchadnezzar's God-given majesty, the cherub's beauty, the cedar's height (2Ch 26:15–16; 32:24–25; Dan 5:18–20; Eze 28:17; 31:9–10).
   - This matches the researcher's "mostly assumed": the standing is real, but it is taken up as if it were not received.

**Refinement 1: "undisputed" in the sense of never overturned.** God's majesty *is* disputed: by the enemy at the sea (Exo 15:9), by *"all that is proud and lofty"* (Isa 2:12), and by the kings *"against the Lord and against his Anointed"* (Psa 2:2). Every dispute ends with the one who rose brought down, and God's derision is his answer to it (Psa 2:4).

**Refinement 2: the same words for people are not always pride.**
- God *gives* a "pride" or majesty to his people: chosen (Psa 47:4), given (Isa 4:2), made (Isa 60:15), restored (Nah 2:2).
- A heart can be *"courageous [lifted up] in the ways of the Lord"* (2Ch 17:6).
- The boast can be moved to God (1Co 1:31; Rom 5:2–3, 11).
- The measure assigned by God is the right measure (Rom 12:3; 2Co 10:13).
- **I read:** the verses draw the line by source and direction, not by the word. There is height that is one's own by nature (God), height given by God (his people, in his ways), and height taken, held against the giver, or set over others (pride).

**What God's height does, as against what pride does.**

| God's height | Human pride |
| --- | --- |
| Turns toward help (Deu 33:26) | Turns away from God: *"does not seek him"* (Psa 10:4) |
| Regards the lowly (Psa 138:6) | Stands over the poor and the widow (Psa 10:2; Pro 15:25) |
| Gives lifting up (Psa 75:6–7) | Takes lifting up |
| Its derision answers only those who rise against it (Psa 2; 59; Pro 1; 3:34) | Mocks the weak, the poor and the sufferer (Pro 17:5; Psa 22:7) |
| Is known through God's answer to arrogance (Exo 18:11) | Will not be taught or rebuked (Pro 13:1; 15:12) |

**The Son.**
- His majesty is *"received ... from God the Father"* (2Pe 1:17).
- It stands beside his being *"delivered into the hands of men"* (Luk 9:43–44).
- His seat is taken *"after making purification for sins"* (Heb 1:3).
- He is preeminent because the fullness dwells in him (Col 1:18–19).
- He is mocked on that very standing (*"Hail, King of the Jews!"*), and he answers *"Father, forgive them"* (Luk 23:34).
- **I read:** in the Son, majesty that is undisputed does not defend itself against mockery. This bears on 13.1 *Lowering himself* (M09) and on the researcher's ruling that Jesus' own acts get their 13.1 face (#2148).

**Other beings, held and not read in:**
- Leviathan, *"king over all the sons of pride"* (Job 41:34): 13.4.
- A messenger of Satan given against conceit (2Co 12:7), and *"the condemnation of the devil"* (1Ti 3:6): 13.3.
- Angels who do not blaspheme where the willful do (2Pe 2:11), and an angel mediator (Job 33:23): 13.2.

## 3. The main threads for the account (for when weaving is directed)

1. **Given, then held as one's own:** 1Co 4:7, and the kings' accounts. This bears on Ch 12 claim C-209, which now has much wider support.
2. **Pride against hearing:** Jer 13:15–17; Pro 13:10; the scoffer who will not be rebuked (Pro 13:1; 15:12; 9:7–8). This bears on 10.2 *Humility and hearing*.
3. **Presumption:** acting where not sent, speaking what was not commanded (Deu 1:43; 18:20). This bears on 10.7.
4. **Pride over others:** the poor, the widow, the righteous; contempt; mocking the poor (Pro 17:5). This bears on 10.10 and OT-105.
5. **The boast moved to God,** and boasting in weakness (Jer 9:23–24; 1Co 1:31; 2Co 12:9). This bears on Ch 12 claim C-182 and Ch 11 §4.
6. **Pride against love** (1Co 8:1; 13:4), against rest (Hab 2:5 against Psa 131:2), and in place of mourning (1Co 5:2).
7. **The other face** (2Ch 17:6; Psa 138:3; Psa 47:4; Isa 60:15) goes to Ch 11 §4, as a change of character.
