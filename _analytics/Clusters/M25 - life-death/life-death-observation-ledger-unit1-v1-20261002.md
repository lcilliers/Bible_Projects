## C. Change of character: what differs between the faces (#1919, for Ch 11 §4)

- **"To live" (H2421)** moves between:
  - duration ("lived 930 years")
  - survival ("that we may live and not die")
  - being let live by another (Exo 1; Jos 9)
  - recovery from sickness
  - the return of life to the dead (1Ki 17; 2Ki 13)
  - the revival of spirit, heart and soul (Isa 57:15; Psa 69:32)
  - life given by the word and asked for in prayer (Psa 119)
  - life as the outcome of turning (Eze 18; 33)

  **What differs is what life is set against.** It may be set against death by hunger, a sword, sickness, a crushed spirit or a heart worn down, or against "the death of the wicked". The word does not change; what threatens it does. *(R: I read the contrast from the verse's own pairing, e.g. "that we may live and not die", "turn, and live … why will you die".)*
- **"Breath" (H5397 / H5301 / H6315)** has four faces:
  - life given (Gen 2:7)
  - understanding (Job 32:8; Pro 20:27)
  - force and wrath (Job 4:9)
  - what a person breathes out: lies, contempt, longing (Pro 14:5; Psa 10:5; Psa 12:5)

  The same breath that God gives comes back out as lies or longing. *(R: the verbs are the same root group, but no verse sets them side by side; this is noted as a range of the word, not as a teaching.)*
- **"Make alive" (Greek)** moves from:
  - the body (Rom 8:11, "mortal bodies")
  - to Christ's own death and life (1Pe 3:18, "in the spirit")
  - to those "dead in your trespasses" (Col 2:13)

  The last is not the body's death.

## D. The researcher's questions: what unit 1 shows

These are partial. Units 2 and 3, and the focused pulls for Q2–Q4, follow.

- **Q1 What is death?**
  - The breath is gone: "Everything … in whose nostrils was the breath of life died" (Gen 7:22); "no breath left in him" (1Ki 17:17).
  - God holds both ends: "I kill and I make alive" (Deu 32:39).
  - Scripture also calls the living dead: "dead in your trespasses" (Col 2:13; Eph 2:5).
  - It is asked whether death ends life: "If a man dies, shall he live again?" (Job 14:14).
  - Unit 2 (die, death) is the main source.
- **Q2 Spirit and soul at death.**
  - Unit 1 has the life coming back *into* a body: "the life of the child came into him again" (1Ki 17:22).
  - It has a soul brought up from Sheol while the person is alive: "you have brought up my soul from Sheol" (Psa 30:3).
  - It has Christ "put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit" (1Pe 3:18).
  - It has no verse on where the spirit or soul goes. A focused pull of soul and spirit with the death words is needed.
- **Q3 The heart after death.**
  - **Unit 1 has no verse on the heart after death.**
  - The nearest are the living heart revived (Isa 57:15; Psa 69:32) and the wish "May your hearts live forever!" (Psa 22:26), which is not about death.
  - A focused pull (heart with die, death or Sheol) is needed before anything is said.
- **Q4 Body and resurrection.**
  - "their bodies shall rise" (Isa 26:19)
  - bones, sinews, flesh, skin, then breath (Eze 37:5–6)
  - "give life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit" (Rom 8:11)
  - "What you sow does not come to life unless it dies" (1Cor 15:36)
  - "the last Adam became a life-giving spirit" (1Cor 15:45)
