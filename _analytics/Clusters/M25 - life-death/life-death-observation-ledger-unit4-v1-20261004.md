## C. Change of character: what differs between the faces (#1919, for Ch 11 §4)

- **Who rouses, and whom.** God rouses himself (Zec 2:13). People call on God to rouse himself (Psa 44:23). God rouses a human spirit (Ezr 1:1). God rouses people against people (2Ch 21:16; Eze 23:22). A person rouses himself, or does not (Isa 64:7). Hatred rouses strife (Pro 10:12). An angel rouses a prophet from sleep (Zec 4:1). Love is not to be roused before its time (Song 2:7).
- **Toward what.** The same verb rouses toward building (Ezr 1:5; Hag 1:14), toward destruction (Jer 51:1, 11), toward praise (Psa 57:8; Judg 5:12), toward hearing (Isa 50:4), and toward strife (Pro 10:12).
- **What cannot be roused.** The dead man "till the heavens are no more" (Job 14:12), and the idol with "no breath at all in it" (Hab 2:19). Leviathan, whom "no one is so fierce that he dares to stir … up" (Job 41:10), stands outside the inner being and stays in the data.
- **Held back.** God "did not stir up all his wrath" (Psa 78:38): the verb's power is shown by its withholding.

## F. Proposed weave (after approval)

**What emerges.** Three things come out of this unit that the narrative does not yet have:
1. **Sleep and waking as Scripture's own image across life and death.** Death is a sleep not roused (Job 14:12). Jesus and Paul speak of death as sleep (already in Ch 2). God is called to wake when life is sought (Psa 44:23). The resurrection is a waking (Dan 12:2; Isa 26:19; Psa 17:15, already in Ch 2 under other headings).
2. **The cry to God when life is sought** is a movement of the inner being of its own, with its own words ("Why are you sleeping, O Lord?"), different from asking for life (Ch 2 "Life asked for") and from the anguish of facing death (Ch 2 "Facing death").
3. **Life kept by what God holds back** (Psa 78:38): life preserved because wrath is not stirred.

**Decisions for the researcher:**
1. **A new Ch 2 section, "Sleep and waking"** (recommended), drawing on the verses' own image: Job 14:7–15 read whole (LD-143); death as sleep (moved from "What death is", where it is now one paragraph); the cry "Awake! Why are you sleeping, O Lord?" with its settings (LD-147); and the waking of the dead (Dan 12:2; Isa 26:19; Psa 17:15, pointed to, not moved). The alternative is to put LD-143 in "What death is" and LD-147 in "Facing death". I recommend the new section, because the image runs through all of these verses and is lost if they are split.
2. **Isa 14:9–11 (LD-144).** Hold it for OT-03, and weave it only in Ch 12, under pride ("Your pomp is brought down to Sheol") (recommended). The alternative is to put it in Ch 2 "After death" as a third kind of verse, with its genre stated.
3. **Psa 121:4 beside Psa 44:23**, unreconciled (recommended), the way LD-46 and LD-47 stand. The alternative is to leave Psa 121:4 out, since it is another word.
4. **H6974** (*qûṣ*, "to awake"; tagged T3, not M25). Pull and read it as a short supplement to this unit before weaving (recommended), because it carries the resurrection verses and the first half of Job 14:12 ("he will not awake"). Decision 1 depends on it. The alternative is to leave it for the Q2 focused pull.
