# Why `perek` (ruthlessness) was excluded from the study — research finding

- **File:** research/investigations/wa-perek-ruthlessness-registry-exclusion-20260628.md · 2026-06-28 · Author: Claude Code.
- **Question (researcher):** why does `ruthlessly` (`perek` H6531) have no cluster assignment / no study term?
- **Trigger:** Lev 25:43 raw extract — `perek` is central to the verse but untracked; the verse's own lexical flagged it as an un-accounted coverage gap.

## Finding (structural, not accidental)
1. **`perek` was never extracted as a term.** Zero rows in `mti_terms`, `wa_term_inventory`, `wa_term_root_family`. It is not an OWNER and not even an XREF — it has no study identity at all.
2. **The registry (215 words) has no anchor for cruelty / ruthlessness / harshness / severity.** Scan of the registry:
   - *negative dispositions present:* anger, wrath, bitterness, malice, contempt, pride.
   - *power register present:* strength, power, authority, dominion (all C20).
   - *cruelty / ruthlessness / harshness / severity / brutality:* **none.**
3. Because the study is anchored on ~215 **English inner-life words → Strong's**, a Hebrew word with no matching English anchor is never pulled. `perek` (abstract noun, "harsh/cruel treatment") had no anchor → never extracted.
4. **Even the adjacent "ruthless" word was seen but not promoted.** `arits` (H6184, "violent/ruthless one") appears 7× as a *related word* of dread/oppression owner terms (H6206 dread, H4637 oppression, H6178) — glimpsed at the edge of the study, never made a term.
5. So `perek` survives only as a **ride-along span** in verses pulled for other terms (`radah`, `yare`, `abad`…). The cruelty is in the corpus; it has no home in the model.

## Significance
- **A real inner-being characteristic is missing from the study's vocabulary: cruelty / the will-to-harm.** It is not a marginal word — it is the inner disposition that turns dominion into oppression (Exo 1:13; Lev 25:43,46,53; Eze 34:4), the very thing the Law and Prophets condemn.
- This is a **registry-completeness gap**, and it shows the methods are complementary:
  - the **cluster method** (registry-anchored) was **structurally blind** to cruelty — no anchor word, so no cluster, so no digestion;
  - the **verse-fanout method** surfaces it immediately, because the word is present in the verse regardless of whether it has a registry home.
- Note the contrast within this one verse: `yare` (fear) is richly digested (M01); `radah` (dominion) has a home but is un-worked (M23 not started); `perek` (cruelty) has **no home at all**. The fan-out reaches all three; the registry reaches only the first two.

## Recommendation (for researcher decision — not actioned)
- **Candidate registry addition:** a cruelty/ruthlessness/harshness inner-being word, anchoring `perek` (H6531) and likely `arits` (H6184) + kin. This would give the cruelty disposition a study home and let the cluster layer digest it (as it did fear).
- Alternatively, treat cruelty as **emergent from the verse-fanout** (the new method) without forcing it back into the registry — consistent with "characteristics emerge, not pre-listed." 
- Either way: **flag `perek`/cruelty as a known coverage gap** so it is not silently treated as "nothing here."

## Related
- The `ebed` lesson (role-noun ≠ operation) — opposite case: `ebed` correctly excluded; `perek` wrongly absent. The test is whether the word names an **inner-being operation/disposition** (cruelty = yes).
- Links to the broader term-anchoring limitation: the registry boundary determines what the cluster method can see.
