# Reading Lev 25:44–45 — a verse that corroborates but is NOT in our corpus

> ## ⛔ RETRACTED — 2026-06-27
> **The central conclusion of this document (§4–§5: that `ebed` is an "untracked carrier" and the corpus has a coverage gap) is WRONG and has been withdrawn.**
> The researcher's correction: **`ebed` (slave/servant) is a role-NOUN — a *type of person*, not an inner-being operation.** Treating it as IB-operative would force "child", "father", "son", "stranger" to become IB words too. The IB operation is the **verb** `abad` (to *enslave* — a special form of control over another), which **we already track (M36)**.
> Therefore Lev 25:44–45 ("you may *buy* slaves… they may be your *property*") describe a legal status and a transaction over persons-as-property — **no IB operation** — and the corpus **correctly excluded them.** There is **no corpus gap and no missing carrier** here.
> What survives: the *reading in §3* (the passage permits the institution but forbids ruthless rule over a brother) remains useful as background to Lev 25:43/46, which ARE in the corpus. The real cause of 44–45 appearing as "referenced" was a sloppy `reconsider_at` range ("Lev 25:43-46") on observation #51 — since corrected to `Lev 25:43; 25:46; 25:53`.
> Kept for provenance; do not cite §4–§5 as a finding.

- **Trigger:** the Exo 1:13 observations reach into Lev 25:43–46. Lev 25:43 and 46 are in the corpus; **25:44 and 25:45 are not.** Question (researcher): *if a verse is not in our corpus, our term-list says it carries no inner-being content — so how can it corroborate an observation?* Read the verse and find out.
- **Verse text is from the ESV (these verses are not in our DB).**

## 1. The passage (Lev 25:43–46)
- **43** "You shall not rule over him **ruthlessly** [`perek` H6531] but shall fear your God." — *in corpus*
- **44** "As for your male and female **slaves** [`ebed` H5650] whom you may have: you may **buy** [`qanah` H7069] male and female slaves from among the nations that are around you." — **NOT in corpus**
- **45** "You may also buy from among the strangers… and they may be your **property**." — **NOT in corpus**
- **46** "You may bequeath them… you may make slaves [`abad` H5647 / `ebed`] of them, but over your **brothers** the people of Israel you shall not rule… **ruthlessly** [`perek`]." — *in corpus*

## 2. Why 44–45 are not in the corpus
The corpus is **term-pulled**: a verse is in it only if it contains a tracked study term. The verses split exactly on that line:

| verse | terms present | tracked? | in corpus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 43 | `perek` (ruthlessness) | yes | ✅ |
| 44 | `ebed` (slave-noun), `qanah` (buy) | **no** | ❌ |
| 45 | `ebed`, `qanah` | **no** | ❌ |
| 46 | `perek` + `abad` (serve-verb) | yes | ✅ |

- `ebed` (H5650) — the **central OT noun for slave/servant** — is **not a study term** (1 `mti_terms` row, cluster = NONE). It rides along in **575 corpus verses** but never itself anchors a pull.
- `qanah` (H7069, "buy/acquire") — untracked (64 ride-along appearances).
- `abad` (H5647, the *verb* "to serve") **is** tracked — cluster **M36**.

So the enslavement concept is **split across two words and we track only one**: the verb `abad` (the act of serving) is studied; the noun `ebed` (the slave, the state of servitude) is not. Verses 44–45 carry the slavery content entirely on the *untracked half* — so they were never pulled.

## 3. What the verses are trying to corroborate
Read in context, 43–46 is **one teaching with a two-sided shape**, and 44–45 are the side we were missing:

1. **Slavery as an institution is PERMITTED** (44–45): Israel *may* acquire and own foreign slaves as inheritable property. → This **corroborates D10** of the enslavement operation ("cause-to-serve is morally NEUTRAL"). The Law itself sanctions owning slaves — so Exo 1:13's condemnation is **not of enslavement-as-such**.
2. **What is forbidden is the MANNER and the OBJECT** (43, 46): ruling a **brother/kinsman** with `perek` (ruthlessness), restrained by the fear of God. → This **sharpens D5** (object) and **D12** (the hidden shape): perek's prohibition is **relationally bounded** — toward the kinsman, not the foreigner.
3. Put together: **what Exo 1:13 condemns is the `perek` (the ruthless manner), not the `abad` (the serving).** Lev 25:44–45 supply the missing half that makes that distinction visible — *slavery permitted, ruthlessness forbidden.* Without 44–45 you cannot see that the institution itself is sanctioned; with them, the inner-being indictment lands precisely on the **cruelty**, not the servitude.

So the verse **does** corroborate — strongly — and it corroborates the most important distinction in the whole fan-out.

## 4. The finding (the researcher's real point)
> **Corroboration travels by CONCEPT, not by our term-list.** A verse can carry — and clinch — an inner-being observation while containing **none of our study terms**, because the concept lives in words we do not track.

Three consequences, in order of weight:

- **(a) The observation's reference was correct; the corpus is short.** The fan-out reached the right verse; our corpus simply doesn't contain it. The boundary "verses with a study term" **systematically excludes conceptually-essential verses** — and the excluded one here is the verse that makes the central distinction.
- **(b) The term-list itself is incomplete.** `ebed` — the primary OT slave/servant noun, present in 575 corpus verses — is **unstudied**. The enslavement concept is split (verb tracked, noun not), so we capture half a concept. This is not a Lev 25 problem; it is a *whole-corpus* problem wherever a concept's mass sits in an untracked word.
- **(c) Therefore the corpus must be the whole canon, and relatedness must be conceptual.** If "verse is king" and corroboration travels by concept, the index cannot be the term-pulled subset (≈76% of the Bible) and relatedness cannot be same-term only. This is the lexical-vs-conceptual gap (Logos finding) proven **from the inside**: the verse that fell out of the corpus is the one that most clearly proves the point.

## 5. What this does NOT mean
- It does **not** mean "just add Lev 25:44–45." Patching individual verses chases symptoms. The structural reading is: **(i)** complete the index to the full canon so morphology exists for every verse; **(ii)** stop treating same-term as the relatedness criterion — let concept (the multi-contributor spiderweb) drive the fan-out; **(iii)** treat untracked-but-loaded nouns like `ebed` as first-class carriers of inner-being content.

## 6. Observation updates flowing from this reading
- Enslavement **D10** ("morally neutral / permitted") → strengthen toward **resolved**: the *Law itself* permits owning slaves (Lev 25:44–45); condemnation attaches to the manner (`perek`), not the institution.
- Ruthlessness **D5** (object) → refine: the object the Law protects is the **brother/kinsman**; `perek` toward a foreigner is not what 43/46 forbid — the restraint is **relational**.
- New cross-cutting note (corpus/method): **`ebed` is an untracked carrier of the enslavement concept** — flag for the term-completeness backlog.
