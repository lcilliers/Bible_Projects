## 1. What the passages hold

### 1.1 A person giving what is his own to the Lord, and the will around it (Lev 27)

Lev 27 is the chapter of vowed gifts (27:2). In it, dedicating is a person's own act of giving, and the ESV renders
the choice that follows with "wishes":
- *"When a man dedicates his house as a holy gift to the Lord, the priest shall value it"* (27:14)
- *"And if the donor wishes to redeem his house, he shall add a fifth"* (27:15)
- the field dedicated *"from the year of jubilee"* or *"after the jubilee"* (27:16-18)
- *"if he who dedicates the field wishes to redeem it"* (27:19)
- *"But if he does not wish to redeem the field ... it shall not be redeemed anymore"* (27:20)
- a bought field dedicated (27:22-23)

I read the chapter as setting a person's giving under a rule that measures it and lets it be taken back at a cost.
It is also a giving that can be lost for good, if the giver does not redeem it.

**Checked against the Hebrew (iba.db `verse_lexical`):** the ESV's "wishes" in 27:13 and 27:19 renders *gāʾal*
(H1350A, redeem, T3) with "if" (H0518A). In 27:15 and 27:20 no row carries the surface "wishes". So there is **no will
word in the Hebrew** of this chapter. The choice to redeem is in the conditional, not in a word for willing. The act
of dedicating is the only M64 word here. Verses: Lev 27:14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 22.

### 1.2 What cannot be given, because it is already his

- *"But a firstborn of animals, which as a firstborn belongs to the Lord, no man may dedicate; whether ox or sheep,
  it is the Lord's"* (Lev 27:26). Right after it: *"no devoted thing ... shall be sold or redeemed"* (27:28).
- Set against it, the command: *"Consecrate to me all the firstborn ... is mine"* (Exo 13:2), followed by
  *"Remember this day in which you came out from Egypt"* (13:3). *"All the firstborn males ... you shall dedicate to
  the Lord your God"* (Deu 15:19), right after *"You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and
  the Lord your God redeemed you"* (15:15).

I read this as a limit on the will to give: what is already the Lord's cannot be offered as one's own. Where the
dedication is commanded, the passage sets it beside remembering a redemption (Exo 13:3; Deu 15:15).

### 1.3 Dedication turned to an idol

- Micah's mother: *"I dedicate the silver to the Lord from my hand for my son, to make a carved image and a metal
  image"* (Judg 17:3). It comes after the curse over stolen silver and the blessing on the son who confessed (17:2).
  Then come the silversmith, the shrine and the household gods (17:4-5), and the verdict, *"Everyone did what was
  right in his own eyes"* (17:6).
- Israel at Baal-peor: *"they came to Baal-peor and consecrated themselves to the thing of shame, and became
  detestable like the thing they loved"* (Hos 9:10, H5144A). God's side comes first: *"Like grapes in the wilderness,
  I found Israel"*.
- *"any one of the house of Israel ... who separates himself from me, taking his idols into his heart and putting the
  stumbling block of his iniquity before his face, and yet comes to a prophet to consult me"* (Eze 14:7, H5144A). The
  passage names the heart three times (14:3, 4, 7) and gives God's aim: *"that I may lay hold of the hearts of the
  house of Israel, who are all estranged from me through their idols"* (14:5).

I read these three as the strongest inner-being content in the set. The same act of giving or setting oneself apart
is turned toward an idol. In Hos 9:10 the person becomes like what he gave himself to, and the verse names it as what
"they loved". In Eze 14:7 the separating from God is done with idols taken into the heart. In Judg 17:3 the
dedication is said in the Lord's name and makes an idol, in a time when each did what was right in his own eyes.

### 1.4 Abstaining, and what it was for

- *"Should I weep and abstain in the fifth month, as I have done for so many years?"* (Zec 7:3, H5144A). The answer
  is a question about the inner aim: *"When you fasted and mourned ... for these seventy years, was it for me that
  you fasted? And when you eat and when you drink, do you not eat for yourselves and drink for yourselves?"*
  (7:5-6).

I read this as the abstaining being judged by whom it was for, not by its keeping.

### 1.5 Kings and leaders giving the spoil of victory

- David dedicated the gifts Toi sent, *"together with the silver and gold that he dedicated from all the nations he
  subdued"* (2Sa 8:11), and the passage ends *"the Lord gave victory to David wherever he went"* (8:14). The parallel
  is 1Ch 18:11, ending *"David ... administered justice and equity to all his people"* (18:14).
- The treasuries of *"the dedicated gifts that David the king and the heads of the fathers' houses ... had
  dedicated"* (1Ch 26:26); *"From spoil won in battles they dedicated gifts for the maintenance of the house of the
  Lord"* (26:27); and what *"Samuel the seer and Saul ... and Abner ... and Joab ... had dedicated"* (26:28).

I read these as the giving of what was won back to the one who gave the victory. 1Ch 26:28 names men at odds with
each other (Saul and Samuel; Abner and Joab) in one list of givers.

### 1.6 A dedication undone

- *"Jehoash king of Judah took all the sacred gifts that Jehoshaphat and Jehoram and Ahaziah his fathers ... had
  dedicated, and his own sacred gifts ... and sent these to Hazael king of Syria. Then Hazael went away"*
  (2Ki 12:18). It happened when Hazael *"set his face to go up against Jerusalem"* (12:17). Two verses later his
  servants *"made a conspiracy and struck down Joash"* (12:20).

I read this as gifts given to God by fathers and by the king himself, taken back to buy off a threat. The verse names
no inner state. The fear is in the setting, not in the words.

### 1.7 The house dedicated, and who is able

- Solomon: *"I am about to build a house for the name of the Lord my God and dedicate it to him"* (2Ch 2:4). The
  passage opens *"Solomon purposed to build a temple"* (2:1), a plan word. It goes on: *"But who is able to build him
  a house, since heaven, even highest heaven, cannot contain him? Who am I to build a house for him, except as a place
  to make offerings before him?"* (2:6).

I read the dedication as set inside a purpose (2:1) and a humbling question (2:6).

### 1.8 Giving in abundance and with joy

- Under Hezekiah, *"the people of Israel gave in abundance"* (2Ch 31:5) and brought *"the tithe of the dedicated
  things"* (31:6), and Hezekiah and the princes *"saw the heaps, they blessed the Lord and his people Israel"* (31:8).
- In Nehemiah, *"they set apart that which was for the Levites"* (Neh 12:47), in the passage where *"God had made them
  rejoice with great joy"* and *"Judah rejoiced over the priests and the Levites who ministered"* (12:43-44).

### 1.9 Set apart by God before birth, and the person's answer

- *"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a
  prophet to the nations"* (Jer 1:5). Jeremiah answers, *"Ah, Lord God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am
  only a youth"* (1:6), and is told, *"Do not say ... for to all to whom I send you, you shall go ... Do not be afraid
  of them, for I am with you"* (1:7-8).

I read God's dedicating as coming before the person's knowing or willing. The person's answer is a shrinking that God
answers with a sending and a presence. Route A and B.

### 1.10 Holiness rules, with little or no inner act

- *"Speak to Aaron and his sons so that they abstain from the holy things ... so that they do not profane my holy
  name"* (Lev 22:2), followed by the uncleanness rules (22:3-5). Priests keeping the holy things holy.
- *"Thus you shall keep the people of Israel separate from their uncleanness, lest they die in their uncleanness by
  defiling my tabernacle"* (Lev 15:31). The close of the discharge laws.
- *"Aaron was set apart to dedicate the most holy things, that he and his sons forever should make offerings ...
  and pronounce blessings in his name"* (1Ch 23:13). An office.

## 2. What this changes

- **The verses are not empty of will.** Dedicating in them is a person giving what is his own. It is measured,
  redeemable, limited by what is already God's, turned to idols, undone under threat, questioned for its aim, and
  given with joy. In Hos 9:10 and Eze 14:7 it is the self given or withdrawn, with love and the heart named. Most of
  this is the M64 will working through a cultic act, not holiness as such.
- **The H5144A verses are not all one kind.** Hos 9:10, Eze 14:7 and Zec 7:3 hold the inner act of giving oneself,
  separating oneself, or abstaining, and its aim. Lev 22:2 and Lev 15:31 are holiness rules, the "consecration" your
  #1989 ruling describes. Whether the ruling was meant to cover the first three is for you to say. As OT-91.5 records
  it, the ruling covers all five.
- **OT-91.1's question** ("whether it is will or holiness"): read in their passages, the H6942H verses are mostly the
  will's act of giving to God. The holiness that results is the thing given, and the priest's or the law's concern.
  This is my reading of 22 verses only. It has not been checked against the M61 sister senses, which is what OT-91.1
  asks for.
- **My recommendation (c) in the decision file was made on the wrong base**, and I withdraw it. On the verses
  themselves, option (a), reading them now as M64, is supported by what they contain. The holiness side (Lev 22:2,
  Lev 15:31, 1Ch 23:13, and the M61 senses of the word) would go forward to an item for M61. The decision file is
  revised to v2 accordingly.
