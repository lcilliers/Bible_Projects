## 1. The flow in practice

**A. Before analysis: at the clustering stage (E3a).**
1. `iba/app/ps/Cluster-Assign.ps1` runs as it does today: codes into clusters (`cluster_strong`).
2. A new step, `cluster.surface_map`, runs per M cluster (T-2). For a cluster such as M18 it:
   - lists the (code, surface) pairs with their hits;
   - sets the cluster's surface groups as `cluster_subgroup` rows, each with a kind (B3): `group`,
     `qualifier` (E4), `not_this_sense`, or `anomaly` (one per cluster, B2);
   - allocates each pair to one group with its reason. Claude sets the groups and allocates the head
     (the pairs covering 80% of the cluster's hits) in session; the API allocates the tail (B1, C1).
     Pairs that fit no group go to the anomaly group (C4);
   - flags two-sense pairs (C2);
   - runs a size check per group (C4);
   - marks every allocation provisional (C3).

   For M49, M67 and M83 the step first takes over the groups already in the tables (B5, section 3).
3. `cluster.validate` reports any M pair with no group, and any pair whose code has left its cluster.
   While a cluster has stale pairs, `reading.open` refuses it (C5).

**B. A work unit (0.C).**
4. A scope request goes subgroup → (code, surface) pairs → the `verse_lexical` rows that carry them.
   It is exact to the occurrence, and it never uses `word_strong` (S4). A settlement observation
   (section 2) places its occurrence in its own group instead of the pair's default.
5. The output JSON lists, for each verse, all of its occurrences: code, surface, subgroup, cluster,
   the two-sense flag and whether the occurrence is in the home group (S1+S2). The other subgroups are
   shown, to be read (S5). They don't add verses. T-coded occurrences come in as context (T-b).
6. A `qualifier` group is not opened as a home scope (E4).

**C. The reading.**
7. Each new node records the occurrence it reads (`verse_lexical.id`, S3).
8. The observe reading (`reading.record`) records what the verse says about each word: its role
   (RG-03) and its face (RG-09). It doesn't change any group.
9. The claims step (`reading.apply`), working from the tables (B:114), settles the flagged pairs per
   verse and decides any pair move. Both are written to `ib_observation` (B4, section 2).
10. A T-coded word found to be the characteristic is added to the cluster and its subgroup, with the
    reason (T-a, rule 70). Step 3 then applies (C5).
11. Claims: duplication is grouped in `ib_observation` as it is today.

## 2. B4: settlements and moves are observations

**What is recorded.** The mapping places each pair in one group everywhere it occurs. The claims step
can find two kinds of departure from that default:
- **a settlement (C2):** in this verse, this occurrence of a two-sense pair belongs to another group.
  For example, *ṭôb* "please" in "if it pleases the king" goes to S4 favour, not S2 delight.
- **a move (C3):** the pair belongs to another group in every verse.

**How `ib_observation` carries it *(inferential, follows the ruling)*.** It already has
`cluster_subgroup_id`, a governed tag list (`cfg_enum` `ib_observation.tag`, with guidance in
`cfg_setting` `ib_observation.tag_guidance`) and supersession (`supersedes_observation_id`, status
`withdrawn`). So:
- A new tag, `subgroup-settlement`, is added to the enum with its guidance entry, the way the
  existing tags are (B:103). It is written by `reading.apply` only.
- **A settlement** is one observation:
  - tag `subgroup-settlement`;
  - `cluster_subgroup_id` set to the group the occurrence belongs to;
  - `obs_text` holding the reason;
  - one node per settled occurrence (`verse_lexical_id`).
- **A move** is the same observation with one node per occurrence of the pair, and the pair's row in
  `surface_subgroup` set to the new group. The observation is the reason the move cites.
- **A later change of mind** supersedes the observation, so the resolver reads only the current one.
- **Until the claims step runs,** the occurrence stays in its pair's default group, with the
  two-sense flag shown in the scope JSON.

## 3. B5: the existing subgroups are taken over

**The ruling (v38):** `cluster.subgroup` is retired and replaced by the surface subgroup routine. The
existing table data for M49, M67 and M83 is incorporated. A new run comes in only where that data is
in error or missing.

**What the tables hold (facts):**

| cluster | codes in cluster | codes placed in a subgroup | (code, surface) pairs | pairs placed | hits placed | subgroups |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M49 Thanksgiving | 5 | 5 | 41 | 41 | 201 of 201 | 5 |
| M67 Sloth & Diligence | 11 | 9 | 44 | 24 | 37 of 74 | 4 |
| M83 Seeking & Inquiring | 7 | 7 | 104 | 104 | 539 of 539 | 4 |

- No placed code has left its cluster.
- M67 has two codes with no subgroup, and those are the data that is missing:
  - H5647I (work, labor, till, slaves and others; 32 hits);
  - H7423B (slack, slothful, idle; 5 hits).
- 1,847 nodes carry these subgroup codes (M49 745, M67 594, M83 508).

**How the incorporation works *(inferential, follows the ruling)*:**
1. The 13 `cluster_subgroup` rows stay as they are: same ids, codes, labels, descriptions and anchor
   verses. They get `kind = group`, and each cluster gets its anomaly group. The 1,847 nodes stay
   valid with no change.
2. Each existing placement (code → subgroup) becomes one `surface_subgroup` row per surface of that
   code, with `allocated_by = inherited`: 169 pairs over the three clusters.
3. **Missing:** the 20 M67 pairs of H5647I and H7423B go through the new run.
4. **Error:** each inherited pair's surface is checked against its subgroup's description. A pair
   that doesn't fit is moved, with its reason, as a correction of the data. A pair is never moved
   just because another group fits better. Every move is listed in the pilot report.
5. `cluster.subgroup`, `cluster.reading` and `cluster.answer` are retired in U9.

## 4. B6: the 606 nodes go to the anomaly bucket

The 606 nodes are on 139 verses, 532 observations and 66 codes. In each of those verses, the same word
with the same rendering occurs twice or more. For example, 1 Cor 7:27 has "seek" (G2212) twice. The
node doesn't say which occurrence it reads. In U6 they are listed in their cluster's anomaly bucket,
as a second kind ("node on more than one occurrence") beside the unplaced pairs, and cleared by hand
(B6).
