## The rulings, as I read them (confirm or correct)

- **R1 (ruling):** the surface-level group of a surface is `behaviour` if the surface operates like a behaviour in **any** of its verses. The difference between the verses (behaviour in one, descriptive in another) is carried by the observations per verse, not by the group. *My reading of the other half, to confirm:* a surface that operates like a behaviour in none of its verses is `inner_being_state` if it has an inner-being role (a descriptive characteristic or qualifier), and a surface with no inner-being role in any verse leaves the study group (`other`). The second half is not in your words, so it is a question (Q1 below).
- **R2 (ruling):** the kind of an observation is chosen from the existing kinds (face, qualification, party, statement, link, use, term, silence) according to the surface, and one surface may take more than one. Where none of the kinds fits, the test is whether the surface operates as an inner-being behaviour, and if so it is treated as one (that is, as the characteristic). I do not fix a table of kinds in advance.
- **R4 (ruling):** the working file carries the collection's own surfaces only. No outside word is brought into the evaluation. If the process shows a collection to be malformed (for example a member surface that is not a member of the meaning it was gathered for, or a verse that the surfaces cannot account for), that is raised as a question on the collection and not repaired by this step.

## R3, explored: the role of the surface is the subject of the assessment

My v2 question ("which role does a surface take when the finding is about the surface itself") was a confusion. The reading process already defines it, and it is the same distinction you gave under R1.

**What the existing definitions say.**
- A surface is **relevant** when it has an inner-being role: the **characteristic**, or a **qualifier** of it (spec 4.1; `reading.node_roles`: relevant = characteristic or qualifier). A surface that bears on a relevant act without being either (what the act is directed at, the party engaged, the outward act) is `bears_on`, and takes the role object, relational target or enactment on the observation of the act. A surface with neither is `not_relevant`.
- One observation is "one finding about one inner-being operation or characteristic in this verse"; its nodes are the words it covers, each with its role (spec 4.2). The role of a qualifier includes "what it gives the characteristic (grade, nature, impact, size, relation or other; there is no fixed list)".

**Applied to your example.** In "I cried aloud to the Lord" (Psa 3:4) the operation is the crying out to the Lord. The surface *cried* is its characteristic. The surface *aloud* is a qualifier giving it volume; if the verse treats the loudness as a purpose or effect of its own, *aloud* is part of the characteristic. The assessment of the surface *aloud* is exactly this: in each verse, what is its role, and what it gives. That is also the sentence I wrote per surface in part b of the exploration, but written without the role, which was the gap. The role is per verse, as the node's own column.

**What this means for the design.**
- The observation of a surface is about the operation in the verse and **names the role of the surface in it**; the surface has a node per verse where it plays that role, and the role is set on the node (characteristic, qualifier, or for a surface that bears on the act: object, relational target, enactment).
- The same surface can have a different role in different verses. By D3 that is another observation for the same surface, applied to the verses where that role holds. R1's ruling then follows from the nodes: a surface with a **characteristic** node in any verse is `behaviour`.
- The existing observations of the verses (the pull passes them in) are mostly of stage `prior-capture`. For the 8 surfaces of `aloud` they are few: `quiet` 17 nodes (1 characteristic, 12 qualifier, 4 standalone), `silent` 16 (2, 7, 7), `silence` 4, `quieted` 4, `quietness` 2, `quietly` 1, `uttered` 1, and none for `aloud`. Several carry the role `standalone`, which the reading process says is not a role of a new node.

## Two conflicts with the existing rules, which need your ruling

**C1. A surface with no inner-being role in a verse.** Your D4 asks for it to be recorded as an observation about the surface. The Analysis_phase_1 specification (4.1) says the opposite, quoting you: such a surface "is not recorded in the observations", its evaluation is kept in the scope JSON, and the check proposes deleting a silence-kind observation. The silence kind itself exists ("records a surface with no inner-being bearing; it has no use in Analysis_phase_1"). So the kind is available; the recording and the check would have to allow it for this step. **Proposal:** for this step only, a surface with no inner-being bearing in a verse is recorded as a silence-kind observation with a node on that surface and verse; the Analysis_phase_1 rule is unchanged and the check treats the two steps differently. Your ruling is needed because it changes a rule you gave.

**C2. The test for "operates like a behaviour".** R1 and R2 both rest on it. The specification has tests for relevance (4.1: inner-being vocabulary, an idiom, a volitional sense behind a plain action, a party, a negation or modifier, a link in sequence, nothing beyond grammar) but none for "behaviour or description". Either those tests are used as they stand, or you give the test. **Proposal:** use the 4.1 tests, and record in each observation which test the surface passed, so the first collections show where the line falls and you can set it.

## The step, in order (v3)

1. **Pull (new, read-only):** one collection into a working file: the collection, its links and tiers, its member surfaces, the verses of each surface with the ESV text, and for each verse the existing observations and nodes (live, any stage). The assessment reads the file, not the tables (D5).
2. **Assess each surface in each verse:** evaluate it as in Analysis_phase_1 (`relevant` / `bears_on` / `not_relevant`, with a reason), and for a relevant surface give its role and what it gives the operation.
3. **Write (Claude, D5):** one observation per surface and role, with a node for each verse where it holds; another observation for the same surface where the role differs in other verses (D3). Existing observations of the verses are accepted, modified in place or merged as the reading process does, with the reason kept in `ib_observation_change`.
4. **Group (D1, R1):** the surface-level group is set by the ruling above, replacing the draft in one write with the old value kept in `surface_event`; refused unless the surface has its assessment.
5. **Review of what the surfaces left out (D4, R4):** the verses of the collection not covered by any surface's assessment are read again, surface by surface; what is found, including no bearing (C1), is an observation about the surface in that verse, and a verse is set aside only when all its surfaces are. A verse that the collection's surfaces cannot account for is raised as a question on the collection (R4).
6. **Check, documents, tests:** a check that every member surface is assessed in every one of its verses and every collection verse is covered or set aside, with a report at a configured path; USER-GUIDE.md and BUILD.md; a test plan run on a copy of the database (a surface that is a characteristic in some verses and a qualifier in others, a surface with no bearing, a shared surface, a verse with several surfaces, existing observations modified and merged, a refusal when no assessment exists). Later (D5): the prompts for `qwen3:30b`, written after the first collections.
