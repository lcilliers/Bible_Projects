# Draft for the programme prose: Programme book, chapter 4 'Data architecture' (#2548)

Status: DRAFT by Claude for the researcher's reading, 2026-11-24. Not in the prose store. It describes the surface tables as they stand once the build of #2548 is done (`surface_relation` and `surface_network_group` do not exist yet). Figures are from the WordNet run of 2026-11-23 and the database of 2026-11-24; the build session checks them again before the text is applied. Suggested heading: **Understanding a surface: how the surface tables work together**.

---

A verse reaches the study as English words. Each distinct word or phrase of the ESV text is a surface, held once in `surface_term`, and the same text is how a verse finds it: `verse_lexical` carries each verse's surface text and the Strong's code under it. Everything the study knows about a surface is hung on that one row or on a table that points to it, so that a verse can be taken apart into its surfaces and each surface read in the same way.

The study asks three questions of a surface, and each has its own place. The first is what kind of word it is: its surface group (behaviour, inner_being_state, entity or other) says whether it belongs to the study of the inner being, with the reason and the state of the judgement. The second is what it is a form of: its base (`base_id`, itself a row of `surface_term`) draws together sins and sin, boldly and bold, and the evidence for each base is kept in `surface_derivation`. The third is what it stands near in meaning, and here the study holds more than one view, each a different cut through the same surfaces.

The collections are the first view. A collection gathers bases of like meaning round a core word, and a surface is read into a collection through its base (`collection_link`, with the tier that says how it relates). The second view is the surface relations: the pairs of surfaces that an outside dictionary, WordNet 3.0, relates by synonymy, opposition, derivation, similarity or a shared base (`surface_relation`, one row per pair with its type and weight), and the groups those pairs make when followed from surface to surface (`surface_network_group`). WordNet is an outside source and modern English, so its agreement with a placement supports that placement, and its disagreement is a case to read, not a verdict. Two further tables keep the work honest: `surface_flag` holds each open question about a surface, among them the surfaces WordNet could not pair and what each needs from the researcher, and `surface_event` keeps every change with its state before and after. What a model judged stays inferential until the researcher confirms it.

Neither view is the basis of the study by itself, and the researcher's own readings of them are recorded as such. The collections divide words that the study needs to read together: love and grace, for example, run through several collections, so a collection cannot be the unit of a study of behaviours. The WordNet network joins most surfaces into one connected mass (2,321 of 2,742) and leaves 85 small groups of two to fifteen surfaces that the researcher takes as clear concepts, and 154 surfaces with no pair that are dealt with by hand. Neither the collections nor the WordNet groups give a comfortable basis for narrative or a holistic picture of the workings of the inner being: each is a way of locating and relating words, and the two overlap rather than nest.

This is how the tables are used on a verse. Take the verse's surfaces from `verse_lexical`. The group of each says whether it enters the study. The base unites its forms, and the Strong's code ties it to the Hebrew or Greek word beneath. The surface relations show its neighbours, near in meaning or opposite, and the collections show where the study has already placed its base. The neighbours and the collection's members lead to the other verses where those words stand, which is how a verse is read against the rest of Scripture's speech about the same matter. A flag on the surface says where the record is still open. The tables find the words, relate them and show what is unsettled; what the verse means for the inner being is established by reading it, in the collection readings and the layers built on them, and the narrative states it with every claim traced to verses.
