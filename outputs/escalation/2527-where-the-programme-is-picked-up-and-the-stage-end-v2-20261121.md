## 1. The extract: where the programme is picked up

From the Programme book, Programme flow, unchanged.

> **Analysis** reads the inner being's working from the verses of the collections. [...] The work is therefore split into units, and the units are read in layers and in stages. [...] The lexical layers and the inner-being layer are separate processes and never run together, a step closes before the next opens, and every aspect a step demands is answered by an observation with its nodes, or by a recorded none with its reason.

## 3. The sequence from here, in the programme's order

Each step names who acts, what it leaves and the gate it ends on. The researcher reads at the steps marked Review. Nothing in the sequence asks the researcher to rule on the flow itself.

**For End A (thought through stage 3), #2526**

1. **Revisit stage 1 on thought.** Check the anomalies of the first stage 1 run against the span and STEP, and adjust or delete any observation found wrong (researcher, #2515 Q2; the programme text above): H1961 in Eze 19:10 (surface reason, from the phrase by reason of, which belongs to the preposition); G1161 in Rom 2:3 (suppose on a particle); H0413 in Jer 40:4 (think on a preposition); H6424 in Pro 5:21 (stored to envy, lexicon range to weigh, make level); G6063 and G1097 in 2Cor 5:16 (regard, once regarded). The same pass settles whether a word row for every code of a multi-code unit makes function words rows. Claude acts. Gate: stage 1 closed with every row answered and validated (LayerCheck).
2. **Stage 2, the grouping of thought.** The method that formed the earlier subgroups is withdrawn (researcher, #2515 Q3: nothing previously done stands, the subgroup methods are rerun). Claude designs the grouping method from what the Programme book states the subgroup is for (a finding stated once for the concept, the verses of every surface travelling with it through the nodes; the subgroup judged from the dictionary meaning without the verses) and from the researcher's own account of the groups (starting, weighing, the types of thought; #2463 v16). **Review:** the design is shown to the researcher and the grouping of thought is read by the researcher before any other collection is grouped, because this is the step at which the earlier method failed. Gate: every surface of thought placed or set aside with its reason, every subgroup holding a surface.
3. **Stage 3 on thought.** Steps 1.1, 1.2 and 2.1 through the ledger, in order, each closed before the next opens (LayerOpen, LayerPull, LayerRecord, LayerCheck). Claude acts. **Review:** the researcher reads the stage 3 account of thought. Gate: End A.

**For End B (thought through stage 4), #2528, starting beside step 3 because its definition does not depend on stage 3 results**

4. **Attach the inner-being reading to the collection.** The inner-being observation rules already exist (section 4). The work is to cut them to the stage 4 scope, take out what stages 1 and 3 now do, put the rest on the collection, the subgroup and the ledger, and record it as the stage 4 steps in the layered reading's configuration. A design to the researcher first (the Programme book says what is set, and behaviour rule 105 sends a design to the researcher before a build). **Review:** the researcher reads the design.
5. **Stage 4 on thought.** Run on the stage 3 account of thought, closing on the same gate form. Re-read into it the earlier observations of the inner-being kinds (the layers' rule is that an earlier observation is improved or retired with a reason, not replaced), reading the documents that name a verse of the collection for those verses only (#2448 v11, #2498). **Review:** the researcher reads the stage 4 account of thought. Gate: End B.

**After End B**

6. **The other collections**, one at a time, the same path, the order taken from what thought showed (nothing in the configuration sets one; the order is proposed after End B, not before).
7. **Stage 5, the claims (#2530)**, on hold until End B. The 294 claims of the earlier method, tied to the observations and the collections, and the open claim questions of #2486, are carried there.
8. **The narrative (#2529)**, on hold until the claims.

## 4. Stage 4 is largely defined in the record

The Programme book reads stage 4 as "not built, and not defined". The record and the configuration hold a large part of its definition, in the settings and rules of the inner-being reading (the reading step that Reading.ps1 runs), scoped to a cluster or a subgroup and mixing the lexical work with the inner-being work in one pass. What exists, from the live configuration:

| Part | Where it lives | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| The items to explore for every characteristic | cfg_setting reading.observation_items, rule RS-10 | Nine items, each with its kinds and basis: meaning (face), role (qualification, statement), polarity, object, enactment, party with route, circumstance, operation, chain of operations, flag. |
| The kinds of finding | cfg_setting reading.observation_parts, rule RW-02 | face, qualification, party, statement, link, use, term, silence. |
| Node roles | cfg_setting reading.node_roles | relevant (characteristic, qualifier), bears_on (object, relational_target, enactment), recognised (standalone), and the lexical role. |
| How a verse is read | Method rules RG-01 to RG-25 on reading.observe | Read outward from the verse; passage and genre; role per occurrence; qualifier binding; parties and route; the original; never close on "not X"; the hidden behind an act; source against enablement; morph read; a difference is its own observation; simile tenor; faculty last; and the rest. |
| How a finding is written | RW-01 to RW-11, RO1, RO2, RO11 | Parts, no duplicates, concise and verse-specific, span-grounded, resolved on evidence, flags carried, each slant its own row, a re-read builds on prior observations. |
| What counts as covered | RS-01 to RS-06 | Every surface evaluated for an inner-being role, existing observations checked, non-relevant surfaces not recorded. |
| The nuances to watch | cfg_setting reading.nuance_pointers | Sixteen: a negated act is a refusal, a qualifier shared across characteristics, a refrained act with something hidden behind it, a source against an enablement, a figure or simile, the mode of an operation, and others. |
| The question catalogue | cfg_setting reading.observe_prompts | The M-code and D-code prompts for the areas to explore. |
| The node and observation rules | Behaviour rules 130 and 131 | Already govern every layer. |

What remains to do is not the definition of the content; it is the attachment: take the items that stage 1 and stage 3 now cover out of the pass (the meaning and face of a word, the qualifier as a lexical aspect), scope the rest to the subgroup and the collection instead of the cluster, put it on the ledger as the stage 4 steps, add the level "across subgroups and collections" the Programme book names, and bring the interventions the Programme book lists but says are not yet carried into the layered reading (read beyond the verse, read outward, check the original, reclassify a word whose role here is not its collection's, do not force a meaning, record a discovery outside the verse separately). This is inferential until the researcher confirms it.
