## 1. What the report does not yet do

The v2 report counts typed pairings (divine, human, negator, other collection, qualifier candidate) at three levels. That tells you that divine occurs 164 times and a negator 158 times. It does not say with what a divine word or a negator pairs, whether that differs between kinds of thinking, or what the pairing does. It has no level between the Strong's code and the whole collection, so the differences between the kinds of thought are averaged away. It does not look at a verse as a whole, so a verse where the member word has nothing to pair with but a divine name is not seen as a possible scope question.

## 4. Divine: what it pairs with, and what the verse is about

**What the data gives.** The divine words in thought verses are God, LORD, Christ, Jesus and a few forms; by group, God and LORD lead in each. Divine is the only typed neighbour of a member word in two occurrences (Psa 80:14 `regard`, Eph 3:1 `reason`). In both the member word is not about thinking ("have regard for this vine"; "For this reason I, Paul"): in my reading these are verses that fall outside the scope, which is what you suspected. The test is narrow: it finds two of 164. A wider test (no human entity and no other collection word beside the divine word) is the next step, and I have not run it.

**What reading adds.** The same pairing type has different roles. Seven verses, one for each of seven groups, each with the divine word nearest to the member word (illustration, not a finding, B:4):

| Verse | Group | The divine word is, in my reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1Cor 2:11 "the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God" | the collective word | the owner of the thoughts, set against a person's thoughts |
| 1Cor 4:9 "I think that God has exhibited us apostles..." | thinking | the subject of what is thought; the thinker is Paul |
| 2Cor 5:16 "we once regarded Christ according to the flesh" | not placed | the object of the regard |
| Job 1:9 "Does Job fear God for no reason?" | reasoning | a bystander: "reason" means cause, not a mental act; outside the scope |
| Mar 8:33 "you are not setting your mind on the things of God, but on the things of man" | minding and musing | one of two targets of the setting of the mind, in contrast to man |
| Pro 5:21 "the Lord ... ponders all his paths" | pondering | the thinker: God ponders, not man |
| Heb 11:19 "He considered that God was able to raise him" | weighing | the content of the consideration |

My reading: the divine word may be the owner, the thinker, the object, the content, one pole of a contrast, or not related to the mental act at all. Whether the verse is about the inner being of a person is a different matter in each. The data gives the word and its distance; it does not give which of these it is.

**Questions for each divine pairing** (individually): whose act or thought is it, God's or man's? Is the divine word the thinker, the owner, the object, the content or the target? Is the member word used as a mental act here at all? Is a human entity named in the verse? **Collectively:** which groups pair with the divine word as the thinker, which as the object? Do the groups that never pair with a human entity (meditation, 9 per cent) pair with the divine word more? Is there a pattern in the verses where the divine word is the thinker, and are those outside the study's scope?

## 5. Negator: how it pairs, with which words, and with what impact

**What the data gives.** The negator is `not` (and `no`, `nor`, `neither`, `nothing`, `no longer`). By group, `not` is the neighbour in most; the distance differs (adjacent in 12 of 33 pairings of the unplaced group and 9 of 35 of the collective word, 5 of 40 of thinking, where it is mostly far). Qualifier candidates and words of another collection are in the same verse in most occurrences (for the collective word, a negator and another collection word in 29 of 29; a qualifier candidate in 13).

**What reading adds.** The negator is near the member word in the verse, but in many verses it does not negate the member word. Seven verses (illustration, not a finding):

| Verse | Group | What the negator negates, in my reading |
|---|---|---|
| Jam 1:7 "must not suppose that he will receive anything" | suggestion | the act itself: a prohibition of the supposing (adjacent) |
| Mar 8:33 "you are not setting your mind on the things of God, but on the things of man" | minding and musing | the act, in a contrast with "but on" |
| 2Cor 5:16 "we regard no one according to the flesh ... we regard him thus no longer" | not placed | the object of the regard (`no one`) and the continuation of the act (`no longer`): two different negations in one verse |
| 1Sa 20:3 "he thinks, 'Do not let Jonathan know this'" | thinking | the content of the thought, not the thinking |
| 1Sa 18:17 "Saul thought, 'Let not my hand be against him'" | the collective word | the content of the thought: the negator is the word right after `thought` (adjacent) and opens what was thought |
| 1Sa 20:29 "For this reason he has not come to the king's table" | reasoning | another verb; `reason` is a cause here, not a mental act |
| Judg 13:3 "you have not borne children, but you shall conceive" | starting | another verb: the negator is on `borne`, set against `conceive` |

My reading: the negator may negate the act, the object, the content of the thought, a different verb, or sit in a contrast. Adjacency is not the same as scope: in six of the seven verses above (Jam 1:7, Mar 8:33, 2Cor 5:16, 1Sa 20:3, 1Sa 18:17, 1Sa 20:29) the negator is the word next to the member word, and in Judg 13:3 it is two positions before it; yet it negates the act itself only in Jam 1:7, Mar 8:33 and 2Cor 5:16 (`no longer`), the content in 1Sa 20:3 and 1Sa 18:17, and another verb in 1Sa 20:29 and Judg 13:3. The qualifier candidates sit in the same verses (for example `no one`, `no longer`, `only`, `now`) and change what is negated.

**Questions for each negator pairing:** which word or clause does the negator negate? Is the member word the act, the content or only a neighbour? Is the negator part of a contrast (`but`)? Which qualifier travels with it and what does it change (`no longer`, `no one`)? **Collectively:** does the share of acts negated differ by group (suggestion 57 per cent of verses, meditation 4 per cent)? Is a prohibition of a mental act (Jam 1:7) a different phenomenon from a negated content, and which groups carry each? Does a negator with a qualifier behave differently from one without?

## 6. The questions for every type of pairing

| Pairing type | Individual questions | Collective questions | What the stored data gives now | What needs the context reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Divine entity | whose act is it; the thinker, owner, object, content or target; is the word a mental act here | which groups pair with the divine word in which role; verses without a human entity | the word, distance, side, group | the role; the scope |
| Human entity | who thinks (the same person or another); self or other; the person as the subject, the object or the content | do the groups differ by whose thought (own, another's, a group's) | the word, distance, side, group | the role and the person |
| Negator | what is negated (act, object, content, another verb); contrast; with which qualifier | by group; prohibition against negated content; with and without a qualifier | the word, distance, side, group, qualifier candidates in the verse | what is negated |
| Qualifier candidate | what it qualifies (the member word or another); what it does to the sense (degree, time, kind, condition) | which qualifiers go with which group; do they change the direction or the object | the word, why a candidate, distance, side | what it qualifies and what it changes |
| Word of another collection | how the two relate (cause, object, content, sequence, contrast, repetition); who acts | which collections pair with which group, in which role | the collection, distance, side, group | the relation |
| Connective | what the connective joins (the thought to its content, a reason, a result, a condition) | which connectives go with which group | the word, distance | the join |
| Several at once | does a negator, a qualifier and an entity in one verse form one statement | the combinations that recur by group | the types present in each verse (not yet shown by the report) | the whole verse |

## 7. What the report would need to carry this (a proposal; nothing built, B:105)

1. **A group level.** A group column on the occurrences, and the pairing counts and partner lists by group. The groups are the layer 1 output; until they are stored somewhere, the report would read them from a list the researcher approves. Where the layer 1 groups are held is open (comparison v4, point d); this paper used a hand placement.
2. **The pairing detail by type and group:** the neighbour words themselves (not only counts), for each type, by group, with the verses; for the divine and the negator pairings the words and distances shown in sections 4 and 5.
3. **A verse profile:** for each verse, the types present (member group, divine, human, negator, qualifier, other collections) and flags where the member word has no human entity or no other collection word beside it (a scope question), as with Psa 80:14 and Eph 3:1.
4. **A clause or speech boundary.** The negator examples show that distance in positions does not give scope: 1Sa 18:17 puts the thought's content before and a long way away. `verse_lexical` has no clause or quotation marker. The verse text carries the punctuation; whether the report approximates a boundary from it, or whether the reading marks it, is a decision.
5. **The role, recorded by the reading.** The role of an entity (thinker, owner, object, content) and what a negator negates cannot be stored from the data; the `reading` column of the `Pairings` sheet is where they go, and a layer 2 observation cites them.
