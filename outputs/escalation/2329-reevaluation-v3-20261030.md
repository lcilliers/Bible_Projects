## 1. What I now understand

- The verses come first. A verse can carry several tracks, each a way an inner characteristic works (what moves it, what it acts on, what it leads to). Tracks are found by comparing verses; they are not defined beforehand, and a verse is not read only for the track that brought it into the scope.
- An observation is the verse's own record. Everything the claims need to know about what the verse says of the inner being has to be in it, in a form that lets the same configuration be found again in other verses.
- A claim articulates a track: how a characteristic works in the inner being, as the verses show it. The narrative is built from the claims, and the claims from the verses (RN-05: woven in, not fitted).

## 2. The rule already said this, and I did not follow it

`RG-01 read-outward` (rule 143): 'Read outward from the verse, not inward from the scope's question. First record everything the verse carries... Two rules in the same set pull the other way, and the build follows them: `RG-03 role-per-occurrence` (rule 145), 'Find the main inner-being operation, and give every relevant surface its role in this finding', and `RS-08` (rule 226), 'One observation of the primary_operation kind names the primary operation... So the nodes (Strong's code, cluster, subgroup, role) gather occurrences for analysis; they do not say which dimension or use of the operation a verse shows.

The reading `m21-asking` (271 verses of one subgroup) is one analysis grouping, a slice of the surface; it is an entry to the verses, not a bound on the track.
