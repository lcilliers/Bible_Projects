## 0. The ruling (v24, verbatim)

> The T-code-cluster analysis encapsulate the outcome previously decided on - which I agree with. To
> confirm, A t-code word comes into focus where the word itself turns out to be a characteristic within
> the context of the verse. In this case it would be directly associated with a cluster subgroup. This
> is likely to only be discovered during verse context reading. In this case the T-code must be added
> to the cluster, and the subgroup must include the T-code. There are already examples of this
> occurance. Next, T-codes come into focus as qualifyers (parties, process, outcomes or result of
> characteristics, poetic use, analogies, etc) in these cases it would surface during verse context
> reading and be used to elaborate the findings. T-codes of this nature are not analysed by itself and
> these T-codes can span many clusters. They are not adopted into sub clusters and will not be a work
> unit on its own. It is not inconceivable to during analysis a T-code is elevated to a M-code status,
> but as a rule the occasional application of a T-code with a M-code is not a reason for elevation.

## 1. What is decided

| # | decision |
|---|---|
| T-1 | **A T cluster is never a work unit of its own.** |
| T-a | **A T-coded word that is itself the characteristic in a verse.** This is found during verse-context reading. The word is **added to the cluster** whose characteristic it is, and **its subgroup includes it**. It keeps its T tag: the word is then in both. |
| T-b | **A T-coded word that qualifies.** As a party, process, outcome or result of a characteristic, poetic use, analogy and the like, it surfaces during verse-context reading and **is used to elaborate the findings**. It is not analysed by itself, may span many clusters, is **not adopted into a subgroup**, and is not a work unit. |
| T-c | **Elevation of a T-code to M status** is possible during analysis, but is exceptional. Being used now and then with an M-coded word is not a reason for it. |
| T-2 | So **the bulk surface mapping covers the M clusters only.** A T-coded word gets surface pairs in a subgroup only when it joins a cluster under T-a. |

**The examples already in the data.** 47 codes are in both an M and a T cluster. For example,
ruling #1880 (2026-09-28) added *to die* (H4191, G0599) to M25 Life & Death while they stay in T3
Operations. It also added *death* (H4194, G2288) to M25 alongside T10 Places, and *affection,
entrails* (G4698) to M50 alongside T14 Body Parts. Rows sourced to #1714 (an anomaly fix, 2026-09-17)
hold the same dual tag for *to confess* (G3670, M60) and the M67 idleness words. I have not checked
whether those were T words added to M, or the other way round.

## 2. What follows for the design (inferential)

- **When a T word joins a cluster (T-a), that is a cluster change,** so C5 applies: its
  (code, surface) pairs are mapped into the cluster's subgroups, and this is enforced.
  - The pairs where it acts as the characteristic go into the subgroup.
  - Its other uses in that cluster go to the cluster's not-this-sense group.
  - Its uses as a qualifier elsewhere stay T-b.
  - This is the two-sense flag (C2) at code level: the same word is a characteristic in some verses
    and a qualifier in others.
- **The work unit then reaches it in the normal way.** Once a T word is in a subgroup, path a
  (cluster, subgroup, verses) and the reverse paths c and f (step 3) find its verses with no special
  route.
- **T-b words are read in the verse and recorded as part of the findings** they elaborate: party,
  qualification and link findings (RW-02; RG-04, RG-06, RG-07). Nothing new is needed for them beyond
  the rules already in force.

## 3. Two points to settle

**P1. Who adds the T word to the cluster (T-a), and how.** Rule B:70 (cluster-tags-loose-no-retagging)
says a reading "never proposes, raises or applies a re-tag or re-allocation of a single word". The
only exception it allows is a group of words wrongly clustered. The T-a ruling asks for a single word
to be added to a cluster when the reading finds it is the characteristic. **On its wording, B:70
forbids that.** The examples (#1880, #1714) were each made by researcher ruling. The options:

| option | |
|---|---|
| P1-a | The reading adds the word itself, with its reason recorded. B:70 is amended so that adding a code to a cluster (dual tag) is allowed, while re-allocation (moving a word out) stays forbidden. |
| P1-b | The reading records the finding and raises it. The addition is made by the researcher's ruling, as with #1880. B:70 is amended to say so. |

**P2. E4: does the same principle apply to M-coded words that qualify?** M18 S8 ("good, better,
well"; 344 verses) is a qualifier group inside an M cluster. Read as the T-b ruling reads a T-word,
the S8 uses that qualify are used to elaborate the findings of the characteristic they qualify. They
have no work unit of their own (E4-a). Where "good" is itself the characteristic in the verse (for

where "good" qualifies only a T3 operation are then settled by T-a: either that operation is the
characteristic there (and joins a cluster), or the verse is data.
