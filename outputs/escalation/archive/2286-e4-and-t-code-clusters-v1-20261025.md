**Against the M clusters:** the T clusters hold about 485,000 hits and 89,000 pairs. The M clusters
hold 64,460 hits and 13,411 pairs. 47 codes are in both an M and a T cluster: 15 of them in T3, 10
in T12, 8 in T2.

**T2 is mixed.** At least 242 of its codes (176,340 hits) are function words: the article,
prepositions, pronoun suffixes, the object marker. That is a rough count by sense and code range, and
the true number is higher. The rest includes plain content words: son (2,487 hits), day (1,895),
father (1,157), house (883), name (864), great (497).

**The rules that already reach T-coded words in a reading:**
- RG-07: read T3 operations, movement and force, negators, connectives, body parts, places, objects
  and nature for what they carry, and read every row, untagged ones too.
- RG-22: content words carry the inner-being impact even when no cluster tags them. Function words
  get no observation, except negators and connectives. A T2 word never stands alone; it is read as the
  qualifier of what it binds to.
- RG-03: T-code words found relevant get a role.
- RG-06 and B:72: the parties.
- B:94: before a scope is set, the glosses of its theme are scanned across M **and** T codes. The
  example given is the dead and die words, G3498 and 16 others, which sit in T-codes.

So in today's rules, a T-coded word is read **inside a verse** that some M scope brought in. No rule
makes a T cluster a scope of its own.

## 3. Why I think E4 and the T-codes are one question

These are my reading, not rulings.

- **M clusters are themes of the inner being:** what it desires, fears, wills. **T clusters are kinds
  of word:** who (parties), where and what (places, things, nature, body), what is done (operations),
  and grammar (negators, connectives, supplementary). A qualifier group inside an M cluster, like S8,
  is a T-like group: its words qualify something else (RG-04, RG-22).
- So both concerns ask the same thing: **how does a word that is not itself a theme enter a work
  unit?** There are three ways it can:
  1. **Through the verse only.** An M scope brings the verse in, and the T-word or qualifier is read
     there. That is today's rule.
  2. **Through what it binds to.** A qualifier is reached by the scope of the word it qualifies. That
     is the 2026-10-09 ruling for S8. It fails where the bound word has no M cluster: for S8, the 37
     T3-only verses and the 5 thing-only verses.
  3. **As a scope of its own.** A T cluster, or a qualifier group, becomes a work unit.
- **Where 3 may be needed:**
  - **T3 Operations.** Its acts (say, do, give, see, eat, die, smite) are how the inner being acts
    outward. B:94's own example (the dead and die words) sits in T-codes. If T3 forms work units,
    the 37 S8 verses that bind only to a T3 operation have a home.
  - **T14 Body Parts.** These carry figurative weight (hand, eye, mouth) and have an open signpost,
    #1893 "the inner being acting through the hands".
  - **Judging phrases in S8** (25 verses: "it seemed good to him"). A person's evaluation is itself an
    inner act. These may belong with a theme, not with the qualifiers.
- **Where 3 is not needed:** function words (most of T2), negators and connectives (T5, T6, read in
  every verse by RG-07), and the party clusters (T4, T7, T8, T9, T11), which feed party findings
  (RG-06, B:72).
- **For the bulk surface mapping (E3):** mapping all T clusters would be about 89,000 pairs, 58,710 of
  them in T2, mostly function words. Mapping only the T clusters that could form work units (T3, T14,
  and perhaps T10, T12, T13) would be about 18,600 pairs.
