## 2. The design

### 2.1 Steps and gates

Three steps in a new layer 3, on the same ledger, gates, check and files as stages 1 to 3. Settings keys, in `layer.reading`:

| Key | Name | Needs (all closed: every row answered and validated) | Items |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | specific behaviours | 1.1, 1.2, 2.1 (the three steps of stage 3) | verse-working |
| 3.2 | groups of behaviours | 3.1 | subgroup-working |
| 3.3 | across subgroups and collections | 3.2 | interworking |

`stages` gains `"3": "layer-3"`. Every step of layer 1 or more still needs the grouping finished (`grouping_gate`); that applies to layer 3 unchanged. The Programme book's stage 4
is these three steps; the numbering difference is the one B3 resolves in one wording.

### 2.2 Rows (what each step demands, derived from the data when the step opens)

| Item | Row | Subgroup column | Subject column | Nodes the row may carry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| verse-working | one for each subgroup and verse in which the subgroup has an occurrence | the subgroup | the verse osis id (for example `Pro.6.14`) | occurrences of the subgroup in that verse (member), and any other occurrence of the verse as context (a word of another collection or of none carries its collection through `supports`, as `orec.check` already does) |
| subgroup-working | one for each subgroup | the subgroup | empty | occurrences of the subgroup, and traced nodes pointing at the 3.1 observations it rests on |
| interworking | one for each pair of subgroups that share at least `relation_min_verses` verses, and one for each partner collection that shares at least `pair_min_verses` verses with a subgroup (the thresholds stage 3 uses) | the lower subgroup id | `group:<other id>` or `collection:<name>` | occurrences of both sides in the shared verses, and traced nodes |

The subject text is namespaced so a partner collection name and a group id cannot collide in the unique index (C7).

### 2.3 What a filled row is (the working file)

A filled 3.1 row is a verse sheet in the form `orec.check` already takes. The fill is:

- `evaluation` for each occurrence group of the row (a surface and its lettered Strong's code in the verse): `{"result": relevant | bears_on | standalone, "reason": ...}` (decision D2).
- `findings`: a list; each finding is `{key, kind, text, basis, route (where the item needs it), parts {item, mode / polarity / flag_type / grounds as the item needs}, nodes [{occurrence, role, supports?}]}`, the shape `orec.check` takes, with the role chosen for each node by the reading.
- `existing`: one action for each earlier inner-being observation on the verse: accept, modify, delete, merge or leave, with its reason (RS-04, RW-01, RW-09). The flags of C5 are printed with their proposed fixes.
- `items_absent` for each characteristic: `{item: reason}` where the verse shows none of that item.
- `senses` is not used: the lexical senses of stage 1 are read, not re-recorded.

3.2 and 3.3 use `findings` only (with `existing` where an earlier observation of the group stands), each finding naming in `parts.grounds` or by traced nodes the observations it rests on.

### 2.4 The items taken from the nine

Stage 4 uses the items of `reading.observation_items` and does not copy them (rule 3). The ledger items point at the keys:

- verse-working takes role, polarity, object, enactment, party, circumstance, operation, chain, flag. **Not** `meaning` (the sense and face of the word are stages 1 and 3.1 of the lexical layers) and not the qualifier by the grammar (step 2.1). `surface_always: ["meaning"]` is therefore not applied to layer 3; `verse_always: ["operation"]` is.
- subgroup-working takes operation, chain, circumstance, flag.
- interworking takes operation, chain, party, circumstance, flag.

A setting key `uses` on each layer item lists the observation item keys (a pointer; the kinds, bases, polarity, mode, flag and route rules are then those of `check_item_parts` unchanged).

### 2.5 The recorder and the check

`layerinner.py` (new) holds derive, expected occurrences, pull, record and check for layer 3; `layerreading.py` dispatches to it when the step's layer is 3, so one set of verbs
(LayerOpen, LayerPull, LayerRecord, LayerCheck) serves all steps and `Collection-Build.ps1` changes only its help text (the Step parameter has no ValidateSet, read at line 77).

Record, on a whole file or nothing (the same savepoint rule as `layerreading.record`):

1. Every row of the file is a live open or unvalidated ledger row of the step.
2. Every evaluation and finding is validated by `orec.check` and the coverage rules of C3 for the row.
3. An earlier observation named in `existing` takes its action through `orec.modify_observation`, `modify_node`, `merge_observation`, `soft_delete_observation`, each with its reason.
4. A re-record withdraws the earlier observations of the row (found by `stable_key` `layer:<ledger id>:%`) with their nodes, then writes the new ones, through `orec.write` with `stage="layer-3"` and the stable key of C2.
5. The ledger row becomes `observed` (or `none`, where the item allows it and a reason is given); `validated` stays 0 until LayerCheck.

Check re-derives, from the tables only, for every answered row: the occurrences the row demands; at least one live observation with stage `layer-3` and a kind its item takes; each observation's parts still valid; each node with a role from `reading.node_roles`, a collection and a subgroup; no observation whose live nodes are all of the bears_on group; every member occurrence of the row carried as a node of some observation of the row; the operation observation present (3.1); and no observation restating a run of verse text longer than `max_verse_words_copied`. A change to the grouping makes the re-derived demand differ from the ledger and reopens the rows (as LayerOpen already does for stages 1 to 3).

### 2.6 What the pull shows on a row

The verse in its passage; the subgroup's definition (the 1.1 observation); the stage 1 sense of each occurrence; the stage 3 observations that cover the verse (pairing, qualifier, entity, direction, relation, double meaning); the earlier inner-being observations on the verse (every stage not in lexical, layer-1, layer-2, layer-3), each with its flags and proposed fix; the meaning evidence by exact code (`verseevidence.meaning`); the nuance pointers and prompts for the items of the row (`reading.nuance_pointers`, `reading.observe_prompts`); the interventions (below). Rows go out in batches of `layer.reading` `pull_max_rows` (new, 5, the value `reading.verse_loop` `verses_per_sheet` holds for the old loop).

### 2.7 The interventions

`layer.interventions` (new setting) lists, as prompts carried on each row: read beyond the verse in its passage, genre and speaker; read outward and not inward from a question; check the original; reclassify a word whose role here is not its collection's (the move is made by the collection change procedure and the step reopens); do not force a meaning and never close on "not X" (RG-10); record a discovery outside the verse separately as a flag finding of type `discovery`.

### 2.8 Grain across several nodes

One observation for each finding, never a duplicate; a finding that applies to several words or verses carries several nodes (RO11, RW-03). The chain of operations of a verse is one link observation. This follows rules already in force; nothing new is decided.

## 3. Decisions for the researcher

These are the points where the code or the Programme book does not state the answer. Each carries my recommendation and its basis. Nothing is built on them until they are settled (rule 44, rule 105).

| No | Question | Recommendation | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | A verse holds occurrences of two subgroups, so it has two 3.1 rows. Where does a finding that links them belong? | On the row of the lower subgroup id, with its nodes on both subgroups' occurrences (the nodes of the second subgroup as context roles). Each row still needs its own operation observation. | `orec.check` already allows nodes of any word of the verse; the ledger links an observation to one row by `stable_key`. The alternative (the link only in 3.3) leaves a verse shared by fewer than three verses unrecorded. |
| D2 | How is "this occurrence has no inner-being role" held? The old reading kept a `not_relevant` evaluation in its JSON with no node. Stages 1 to 3 hold no such evaluation, and a row with an unread occurrence cannot be told from one judged empty. | Every member occurrence carries a node. An occurrence with nothing for the inner being takes the role `standalone` (RS-05: "a role and not an absence") on the verse's operation or flag observation. There is no node-less result at layer 3. | RS-05 and the researcher's ruling recorded on #2525 (a surface judged standalone is written to the observation). It makes the check able to prove that every occurrence was read. |
| D3 | 3.2 and 3.3 observations rest on observations of the step below. How is that held? | A traced node (`ib_node.traced_observation_id`, which `orec.check` already supports) for each 3.1 or 3.2 observation relied on, plus the member occurrence nodes (a traced node alone does not satisfy rule 130). | Existing column and existing check; no new column. |
| D4 | Rows per file. | Batches of 5 verse rows (`pull_max_rows`), as the old loop's `verses_per_sheet`. | The old value; a file is read whole by one reader. |
| D5 | The names of the new ledger items and the step keys 3.1 to 3.3. | As in section 2.1 and 2.2. They match the item names already in use (word-sense, group-definition) and continue the step keys. The Programme book's stage 4 names the same three levels. | Brief section 4; Programme book. |

D1 and D2 are the two that change what a row must contain. D3 to D5 follow existing mechanisms.
