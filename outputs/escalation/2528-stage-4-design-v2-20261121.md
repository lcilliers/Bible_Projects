# #2528 Stage 4 design v2: the inner-being analysis made from the stage 3 observations (2026-11-21)

Supersedes `2528-stage-4-design-v1-20261121.md` (verse-first, built and applied earlier today, now replaced). Basis: the researcher's answer to Q7 on #2526: "the subgroups and observations of a collection will be the input for stage 4. in most cases the subgroup is likely to form into a concept/behaviour but that is not a given. the behaviours will be discovered from analysing the Stage 3 observations, with the occasional reading back into the base data." And the clarifications of the same day: stage 4 articulates and sets the observations for all the elements of the behaviour; stage 5 pulls them into behaviour statements; stage 4 formulates no claim and stage 5 discovers no building block; the surface subgroups fall away in stage 5; the lexical variation of stage 3 becomes the dimensions of how a behaviour works.

## 1. What changes from v1, and why

| v1 (replaced) | v2 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of work: a subgroup and a verse (about 58,000 rows) | Unit of work: a subgroup's stage 3 observations (3.1), a collection's element observations (3.2), a collection and a partner collection (3.3). Hundreds of rows, not tens of thousands | The input is the observations; the verse is base data read back only occasionally |
| Every occurrence of a subgroup in a verse needs a node with a role | An observation's occurrence nodes must lie within the occurrences of the stage 3 observations it rests on, unless the finding records a read-back (verse and why) | Grounds each element in the stage 3 observations and makes a departure to the base data a recorded act |
| A subgroup is a behaviour | A behaviour is **discovered** in 3.2, as a discovery flag that gathers element observations (it may cross subgroups, use a part of one, or a subgroup may form none) | Q7 |
| Earlier-method observations on the verse each need an action | Not required. An earlier observation that bears on a row is shown and may be actioned (accept, modify, delete, merge, leave), but nothing is demanded of it | Verse-keyed demand is gone; the old observations are not stage 3 input |
| Completeness: every occurrence read | Completeness: every stage 3 observation of the subgroup is taken up by a finding or declared not used, with its reason; every element observation is gathered into a behaviour or declared ungathered, with its reason | Stage 5 receives observations only; nothing from stage 3 may be lost on the way and nothing from stage 4 may be left out of a behaviour without a reason |

## 2. The design

### 2.1 Steps and gates (config `layer.reading`, layer 3, study stage 4)

| Step | Name | Needs | Item | Row |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | elements of the subgroups | 1.1, 1.2, 2.1 | subgroup-analysis | one for each subgroup that has stage 3 observations |
| 3.2 | behaviours of the collection | 3.1 | collection-gathering | one for the collection |
| 3.3 | across collections | 3.2 | interworking | one for each subgroup and partner collection that share at least `pair_min_verses` verses (the stage 3 threshold) |

### 2.2 What a row reads (the pull)

- 3.1: the subgroup's definition (1.1), the Strong's enrichment (1.2), every other stage 3 observation of the subgroup (pairings, qualifiers, entities, direction, relations, double meanings), the stage 1 word senses of its surfaces, each with the verses it covers (references, and the text of the first `pull_verse_texts` so the base data is at hand for a read-back), and the earlier observations on its occurrences for information.
- 3.2: the 3.1 observations of every subgroup of the collection, each with the subgroup it came from, and the stage 3 observations that relate subgroups.
- 3.3: the 3.1 and 3.2 observations of the collection that touch the shared verses, the shared verses with the partner words (from the typed pairings).

### 2.3 What a filled row is

A finding has the shape the observation recorder already takes (`kind`, `text`, `basis`, `parts` with `item`, `nodes`), plus:
- `nodes`: occurrence nodes with a role (reading.node_roles, without the lexical role) and **traced** nodes naming the observations it rests on (a stage 3 or stage 1 observation of the subgroup in 3.1; a 3.1 observation in 3.2; a 3.1 or 3.2 observation in 3.3). At least one traced node is required (rule 130: a traced node alone is not enough, so an occurrence node as well).
- `read_back`: optional list of `{verse, why}`. A node on an occurrence that the traced observations do not cover is allowed only when its verse is in `read_back`, with the reason.

The row's fill carries `not_used` (3.1: `{stage 3 observation id: reason}`) or `not_gathered` (3.2: `{element observation id: reason}`).

**Items.** The existing observation items are the elements of behaviour and are not copied: operation, polarity, object, enactment, party, circumstance, role (what a qualifier gives the characteristic: the dimension of the working), chain, flag. A **behaviour** (3.2) is a `flag` finding of type `discovery` that names the behaviour and gathers its element observations as traced nodes; it states which elements belong together and why, and makes no claim about the behaviour (stage 5). 3.2 may also record `operation`, `chain`, `party` and `circumstance` findings that relate elements across subgroups.

### 2.4 The check (from the tables only)

For each answered row: each finding is a live observation of the row's step stage (layer-3) with an item the row uses and valid parts; each node has a role in the stage 4 roles and a collection; every finding has a traced node on an observation of the sources the step allows; every occurrence node lies in the occurrences of the traced observations or its verse is in the finding's `read_back` (read from the row's state) with a reason; no finding restates the verse; and the completeness rule above (3.1: every stage 3 observation of the subgroup is traced by a finding or in `not_used` with a reason; 3.2: every live 3.1 observation of the collection is traced by a behaviour or in `not_gathered`, and at least one behaviour unless the row is recorded as none with a reason). A 3.3 row's nodes lie in the shared-verse occurrences. A change to the grouping reopens the rows (as LayerOpen does for stages 1 to 3).

### 2.5 What this does not do (stage 5)

No claim is recorded in stage 4. A behaviour flag names and gathers; it does not state what the behaviour is. Everything stage 5 needs from the subgroups and verses is an observation by the end of 3.3, and stage 5 does not read the subgroups. A missing building block found in stage 5 reopens stage 4.

## 3. Decisions taken (my basis; for the researcher's reading, B:105)

| No | Decision | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | A behaviour is a discovery flag finding gathering element observations by traced nodes, not a new table or item | Uses the existing `flag` item with `flag_type: discovery` ("for the claims process") and the existing traced-node column; no schema change |
| E2 | The dimension of a working is a `role` finding (what a qualifier or a variation gives the characteristic) | The item already means this (RG-04); the lexical variation from stage 3 (double meanings, enrichment codes) is read into it |
| E3 | Reading back is recorded, not forbidden: an occurrence outside the traced observations needs the verse and the reason in `read_back` | "occasional reading back into the base data" |
| E4 | Earlier-method observations are shown and optionally actioned, never demanded | They are not stage 3 input and the verse-first demand that required them is gone |
| E5 | 3.3 is kept for subgroup and partner collection pairs and drops the pairs of subgroups of one collection | Relations between subgroups of one collection are read in 3.2 where the behaviours are discovered |
| E6 | The ledger items are renamed subgroup-analysis, collection-gathering, interworking | Names that say what the row is, so a verse-row item is not mistaken for the current design |
