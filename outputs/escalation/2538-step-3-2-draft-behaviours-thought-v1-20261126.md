## 2. How the 3.2 observations are reached

The question of 3.2 (cfg_setting layer.reading, collection-gathering): which behaviours does the collection show, discovered from the element observations of its subgroups. A 3.1 finding is an *element*: one aspect of a working in some verses (what is done and in what mode, what it is set on, pursuit or refusal, the act it serves, its circumstance, what it leads to). 3.2 asks which elements are the same working.

**The rule applied (proposed IB-08, not yet a rule):** a behaviour is one working of the inner being, one core operation (who or what does what), shown by several elements. Elements that show the same operation belong to one behaviour, whatever their mode, polarity, object, party or circumstance; those differences are written as relating findings. Different operations linked in sequence are a chain between behaviours, not one behaviour. A behaviour's name says what is done, never what it means (that is stage 5).

**The steps, as done for this draft:**

1. Read every **operation** finding first, across all ten subgroups, and write down its core operation in a few words, ignoring the word used for it: 'a person sets the mind on a matter and stays with it', 'a person holds that something is the case', 'a person counts something as having a standing', 'one ground is argued against another', 'a purpose is formed and carried out', 'the mind is turned toward one of two opposites', 'a person says a sentence within'.
2. Put together the operation findings whose core operation is the same, **even where they sit in different subgroups**. This is where 3.2 differs from 3.1: the subgroups were made from the dictionary meaning of the words (stage 2), the behaviours from what the elements show the person doing. So 'giving thought' (Pro 16:20), 'thinking on his ways' (Psa 119:59) and 'meditating' join one behaviour, and 'conflicting thoughts' joins reasoning.
3. Attach to each group the other findings (object, polarity, enactment, role, circumstance, chain) that describe the **same verses and the same act**. Each is placed with the operation it describes, not with its subgroup: the meditating of treachery (Psa 38:12) and the imagining of harm (Psa 41:7) go to devising, not to meditating.
4. Test each group against the definition: one operation? If a group holds two operations, split it (counting someone as worthy and supposing someone is dead are both called 'think', but one confers a standing and the other believes a fact: B3 and B2). If two groups hold one operation, join them.
5. Name each behaviour by what is done ('taking something to be so'), never by what it means for the inner being.
6. Read across the behaviours for what relates them: a chain from one into another (attending into conduct, thought into deed), a party that acts on several (the Lord who knows the thoughts, route B), a dimension that runs through several (pursuit or refusal toward God), a circumstance named in several (the heart, fear). Each is a relating finding resting on the elements that show it.
7. Every element is now in exactly one behaviour, or declared not gathered with its reason. The elements not gathered are those that show no working of a human inner being: the Lord's own thinking (route A, B:72) and words that are not acts of the mind. The script that builds this file refuses to write it if any element is missing, doubled, or both gathered and declared.

What the recorder will check when it is recorded (RecordBehaviours): each behaviour is a flag finding of type discovery with a traced node on every element it gathers and occurrence nodes only on occurrences those elements hold; every 3.1 observation is gathered or declared; no text restates a verse.

### B5. Devising: forming an intent within and carrying it into the deed

*Gathers 8 elements from 3 subgroup(s): meditating and pondering, supposing and imagining, thinking and thought.*

**Why these belong together:** The operation is planning: a purpose is formed inside and issues in an act (to kill David, to destroy nations, to afflict Paul, the worst against the psalmist). The meditating of treachery (Psa 38:12) and the imagining of harm (Psa 41:7) are the same operation under the words of other subgroups. The figure of conceiving and bearing (Job 15:35, Isa 59:4) states the same move from within to without.

<details><summary>The elements gathered</summary>

- **23121** (object) In Psa 38:12 the same verb of meditating is set on harm: the ones who seek the speaker's life turn treachery over in thought through the whole day, beside their talk of ruin.
- **23355** (operation, done) In Psa 41:7 imagining is devising harm: those who hate the psalmist plan the worst for him while they whisper together.
- **23356** (polarity, pursuit) The devising of Psa 41:7 is a pursuit of harm against a person, and it comes from hatred: all who hate the psalmist whisper and plan the worst against him.
- **23550** (chain) Thoughts arise and lead out into acts: in Eze 38:10 thoughts come into the mind and a scheme is devised, in Dan 2:29 they come to the king in bed, and in Mat 15:19 and Mar 7:21 evil thoughts come out of the heart first, before murder, adultery and the rest.
- **23555** (object) What the thoughts are set on is evil in Gen 6:5, Job 21:27, Psa 56:5, Psa 64:5, Pro 15:26, Isa 59:7, Jer 4:14, Eze 38:10, Mat 9:4, Mat 15:19, Mar 7:21 and Jam 2:4: wrong against another, iniquity, an evil scheme, judging with evil.
- **23558** (operation, done) Thinking is planning or intending in Isa 10:7, Jer 23:27, Dan 7:25, 1Sa 18:25, Job 6:26, 2Ch 13:8, 2Sa 21:16 and Phili 1:17: to destroy nations, to make the people forget the name, to change times and law, to make David fall, to withstand the Lord's kingdom, to kill David, to afflict Paul. The person intends an act.
- **23559** (chain) The thought is carried into the deed in the same verse: in Num 33:56 the Lord will do as he thought, in 1Sa 18:21 Saul gives his daughter as a snare, in Gen 32:20 Jacob sends the present to appease Esau, and in 1Sa 27:11 David leaves none alive.
- **23574** (chain) In Job 15:35, Isa 33:11 and Isa 59:4 what is conceived within is then born: trouble, chaff and mischief conceived, evil, stubble and iniquity brought forth. The figure joins what is formed within to what comes out.

</details>

### B6. Setting the mind: the direction the whole frame of thought takes

*Gathers 13 elements from 3 subgroup(s): setting your mind, the mind on, thinking and thought.*

**Why these belong together:** The operation is orientation, not a single thought: the mind is set toward one of two opposites (the flesh or the Spirit, the things of man or of God), thoughts are taken captive to Christ or led astray, and a person is called to forsake his thoughts and return. The measure of the thinking (mature or childish, sober, futile) describes the state of that frame.

<details><summary>The elements gathered</summary>

- **23597** (operation, done) In Rom 8:5 and Rom 8:6 the mind is set on something: a person directs his mind to an object and holds it there, so that the mind-set becomes his frame of thought. It is stated as done by those who live one way or the other.
- **23598** (object) In Rom 8:5 and Rom 8:6 what the mind is set on is one of two opposites: the flesh and its things, or the Spirit and its things.
- **23599** (chain) In Rom 8:5 the way a person lives, by the flesh or by the Spirit, goes with where the mind is set, and in Rom 8:6 the set of mind is itself death, or life and peace: living, the set of mind and its outcome are joined.
- **23600** (polarity, pursuit) In Rom 8:5 and Rom 8:6 the setting of the mind is a pursuit of its object, in either direction: toward the flesh or toward the Spirit.
- **23614** (operation, negated) In Mar 8:33 setting the mind on the things of God is denied of Peter: the act is stated as not done, and the mind is set elsewhere.
- **23615** (object) In Mar 8:33 what the mind is set on is one of two opposites: the things of God or the things of man.
- **23616** (polarity, refusal) In Mar 8:33 the set of mind is a refusal of the things of God in favour of the things of man, as the verse states it.
- **23617** (circumstance) In Mar 8:33 the set of mind is named in a rebuke: Jesus turns, sees the disciples, and rebukes Peter, so the correction is spoken in the hearing of the others.
- **23570** (polarity, pursuit) Where the thought is turned toward God it is a pursuit: in Psa 139:23 the psalmist asks God to search and know his thoughts, in Psa 139:17 God's thoughts are precious to him, in Psa 48:9 the people dwell on his steadfast love, in 1Ch 29:18 David asks that such purposes be kept in the hearts of the people, and in Psa 119:59 the thinking turns the feet to the testimonies.
- **23572** (chain) In 2Cor 10:5 the thoughts are taken captive to obey Christ, and in 2Cor 11:3 Paul fears they will be led astray from devotion to him by deceit: the thoughts are what is either brought under Christ or drawn away.
- **23571** (role) The thinking has a measure: mature or childish in 1Cor 14:20 and 1Cor 13:11, sober in Rom 12:3, the same mind or another in Phili 3:15, futile in Rom 1:21, and armed with Christ's mind in 1Pe 4:1. The words give the thinking its maturity or its emptiness.
- **23556** (operation, commanded) In Isa 55:7 the unrighteous man is called to forsake his thoughts, and in Jer 4:14 Jerusalem is asked how long wicked thoughts will lodge within her: the thoughts are to be given up.
- **23557** (chain) In Isa 55:7 forsaking the thoughts leads on: the man returns to the Lord, who has compassion and pardons abundantly.

</details>

### B7. Thinking as inner speech: the sentence said within, lodged in the heart

*Gathers 7 elements from 1 subgroup(s): thinking and thought.*

**Why these belong together:** The operation is the person saying something to himself. It is the form in which many views and intentions appear, and the verses describe it as a thing of its own: it is located in the heart or mind, it arises in fear and can alarm the one who has it, and it lies open to the Lord and to Jesus though it is said within.

<details><summary>The elements gathered</summary>

- **23545** (operation, done) In thirty-five verses of narrative and saying (Gen 20:11, 1Sa 18:11, 2Ki 5:11, Psa 64:5, Jer 3:19, Luk 12:17 and the others) the thought is inner speech: the person says a sentence within, and the sentence is the thought, usually about what another will do or what will follow. The person does it, in the first person.
- **23547** (chain) Fear comes first and the thought gives its reason in Gen 31:31, Exo 2:14, 2Sa 14:15 and Job 32:6; in Neh 6:9 the enemies mean to frighten, expecting the work to stop, and in Luk 24:37 the startled disciples take what they see for a spirit.
- **23548** (circumstance) In Dan 4:19, Dan 5:6, Dan 5:10 and Dan 7:28 the thoughts themselves alarm the one who has them, and the color of the face changes: the thoughts work on the person and show in the body.
- **23549** (circumstance) The heart, or the mind, is named as where the thoughts are: in Gen 6:5, Deu 15:9, 1Ch 29:18, Isa 10:7, Jer 4:14, Mat 9:4, Mat 15:19, Mar 7:21, Luk 1:51, Luk 2:35, Luk 5:22 and Dan 7:28 they are of the heart, lodge within it, come out of it or are revealed from it; in Eze 11:5, Eze 20:32, Eze 38:10 and Dan 2:30 they come into the mind.
- **23551** (object) The thoughts of people are what the Lord knows, searches and discerns: in Psa 94:11, Psa 139:2, Psa 139:23, 1Ch 28:9, 1Cor 3:20, Eze 11:5 and Isa 66:18 he knows them, and in Heb 4:12 the word discerns them with the heart's intentions. The thoughts lie open to him.
- **23552** (object) In Mat 9:4, Mat 12:25, Luk 5:22, Luk 6:8 and Luk 11:17 Jesus knows or perceives the thoughts of those around him and answers them: their thoughts are what his knowing reaches.
- **23553** (circumstance) In 1Cor 2:11 only the spirit of a person, within him, knows his thoughts, and in Ecc 10:20 even what is cursed in thought may be carried abroad: the thought is inward and yet not hidden.
