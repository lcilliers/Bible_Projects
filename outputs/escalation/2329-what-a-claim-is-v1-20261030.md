## 1. The objective the claims serve

The project is a study of how Scripture describes the workings of the human inner being (`CLAUDE.md`). Its product is the narrative *Inner Being in Operation*, kept as blocks in `iba.db` (`governance.narrative_home`). The chapter table is its structure: the words (heart, soul, spirit, flesh, conscience), the eleven activities of Part 10 (feeling, knowing and hearing, remembering, thinking, speaking, wanting, choosing, right and wrong, relating to God, relating to others, hiding and disclosing), four key characteristics (fear, anger, humility, pride), the other sides, what goes wrong, other beings, being made new, and the inner life beyond the named words. One phenomenon gets one account (RN-06). A claim is one small piece of evidence in one such account.

## 2. What a claim is

A claim is **one plain sentence saying what Scripture shows about the inner being at work, resting on a set of verses and going beyond any one of them in a way that is named.** In the register:

## 3. The variety, from the 37 read

- **A gathering over a set.** C-102: 'The verses that name Sheol or Hades speak of the soul and never of the spirit' (74 verses checked). C-226: who pride stands over, 13 verses.
- **A joining of verses.** C-300: God's call unanswered in Isa 50:2, the servant's ear awakened in Isa 50:4. C-351: where God hardens, the verse names the person's unwillingness beside it.

- **A word's sense read from another verse.** C-328: 'Appetite can be set in the place of God and served' (belly in Php 3:19 read through Rom 16:18). C-327: doxa as a boast turned upside down.
- **A single verse read.** C-294: 'In Ezekiel 21:15 the melting heart is what the sword is given for'. C-267: poverty placed in the spirit (Mat 5:3).

All of them say what the inner being does or undergoes (the heart tells itself it will not be moved, pride credits its own hand, thoughts arise unbidden), or what is set against it, and each sits in one activity or characteristic account.

## 4. The cross-referencing, and where it stands

The chain is: claim, source verses, observation, told block, change history. Today a claim reaches its verses (2,096 rows) and its blocks (913 tellings), but only 87 of the 2,228 source rows (3.9%) name an observation. For the register's existing claims the observations are not yet the base; the claims were built from the verses and the study files. The aim you state is the reverse: observations as the base, so a change to an observation can be followed to every claim it supports.

## 6. What this means for the observations (inferential)

- A claim draws **one specific thing from each of several verses** and then joins, gathers, contrasts or counts them. The observation's job is to hold that thing in a form that can be found and matched across verses: which act, whose, towards what, in what circumstance, what it led to in the verse, with the words that carry it.
- Because a claim's verses are chosen by the claim, not by a subgroup (C-328 joins Php 3:19 and Rom 16:18; C-300 joins two verses of one passage), an observation has to be findable by what it says as well as by the scope that produced it.
- Source rows have to be able to name the observation (`observation_id`) for every verse used, so the cross-reference holds.
- The `beyond_text` is where the claim says what it adds. The observation should not hold that step: the claims part joins verses, reading the neighbours (your 2319 v6).

## 7. What this leaves open

The claims come from the verses and the narrative is built from them; the verses are not chosen to support an account. A reading's objective therefore cannot be the account its claims will serve. A verse carries any number of tracks, found by comparing verses (`outputs/escalation/2329-reevaluation-v3-20261030.md`).
