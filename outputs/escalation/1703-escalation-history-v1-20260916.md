> **comment (set this version):** Researcher's own concern, verbatim this chat turn: 'how are we effectively going to deal with verse reading dependency on surrounding verses. the current view is to deal with it on a need to have basis at the point of discovery. The practical implications is still untested.' Current design (#1682 process d): adjacent-verse-context needs are FLAGGED, not fetched -- 4 flags observed in the one family tested so far (H_guilt: Zech.11.5, Jer.50.7, 2Chr.33.23, Num.30.15), explicitly marked as a reversible default, not a final decision. Two things genuinely untested: (1) RATE -- one family's 4 flags is a single data point, not a measured frequency across cluster sizes/genres; (2) RESOLUTION -- no mechanism yet defined for what happens once a flag is raised (follow-up read with neighbor verses injected? a permanent caveat on the observation? something else?). Found live, checked not guessed: iba.db already has real, populated passage-grouping infrastructure (passage/verse_passage, 18,558 passages / 25,690 verse-links, char-continuity-segmented, built 2026-07-19 to 2026-08-08 under the now-gated #737 debate-pipeline work) that could serve as the resolution mechanism -- a flagged verse's neighbors would be a verse_passage lookup, not new fetch logic. Tempered by one real limit checked live: average passage size is only 1.38 verses (min 1, max 48) -- most individual verses ARE their own passage boundary (mainly true in non-narrative genres), so this table will not hand back free neighbor context in the majority of cases, only in narrative stretches. Its currency also needs a fresh check -- segmented before this session's cluster-reading rework, under a different, currently on-hold methodology. Recommend an empirical test before committing to a permanent resolution mechanism: re-run process (d) on 2-3 more families of different genre/size, measure the real flag rate, and for each flag actually check via the passage table whether the needed context sits inside the same passage or crosses a boundary -- rather than deciding the resolution mechanism by design reasoning alone.

> **comment (set this version):** Researcher correction, verbatim this chat turn: 'the current passages is the result of a previous process called debate. it was based on the premise of book reading. these passages are not correctly structured for a subgroup based reading - different angle, different resolution points, it may be that the related verse for answering the need, is not even adjacent. so passages in its current form is misplaced.' This directly corrects my v1 comment, which proposed the existing passage/verse_passage structure (18,558 passages, char-continuity-segmented) as a candidate resolution mechanism for adjacent-context flags. WITHDRAWN as a candidate, not just tempered: (1) passage was built for sequential book-reading (the #737 debate pipeline) -- a fundamentally different reading paradigm from subgroup-based reading, which approaches verses lexical-family-first, cross-book, cross-genre; (2) my framing of the problem itself was too narrow -- I was treating the need as 'this verse's physical neighbor,' but the researcher's point is the related verse that actually resolves a need may not be adjacent at all -- it could be anywhere in the corpus (a cross-reference, another occurrence of a related lemma/characteristic, a structurally-linked verse), which passage's proximity-based grouping cannot address by construction regardless of segmentation quality. Consequence for the open resolution-mechanism question (still undecided): whatever answers a flagged need has to be a content/relevance-driven lookup, not a proximity-driven one -- a genuinely different mechanism than 'fetch verse N-1/N+1,' closer in kind to how the family/subgroup grouping itself already works (same-lexical-family, not same-neighborhood). Rate question (one family's 4 flags) and the no-defined-resolution-path gap both still stand as recorded in v1; only the passage-table candidate is retracted.
