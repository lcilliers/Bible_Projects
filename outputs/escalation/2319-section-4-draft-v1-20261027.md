## 4.0 The frame and the line

Your frame: *"to arrive at a claim about a characteristic work in the inner being, there must be a solid understanding of the lexical meaning of the verse and the context of the surface words that mean something for the inner being. two parts formulating the claim and the verse understanding."*

- **Analysis_phase_1 is the verse understanding.** It creates the observations. *"Everything necessary for the claims to know must be in the observations."*
- **The claims work formulates the claim**, and uses the surrounding verses when it does.

**The line between the two parts** (this draft's proposal): an observation belongs to Analysis_phase_1 if the verse itself shows it. It belongs to the claims if it needs more than one verse, or needs the characteristic as a whole.

| What the verse itself shows (Analysis_phase_1) | What needs more than one verse (claims) |
|---|---|
| the meaning of the surface in this verse, and the evidence for that sense | how the face differs from the word's other occurrences |
| the surface's role here: the characteristic, or a qualifier of it | how the role works for the characteristic across the corpus |
| the kind of act: refusal or pursuit (polarity) | the backdrop that only the neighbouring verses give (for example the disposition behind a resolve) |
| what the act is directed at, and the outward act it leads to | the motive readings built on that structure |
| the other party the verse names, and the sequence | how the parties interact across verses |
| the chain of operations stated in the verse, and which one is primary | the face story and the characteristic roll-up |

The face rule as written today asks the verse reading for how the word differs from its other occurrences. That is the claims side of the line. Analysis_phase_1 records how the word appears here.

## 4.1 Which surfaces are evaluated, and what is recorded

- **Every surface of the verse is evaluated.** Every live word row is enumerated, with no pre-filtering by role or gloss before the tests run. Verses in the scope come in through the subgroup, but all their surfaces are evaluated (#2318 row 1.7).
- **Relevance is judged by the tests of the earlier lexical work, not by the gloss or the cluster tag.** In plain words: does the gloss carry inner-being vocabulary; is it part of an idiom or combined span whose surface gloss diverges; does it carry a volitional sense behind a plain action; does it name or imply another party (tested against "relates" in the five-part definition: thinks, feels, chooses, relates, orients toward meaning, others and God); is it a negation or modifier on an operation; does its morphology link it to another operation in sequence; is it contributing nothing beyond grammar.
- **A surface is relevant when it has an inner-being role: the characteristic, or a qualifier of it** (your ruling). The inner-being content is often not declared but discoverable, so a surface that bears on the inner being only by what it does (the outward act, the party engaged, the refusal) is relevant through the tests.
- **A relevant surface gets observations. A surface with no inner-being role is not recorded in the observations** (your ruling). The evaluation of every surface, with its result and reason, is kept in the JSON, so that the gate can see that it was evaluated. It is not written to `ib_observation` or `ib_node`.
- **Words that bear on a relevant act without being the characteristic or its qualifier** (what the act is directed at, the party engaged, the outward act, the polarity) are not recorded as surfaces of their own. They are part of the observation of the act they bear on, as its content, target, enactment and polarity. This is how the earlier lexical work records them (the content clause is part of the finding, not a second finding). Decision D-a below.

## 4.2 What an observation of a relevant surface covers

One observation is one finding about one inner-being operation or characteristic in this verse. It is written once; it gains nodes when it applies to other words or verses. It covers, where the verse shows it:

1. **The surface and its meaning here:** the sense active in this verse, with the evidence for it (the idiom or combined span, the morphology, the ESV rendering, the three meaning sources read together). Never the dictionary sense restated, never from memory; missing data is named as a gap.
2. **Its role:** the characteristic, or the qualifier and what the qualifier gives the characteristic (grade, nature, impact, size, relation or other; there is no fixed list).
3. **Its polarity:** whether the act is a refusal or a pursuit. This is in the observation, not the node (your ruling).
4. **Its content or object:** what the act is directed at or against.
5. **Its enactment:** the outward act the inner act produces, if the verse states one.
6. **The other party and the sequence:** who else is engaged and in what order, with the route (A, B or C) on a party or link finding.
7. **How it appears here:** the circumstance in this verse. (How it differs from other occurrences is the claims side.)
8. **The chain and the primary operation:** the operations the verse links in sequence (the Hebrew narrative sequencing mark is one signal; Greek needs its own test, not yet built), and which operation has nothing upstream of it. There can be more than one primary operation.
9. **Typed flags:** a data-quality flag (the data itself is doubtful) is kept apart from a discovery flag (a hypothesis the verse cannot settle, needing neighbouring verses). A discovery flag is carried to an open item for the claims work.

Each part is a structured field or a clearly delimited part of the observation, never an unranked sentence, and no part is ranked below another. A structural fact (a relational transaction, the polarity, the sequence) is preserved even when nothing in the verse explains it.

**Nodes** are the classification and relational record of the observation (your ruling): for each word the observation covers, its verse, word, subgroup, occurrence, cluster and its role in the finding.

**Kinds** (the observation's tag): the eight kinds in use cover most of the nine parts: the face kind (how it appears here), qualification, party, statement, link, use, and term. The silence kind records a surface with no inner-being bearing, which your ruling says must not be recorded; it has no use in Analysis_phase_1. Where polarity, the primary operation and the chain are recorded is decision D-b.

## 4.3 What the claims work records

The characteristic-level observations (how a face differs from the word's other occurrences, how the parties interact, how the role works for the characteristic, and what the neighbouring verses add) are written during the claims processing, as observations of the claims stage. The observation table already has stage values for characteristic-level work (characteristic synergy, characteristic reading, characteristic answers, characteristic subgroup). I have not checked whether any of them fits, or how they are used, and none of them holds a live observation of the characteristic synergy stage today. This is decision D-c, and it is work for the claims process, not for Analysis_phase_1.

## 4.4 The gate under this section

The Observe gate passes when:
- every surface of every scope verse has an evaluation recorded in the JSON, with its result and reason;
- every relevant surface has observations covering the parts of 4.2 that the verse shows;
- no surface with no inner-being role has an observation;
- every discovery flag is carried to an open item (4.5 of the specification, open items);
- every finding is written and the verses are marked analysed.

The current gate asks role, face and term of every content surface, or a silence finding. That test is replaced. Two method rules change with it: the rule that each surface needs a role, a face and a term result (and silence otherwise), and the face rule (the part about other occurrences moves to the claims). These are config changes for the build, listed on #2318.
