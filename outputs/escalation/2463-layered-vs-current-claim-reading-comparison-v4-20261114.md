## 5. Comments on the comparison (researcher, 2026-11-14) and what follows

1. **Clusters and sub clusters are no longer the starting point or a readying unit** (comment 4.1). Behaviour rule B:120 as stored says the opposite: a subgroup is the home of a work unit (rule `scope-home-and-multiplicity`), and B:119 has a word enter through the subgroups of its surface. Researcher (v20): B:120 is no longer active, or needs modifying to collections. Stored, it is still active (`cfg_behaviour_rule` id 120, active = 1), and B:119 likewise names subgroups; amending or retiring them is a change the researcher has not yet asked to be built (open question 1).
2. **The final layer is the formulation of the claims for the claims tables** (4.2). Layers 1 and 2, and any later layers, only prepare the base data for it. This answers open point (f) in part: the claim is written at the last layer, so a claims gate would sit after the last layer.
3. **Previously the claims process never ensured that what layer 2 finds was captured in observations** (4.3). The researcher names this as one of the most important failures behind the change. Layer 2 findings are therefore observations, each with its nodes.
4. **The direction changed because the old approach made mistakes and missed items** (5.1), and the earlier work is complementary: the new layers fill gaps and enhance or correct it (5.2).
5. **AI decides in the majority of cases; AI judgement is scrutinised early and later** (5.4). If the researcher has to approve or judge only because the AI did not read the evidence, the process is at fault. A design consequence (my reading): each step's evidence is prepared and read by the AI, and what reaches the researcher is a case the evidence could not settle, with that evidence.
6. **Depth in the verse is lexical work, assumed done when layer 1 starts** (5.5). If it is not done, it is completed first; lexical work and claims are two processes and must not run concurrently.

## 6. What stands open

1. **B:120 and B:119 still stand in the config.** The researcher says B:120 is no longer active or needs modifying to collections. Both rules are active and name subgroups (B:120: the subgroup is the home of a work unit; B:119: a word enters through the subgroups of its surface). Retiring or rewriting them is a rule change (B:31, `configmaint`); it needs the replacement wording and is not made here. In the meantime a reading by collections would contradict them.
2. **The lexical observation rule that lets the border blur is not in the observation rules.** Checked (v20): `cfg_setting reading.observation_rules` (stage, unused kinds, route, bears_on, merge); the active method rules of `reading.observe` and `reading.record` (RG-02, 03, 09, 10, 17, RS-03, 05, 06, 10, RW-02, 10); the Analysis_phase_1 specification and the 2329 files (no mention of blur or border). What the observation rules say that bears on it: RG-09 records each relevant word's face as it appears in the verse, and where the sense departs from the general gloss. The researcher (v22): identifying and clarifying the faces is lexical work; articulating how the faces affect the inner being and what they do is layer 2. RG-09's last sentence sends "how the face differs from the word's other occurrences" to "claims work". I first wrote that the layered approach moves face work into the layer 1 and 2 observations and so conflicts with RG-09; that was wrong, because it ran the two together. What stays open is the wording of that last sentence, which says "claims work" where the researcher's split has lexical work (identifying and clarifying the faces) and layer 2 (what they do for the inner being). RG-03 and RS-05 leave a standalone word that could be a qualifier "recorded inside the observation for the claims to decide"; RW-02 holds findings of eight kinds. The blur allowance itself is still not written in the observation rules and needs one home.
3. **Concepts in the database (checked).** `concept` (101 live rows) holds a sub-chapter of a domain, named for what its subgroups share; `concept_subgroup` links it to `cluster_subgroup`, and `concept_domain` (14) holds the domains (#2339). The `collection` table has `concept_id`, "NULL until the rollup of collections into concepts, which will replace concept_subgroup". So the concept in the database is a level above the collection (or above the subgroup), not a group of surfaces inside one collection. Nothing I read holds the layer 1 groups (starting, weighing, the types of thought) of surfaces within a collection, and `ib_node` has `collection_id` but no concept or group column. If the researcher's "these sub groups are concepts" means this existing table, then a concept would need to be linkable to surfaces and carried on the nodes; I did not check `surface_term.surface_group` beyond its column names, and I have designed nothing (B:105).
4. **Points f, g and i** (gates, skeleton and claim levels, configs): not yet dealt with, as the researcher says.
5. **The answers as rules.** The answers on the border and blur (b), the Strong's application (c), concepts on nodes (d), isolation by binding (e), no concurrency and separate lexical observations (5.5, v20), and AI judgement (5.4) must be captured in the authoritative record (B:31).
