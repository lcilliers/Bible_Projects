## 1. Prayer, petition, crying out and speaking (M42)

### 1.1 The heart prays — voiceless prayer
- **Hannah:** "She was deeply distressed [*bitter of soul*] and prayed to the Lord and wept bitterly" (1Sa 1:10). "Hannah was speaking in her heart; only her lips moved, and her voice was not heard. Therefore Eli took her to be a drunken woman" (1Sa 1:13). Afterwards: "My heart exults in the Lord" (1Sa 2:1).
- **Answered before it is spoken:** "Before I had finished speaking in my heart, behold, Rebekah came out" (Gen 24:45).
- **The heart crying:**
  - "Their heart cried to the Lord" (Lam 2:18); "Pour out your heart like water before the presence of the Lord" (Lam 2:19)
  - "With my whole heart I cry; answer me" (Psa 119:145)
  - "I call to you when my heart is faint" (Psa 61:2)
- **The heart finding courage to pray:** "your servant has found courage [*found his heart*] to pray this prayer to you" (2Sa 7:27).
- **The heart's affliction as the content of prayer:** "whatever prayer, whatever plea … each knowing the affliction of his own heart" (1Ki 8:38).
- **Petition = the heart's desire:** "he will give you the desires [*petitions*] of your heart" (Psa 37:4); "my heart's desire and prayer to God" (Rom 10:1).
- **Calling from a pure heart:** "those who call on the Lord from a pure heart" (2Ti 2:22).
- **The Lord who knows hearts is prayed to:** "You, Lord, who know the hearts of all, show which one" (Act 1:24).
- **Crying without the heart:** "They do not cry to me from the heart, but they wail upon their beds" (Hos 7:14).
- [Claude reading] **Prayer is located in the heart, and can be complete there without voice** (1Sa 1:13; Gen 24:45). Hos 7:14 is the converse: a loud cry that does not come from the heart does not count as crying to God. Cf. "said in heart" (depiction v1 §2.1c): the same organ holds private speech to oneself and silent speech to God.

### 1.2 The soul and the spirit pray, complain and cry
- **Soul:**

  - "O Lord, I pray, deliver my soul!" (Psa 116:4)

- **Complaint from the soul and spirit:**

  - "What shall I say? … because of the bitterness of my soul" (Isa 38:15)
  - "Why should I not be impatient [*short of spirit*]?" (Job 21:4) [T]
- **The spirit prays with or apart from the mind [H1]:**
  - "if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays but my mind is unfruitful" (1Co 14:14)
  - "I will pray with my spirit, but I will pray with my mind also; I will sing praise with my spirit, but I will sing with my mind also" (1Co 14:15)
- **The flesh prays:**
  - "In the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears" (Heb 5:7)
  - "Watch and pray … The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak" (Mat 26:41; Mar 14:38) [H1]
  - "O you who hear prayer, to you shall all flesh come" (Psa 65:2)
- ◆ **The bosom:** "I prayed with head bowed on my chest [*my prayer returned to my bosom*]" (Psa 35:13) [lexical].
- [Claude reading] **1Co 14:14–15 is the plainest statement in the corpus that the human spirit and the mind (*nous*) are distinct faculties.** Each can pray and sing; one can be active while the other is "unfruitful". (*Nous* itself is excluded from this study by ruling 4.)

### 1.3 Speaking in the heart; heart and mouth

### 1.4 Speaking *to* the heart — the heart as addressee (§0.7)

### 1.5 The Spirit prays, cries and intercedes — divine speech in the human seat

## 3. Praise, song, glory and thanksgiving (M22, M49)

- **Heart and flesh sing:** "My soul longs, yes, faints … my heart and flesh sing for joy to the living God" (Psa 84:2).
- **The steadfast heart sings:** "My heart is steadfast, O God, my heart is steadfast! I will sing and make melody!" (Psa 57:7; 108:1).
- **The overflowing heart:** "My heart overflows with a pleasing theme … my tongue is like the pen of a ready scribe" (Psa 45:1).
- **Singing with the heart:** "making melody to the Lord with your heart" (Eph 5:19); "singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, with thankfulness in your hearts" (Col 3:16).
- **Singing with the spirit:** "I will sing praise with my spirit … with my mind also" (1Co 14:15) [H1].
- **Praise replacing a faint spirit:** "the garment of praise instead of a faint spirit" (Isa 61:3) [H1].
- **Gladness vs breaking:** "my servants shall sing for gladness of heart, but you shall cry out for pain of heart and shall wail for breaking of spirit" (Isa 65:14) [H1].
- **Causing another's heart to sing:** "I caused the widow's heart to sing for joy" (Job 29:13).
- ◆ **Song to a heavy heart:** "Whoever sings songs to a heavy heart is like one who takes off a garment on a cold day" (Pro 25:20). Song can wound the heart it is meant for.
- [Claude reading] **Praise is typically commanded of one's own soul** ("O my soul"). The self addresses its soul as a separate hearer who must be roused. This is the praise-side of the Psa 42 inner dialogue (Batch C §6.2).

### 3.2 *kāvôd* "glory" = the self [lexical]

- "I will sing and make melody with all my being [*with my glory*]" (Psa 108:1)
- "the Lord will destroy, both soul and body" — "the glory of his forest" (Isa 10:18)
- [Claude reading] ◆ **In Hebrew poetry *kāvôd* can stand for the person's inner self, parallel to *nephesh*.** ESV renders it "whole being". It is a candidate inner-being term not in M47's membership. **Flagged for the researcher, not raised as a ruling.**

## 13. Cross-cutting observations for Batch E [Claude reading]

1. **Relating to God engages the whole inner seat, and the texts say so by formula.**
   - "With all your heart and with all your soul" governs:
     - serving (Deu 10:12; Jos 22:5)
     - keeping the law and renewing the covenant (2Ki 23:3; 2Ch 34:31; Deu 30:10)
     - loving after circumcision of heart (Deu 30:6)
     - praying and returning (1Ki 8:48)
     - being tested (Deu 13:3)
   - "With my whole heart" governs thanks, praise, crying and seeking.
   - The formula also appears of **God** (Jer 32:41).
2. **The heart is where the relation to God is located, examined and known.**
   - It prays without voice (1Sa 1:13) and cries (Lam 2:18).
   - It holds the law (Psa 37:31) and trusts (Psa 28:7).
   - It believes (Rom 10:10) and is circumcised (Deu 30:6).
   - **It is the organ God tests** (§8.1). **Knowing hearts is the ground of God's forgiveness** (1Ki 8:39) and of his giving the Spirit (Act 15:8).
   - Where heart and lips diverge, the lips do not count (Hos 7:14; Isa 29:13).
3. **The soul is the self in address.** It is spoken to by its owner ("Bless the Lord, O my soul"; "Why are you cast down, O my soul"), and asked to be spoken to by God ("Say to my soul, 'I am your salvation'", Psa 35:3). It is also the life that is:
   - asked for (Est 7:3; 1Ki 17:21)
   - asked to die (1Ki 19:4; Jon 4:8)
   - redeemed, ransomed and atoned for (§9.1, §11.1)
   - delivered or lost (§11.3)
4. **The human spirit is most active in this batch.**
   - It prays and sings apart from the mind (1Co 14:14–16).
   - It serves (Rom 1:9) and worships in spirit (Joh 4:23–24).
   - It rejoices in the Savior (Luk 1:47) and is renewed (Eze 36:26; Eph 4:23).
   - It receives the benediction "grace be with your spirit".
   - **At death it is the part committed to God** (Psa 31:5; Luk 23:46; Act 7:59; Mat 27:50).
   - Its complaint voices anguish (Job 7:11), and its brokenness blocks hearing (Exo 6:9).
5. **The divine Spirit relates to God on the person's behalf.**
   - It cries "Abba" in the heart (Gal 4:6) and intercedes within the searched heart (Rom 8:26–27).
   - It bears witness (Heb 10:15) and writes on hearts (2Co 3:3).
   - It circumcises the heart (Rom 2:29) and renews (Tit 3:5).
   - It gives faith and gifts (1Co 12:9; Gal 3:2) and speaks through the prophet (2Sa 23:2).
   - **It can itself be tested (Act 5:9), resisted (Act 7:51) and outraged (Heb 10:29).**
6. **Other spirits relate to God too, and are judged by that relation.**
   - Unclean spirits **recognise** the Son of God (Mar 3:11) and **obey** him (Mar 1:27).
   - Spirits either **confess** Jesus come in the flesh or are "the spirit of the antichrist" (1Jo 4:2–3).
   - They must be **tested** (1Jo 4:1), and the spirit of error stands against the Spirit of truth (1Jo 4:6).
   - The Lord can dispatch a lying spirit (1Ki 22:23). Ministering spirits serve (Heb 1:14).
7. **Flesh in relating to God:**
   - **"All flesh"** is humanity as it comes to God: all flesh comes in prayer (Psa 65:2), sees salvation and glory (Luk 3:6; Isa 40:5), and blesses (Psa 145:21).
   - **Covenant is cut in the flesh**, and its inner counterpart is the circumcised heart (§7.2).
   - **Flesh vs spirit as powers:** Isa 31:3; Joh 6:63; 1Co 5:5; Jer 17:5.
   - **The "life of the flesh is in the blood"** (Lev 17:11) is the ground of atonement.
8. **God is shown with his own heart and soul in these relations:**
   - his soul avenges (Jer 5:9) and he swears by it (Amo 6:8)
   - he acts with all his heart and soul (Jer 32:41)
   - he wishes for a heart in his people (Deu 5:29)
   - his heart recoils in compassion (Hos 11:8)
   - his soul delights in the servant (Isa 42:1)
   - he gives up the beloved of his soul (Jer 12:7)
   - the priest acts according to his heart and soul (1Sa 2:35)
9. **Speech and the heart.** The heart speaks inwardly ("in the heart", §1.3), is spoken to ("to the heart" = comfort, §1.4), and its speech either matches the mouth or is betrayed by it. **Hebrew prayer is an act of the heart that may or may not reach the lips.**
10. ◆ **Fringe worth keeping:**
    - *kāvôd* as the inner self (§3.2) — a possible inner-being term outside M47
    - the soul asked to die (§2)
    - the double portion of another's spirit (2Ki 2:9)
    - the dying soul naming a child and the returning spirit naming a spring (§1.8)
    - Isaac's soul giving the blessing (§12)
    - Qoheleth testing his own heart (Ecc 2:1)
    - the Gentile's heart-and-conscience law-court (Rom 2:15)
    - the flesh-then-breath sequence of the dry bones (Eze 37:6–8)
    - "gain the whole world and forfeit his soul" (Mat 16:26)
    - "transgression speaks [as an oracle] deep in his heart" (Psa 36:1)
    - the heart with "highways to Zion" (Psa 84:5)
    - "absent in body … present in spirit" (Col 2:5)
