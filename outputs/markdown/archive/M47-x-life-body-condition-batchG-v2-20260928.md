## 1. Life given, held and withdrawn — breath, spirit, flesh

### 1.1 "The breath of life" is shared by all flesh
- "the Lord God formed the man of dust … and **breathed into his nostrils the breath of life**, and the man became **a living creature** [*nephesh ḥayyâ*]" (Gen 2:7)
- The **same phrase *nephesh ḥayyâ*** is used of fish, birds and beasts: Gen 1:20, 21, 24; 2:19; 9:10, 12, 15, 16; Lev 11:10, 46; Eze 47:9.
- The flood falls on "**all flesh in which is the breath of life**" (Gen 6:17; 7:15, 22).
- "In his hand is **the life of every living thing and the breath of all mankind**" (Job 12:10).
- [lexical; Claude reading] **"Living soul" does not mark man off from the animals lexically.** What Gen 2:7 adds is **God's own breathing** into one creature.

### 1.2 Withdrawal — God could gather it back
**Context Job 34:13–15:**
- "Who gave him charge over the earth?" (13)
- "If he should **set his heart** to it and **gather to himself his spirit and his breath**" (14) [U]
- "**all flesh would perish together**, and man would return to dust" (15)

[Claude reading] Human life hangs on **God's heart**: one decision of God's heart would recall every breath. Compare:
- "the spirit would grow faint before me, and **the breath of life that I made**" (Isa 57:16) — God's restraint (Batch F §5)
- "as long as my breath is in me, and **the spirit of God is in my nostrils**" (Job 27:3) [B]
- "**The Spirit of God has made me**, and the breath of the Almighty gives me life" (Job 33:4) [D1+B]

### 1.3 Corrupt flesh, God's heart, and the covenant with "every living creature"
**Context Gen 6:5–13 → 8:20–21:**

| Verse | The human heart | God's heart | What God does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6:5–6 | "every intention of the thoughts of **his heart was only evil** continually" | "it **grieved him to his heart**" | "I will blot out man" (6:7) |
| 6:12–13 | "**all flesh had corrupted their way**" | — | "I have determined to make **an end of all flesh**" |
| 8:20–21 | "the intention of **man's heart is evil from his youth**" | "the Lord smelled the pleasing aroma … **said in his heart**" | "**I will never again** … strike down **every living creature**" |

- [Claude reading] **The diagnosis of the human heart is the same before and after the flood.** What changes is **God's heart**: grief (6:6) becomes resolve (8:21).
- Between the two come the flood and **Noah's offering**. The resolve is spoken in God's heart as he receives the offering.
- The covenant then names "every living creature of **all flesh**" (Gen 9:15–16). **The same flesh that corrupted its way becomes the partner of the covenant.**
- **Revisit:** Batch A §3.4 cites only Gen 6:6 ("it grieved him to his heart"). With 8:21, God's heart appears as grieving and then as resolving.

### 1.4 Breath, then Spirit — the valley of bones
**Context Eze 37:1–14:**
- "he brought me out **in the Spirit of the Lord**" (1)
- "can these bones live?" (3)
- "I will cause **breath** to enter you, and you shall live" (5)
- "I will lay sinews … flesh … skin, and put **breath** in you" (6)
- The bones come together and flesh covers them, "**But there was no breath in them**" (8)
- "Prophesy to the **breath** … Come from the four **winds**, O **breath**" (9)
- "the breath came into them, and they lived and stood on their feet" (10)
- "**Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost**" (11)
- "I will open your graves" (12–13)
- "**I will put my Spirit within you, and you shall live**" (14) [D1]

[lexical; Claude reading]
- **One word (*rûaḥ*) runs through four senses in one chapter:**
  - the Spirit that carries the prophet (1)
  - the breath that animates bodies (5–10)
  - the winds it comes from (9)
  - God's own Spirit put within (14)
- **Assembly is not life.** Flesh on bones without breath is still a corpse (8).
- The vision answers the people's own words: "our hope is lost" (11). **The breath of the vision becomes "my Spirit" in the interpretation.** Animation is re-read as a new indwelling.
- ◆ Rev 11:11 repeats the pattern: "**a breath of life from God entered them**, and they stood up on their feet".

### 1.5 ◆ "Living soul" and "life-giving spirit"
- "The first man Adam became **a living being** [*psychē*]; the last Adam became **a life-giving spirit**" (1Co 15:45) [U]
- Read with 1.1: **Gen 2:7's receiver of breath is set against one who gives it.**
- ◆ Other spirit: "**the spirit of the living creatures** was in the wheels" (Eze 1:20–21; 10:17) [O]

## 2. Revival — the spirit returns

| Text | What revives | By what |
|---|---|---|
| Judg 15:19 | "**his spirit returned**, and he revived" [H1] | water from God after a prayer |
| Gen 45:27 | "**the spirit** of their father Jacob **revived**" [H1] | words **and** sight of the wagons |
| Isa 57:15 | "to revive **the spirit** of the lowly, and to revive **the heart** of the contrite" [H1] | God dwelling with them |
| Lam 1:16 | "a comforter is far from me, **one to revive my spirit** [*nephesh*]" | a comforter — absent |
| Psa 69:32 | "you who seek God, **let your hearts revive**" | seeing God's help to the humble |
| Psa 22:26 | "The afflicted shall **eat and be satisfied** … **May your hearts live forever**" | eating before God |
| Psa 119:25 | "**My soul clings to the dust; give me life** according to your word" | the word |
| 1Ki 17:22 | "**the life of the child came into him again**" | Elijah's prayer heard |

**Context Judg 15:14–19 — two spirits in five verses:**
- "**the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him** … his bonds melted" (14) [D1]
- A thousand struck with the jawbone (15). The boast in verse (16).
- "he was very thirsty … '**shall I now die of thirst** and fall into the hands of the uncircumcised?'" (18)
- "God split open the hollow place … when he drank, **his spirit returned, and he revived**" (19) [H1]

[Claude reading] The **divine Spirit** rushes upon Samson for the fight. Then **his own spirit** runs out with thirst and comes back with water. The victory and the collapse sit side by side. **The human spirit is here the life-energy that drains and returns**, distinct from the Spirit that empowers.

**Context Gen 45:25–28 — heart first, then spirit:**
- "Joseph is still alive" (26)
- "**his heart became numb**, for he did not believe them" (26)
- "when they told him **all the words of Joseph** … and when **he saw the wagons** … **the spirit** of their father Jacob **revived**" (27)
- "It is enough; **Joseph my son is still alive**. I will go and see him **before I die**" (28)

[Claude reading]
- **The heart receives the news and goes numb. The spirit revives only when words are joined by something seen.**
- "Alive" runs through the scene: the son is alive (26, 28), the father's spirit comes alive (27), and the father now speaks of his own death (28).
- As in 2Ki 2:10–15 (Batch F §9.5), **seeing** is the hinge.

**How revival shifts [Claude reading]:** what revives the spirit depends on what exhausted it:
- thirst → water
- disbelief → words with evidence
- lowliness → God's presence
- grief → a comforter (in Lam 1:16, missing)

The heart "lives" by **eating before God** (Psa 22:26). This is the positive face of the eating–heart link in Batch F §7.

## 3. Strength of heart, soul and spirit

### 3.1 The heart sustained with bread (*sāʿad*)
- "a morsel of bread, that you may **refresh** [*sustain*] **your hearts**" (Gen 18:5)
- "bread to **strengthen** man's heart" (Psa 104:15), beside "wine to gladden the heart"
- **Context Judg 19:3–9** — the father-in-law's hospitality:

| Verse | Offer |
|---|---|
| 19:5 | "**Strengthen your heart** with a morsel of bread, and after that you may go" |
| 19:6 | "let **your heart be merry**" |
| 19:8 | "**Strengthen your heart** and wait until the day declines" |
| 19:9 | "let **your heart be merry**, and tomorrow you shall arise early" |

- [Claude reading] **The hospitality alternates two heart-states: sustained by bread, then merry with wine.** Each round delays the departure. The last delay puts the travellers in Gibeah at nightfall (Batch F §7).
- **Revisit:** Batch F §7 had only the "merry" verses (19:6, 9). The "strengthen your heart" verses (19:5, 8) are **the first step of the same threshold sequence**.

### 3.2 Strength of soul given by God
- "**my strength of soul you increased**" (Psa 138:3)
- **Context 1Sa 30:3–8:**
  - Ziklag is burned and the families are taken (3)
  - "David and the people … wept **until they had no more strength to weep**" (4)
  - "David was greatly distressed, for **the people spoke of stoning him**, because all the people were **bitter in soul** … **But David strengthened himself in the Lord his God**" (6)
  - He calls for the ephod and inquires: "Shall I pursue?" (7–8)
  - [Claude reading] **One grief, two directions.** The people's bitterness of soul turns outward, against David. David's strength is found "in the Lord", and it turns at once into **inquiry and action**. **Strength comes after strength has run out in weeping.**
- ◆ **Self-address to the soul in battle:** "**March on, my soul, with might!**" (Judg 5:21). This is a **fifth setting** for the self-address noted in Batch F §15, after praise, lament, rest and self-indulgence.
- Other texts:
  - "**strengthening the souls** of the disciples … through many tribulations" (Act 14:22)
  - "the child grew and **became strong in spirit**" (Luk 1:80) [H1]
  - "**strengthened with power through his Spirit in your inner being**" (Eph 3:16) [D1]
  - "a sure and steadfast **anchor of the soul**" (Heb 6:19)
  - "absent in body … with you **in spirit**, rejoicing to see … **the firmness** of your faith" (Col 2:5) [H1]

### 3.3 One verb: courage or hardening (*ḥāzaq*)
The same verb "be strong / strengthen" [lexical] is used for three things:

| Use | Texts |
|---|---|
| **Courage toward God** | "**be strong, and let your heart take courage**; wait for the Lord!" (Psa 27:14; 31:24); "Say to those who have an anxious heart, '**Be strong**; fear not!'" (Isa 35:4) |
| **Courage for murder** | "Mark when Amnon's heart is merry with wine … then kill him. Do not fear … **Be courageous and be valiant**" (2Sa 13:28) |
| **Hardening** | "I will **harden** [*strengthen*] his heart" (Exo 4:21); "Pharaoh's heart **was hardened**" (Exo 7:13, 22; 8:19; 9:12, 35; 10:20, 27; 11:10; 14:4, 8, 17); "to **harden** their hearts … that they should come against Israel" (Jos 11:20); "you shall not **harden** your heart or shut your hand against your poor brother" (Deu 15:7); Zedekiah "**hardened** his heart against turning to the Lord" (2Ch 36:13) |

Related forms:
- "**strong of heart**" = "**stubborn**" (Eze 2:4; 3:7)
- "the Lord your God **hardened his spirit** and **made his heart obstinate** [*strong*]" (Deu 2:30) [H1]
- Nebuchadnezzar: "**his spirit was hardened**" (Dan 5:20) [H1]

**Context Exo 14:4–8, 17–18:**
- "I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and **he will pursue** them, and **I will get glory** over Pharaoh" (4)
- "**the mind [*heart*] of Pharaoh and his servants was changed** … '**What is this we have done**, that we have let Israel go?'" (5)
- The chariots are made ready (6–7). "the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh … and he pursued" (8).
- "the Egyptians **shall know that I am the Lord**" (17–18)

[Claude reading]
- **Hardening is strengthening in the direction the heart already leans.** Pharaoh's heart **changes of its own accord** (regret at the loss of slaves, 14:5). The hardening (8) gives that regret the **firmness to pursue**.
- The same strength, set toward God's promise ("wait for the Lord"), is courage. Set against God's word, it is hardening. Set toward a merry-hearted victim, it is the nerve to kill (2Sa 13:28).
- This is the mirror of Batch F §6 (the tender heart). **Neither softness nor strength of heart is good or bad in itself. What it yields to, or holds firm against, decides.**
- **Revisits:**
  - Batch A §3.6 (*kāvēd* "heavy" = hardened) and Batch C §9.3 (hardening) read the hardening vocabulary without *ḥāzaq*. *ḥāzaq* is filed in **M23 Strength & Courage**. The data thus hold "hardening" under grief (heavy), rebellion and strength at once, according to the root.
  - Batch A v3 §3.6 carries a pointer. C §9.3 needs no change, since its verses stand; the pointer is in this section.

### 3.4 Might located in one's own heart fails
- "he who is **stout of heart** among the mighty shall flee away naked" (Amo 2:16)
- "**The stouthearted** were stripped … they sank into sleep" (Psa 76:5)
- "the valiant man, **whose heart is like the heart of a lion**, will utterly melt with fear" (2Sa 17:10)
- "Beware lest you **say in your heart**, '**My power and the might of my hand** have gotten me this wealth'" (Deu 8:17)
- "**when he was strong, he grew proud, to his destruction**" (2Ch 26:16) — strength → pride → ruin (M23 + M55 in one verse)
- "his heart shall be exalted … but **he shall not prevail**" (Dan 11:12)
- "**Can your courage [*heart*] endure**, or can your hands be strong, in the days that I shall deal with you?" (Eze 22:14)
- "guilty men, **whose own might is their god**" (Hab 1:11) (*wind*)

**Against these — strength given from outside:**
- "the eyes of the Lord run to and fro … **to give strong support to those whose heart is blameless** toward him" (2Ch 16:9)
- "The Lord is my strength and my shield; **in him my heart trusts**" (Psa 28:7)
- "Blessed are those **whose strength is in you, in whose heart are the highways** to Zion" (Psa 84:5)
- "**Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit**" (Zec 4:6) [D1]
- "I am **filled with power, with the Spirit of the Lord**" (Mic 3:8) [D1]
- New Testament — **Spirit and power** as a fixed pair [D1/D2]:
  - Luk 1:35; 4:14; Act 1:8; 10:38
  - Rom 15:13, 19; 1Co 2:4; 1Th 1:5
  - "God gave us **a spirit … of power** and love and self-control" (2Ti 1:7) [X]
- "**men of valor whose hearts God had touched**" (1Sa 10:26)

[Claude reading] **The heart that holds its own might is the one that melts, flees or is stripped. The heart that has its strength "in" God is supported.** In the NT the Spirit is the regular carrier of power.

### 3.5 Strength failing
- "**My heart throbs; my strength fails me**, and the light of my eyes — it also has gone from me" (Psa 38:10)
- "my iniquities have overtaken me … **my heart fails me**" (Psa 40:12)

### 3.6 Samson — heart, strength and soul in one sequence
**Context Judg 16:15–30:**

| Verse | Event |
|---|---|
| 15 | "How can you say, 'I love you,' **when your heart is not with me**? … you have not told me **where your great strength lies**" |
| 16 | "she pressed him hard with her words day after day … **his soul was vexed to death**" |
| 17–18 | "**he told her all his heart** … 'If my head is shaved, then **my strength will leave me**'" — "**he has told me all his heart**" |
| 19–20 | "**his strength left him** … he did not know that **the Lord had left him**" |
| 21–22 | Eyes gouged out; he grinds at the mill; the hair begins to grow |
| 23–25 | The Philistines praise Dagon; "**when their hearts were merry**, they said, 'Call Samson'" |
| 28 | "O Lord God, please **remember me** and please **strengthen me only this once**" |
| 30 | "**Let me die** [*let my soul die*] **with the Philistines**" — "he bowed **with all his strength**" |

[Claude reading]
- **Delilah joins heart and strength from the start** (15). A heart "not with" her is a heart that keeps back the secret of strength.
- **The soul gives way first** ("vexed to death", 16). **Then the heart is given** ("all his heart", 17). **Then the strength goes** (19), and it goes **because the Lord goes** (20). Samson does not know it.
- The Philistines' merry hearts (25) are the threshold (Batch F §7). Samson's last strength is **asked for, not possessed** (28). It is joined to **the soul's consent to die** (30).
- **Two prayers of Samson, set against each other:**
  - At Lehi he asks **not** to die, and **his spirit returns** (15:18–19, §2).
  - At Gaza he asks to die, and **his soul goes** with his strength (16:30).
- **Revisits:**
  - Batch B §4.3 (Judg 16:17–18, "told her all his heart") and §4.4 (16:16, "soul vexed to death")
  - Batch F §7 (Judg 16:25)

  All three are now pointers to this sequence.

## 4. "As your soul lives" — the soul as what one swears by

**The formula:**
- "**as the Lord lives and as your soul lives**" (1Sa 20:3; 25:26; 2Sa 11:11); "as the Lord lives, and **as you yourself live**, I will not leave you" (2Ki 2:2, 4, 6; 4:30)
- "**As your soul lives**, O king, I do not know" (1Sa 17:55); "As you live, my lord" (1Sa 1:26; 2Sa 14:19)
- "As the Lord lives, **who has redeemed my soul** out of every adversity" (1Ki 1:29; 2Sa 4:9)
- "As the Lord lives, **who made our souls**" (Jer 38:16)
- "As God lives, … **who has made my soul bitter**" (Job 27:2)

[lexical; Claude reading] **The oath sets the other person's *nephesh* beside the Lord's own life** as the thing that guarantees the word. To swear by someone's soul is to stake one's word on their life. In the second form, the Lord is identified by what he has done **to** the soul: redeemed it, made it, made it bitter.

**Context read for each narrative use:**

| Text | Before | Oath | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1Sa 17:55 → 18:1 | David goes out against the Philistine | Abner: "**As your soul lives**, O king, I do not know" whose son he is | David is brought in with the head; "**the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David**" (18:1; Batch F §1.1) |
| 2Sa 11:8–15 | David: "Go down to your house" (8); Uriah sleeps at the king's door (9) | Uriah: "Shall I then go to my house, to eat and to drink and to lie with my wife? **As you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this thing**" (11) | David "made him drunk" (13) — **he still does not go down**. Next morning, the letter: "that he may be struck down, and die" (15) |
| 2Ki 2:2–6 | Elijah: "Please stay here" (three times) | Elisha: "**As the Lord lives, and as you yourself live, I will not leave you**" (three times) | "Let there be a double portion of your spirit on me" (2:9) → "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha" (2:15; Batch F §9.5) |
| 2Ki 4:27–30 | The Shunammite "caught hold of his feet … **she is in bitter distress** [*her soul is bitter*]" (27); "Did I not say, 'Do not deceive me?'" (28); Gehazi is sent with the staff (29) | "**As the Lord lives and as you yourself live, I will not leave you**" (30) | "So he arose and followed her" (30) |
| Jer 38:14–24 | "hide nothing from me" (14); "will you not surely put me to death?" (15) | Zedekiah "swore **secretly**, 'As the Lord lives, **who made our souls**, I will not put you to death'" (16) | "**your life shall be spared**" if you surrender (17, 20); "**I am afraid**" (19); "Let no one know of these words, and you shall not die" (24) |

**What the sequences show [Claude reading]:**
1. **The oath marks adherence at a cost.**
   - Elisha's triple oath is the price of the double spirit.
   - The Shunammite uses **Elisha's own oath** against him. Her bitterness of soul becomes a hold on the prophet that he cannot refuse.
   - Uriah's oath by David's soul is **kept against David's wish**. The wine that should have lowered his guard fails (a failed threshold — Batch F §7). He dies for keeping it.
2. **The oath by the soul can guard a life while refusing a life.** Zedekiah swears secretly by "who made our souls" to spare Jeremiah's life. He then declines the life offered to him (38:17, 20), from fear (38:19). The oath and the refusal are in one conversation.
3. **1Sa 17:55 stands on the threshold of 18:1.** A formal oath by Saul's soul immediately precedes the knitting of Jonathan's soul to David's.
4. ◆ **The oath misused:** "**As the Lord lives**, I will run after him and get something from him" (Gehazi, 2Ki 5:20; §13.2).
5. **Revisit (Batch H):**
   - **God swears by his own soul:** "The Lord God has sworn by himself [*his nephesh*]" (Amo 6:8; Jer 51:14; read in Batch D and E). The oath by a *nephesh* is used of God as well as of men.
   - **The oath answers a claim of vengeance.** The murderers of Ish-bosheth: "your enemy, who sought your life. **The Lord has avenged** my lord the king" (2Sa 4:8). David: "As the Lord lives, **who has redeemed my life** out of every adversity" → he has them killed (4:9–12). **The oath on David's own redeemed soul refuses a vengeance done on his behalf** (Batch H §1.4).

## 5. Life spared, life hunted — the *nephesh* as a prize

### 5.1 Surrender for life
- "he who goes out and surrenders … **shall have his life as a prize of war**" (Jer 21:9; 38:2)
- "**your life shall be spared**" (Jer 38:17, 20) — see §4
- Ben-hadad's servants in sackcloth and ropes: "**Perhaps he will spare your life** … 'Please, **let me live**.' And he said, 'Does he still live? **He is my brother**'" (1Ki 20:31–32)

### 5.2 Life saved by others — or by deceit
- "Say you are my sister … **that my life may be spared for your sake**" (Gen 12:13) — a life saved by putting another at risk
- "you have shown me great kindness in **saving my life**. But I cannot escape to the hills … **my life will be saved**" in a little city (Gen 19:19–20)
- "**save alive** my father and mother … and **deliver our lives** from death" (Jos 2:13)

### 5.3 Hunting souls
- "Woe to the women who sew magic bands … **in the hunt for souls! Will you hunt down souls** belonging to my people **and keep your own souls alive?**" (Eze 13:18)
- "**for handfuls of barley and for pieces of bread**, putting to death souls who should not die and keeping alive souls who should not live" (Eze 13:19)
- "you have **disheartened the righteous** falsely … and encouraged the wicked … **to save his life**" (Eze 13:22)
- **The opposite sense:** "**whoever captures souls is wise**" (Pro 11:30) [lexical — "take"; [verify] the verb]
- [Claude reading] The soul is prey, sold for bread, and "captured" by the wise. **Taking souls is predatory or life-giving according to who takes and for what.**

### 5.4 Is there a price for a soul?
- **Context Mic 6:6–8:**
  - "With what shall I come before the Lord?" (6)
  - "**Shall I give my firstborn for my transgression, the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?**" (7) (M37)
  - "He has told you, O man, what is good … **to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God**" (8)
- **Context Mat 16:24–26:**
  - "let him deny himself and take up his cross" (24)
  - "whoever would **save his life will lose it**" (25)
  - "what will it profit a man if he **gains the whole world and forfeits his soul**? Or **what shall a man give in return for his soul**?" (26)
- [Claude reading] **Both passages refuse a price for the soul.** In Micah the highest human price (the firstborn) is set aside for a way of life. In Matthew nothing, not the world, buys the soul back. This bears on Batch F §3.2 (lose / save).
- **Revisit (Batch H §2.2, §1.4) — the refusal is qualified.** The law and the narratives **do** price a life:
  - "**The ransom of a man's life is his wealth**" (Pro 13:8)
  - "a ransom for his life … **The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less**" (Exo 30:12–16) — **the same price for every life**
  - "your life shall be for his life, **or else you shall pay a talent of silver**" (1Ki 20:39) → the verdict drops the silver: "your life shall be for his life" (20:42)
  - Satan: "**All that a man has he will give for his life**" (Job 2:4)
  - [Claude reading] **A life can be ransomed within the covenant, but a soul cannot be bought back from God.** Mic 6 and Mat 16 refuse a price **before God**. They do not deny that the law sets one.

### 5.5 The soul's life and its failure to be satisfied
- "his soul is **puffed up**; it is not upright within him, but **the righteous shall live by his faith**" (Hab 2:4)
- **Its NT reuse shifts whose soul it is:** "**my soul has no pleasure** in him … we are … of those who have faith and **preserve their souls**" (Heb 10:38–39)
  - [Claude reading] In Habakkuk the soul is the **proud man's**. In Hebrews "my soul" is **God's**, displeased by shrinking back, and the believers' souls are **preserved**.
- "lives many years … but **his soul is not satisfied with life's good things**" (Ecc 6:3)
- "**the life of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of the living** in the care of the Lord" (1Sa 25:29) — see §9.3 for its sequel (25:31)

## 6. Hating one's life — exhaustion, not discipleship

**Context Jon 4:1–11:**
- "it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and **he was angry**" (1)
- "I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful … and relenting from disaster" (2)
- "**please take my life from me, for it is better for me to die than to live**" (3) → "Do you do well to be angry?" (4)
- The plant: "**Jonah was exceedingly glad**" (6)
- The worm; "God appointed **a scorching east wind** [*rûaḥ*] … so that he was faint. And he asked that he might die" (7–8)
- "Yes, I do well to be angry, **angry enough to die**" (9)
- "You pity the plant … should not I pity Nineveh?" (10–11)

[Claude reading]
- **The death-wish is joined to anger** (3, 9), and it **follows the loss of comfort** (the plant, 7–8).
- The *rûaḥ* here is **a wind God appoints**, working on the body ("faint") and exposing the soul.
- God answers the soul's wish to die **with a question about pity**. The life Jonah wants to give up is set beside the 120,000 lives God will not give up.

**Other texts where the soul turns against its own life:**
- "**I loathe my life**; I will give free utterance to my complaint; I will speak **in the bitterness of my soul**" (Job 10:1; 9:21)
- **Context Ecc 2:17–20:** "So **I hated life** … a striving after wind" (17) → "I hated all my toil" (18) → "**I … gave my heart up to despair**" (20)
- "Why is light given to him who is in misery, and **life to the bitter in soul**?" (Job 3:20)
- Samson's "**let my soul die** with the Philistines" (Judg 16:30; §3.6) — **a third kind**: death accepted as the last act of strength.

**The meaning shift [Claude reading]:**
- In Batch F §3.2, **hating one's life** (Joh 12:25; Luk 14:26) is **chosen** — for another, and for keeping it.
- Here the same attitude is the soul's **exhaustion**: bitterness (Job), futility (Ecclesiastes), anger at mercy (Jonah).
- The words overlap. What differs is **what the hatred is for**.
- **Revisit:** Batch F §3 now points here for the despairing counterpart.
- Also: Batch A §7 records "When my life was fainting away, I remembered the Lord" (Jon 2:7). **In the same book, the fainting of chapter 2 turns to prayer; the fainting of chapter 4 turns to a death-wish.**

## 7. Flesh and Spirit — who gives life

### 7.1 One discourse, two valuations of flesh
**Context Joh 6:51–63:**
- "the bread that I will give for the life of the world **is my flesh**" (51)
- "How can this man give us his flesh to eat?" (52)

[Claude reading]

| **Revived with Christ** | "you, who were dead in … the uncircumcision of your flesh, God **made alive together** with him" (Col 2:13) |

[Claude reading] **Context sets "flesh" as:**
- mortality to be lived in faithfully (Gal 2:20; Phili 1:22)
- Christ's own path (Heb 10:20)
- an orientation that ends in death (Rom 8:6, 13)

Paul uses all three **within a few chapters**. In Phili 1:21–24 he weighs "to live in the flesh" against "to depart" **for the sake of others**.

### 7.3 Spirit willing, flesh weak — the scene
**Context Mat 26:38–45:**

- "your will be done" (42)

[Claude reading]
- The saying is addressed to the disciples, but **it is framed by Jesus' own sorrowing soul (38) and his willing submission (39, 42)**.
- The **willing spirit** is **enacted in his prayer**. The **weak flesh** is **enacted in their sleep** ("eyes were heavy" — *heavy*, the root of Batch A R1).

**Context Neh 2:1–5:**

[Claude reading]

- [Claude reading] **The sad heart at the feast is the counter-case to the merry heart.** It too is a threshold, but what enters is **prayer**, and the outcome is a **gift**.
- **Revisits:**

## 12. Glory, greatness and the heart (M71)

## 14. Cross-cutting observations for Batch G [Claude reading]

1. **Life is held in the breath and the soul, and it is God's to give and gather.**
   - "Living soul" is shared with the animals. **God's breathing** is what marks Gen 2:7.
   - One decision of **God's heart** could gather every breath back (Job 34:14–15).
   - In the flood narrative **God's heart changes while man's heart does not** (Gen 6:5–6 → 8:21).
2. ***rûaḥ* moves across senses within a single passage**, and the passage uses the movement:
   - Eze 37: carrying Spirit → breath → winds → "my Spirit within you"
   - Judg 15: rushing Spirit → one's own spirit returning
   - Jon 4: an appointed wind that makes the soul wish to die
3. **Strength and hardness are one word.** *ḥāzaq* is courage, hardening and the nerve to kill, **according to what the heart is set toward** (§3.3). This mirrors the tender heart (Batch F §6): **neither softness nor strength of heart has a fixed moral value.**
   **Revisit (Batch H §7.4):** more words of this kind — *nāṣar* (keep the heart / the "guarded" wily heart), *šāmar*, vengeance, fatness of soul and of heart, *šāgâ*, kinship-flesh.
4. **The inner components give way in order.** Samson (Judg 16):
   - the soul vexed
   - the heart told
   - the strength gone
   - the Lord gone

   The strength returns only when **asked for**, and joined to the soul's consent to die.

   Jacob (Gen 45): heart numb → spirit revived by words **and sight**.
5. **The soul is what one swears by and what one bargains for.**
   - Oaths "as your soul lives" mark adherence at a cost (Elisha, the Shunammite, Uriah) or protection with fear (Zedekiah).
   - Souls are hunted for bread (Eze 13) and "captured" by the wise (Pro 11:30).
   - **Nothing can be given in return for a soul** (Mic 6:7–8; Mat 16:26).
6. **Eating and drinking recur at inner thresholds**, now in three registers:
   - the merry **heart** (Batch F §7)
   - the **heart** sustained by bread before it is made merry (Judg 19:5, 8)
   - the **soul** that blesses after a meal and wine (Gen 27:25)

   Also: the **sad** heart at a feast that turns to prayer (Hannah, Nehemiah), and the wine-threshold **refused** (Uriah).
7. **Enticement and stirring show how other agents reach the inner seat:**
   - God stirs **human** spirits (Cyrus, Pul, the remnant).
   - God **sends** an other-spirit to entice through prophets' mouths (1Ki 22).
   - God **allures** and speaks to the heart (Hos 2:14).
   - Desire, gods and false teachers entice hearts and souls.
8. **The flesh is valued by circumstance:** all living beings; mortal life lived by faith; Christ's own flesh; weakness; an orientation opposed to the Spirit. Joh 6 shifts its value **within one discourse**.
9. **Life-hatred has two faces.** The despairing soul of Job, Qohelet and Jonah hates life from exhaustion. The disciple hates it for another's sake (Batch F §3.2). The words overlap; the purpose differs.
10. ◆ **Fringe worth keeping:**
    - the dead-but-assembled body without breath (Eze 37:8)
    - Jacob's spirit reviving at the sight of wagons (Gen 45:27)
    - Samson's two prayers (Judg 15:18; 16:28)
    - Uriah's oath by the king's soul, kept against the king
    - the Shunammite turning Elisha's oath on him
    - Eli reading heart-prayer as drunkenness
    - "stumbling of heart" averted by Abigail
    - the lying spirit volunteering in the council
    - the idol told "Awake" with the verb that stirs Cyrus's spirit
    - Nebuchadnezzar's heart replaced by a beast's
    - Elisha's heart going out to meet Naaman's chariot
    - Isaac's soul blessing after wine
