##### Section 370: chapter 10.14 Humility, Jesus

> ## Jesus
> 
> In the New Testament, humility rests on what the verses say of Jesus. God's own part is in 13.1, *Lowering himself*. Here is what it means for the one who humbles himself.
> 
> **He is lowly.** "learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls" (Matthew 11:29). His own word on his own heart. Rest is found by learning from it.
> 
> **He came humble.** "your king is coming to you, humble, and mounted on a donkey" (Matthew 21:5; Zechariah 9:9). As a boy who knew "I must be in my Father's house", "he went down with them and came to Nazareth and was submissive to them" (Luke 2:49, 51). "In his humiliation justice was denied him", and like a lamb he "opens not his mouth" (Acts 8:32–33).
> 
> **He humbled himself, and was exalted.** "who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross. Therefore God has highly exalted him" (Philippians 2:6–9). "we see him who for a little while was made lower than the angels, namely Jesus, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death" (Hebrews 2:9). I read the saying he taught, "whoever humbles himself will be exalted", as what is told of him.
> 
> **He is the ground of humility toward others.**
> - "Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 2:5) is the reason for "in humility count others more significant than yourselves" (2:3).
> - "leaving you an example, so that you might follow in his steps" (1 Peter 2:21).
> - "out of reverence for Christ" (Ephesians 5:21).
> - Paul appeals "by the meekness and gentleness of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:1).
> 
> **Others lower themselves before him.** John the Baptist: "He must increase, but I must decrease" (John 3:30). He says it after "I am not the Christ", and with "this joy of mine is now complete" (3:28–29). His lowering is said with joy.
> 
> **At his coming, a low estate is looked on.** Mary: "he has looked on the humble estate of his servant" (Luke 1:48). Her song goes on as Hannah's had: he scatters "the proud in the thoughts of their hearts", brings down the mighty and exalts the humble, fills the hungry and sends the rich away (Luke 1:51–53; 1 Samuel 2:7–8).

##### Section 371: chapter 10.14 Humility, The Old Testament and the New

> ## The Old Testament and the New
> 
> Everything above is in both Testaments:
> - humbling oneself before God, and God's answer
> - the proud brought low and the humble raised
> - favour to the humble
> - the lower seat
> - the meek inheriting
> - God humbling for good
> - a humbling he does not accept
> - humility beside hearing
> - the humble king
> - God lowering himself
> 
> The New Testament quotes the Old on three of them (Proverbs 3:34; Zechariah 9:9; Psalm 8:5–6).
> 
> What differs is emphasis, and one thing that is new.
> 
> - **Before God, then also toward one another.** In the Old Testament humbling is almost always before God. Kings and the people humble themselves, and the answer is wrath turned or disaster delayed. In the New, much of what is said is about humility toward one another. It always has a reason that faces God, and the answer is named as being justified, grace, and rest for the soul.
> - **The new thing: Christ.** He is the one who is lowly in heart, who humbled himself to death, and in whose mind the humble live. What God did in looking down, in his gentleness and in his breaking over a heart, the Son did in a human life. What the Old Testament said of man, "you have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings … you have put all things under his feet" (Psalm 8:5–6), the letter to the Hebrews reads as "namely Jesus" (Hebrews 2:6–9).

##### Section 380: chapter 13.1 Divine, Lowering himself

> ## Lowering himself
> 
> The Lord is high, and the verses keep saying that he looks low.
> - "Who is like the Lord our God, who is seated on high, who looks far down on the heavens and the earth? He raises the poor from the dust" (Psalm 113:5–7). The verb for looking far down is the one used when he brings the proud low.
> - "For though the Lord is high, he regards the lowly, but the haughty he knows from afar" (Psalm 138:6).
> - "I dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit, to revive the spirit of the lowly, and to revive the heart of the contrite" (Isaiah 57:15).
> - David: "You have given me the shield of your salvation, and your gentleness made me great" (2 Samuel 22:36; Psalm 18:35). The word rendered "gentleness" is the word for humility.
> - Over his people's unfaithfulness: "how I have been broken over their whoring heart that has departed from me" (Ezekiel 6:9).
> 
> **In the Son.** "who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross. Therefore God has highly exalted him" (Philippians 2:6–9). He says of himself, "I am gentle and lowly in heart" (Matthew 11:29). He came "humble, and mounted on a donkey" (Matthew 21:5). As a boy, "he went down with them and came to Nazareth and was submissive to them" (Luke 2:51). "In his humiliation justice was denied him" (Acts 8:33). He "for a little while was made lower than the angels … crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death" (Hebrews 2:9). These are the words of Psalm 8 about man, read of Jesus (Psalm 8:5). All things are put under him, and at the end "the Son himself will also be subjected to him who put all things in subjection under him, that God may be all in all" (1 Corinthians 15:28).
> 
> I read the lowering that the Old Testament tells of God, looking down, dwelling with the lowly, his gentleness, his being broken over a heart, as done in the Son in a human life.
> 
> **Toward the humble and the proud.** He sees the one who humbles himself and answers: "When the Lord saw that they humbled themselves" (2 Chronicles 12:7); "Have you seen how Ahab has humbled himself before me?" (1 Kings 21:29). He gives favour: "to the humble he gives favor" (Proverbs 3:34); "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (James 4:6). He humbles to test, "to do you good in the end" (Deuteronomy 8:16). He brings the proud low, "and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day" (Isaiah 2:11). He looks on a humble estate (Luke 1:48). What this means for the one who humbles himself is in 10.14.
