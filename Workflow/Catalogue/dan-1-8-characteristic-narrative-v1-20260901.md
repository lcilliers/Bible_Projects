## Finding: the characteristic engages the volitional sense of the heart specifically

Where this characteristic appears in Dan 1:8, it is not a generic mental or emotional event. The
term carrying it (*leb*, "heart") has a wide attested range — mind, conscience, seat of emotion,
seat of appetite, seat of courage — and this verse's own idiom selects one sense out of that range:
*inclination, resolution, determination of will*. The evidence for that selection is not asserted,
it is traceable — the combined-span idiom "set...upon the heart" is the translators' own collapse
of the phrase into a single decisive English verb, "resolved," and that collapse is itself the
data. The characteristic, here, is specifically **the heart in its willing/determining capacity**,
not the heart as feeling, conscience, or courage — those senses are present in the term's wider
range but not evidenced by this occurrence.

## Finding: the characteristic does not appear content-free

The resolve is not free-standing. Its content — what was resolved — is not separable from the
finding: *not to defile himself*. A characteristic account that recorded only "the will was engaged"
would say nothing usable; what makes this an instance of the characteristic at all is that it is a
will engaged *against* something specific, marked as a refusal by the negation attached to it, not
a pursuit. Resolve, on this one instance's evidence, characteristically shows up bound to a
concrete moral object, not as an abstract disposition.

## Finding: the characteristic produces outward, relational enactment

The resolve does not remain internal. A second act, sharing the same narrative-sequencing
morphology as the resolve itself, follows it directly: Daniel seeks permission from a named
authority figure. On this instance, the characteristic is not exhausted by an inner state — it
extends into social action, and specifically into *legitimate, mediated* social action (a request
made of the person with actual authority over the provision in question) rather than either
passive acceptance or unilateral defiance. This is a genuine finding about *this* instance: the
resolve's outward form here is petition, not confrontation.

## Inferential: the characteristic's relational shape may reflect a broader disposition

That the resolve is pursued through legitimate channels — asking, not defying — is consistent with
a disposition of honour or deference toward the authority being petitioned. This reading is
well-formed and worth holding, but it is **inferential, not a finding**: nothing in this verse's own
data names honour, respect, or a perception of dishonour. It rests on the *shape* of the sequence
(resolve → request, not resolve → covert action), not on any term's own content. Whether adjacent
verses in the same passage corroborate it is untested — flagged, not resolved.

## Hypothesis, held loosely: the characteristic's word-family suggests permanence, not a passing choice

The term's own lexical family — traceable through a shared root to "to encourage" and, through
that same root, to "to bake" — gestures toward a semantic field of *fixing, installing, making
permanent* rather than a transient mental event. If that family relationship bears on how the
characteristic itself should be articulated, "resolve" may be characteristically closer to
something set immovably at the center of the person than to an ordinary decision, reversible on
reflection. This is offered as a hypothesis the lexical family makes available, not as something
this one verse's usage confirms — a single occurrence cannot establish which sense of a word-family
is operative for the characteristic in general.

## Hypothesis, explicitly unresolved: the characteristic's object-root's own polarity

The very thing this resolve refuses — defilement — shares its consonantal root with "redemption."
STEP's own curated data links them; whether Hebrew lexicography treats that as one root with two
poles or as a documented near-homonym is contested even in the secondary literature, and this
session could not settle it either way (an attempted disambiguation test was run, failed its own
control case, and was retracted). If the relation is real, a characteristic account of "resolve
against defilement" would be incomplete without naming that the same linguistic root that names
the danger also names its remedy — but this is named here as an open question the word-family
raises, not a claim this instance's evidence establishes.

## What this instance does not, and cannot, establish

This is one verse, carrying one occurrence of the characteristic. The programme's own principle —
data collation complete before analysis, corpus-level treatment, not survey-level — applies here
directly: nothing above should be read as a characteristic-level conclusion. It is what one
well-worked instance yields, offered as the first entry a corpus-level account of this
characteristic would need, not as that account itself.
