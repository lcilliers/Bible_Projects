## Purpose

This document is the cluster science review for M74. It is produced as a pre-analytical asset for the D9.1.1 / D9.2.1 / D11.2.1 / D12.1.1 catalogue prompts. It does not constitute the biblical analysis.

The live gloss list (22 terms, queried 2026-09-17) is dominated by the Hebrew shamar family (H8104, split across four senses: "to obey," "to guard," "to look at," "to be careful") together with natsar, tsaphah, shaqad, and the Greek tēreō/phulassō/prosechō/agrypneō family. This cluster names a single underlying capacity — sustained, attentive monitoring directed toward protecting or preserving something of value — expressed across three registers: perceptual/cognitive vigilance, relational/protective guarding, and self-regulatory careful observance. The programme should note the direct overlap with M75 (obedience is the other face of shamar's semantic range, handled in live M54) and with M01's threat-vigilance material.

## Science sections

### Section 1 — Vigilance and sustained attention

**Primary field:** Cognitive psychology — attention and vigilance research

**Glosses covered:**
to watch out (G4337 prosechō, G4648 skopeō), to watch (H5341 natsar, H6822 tsaphah, H8245 shaqad), to keep watch (G1127 agrypneō), to arise: guard (H6965K)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Mackworth's classic clock-test research (1948) established the "vigilance decrement" — sustained attention to a low-frequency-event monitoring task reliably degrades over time, even in highly motivated observers, because sustained vigilance is metabolically and attentionally costly rather than a simple matter of will. Posner and Petersen's attention-networks model (*Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 1990) distinguishes an alerting network (maintaining a state of general readiness), an orienting network (selecting information from sensory input), and an executive network (resolving conflict among competing responses) — sustained watchfulness recruits primarily the alerting network, which is known to be effortful to maintain and vulnerable to fatigue. Tsaphah (to watch, as a sentinel watches from a height — cf. Ezek 3:17's watchman metaphor) and shaqad (to watch, be wakeful, from a root associated with the almond tree's early blossoming — "the wakeful tree") both carry a specifically anticipatory, forward-scanning sense that maps closely onto the alerting-network literature's account of readiness maintained in advance of an expected but not-yet-present event.

**Emergence — what causes vigilance to arise:**
Vigilance arises when a person or community holds responsibility for detecting an event (a threat, an arrival, a departure from a norm) whose timing is uncertain and whose consequences of missed detection are significant — the classic watchman posture. It is sustained by perceived stakes (higher stakes support longer vigilance before decrement sets in) and by role/identity commitment (a sentinel who has internalised the watching role as a duty sustains attention longer than one performing it as mere task compliance).

**Reduction and absence — what causes vigilance to diminish:**
The vigilance decrement is near-universal under sustained monitoring conditions without relief — attention lapses increase with time-on-task regardless of motivation, which is why watch-keeping in virtually every human tradition (military, nautical, biblical) is structured in shifts rather than assigned to a single indefinitely sustained watcher. Fatigue, monotony (low actual event-rate, which paradoxically makes maintained readiness harder, not easier), and divided attention are the primary reducers.

**Location — where vigilance is seated:**
Primarily cognitive-attentional, with a well-characterised neural substrate (the alerting network, involving thalamic and right-hemisphere frontal-parietal circuitry) and genuine physiological correlates (sustained mild sympathetic activation supporting readiness). It has a clear temporal structure — a state maintained across duration rather than a momentary response, distinguishing it from the acute threat-detection response of M01 Section 1, though the two systems interact closely (vigilance is what makes rapid acute detection possible when the anticipated event finally occurs).

**External influences:**
*Circumstances:* Genuine, recurring threat or responsibility (rather than manufactured anxiety) is the appropriate driver of sustained vigilance; chronic vigilance maintained without a genuine external referent shades into the hypervigilance seen in anxiety and trauma-related conditions (cross-reference M01). *Social:* Shared watch-keeping (shift structures, mutual accountability) is the well-established practical solution to the individual vigilance decrement, and the biblical pattern of appointed watchmen and shared night-watches reflects exactly this structural solution.

**Coverage assessment:** Good. Vigilance and sustained-attention research is a mature, well-developed field with direct application.

### Section 2 — Protective guarding and relational keeping

**Primary field:** Attachment and caregiving psychology

**Glosses covered:**
to keep (H5201/H5202 natsar), to keep: guard (G5083H tēreō, H8104H shamar), to keep/guard: guard (G5442H phulassō), to keep/guard: protect (G5442I phulassō), to keep: protect (G5083I tēreō), to keep (G1301 diatēreō, H7673B), to watch with envy (H7520 — a negative-valence outlier within the cluster, worth flagging for Session B)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Bowlby's attachment theory (*Attachment and Loss*, 1969–1980) and Ainsworth's subsequent empirical work establish the "secure base" concept — a caregiver who reliably watches over and protects the attached person enables that person's confident exploration of the world, precisely because the guarding function is trusted to be in place without needing to be consciously monitored by the one being kept. Protective, vigilant caregiving (distinct from anxious over-monitoring, which produces the opposite effect) is among the most robustly documented determinants of secure attachment outcomes across the lifespan. The shamar/natsar/tēreō/phulassō vocabulary in this section names exactly this protective-relational function — keeping another safe — as distinct from Section 1's more perceptual/anticipatory watching and Section 3's self-directed careful observance.

**Emergence — what causes protective guarding to arise:**
Protective guarding arises from a relational commitment (covenantal, familial, or pastoral) to another's safety and flourishing, combined with the ongoing capacity to monitor for and respond to threats against them. It is strengthened by clear role definition (a shepherd, parent, or covenant partner who has explicitly taken on the keeping role) and by accurate threat appraisal — protection misapplied against non-existent threats produces the over-control dynamics attachment research identifies as harmful (cf. M01's note on overprotective parenting maintaining rather than reducing fear).

**Reduction and absence — what causes protective guarding to diminish:**
Guarding diminishes through the withdrawal of relational commitment, through the guardian's own depletion or incapacity, or through role abdication. Its counterfeit — watching another not to protect but to envy or entrap (H7520's negative sense in this gloss list) — represents a corruption of the same underlying vigilance capacity turned toward harm rather than protection, worth distinguishing carefully from the cluster's dominant positive register.

**Location — where protective guarding is seated:**
Relational, like M70's support vocabulary and M72's authority vocabulary — the keeping function exists in the relationship between guardian and guarded, not as a property of either party alone. Its psychological effect on the one kept (a felt sense of safety enabling confident engagement with the world) is the attachment literature's central finding.

**External influences:**
*Developmental:* Early experience of being reliably guarded and kept establishes the secure-base template that shapes adult capacity both to trust others' protection and to extend protective guarding to others in turn. *Social/covenantal:* Formal role structures (shepherd-flock, parent-child, covenant-partner) provide the recognised basis on which protective guarding is undertaken and can be held accountable.

**Coverage assessment:** Good. Attachment and caregiving psychology provide strong, directly applicable coverage.

### Section 3 — Careful observance and self-monitoring

**Primary field:** Self-regulation psychology — control theory

**Glosses covered:**
to keep: obey (H8104G shamar), to keep: careful (H8104J shamar), to keep: look at (H8104I shamar), to keep/guard: observe (G5442G phulassō), to keep (G5083 tēreō — general observance sense)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Carver and Scheier's control-theory model of self-regulation (*Attention and Self-Regulation*, 1981; subsequent refinements) frames self-monitoring as a continuous feedback loop: a represented standard, ongoing comparison of current behaviour against it, and corrective action to reduce any discrepancy detected. Shamar's "careful keeping" sense (as distinct from mere perceptual watching or relational guarding) maps closely onto this monitoring-and-correcting loop — the person who "keeps" a command or a way of life is continuously comparing their own conduct against a held standard, which is precisely the self-regulatory function control theory describes. This connects M74's careful-observance register directly to M69's self-control vocabulary (enkrateia) — both name the same underlying monitoring-and-correction capacity, applied here specifically to sustained fidelity to a standard over time rather than to resisting a single impulse.

**Emergence — what causes careful self-monitored observance to arise:**
Careful observance arises when a standard is sufficiently internalised (not merely externally imposed) that the person voluntarily maintains ongoing comparison of their conduct against it, and is willing to correct course when discrepancy is detected. Internalisation (rather than externally enforced compliance) is what distinguishes sustained, willing "keeping" from mere rule-following under threat of sanction — the same distinction noted in the retired M30 file's discussion of shamar as attentive keeping rather than compliance.

**Reduction and absence — what causes careful observance to diminish:**
Observance diminishes when the standard is not internalised, when monitoring capacity is depleted (cf. M69's self-control-depletion material), or when competing goals crowd out sustained attention to the standard being kept.

**Location — where careful observance is seated:**
Cognitive and volitional — a sustained, self-directed monitoring process rather than a perceptual or relational one, though it draws on the same general attentional resources discussed in Section 1.

**External influences:**
*Developmental:* Standards that are explained and internalised in childhood (rather than imposed by bare authority) produce more durable adult self-monitored observance — the same finding noted for M30's obedience material. *Social:* Community accountability structures support sustained careful observance by supplementing individual self-monitoring with external checking.

**Coverage assessment:** Moderate. Self-regulation and control theory provide a strong conceptual match, though the specific vocabulary of "keeping a way of life" as observance has less direct empirical literature than either vigilance (Section 1) or attachment-based guarding (Section 2).

## Outside-science inventory

- **keeping God's commandments/covenant (shamar's central OT covenantal use)** — the theological category of covenant fidelity, where the standard kept is God's own revealed word, exceeds a generic self-regulation account; science addresses the monitoring mechanism but not the ground or authority of the standard.
- **God as the one who keeps/guards his people (the reciprocal direction, e.g. Ps 121's "the LORD who keeps you")** — divine protective guarding as a covenantal promise is a theological category; Section 2's attachment framework describes the human analogue and the psychological structure such a promise would address, but not the theological claim itself.
- **eschatological watchfulness ("keep watch, for you do not know the day," Matt 24–25)** — vigilance oriented toward the Lord's return combines Section 1's vigilance psychology with an eschatological object science does not reach.

**Observation:** This cluster's science coverage is genuinely good across all three registers (vigilance, protective guarding, self-monitoring), because each maps onto a mature, independently well-developed literature. The outside-science content is concentrated specifically at the point where the object of keeping becomes God himself or his covenant, rather than a general human standard or relationship.

## Catalogue prompt reference notes (D9.1.1 / D9.2.1 / D11.2.1 / D12.1.1)

**D9.1.1 — Physiological/neural substrate:** Vigilance (Section 1) has the clearest substrate — the alerting attention network, with sustained mild sympathetic activation; protective guarding (Section 2) engages attachment-related neuroendocrine systems (oxytocin-mediated bonding and caregiving circuitry); self-monitored observance (Section 3) draws on the same prefrontal executive-control substrate discussed for M69 self-control.

**D9.2.1 — Generational transmission:** Protective-guarding capacity and the disposition to trust others' guarding are both substantially transmitted through early attachment experience (secure base modelling across generations, a well-documented intergenerational-transmission-of-attachment-style literature — Main and Hesse). Internalised (versus merely compliant) observance is likewise transmitted through parenting style that explains rather than merely commands.

**D11.2.1 — Innate endowment vs. acquired:** The capacity for sustained attention and for attachment-based protective bonding are innate, early-developing human capacities; their specific direction (what is watched for, whom one guards, what standard is kept) is entirely acquired through relationship, community, and — in the theological register — covenant instruction.

**D12.1.1 — Social/behavioural manifestation:** Highly behaviourally visible across all three sections — watching is observable posture and action, guarding is observable protective behaviour, and careful observance is legible in consistent conduct over time; this cluster gives the programme a strong test case for whether "keeping" verses register primarily as an inner disposition, an outward behaviour, or (most often, per the shamar evidence) both inseparably.
