## Science sections

### Section 1 — Praise, extolling, and social appreciation

**Primary field:** Positive psychology — recognition and appreciation research

**Glosses covered:**
praise (ainos, H1960, H4110, H8416), to boast: praise (H1984B), praise (G0133, G0136), to praise (G0134, G1867, G5214, H3029, H7623B, G0437), to magnify (H1431, G3170), to exalt (G5251, H7311A, H7426, H7682), praiseworthy (G2128), praise (G2129)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Recognition and appreciation research in organisational psychology (Buckingham and Clifton, *Now, Discover Your Strengths*, 2001) identifies genuine recognition of another's worth or achievement as among the strongest motivators of engagement and wellbeing — a close secular analogue to the act of praise directed at a person or achievement. Worthiness and merit attribution (moral evaluation research — Alicke's work on blame and praise attribution) addresses the cognitive process by which an agent is judged deserving of positive regard. The magnifying/exalting vocabulary (ro.mam-family, H7311A/H7426/H7682) names an active, verbal elevation of the praised object's perceived stature — a public speech-act, not merely a private evaluation.

**Emergence — what causes praise and extolling to arise:**
Praise arises when an agent or object is appraised as possessing genuine excellence, achievement, or worth, and that appraisal is given public, verbal expression. Unlike thanksgiving (now a separate cluster), praise in this narrowed sense does not require that the speaker has personally received a benefit — it can be offered purely in recognition of another's excellence or greatness. Communal praise is amplified by shared appraisal — a community recognising the same excellence together reinforces and intensifies the individual act of praising.

**Reduction and absence — what causes praise to diminish:**
Praise diminishes through habituation (repeated exposure to the praised object or achievement reduces its perceived exceptionality), through cynicism or envy that resists acknowledging another's excellence, and through social norms that discourage open verbal appreciation. Where the appraised worth is disputed or contested, praise gives way to neutral description or criticism.

**Location — where praise and extolling are seated:**
Primarily cognitive (the evaluative appraisal of worth) and expressive/behavioural (the act of vocalising that appraisal). It has a social dimension distinct from private admiration: praise as this cluster's vocabulary defines it is characteristically spoken, sung, or otherwise given public form, not merely felt.

**External influences:**
*Cultural:* What is considered praiseworthy, and the social conventions for expressing praise, are strongly culturally shaped. *Developmental:* Communities that model and reward open appreciation cultivate the habit of praise in their members. *Circumstances:* Extraordinary achievement, deliverance, or display of excellence are the primary situational triggers for spontaneous praise.

**Coverage assessment:** Moderate. Praise as social appreciation has indirect coverage through recognition and merit-attribution research; it is less developed as its own research literature than gratitude (which no longer belongs to this cluster) or awe (Section 2).

### Section 2 — Glory, splendour, and elevation

**Primary field:** Psychology of awe and elevation / positive psychology

**Glosses covered:**
glory (doxa, H1926, H3519, H1925), glorious/glory (ka.vod), splendor (hod), adornment (ha.da.rah), to honor (ha.dar), good report (eufēmia), to glorify (G1392), be glorified (G1740)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Keltner and Haidt's awe research (2003) identifies perceived vastness and the need for cognitive accommodation as the two core features of awe; glory-perception in this section produces the same signature response. Haidt's elevation construct (*Daedalus*, 2000) addresses the emotional response to witnessing great virtue or moral beauty specifically — a warm, expansive chest sensation, motivation toward becoming better, and prosocial action. Glory vocabulary, particularly ka.vod (weight, heaviness, as glory), carries the semantic sense of impressiveness-as-weight that awe research independently identifies as central to the awe response.

**Emergence — what causes glory-perception and elevation to arise:**
Glory-perception arises when something is encountered as genuinely impressive — possessing weight, beauty, majesty, or moral excellence that exceeds ordinary experience. Elevation arises specifically in response to moral beauty: witnessing another act with exceptional virtue, generosity, or courage. Both are triggered by encountering something whose worth exceeds the observer's ordinary categories.

**Reduction and absence — what causes glory-perception to diminish:**
Familiarity and habituation reduce the perception of glory — the extraordinary becomes ordinary through repetition. Cynicism and moral disengagement reduce the capacity for elevation. Narcissism reduces both, by making the self the primary measure of worth.

**Location — where glory-perception is seated:**
Elevation has a distinctive somatic signature (Haidt describes a warm, open feeling in the chest, distinct from other positive emotions). Awe more broadly produces physiological responses (goosebumps, tingling, altered time perception). Both are primarily emotional and relational — inherently responsive, requiring an encounter with something external to the self.

**External influences:**
*Cultural:* What counts as glorious is culturally shaped — standards of beauty, excellence, and moral greatness vary by culture. *Developmental:* Exposure to genuine moral beauty and excellence in childhood cultivates the capacity for elevation. *Circumstances:* Encounters with unexpected greatness — moral, aesthetic, or natural — are the primary triggers.

**Coverage assessment:** Moderate. The glory vocabulary's primary referent is divine glory, which is substantially outside science's scope (see the outside-science inventory below). Science contributes chiefly at the experiential edge.

### Section 3 — Music, song, and communal celebration

**Primary field:** Music psychology / social psychology

**Glosses covered:**
melody (zim.rah, miz.mor), musical (na.im), to sing praise (psallō), to praise (humneō), to celebrate (eufrainō), to conduct (na.tsach), to sing (G0103, H2167, H7442B, H7891, H6030C), song (G5603, H2158, H2176, H7438, H7892A, H7892B)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Dunbar's social bonding through music research (*Behavioural Sciences*, 2012) demonstrates that communal singing produces oxytocin release and social bonding comparable to physical synchrony and touch. Music psychology (Juslin and Sloboda, *Music and Emotion*, 2010) addresses the mechanisms by which music produces emotional responses. Communal singing in particular is associated with increased in-group cohesion, shared emotional experience, and collective identity. Cross-cultural research confirms that music is a universal human practice, present in all known cultures, and functions primarily in social and ritual contexts.

**Emergence — what causes communal song and celebration to arise:**
Communal celebration arises from shared positive experience — victory, deliverance, harvest, religious observance. Song arises as a natural expression of emotions that exceed ordinary verbal formulation. Musical praise in religious contexts arises from the combination of awe (Section 2) and social bonding — a response to encountering something impressive that a community shares and reinforces together.

**Reduction and absence — what causes communal song to diminish:**
Communal song diminishes in contexts of grief, oppression, and social fragmentation (Psalm 137's "how shall we sing the LORD's song in a strange land?" maps onto the social psychology of song's dependency on community and safety). Individual singing is suppressed by self-consciousness; communal singing is suppressed by the loss of shared occasion.

**Location — where music and song are seated:**
Embodied, emotional, cognitive, and social simultaneously. Music engages the body (rhythm, breath, posture), the emotions (affective response to melody and harmony), the mind (text comprehension and memory), and the social dimension (synchrony with others). Of the sections in this review, music is the most comprehensively whole-person.

**External influences:**
*Cultural:* Musical forms, instruments, and occasions are entirely culturally shaped, though the practice of communal singing is universal. *Developmental:* Early musical experience — parental singing, communal worship — shapes musical capacity and its emotional associations. *Circumstances:* Celebration arises from positive shared events; lament arises from shared loss. Both find musical expression in religious communities.

**Coverage assessment:** Good. Music psychology is a well-developed field with direct relevance to this section's vocabulary.

## Outside-science inventory

- **glory (ka.vod, doxa)** in its divine-attribute register — the manifest, weighty presence of God's own being — exceeds what psychology of awe can address. Science addresses the human experience of encountering glory; the theological claim is that God's glory is real, not merely perceived.
- **to glorify/be glorified (G1392/G1740)** where directed at God — doxological speech ascribing worth to God as he actually is — is not reducible to the psychology of social appreciation.
- **praise (ha.lal)** in its fullest doxological register, ascribing infinite worth to an infinitely worthy God, exceeds the psychology of recognition and merit attribution.

**Observation:** With thanksgiving and holiness content now assigned elsewhere, M22's remaining outside-science content is concentrated even more tightly than before on the single question of divine glory and its worship — the cluster is now a cleaner instance of "praise and song" as a human-and-divine-directed activity, with a correspondingly cleaner divide between its science-addressable (social appreciation, elevation, music) and outside-science (the reality and worthiness of the God being praised) content.

## D9/D11/D12 prompt reference notes

> These four catalogue questions (`D9.1.1`, `D9.2.1`, `D11.2.1`, `D12.1.1`) replace the retired `T7.3` prompt as of the 2026-09-17 catalogue realignment (`catalogue_version='v2-dimension-realign-20260917'`). Each is answered here from this document's science sections as the pre-analytical data input; the verse-evidence side of each question is completed at the characteristic-level answer stage, not here.

**D9.1.1 — Physiological/Neural Substrate:** Elevation (Section 2) has a distinctive, well-documented somatic signature (warm chest sensation, distinct from ordinary positive affect); awe more broadly produces goosebumps, tingling, and altered time perception. Communal singing (Section 3) reliably triggers oxytocin release and measurable physiological synchrony among participants (Dunbar). Praise proper (Section 1) has the thinnest physiological literature of the three — it is better characterised as a cognitive-evaluative and expressive act than as an arousal state.

**D9.2.1 — Generational Transmission:** Moderate evidence. Musical practice and its emotional associations are substantially transmitted generationally through parental singing and communal worship participation (Section 3's developmental findings); the capacity for elevation is cultivated by childhood exposure to moral beauty and religious practice (Section 2). No literature treats praise-giving itself as generationally transmitted in a mechanism distinct from ordinary cultural and religious socialisation.

**D11.2.1 — Innate Endowment:** Partial. The capacity for awe and elevation shows some trait-like, plausibly heritable variation (openness to experience is among the strongest predictors of awe-proneness), and musicality itself has documented heritable components. Praise as a deliberate, verbal, worth-attributing act is not treated in the literature as an innate endowment — it is a learned, culturally-transmitted practice built on innate affective and musical capacities.

**D12.1.1 — Social/Behavioural Manifestation:** Strongly cooperative. All three sections of this cluster are inherently other- or communally-directed and prosocial in function: praise publicly elevates another's worth, elevation motivates prosocial and self-transcendent action, and communal singing is one of the best-documented cooperative bonding behaviours in the social sciences (Dunbar). This cluster shows none of the competitive/adversarial manifestation pattern found in clusters such as M06.
