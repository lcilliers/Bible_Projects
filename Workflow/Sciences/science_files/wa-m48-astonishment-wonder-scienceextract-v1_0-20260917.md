## Science sections

### Section 1 — Astonishment and cognitive shock

**Primary field:** Psychology of surprise — basic emotion and expectancy-violation research

**Glosses covered:**
astonished (G1569 existēmi-family), be astonished (G1605 ekplēssō), to astonish (G2284 katapleō/-family, H1724 charad-adjacent astonish sense), triumphing (H7445 — noted as an outlier gloss in this section, see coverage note)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Ekman's basic-emotion framework (1970s onward) identifies surprise as one of a small set of cross-culturally universal basic emotions, with a characteristic and rapidly-resolving facial signature (raised brows, widened eyes, dropped jaw) distinct from and typically briefer than fear or joy. Reisenzein's cognitive appraisal account of surprise (*Psychological Review*, 1983) treats surprise as arising specifically from schema-discrepancy — the violation of an expectation generated by the person's existing mental model of the situation — rather than from the mere occurrence of an unusual event per se; an event is surprising in proportion to how strongly it violates a held expectation, not simply how objectively rare it is. Meyer, Reisenzein and Schützwohl's expectancy-violation research further documents that surprise functions as an interrupt signal — it momentarily halts ongoing cognitive processing to prioritise attention toward, and updating in response to, the unexpected event, a genuinely adaptive information-processing function distinct from its purely subjective felt quality.

**Emergence — what causes astonishment to arise:**
Astonishment arises when an event or perception sharply violates the person's existing expectations or mental model — the greater the discrepancy between what was anticipated and what actually occurs, the stronger the astonishment. This is a cognitively mediated response (it depends on prior expectation, not merely objective stimulus intensity) though it is experienced as immediate and involuntary.

**Reduction and absence — what causes astonishment to diminish:**
Astonishment is inherently self-limiting and brief among the basic emotions — once the expectation-violating event is cognitively assimilated and the mental model updated to accommodate it, the astonishment response resolves (typically within seconds), distinguishing it from more sustained emotional states. Repeated exposure to the same expectation-violating stimulus produces habituation — the same event no longer astonishes once it is anticipated.

**Location — where astonishment is seated:**
Primarily cognitive (the appraisal of expectancy violation) with a brief, characteristic physiological and expressive signature (facial expression, momentary attentional interrupt). It is one of the shortest-duration basic emotional states in the literature, functioning as a rapid orienting and updating mechanism rather than a sustained mood.

**External influences:**
*Circumstances:* The strength of astonishment depends entirely on the gap between prior expectation and actual event — the same objective event will astonish one observer and not another depending on what each expected. *Developmental:* Children's capacity for astonishment depends on having formed the expectations that can then be violated — very young infants, lacking developed expectations about much of the world, show markedly different (often simply attentive rather than "surprised") responses to novel stimuli than older children and adults.

**Coverage assessment:** Good for the core astonishment/surprise phenomenon. Note: "triumphing" (H7445) appears in the live cluster_strong data for this section but does not obviously belong to the astonishment/surprise semantic field on its face — this may reflect either a genuine but non-obvious semantic connection in context, or a cluster-assignment question worth flagging for the biblical pass rather than silently omitting or reinterpreting here.

### Section 2 — Wonder, marvel, and awe

**Primary field:** Positive psychology — psychology of awe and wonder

**Glosses covered:**
amazement (G1611 ekstasis, G2285 thambos-family), marvel (G2295 thauma), to marvel (G2296 thaumazō), wonder (H4159, H4652, H6382, H8540), to wonder (H6381 pala)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Keltner and Haidt's foundational awe paper (2003) identifies two core features of awe: perceived vastness and a need for cognitive accommodation — the sense that existing mental categories are inadequate to the encountered phenomenon and must be stretched or revised. This is closely related to, but psychologically distinguishable from, the astonishment of Section 1: astonishment is primarily about violated expectation and resolves quickly once the model updates; wonder/awe carries a more sustained, often positively valenced quality of being confronted with something that exceeds ordinary categories altogether, and does not necessarily resolve through simple cognitive updating. Subsequent empirical awe research (Piff, Dietze, Feinberg, Stancato and Keltner, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2015) documents that awe reliably produces measurable self-diminishment (a reduced sense of one's own importance relative to the encountered vastness), prosocial orientation, and increased generosity in controlled experiments — awe is not merely a passive cognitive state but has documented downstream behavioural effects. The Hebrew pala (translated "to wonder" here, but carrying a broader sense of "to be extraordinary, beyond ordinary capacity or comprehension") is linguistically close to the "vastness/need-for-accommodation" structure Keltner and Haidt identify as awe's defining cognitive signature.

**Emergence — what causes wonder and marvel to arise:**
Wonder arises upon encountering something perceived as exceeding ordinary categories of explanation or expectation — not merely surprising (as in Section 1) but genuinely extraordinary, prompting a more sustained cognitive and affective engagement rather than a brief orienting response. Natural phenomena of great scale, extraordinary events, and — in the specific biblical register — divine acts of power are the primary elicitors documented both in the general awe literature and reflected in this cluster's own vocabulary.

**Reduction and absence — what causes wonder to diminish:**
As with awe generally (M01, Section 4), familiarity is the primary reducer — repeated exposure to the same phenomenon eventually assimilates it into an expanded ordinary category, at which point it ceases to prompt wonder. A stance of assumed mastery or comprehensive explanation (treating the world as already fully accounted for) tends to suppress the disposition toward wonder even in the presence of objectively wonder-worthy phenomena.

**Location — where wonder is seated:**
Primarily cognitive (the appraisal that existing categories are inadequate) and affective (a positively toned, often self-diminishing quality distinct from astonishment's more neutral cognitive-interrupt character), with documented downstream behavioural effects (prosociality, generosity) that place it partly in the motivational/behavioural domain as well.

**External influences:**
*Developmental:* Childhood exposure to natural environments, art, and formative encounters with scale and beauty cultivates ongoing capacity for wonder; environments that are exclusively functional or thoroughly explained/managed may reduce its development. *Individual differences:* Openness to experience (the Big Five trait) is among the strongest known predictors of wonder-proneness. *Cultural:* Traditions that ritualise or narratively frame wonder-inducing encounters (as much religious practice does) sustain and validate the disposition; cultures that systematically explain away or dismiss such encounters may suppress it.

**Coverage assessment:** Good. Awe and wonder research is one of the more active areas of contemporary positive psychology, providing solid direct coverage.

## Outside-science inventory

- **Wonder at God's specific acts (miracles, deliverance, creation)** — the cluster's vocabulary is frequently directed at divine acts specifically; science can address the general psychology of wonder at vastness and the extraordinary, but cannot adjudicate whether the biblical events that prompt this wonder actually occurred as described or were genuinely divine in origin.
- **Astonishment/wonder as a fitting response to divine revelation** — the normative claim that astonishment before God's self-disclosure is the right and appropriate human response (as against merely a documented psychological reaction) is a theological claim about fittingness that psychology of religion does not adjudicate.
- **Wonder (pala) in its "beyond human capacity" theological sense** — where the Hebrew vocabulary specifically marks an event as beyond what any human agent could accomplish (a marker of divine action, not merely subjective rarity), this exceeds the purely psychological account of wonder as a response to schema-discrepant or vast stimuli, since it makes an objective claim about the event's actual causal origin.

**Observation:** M48's outside-science content is smaller and more concentrated than in several other clusters in this batch, reflecting the cluster's largely experiential, human-response character. The main theological weight is not in the human response itself (well covered by awe and surprise research) but in what the response is directed at — specifically divine action — which science cannot itself verify or adjudicate.

## D9.1.1 / D9.2.1 / D11.2.1 / D12.1.1 prompt reference notes

**D9.1.1 — Physiological/neural substrate:** Documented for both sections, though more thinly than for some higher-profile emotions. Surprise/astonishment has a well-characterised, rapid facial-expressive and attentional-interrupt signature (Ekman; Reisenzein). Awe/wonder research has documented physiological correlates (goosebumps, altered time perception, parasympathetic accompaniment) though the underlying neural circuitry is less precisely mapped than for basic emotions like fear.

**D9.2.1 — Generational transmission:** Limited direct heritability literature for either astonishment or wonder as traits. What is generationally relevant is largely formational — childhood exposure to wonder-inducing environments and experiences (nature, art, religious practice) shapes adult wonder-capacity and disposition, transmitted through family and community rather than biologically inherited.

**D11.2.1 — Innate endowment vs. acquired:** Surprise/astonishment is a strong candidate for genuinely innate, universal basic-emotion status (Ekman's cross-cultural facial-expression research). Wonder/awe's underlying capacity is plausibly innate (shared with the general awe substrate discussed at M01), but its specific triggers, and the disposition to remain open to it rather than assume comprehensive mastery of one's world, are substantially shaped by acquired experience, openness-trait expression, and cultural formation.

**D12.1.1 — Social/behavioural manifestation:** Astonishment is strongly and briefly behaviourally manifest (characteristic facial expression, attentional interrupt). Wonder/awe has both an internal experiential quality and well-documented downstream behavioural effects (prosociality, generosity, reduced self-focus) — it is not a purely private state but one with measurable social consequence.
