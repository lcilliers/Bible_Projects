## Science sections

### Section 1 — Faith and its degrees

**Primary field:** Psychology of religion

**Glosses covered:**
faith (pistis, G4102G), faith: faithfulness (pistis, G4102H), of little faith (oligopistos), little faith (oligopistia)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Fowler's stages of faith (*Stages of Faith*, 1981) proposes a developmental framework moving from childhood undifferentiated faith through conventional and individualising stages to universalising faith — directly relevant to a vocabulary that names degrees of faith (little faith as against full faith) rather than only its presence or absence. Granqvist's attachment theory of religion suggests that faith in God as an attachment figure develops through psychological processes structurally similar to human attachment formation — facilitated by secure parenting, disrupted by insecure or absent parenting. Pargament's psychology of religion and coping addresses faith functionally, as a resource that expands a person's perceived capacity to cope with adversity.

**Emergence — what causes faith, in its fuller or lesser degree, to arise:**
Psychology of religion identifies multiple pathways: socialisation within a believing community, personal religious experience, gradual religious development, and existential crisis that drives religious seeking. Little faith (oligopistia) suggests a quantitative rather than binary category — partial trust, present but insufficient to sustain confidence under the specific pressure of crisis, distinct from the complete absence addressed in M31's unbelief.

**Reduction and absence — what causes faith to diminish toward "little faith" or beyond:**
Faith diminishes through negative religious experience (unanswered prayer, perceived divine absence), intellectual challenge, loss of the community context that sustains it, and gradual disengagement through reduced practice. Exline's spiritual-struggle research shows unresolved struggle is among the strongest predictors of this diminishment, though it does not inevitably proceed to full unbelief — the "little faith" category may name a stable, if strained, intermediate state.

**Location — where faith is seated:**
Pistis is simultaneously cognitive (belief that something is true), affective (trust as a felt state of reliance), and behavioural (faithfulness as sustained orientation in action). Psychology of religion locates religious faith as a whole-person orientation rather than merely a cognitive belief.

**External influences:**
*Developmental:* Religious formation in childhood (parental faith, community practice, religious education) is the strongest predictor of adult religious belief. *Attachment:* Secure human attachment correlates with secure attachment to God (Granqvist). *Circumstances:* Life crises can drive people toward faith (crisis as religious-seeking trigger) or toward its diminishment (perceived divine failure to protect) — the same circumstance can move a person in either direction depending on how it is interpreted and accompanied.

**Coverage assessment:** Moderate. Psychology of religion provides genuine coverage for the experiential and developmental dimensions of faith; the theological core (pistis as the instrument of justification) is outside science.

### Section 2 — Faithfulness and reliability

**Primary field:** Social psychology / organisational psychology — trust and commitment research

**Glosses covered:**
faithfulness (H0530, H0544), faithful (pistos, H0529), be faithful (a.man, H0539), to deal (H8266 — negative: to deal falsely)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Trust research (Rotter's interpersonal trust scale, 1967; Mayer, Davis, and Schoorman's integrative model of organisational trust, 1995) distinguishes dispositional trust from situation-specific trust, and identifies three components of trustworthiness — ability, benevolence, and integrity — reliability requires all three together. Rusbult's investment model examines the conditions under which people maintain faithfulness even when alternatives are attractive, identifying satisfaction, investment size, and quality of alternatives as the key predictors of sustained commitment.

**Emergence — what causes faithfulness to arise:**
Faithfulness arises from internalised commitment (the decision to be reliable regardless of circumstance), strong relationship investment that makes faithfulness the path of least psychological cost, and community accountability that monitors and reinforces commitment. Dealing falsely (H8266) names the specific behavioural failure of faithfulness — deception or breach within a relationship of trust, the direct negative pole of this section's vocabulary.

**Reduction and absence — what causes faithfulness to diminish or fail:**
Undermined by alternative attractions that raise the perceived cost of faithfulness, by the weakening of commitment through gradual emotional disengagement or specific betrayal, and by the absence of accountability structures that maintain the commitment. Rusbult's investment model specifically predicts that low investment and high-quality perceived alternatives together predict faithlessness even absent any acute crisis.

**Location — where faithfulness is seated:**
Primarily volitional and characterological — a sustained commitment of the will that can be maintained even when feeling has faded, distinguishing it from purely emotional bonds. It is also cognitive: the faithful person has accurate self-knowledge of their commitments and tracks them over time.

**External influences:**
*Relationship history:* Experiences of faithfulness received cultivate the capacity to be faithful; betrayal in early significant relationships undermines the template for it. *Cultural:* Cultures with strong honour norms around promise-keeping show different faithfulness profiles than low-commitment cultures. *Circumstances:* Periods of high temptation, high cost, or weakened self-regulatory capacity test faithfulness most severely.

**Coverage assessment:** Good. Trust and commitment research provide solid, directly applicable frameworks.

### Section 3 — Truth as reliability and trustworthy speech

**Primary field:** Social psychology / moral psychology

**Glosses covered:**
truth (alētheia, H7187, H7189B), be truthful (alētheuō), true (G0227, G0228), truth: faithful / truth: true / truth: certain (e.met-family, H0571G/H/I)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Levine's truth-default theory (*Duped*, 2019) proposes that humans have an evolved default toward believing others — deception is the exception, not the baseline — relevant background for why truthful speech functions as a reliability signal rather than a neutral fact-statement. This section's truth vocabulary, unlike M62's fuller epistemic-truth territory (straightness, sincerity, certainty, confirmation as such), is here bound tightly to faithfulness (H0571G is glossed "truth: faithful") — the e.met family in Hebrew usage frequently names reliability and steadfastness at least as much as correspondence-to-fact, a semantic range trust research's reliability construct maps onto more directly than correspondence theories of truth do.

**Emergence — what causes truthful, reliable speech to arise:**
Arises from a strong moral identity in which honesty is central (Aquino and Reed's moral-identity research), from community accountability that makes deception costly, and from the internal motivation to maintain coherence between conviction and expression. Because this section's e.met vocabulary leans toward reliability rather than pure fact-correspondence, its emergence is closely tied to Section 2's faithfulness findings — truthful speech here functions substantially as an instance of general reliability.

**Reduction and absence — what causes truthful, reliable speech to diminish:**
Reduced by situations where deception is perceived as low-risk and high-benefit, by the suppression of moral self-monitoring through self-justification, and by the progressive erosion of a truthfulness disposition through habitual small deceits (Ariely's incremental-dishonesty research).

**Location — where truthfulness-as-reliability is seated:**
Characterological and cognitive — a stable disposition of character with cognitive and volitional dimensions (recognising truth, choosing to express it reliably), closely bound to the faithfulness disposition of Section 2 rather than sitting as an independent epistemic category.

**External influences:**
*Community:* Truthful communities produce truthful individuals — the social environment that models, expects, and rewards honesty is the primary external shaper. *Parenting:* Parental modelling of honesty cultivates it as an internalised value. *Developmental:* The capacity for deception develops early; principled commitment to truthfulness develops later, through moral development in middle childhood and adolescence.

**Coverage assessment:** Good, with the caveat that this section deliberately reads the cluster's truth vocabulary through the reliability/faithfulness lens rather than the fuller epistemic-truth lens, which now belongs to M62.

### Section 4 — Devotion and godliness

**Primary field:** Psychology of religion

**Glosses covered:**
godly (G2318), devout (G2126), be devout (G4576), holy: saint (G0040H, H6918H)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Pargament's broader psychology-of-religion framework addresses devout practice and reverence as a stable orientation of the whole person toward the sacred, distinct from belief-content alone. The "saint" register of holy:saint in this section names not the abstract quality of holiness (covered in M12's consecration section) but the set-apart status of a person as belonging to God — sociologically, a form of in-group religious identity marking, well documented in the social psychology of religious identity (Ysseldyk, Matheson, and Anisman's social-identity approach to religion).

**Emergence — what causes devotion and the disposition of godliness to arise:**
Arises from sustained religious practice, formation within a community that models devout life, and the internalisation of religious identity as central to self-concept (per social-identity research on religion).

**Reduction and absence — what causes devotion to diminish:**
Diminishes through the same pathways documented for faith's diminishment (Section 1) — reduced practice, negative experience, loss of community — since devout disposition and faith itself are not sharply separable in the literature.

**Location — where devotion is seated:**
Whole-person and identity-level — devout disposition functions as a component of self-concept and social identity, not merely a private feeling or belief.

**External influences:**
*Developmental and social:* Formation within a devout community is the primary shaper; religious identity, once internalised, shows the same in-group stability and salience documented for other strong social identities.

**Coverage assessment:** Thin. Small section; social-identity approaches to religion provide the most directly relevant, though not extensive, coverage.

## Outside-science inventory

- **faith (pistis) in its Pauline theological register** — faith as the instrument of justification, the relational trust through which the believer receives the righteousness of Christ — is a theological category. Psychology addresses the phenomenology and social dynamics of religious belief; it does not evaluate the truth of what is believed or the mechanism by which faith relates to salvation.
- **faithfulness (e.mu.nah) as a divine attribute** — where this cluster's faithfulness vocabulary names God's own covenant faithfulness (the rock-solid reliability of the God who cannot lie), it is a theological category; human faithfulness as covenantal response mirrors it but is addressed by science only as a general human trust phenomenon, without its theological ground.
- **holy: saint (qa.dosh/hagios)** in its ontological sense — a person set apart and made holy by God's own action — exceeds social-identity theory's account of in-group religious identity marking.

**Observation:** M13's outside-science content clusters at the divine-attribute and soteriological ends, consistent with its faith-and-faithfulness core. The human phenomena of faith development, trust and commitment, reliable/truthful speech, and devout practice are all reasonably well covered by science.

## D9/D11/D12 prompt reference notes

> These four catalogue questions (`D9.1.1`, `D9.2.1`, `D11.2.1`, `D12.1.1`) replace the retired `T7.3` prompt as of the 2026-09-17 catalogue realignment (`catalogue_version='v2-dimension-realign-20260917'`). Each is answered here from this document's science sections as the pre-analytical data input; the verse-evidence side of each question is completed at the characteristic-level answer stage, not here.

**D9.1.1 — Physiological/Neural Substrate:** Thin. Faith and religious devotion (Sections 1, 4) share some substrate with the attachment system generally (Granqvist's God-attachment research draws directly on human attachment neurobiology), but faith, faithfulness, and reliable speech (Sections 2–3) are otherwise better characterised as cognitive-relational-volitional constructs than as states with a distinct physiological signature.

**D9.2.1 — Generational Transmission:** Strong. Religious formation in childhood is, across the psychology-of-religion literature, the single strongest predictor of adult faith, faithfulness disposition, and devout practice — this is one of the most robustly generationally-transmitted characteristics reviewed in this batch, second only to M05's kindness/gentleness modelling findings.

**D11.2.1 — Innate Endowment:** Not strongly supported for faith/faithfulness content as such, though the underlying attachment system that God-relationship faith draws on (Granqvist) is itself an innate, evolved human capacity — the object and content of faith is not innate, but the relational-bonding architecture it uses plausibly is. This is a useful case for D11.1: an innate created capacity (attachment) repurposed toward a specific, non-innate object (faith in God).

**D12.1.1 — Social/Behavioural Manifestation:** Strongly relational/cooperative. Faith, faithfulness, reliable speech, and devotion are all fundamentally other-directed in the psychological literature — trust and commitment exist only in relation to another party, and religious devotion is substantially constituted through community identity and practice. This cluster shows a clearly cooperative/bonding manifestation profile, closer to M05 and M39 than to the adversarial clusters in this batch.
