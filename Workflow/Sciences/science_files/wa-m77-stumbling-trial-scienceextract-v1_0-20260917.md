## Purpose

This document is the cluster science review for M77. It is produced as a pre-analytical asset for the D9.1.1 / D9.2.1 / D11.2.1 / D12.1.1 catalogue prompts. It does not constitute the biblical analysis.

The live gloss list (18 terms, queried 2026-09-17) covers stumbling proper (proskoptō/ptaiō family, Hebrew kashal), stumbling blocks and being caused to stumble (skandalon/skandalizō family, Hebrew mikshol/mokesh), enticement (deleazō, Hebrew pathah-family), persuasion (peithō family), sifting (siniazō), and a single "trial" term (H4531B, nissayon). ## Science sections

### Section 1 — Stumbling and situational temptation

**Primary field:** Clinical psychology / self-regulation research

**Glosses covered:**
stumbling block (G4348 proskomma, G4625 skandalon), stumbling (G4349 proskopē, H3783/H4383/H6761), to cause to stumble (G4624 skandalizō), to stumble (G4417 ptaiō, H3782 kashal), to strike/stumble (G4348-related proskoptō), not stumbling (G0677 aproskopos)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Baumeister and Muraven's self-control research (*Psychological Science*, 1998) frames stumbling-inducing temptation as a situation where immediate desire conflicts with longer-term values and commitments. Hofmann's ecological momentary assessment research (*Psychological Science*, 2012) collected real-time reports of desire and temptation across daily life, finding that resistance success varies with the strength of the desire, current self-regulatory resources, and — critically for this section — situational/environmental context. The skandalon vocabulary (a snare or trap that causes falling — the term's root sense is the trigger-stick of an animal trap) maps directly onto the psychology of environmental cues that trigger desire: situational-cue research consistently finds that removing or avoiding a temptation-triggering environment is a more effective and reliable strategy than in-the-moment resistance once the cue is already present and desire is already activated (Gollwitzer's implementation-intention research on pre-committed if-then plans is the strongest evidence-based intervention in this literature).

**Emergence — what causes stumbling to arise:**
Stumbling arises when a desired object or a situational trap is present or made salient, and the person's self-regulatory resources at that moment are insufficient to resist — insufficiency that can result from prior depletion, proximity to the object, social modelling of the stumbling behaviour, or the prohibition itself paradoxically amplifying desire (psychological reactance, cf. M75 Section 1). Skandalon's specific sense — a stumbling block placed or existing in another's path — locates part of the cause outside the one who stumbles: this vocabulary treats falling as sometimes substantially attributable to what (or who) placed the obstacle, not solely to the stumbler's own weakness, a distinction the biblical material treats with real moral seriousness (cf. warnings against causing another to stumble).

**Reduction and absence — what causes stumbling to diminish:**
Situation management — removing the stumbling block or avoiding the triggering environment — is consistently more effective than relying on in-the-moment resistance to strong, already-activated desire. Pre-commitment (implementation intentions formed before the tempting situation is encountered) and social accountability that makes the standard visible to others both measurably reduce stumbling. "Not stumbling" (aproskopos) as the section's positive-pole term names the achieved state of successful navigation through a testing environment.

**Location — where stumbling is seated:**
Affective-motivational (the felt pull toward the object) with a genuine cognitive dimension (appraisal of the conflict between desire and value) and — distinctively for this section — a situational dimension that is not purely internal: the stumbling block is, in the vocabulary's own logic, partly located in the environment/relationship rather than solely within the one who stumbles, mirroring the relational-location pattern already noted for M70, M72, and M74.

**External influences:**
*Social:* Peer contexts that normalise the stumbling behaviour dramatically increase susceptibility; social accountability that makes values visible to others increases resistance. *Developmental:* Delay-of-gratification capacity (Mischel) develops through childhood and is shaped by parental practice and family environment. *Cultural:* What counts as a stumbling block is culturally variable — it must be simultaneously desired/salient and available, and different communities construct different environments of temptation.

**Coverage assessment:** Good. Self-control and situational-cue research are well-developed and directly applicable.

### Section 2 — Enticement and being led astray

**Primary field:** Social psychology of persuasion and manipulation

**Glosses covered:**
to entice (G1185 deleazō, H6601B pathah), enticement (H4065 midcheh), to persuade (G0374 anapeithō, G3982 peithō)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Cialdini's persuasion research (*Influence*, 1984) identifies the six principles of social influence — reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity — several of which are directly exploitable in enticement (deleazō's own root sense is "to bait," as in fishing or trapping, the same underlying image as skandalon's trap-mechanism sense above). Petty and Cacioppo's elaboration likelihood model (1986) distinguishes central-route persuasion (careful consideration of arguments, more resistant to manipulation) from peripheral-route persuasion (responding to superficial cues and heuristics without deep processing, more vulnerable to enticement) — cognitively depleted or emotionally aroused states shift processing toward the more exploitable peripheral route. The deception and manipulation literature more broadly (overlapping with M14 Deceit's own territory) addresses enticement as a deliberate, targeted strategy exploiting a specific known vulnerability in the person being led astray, distinguishing it from ordinary situational temptation (Section 1), where no deceiving agent need be present.

**Emergence — what causes enticement/being led astray to arise:**
Enticement arises when an agent deliberately presents an attractive but harmful object or path, typically concealing or minimising the associated cost, and targets a state (depletion, isolation, unmet need) that increases susceptibility to peripheral-route, low-scrutiny processing. Unlike Section 1's situational stumbling, enticement specifically requires an active enticing agent — a distinction the biblical vocabulary's causative forms (to entice, to cause to stumble) consistently preserve.

**Reduction and absence — what causes susceptibility to enticement to diminish:**
Central-route processing (careful, engaged scrutiny of the actual costs and benefits on offer) is the primary individual-level reducer, though it requires cognitive resources that depletion or strong emotion reduce. Community structures that provide independent perspective (someone outside the enticing relationship who can name what is happening) are a well-documented protective factor against sustained manipulation, since the person being enticed frequently cannot see the dynamic clearly from inside it.

**Location — where enticement is seated:**
Relational and cognitive — the enticing agent and the process of persuasion are the primary loci, with the target's vulnerability (depletion, unmet need, isolation) as the receiving condition that determines susceptibility.

**External influences:**
*Social:* Isolation from independent outside perspective is a major risk factor for sustained enticement/manipulation; embedded, honest community relationship is the primary protective factor. *Circumstances:* States of need, depletion, or unmet desire increase susceptibility to enticement that targets exactly that need.

**Coverage assessment:** Good for the persuasion mechanism; the specific moral seriousness the biblical material attaches to being an enticing agent (as opposed to merely a susceptible target) exceeds what persuasion research on its own addresses (see also M77 Section 1's parallel note on causing another to stumble).

### Section 3 — Sifting and trial under adversarial pressure

**Primary field:** Social psychology — persuasion and adversity research

**Glosses covered:**
to sift (G4617 siniazō), trial (H4531B nissayon), to try (G3987 peiraō)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
Siniazō (used only once in the New Testament, Luke 22:31, of Satan "sifting" Peter as wheat) names an image combining testing, temptation, and separation of the genuine from the spurious in a single act. This is distinct from live M35's own dokimazō/dokimē vocabulary (testing that proves and approves quality, an evaluative-disclosure process) in that sifting names an adversarial process actively imposed by a hostile agent seeking the person's fall, not a neutral or benevolent evaluative process. The general psychology of adversity and stress-testing (Lazarus and Folkman's stress-appraisal framework, already used across M01 and M35) is relevant background: whether an adversarial trial produces collapse or resilience depends heavily on appraisal (threat versus challenge) and on the presence of supporting relationship during the trial, a finding directly paralleled by the biblical narrative's own emphasis on intercession accompanying Peter's sifting ("I have prayed for you," Luke 22:32).

**Emergence — what causes sifting/adversarial trial to arise:**
Sifting arises when a hostile agent deliberately subjects a person to pressure calculated to expose and exploit weakness, distinguishing it from Section 1's situational stumbling (no deliberate adversary required) and from Section 2's enticement (which conceals harm rather than applying open pressure) — sifting is closer to direct adversarial assault than to either.

**Reduction and absence:**
Resilience under adversarial trial is best predicted, per the stress-appraisal literature, by challenge (rather than pure threat) appraisal, by prior successful experience of manageable difficulty, and — most consistently across this literature — by the presence of supportive relationship sustained through the trial.

**Location — where this is seated:**
Combines the situational/relational location of the previous two sections (an external adversarial agent and process) with the internal appraisal and coping response of the one being sifted.

**Coverage assessment:** Thin. This is a narrow, single-instance vocabulary item within the cluster; the general adversity/resilience literature provides useful but indirect coverage.

## Outside-science inventory

- **temptation and sifting by Satan/spiritual adversaries** — science addresses temptation and manipulation as internal and social-psychological phenomena; the theological category of demonic temptation and sifting as an external personal spiritual assault (explicit in the siniazō text) is outside science's descriptive scope.
- **being caused to stumble as covenantal harm to "little ones"** — the specific moral seriousness of causing another believer to stumble (Matt 18:6 and parallels) is grounded in the value God places on his people, a theological warrant exceeding ordinary harm-avoidance ethics.
- **the trial (nissayon) of faith as divinely permitted testing** — where trial is framed as something God permits for the proving and strengthening of faith (paralleling live M35's own outside-science note on purposive divine testing), this exceeds the purely adversarial-pressure account given in Section 3 above.

**Observation:** M77 and M35 divide a single semantic domain (testing/trial/temptation) along a genuinely meaningful line that the science literature independently supports: M35 retains the evaluative, proving, disclosure-of-existing-quality register (dokimazō); M77 covers the falling, entrapment, and adversarial-pressure register (skandalon, kashal, siniazō). The analyst should expect these two clusters' science reviews to be read together where a verse's vocabulary sits near the boundary.

## Catalogue prompt reference notes (D9.1.1 / D9.2.1 / D11.2.1 / D12.1.1)

**D9.1.1 — Physiological/neural substrate:** Stumbling/temptation susceptibility (Section 1) engages the same prefrontal-limbic self-regulation substrate discussed under M69; peripheral-route persuasion susceptibility (Section 2) is associated with reduced prefrontal engagement under cognitive load or strong affect, consistent with dual-process models of judgement (Kahneman's System 1/System 2 framework).

**D9.2.1 — Generational transmission:** Delay-of-gratification and self-regulatory capacity (bearing on stumbling susceptibility) are transmitted through early parental modelling (Mischel's follow-up research on the marshmallow paradigm's family-environment correlates); susceptibility to manipulation/enticement is shaped by whether a person's formative relationships modelled trustworthy or exploitative influence, an intergenerational relational-template transmission rather than a genetic one.

**D11.2.1 — Innate endowment vs. acquired:** The underlying self-regulatory and social-cognitive capacities are part of innate human cognitive architecture (present from early development, maturing through adolescence); specific vulnerability to particular stumbling blocks or enticements is acquired through individual history, need-state, and relational context.

**D12.1.1 — Social/behavioural manifestation:** Behaviourally visible as the observable event of falling, faltering, or being drawn off course; but this cluster is unusual in that a significant share of its vocabulary (skandalon, deleazō, siniazō) names not the faller's own state but another agent's action — making the causative, other-directed behavioural dimension (who places the stumbling block, who entices, who sifts) as analytically significant as the falling person's own inner response.
