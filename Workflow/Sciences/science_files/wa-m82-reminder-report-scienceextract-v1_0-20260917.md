## Science sections

### Section 1 — Reminding as a relational and testimonial act

**Primary field:** Cognitive psychology of retrieval cues / social psychology of testimony

**Glosses covered:**
to remind (G0363 anamimnēskō, G1878 epanamimnēskō, G5279 hupomimnēskō), remembrance (G0364 anamnēsis, G3417 mneia, G3420 mnēma, G5280 hupomnēsis)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
As noted in the old M41 file's Section 1, reminding (hupomimnēskō — "bringing to remembrance") is fundamentally different from remembering itself: it is an external, relational act of activating another person's retrieval process, not a private cognitive event. Cue-dependent retrieval research (Tulving and Thomson's encoding-specificity principle, *Psychological Review*, 1973) establishes that memory retrieval is substantially dependent on the presence of appropriate cues matching the original encoding context — a reminder functions precisely as such a cue, deliberately supplied by one person to trigger another's retrieval of material that might otherwise remain dormant or inaccessible. This gives reminding a genuine, non-trivial cognitive function distinct from simply repeating information: an effective reminder does not teach something new but reactivates something already encoded, which is why biblical reminding language (Peter's repeated "I will always remind you," 2 Pet 1:12–13) is directed at things the hearers already know rather than new instruction. The testimony and eyewitness-memory literature (Loftus's extensive work on memory malleability, and the more recent literature on cognitive interviewing techniques) is separately relevant to the report/testimony dimension of this cluster: how something is recalled and reported is substantially shaped by how the recall is prompted, a finding with bearing on how reminders themselves can (for good or ill) shape what is subsequently reported as remembered.

**Emergence — what causes reminding to arise:**
Reminding arises from a relational commitment to another's faithful retention of something judged important enough to warrant deliberate reinforcement — a teacher, elder, or covenant community member undertaking to counteract the default forgetting trajectory documented in M81 Section 2 on another's behalf, not merely their own. Anamnēsis's specifically liturgical and commemorative sense (used of the Lord's Supper, "do this in remembrance of me") names a formalised, ritualised version of this same reminding function, institutionalised as a recurring communal practice rather than an ad hoc individual act.

**Reduction and absence — what causes reminding to diminish or fail:**
Reminding fails when the cue supplied does not match the original encoding context closely enough to trigger retrieval (encoding specificity), when the relationship carrying the reminder lacks sufficient credibility or relational weight for the hearer to attend to it, or when reminders are so frequent or poorly targeted that they are habituated to and no longer processed as meaningful cues (a general finding from attention and habituation research, relevant to any communication delivered too repetitively without variation).

**Location — where reminding is seated:**
Relational, like several other clusters in this batch (M70, M74, M80) — reminding exists between a reminder and the one reminded, not as a private inner state; its effect (successful retrieval in the hearer) is located in the cognitive systems discussed under M81.

**External influences:**
*Social:* The credibility and relational standing of the reminder substantially determines whether the reminder is attended to and effective. *Cultural/liturgical:* Ritualised, communal reminding (as in the Lord's Supper's anamnēsis) provides a structurally reliable countermeasure to individual forgetting that ad hoc individual reminding cannot match on its own.

**Coverage assessment:** Good. Cue-dependent retrieval research and testimony psychology together provide solid, directly relevant coverage.

### Section 2 — News, tidings, and the social transmission of report

**Primary field:** Social psychology — communication and rumour research

**Glosses covered:**
news (G5334 phasis?/pheme-related, G5345 phēmē), tidings (H8052 shemuah), report (H8088B shema, H8089 shoma)

**Landmark findings and key researchers:**
As documented in the old M41 file's Section 4, Allport and Postman's rumour research (*The Psychology of Rumor*, 1947) addresses how information is transformed as it passes through social transmission chains — a finding relevant to reading biblical "report" vocabulary critically, since the reliability and content of a report are treated in the biblical material itself as things requiring evaluation (cf. the recurring biblical concern with distinguishing true from false report). Rosen and Tesser's "MUM effect" research (*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1970, already noted under M70's bearing-tidings section) documents a robust, well-replicated reluctance to transmit bad news relative to good — people systematically delay, soften, or avoid delivering negative reports even when timely accurate transmission matters, while bearers of good news are disproportionately welcomed (the herald effect). This is directly relevant to the biblical vocabulary's positive valence around "good tidings" (cf. besorah/euangelion, adjacent but not identical to this cluster's own gloss set) and to the narrative weight given to messengers throughout the biblical corpus. Information-diffusion research (social network analysis of how news spreads through communities) addresses the structural, rather than purely psychological, dimension of how tidings move from an origin point through a community.

**Emergence — what causes news/tidings/report to arise and spread:**
Report arises when information judged significant enough to share crosses from one party (with first-hand or received knowledge) to another. Its spread through a community is governed by the social-network structure connecting potential transmitters and receivers, by the perceived stakes and credibility of the information, and — per the MUM effect — by a systematic asymmetry favouring the swifter, more willing transmission of good news over bad.

**Reduction and absence:**
Reports fail to spread, or spread distorted, when transmission chains are long (each retelling introduces potential drift, per Allport and Postman's serial-reproduction studies), when the news is bad (structural reluctance to transmit), or when the social network connecting the origin to potential receivers is sparse or blocked.

**Location — where this is seated:**
Entirely social and communicative — this section's vocabulary names the informational content and its transmission, not an inner state in the receiver, though the receiving of tidings (especially unexpected good or catastrophic news) produces genuine downstream inner-being effects (joy, grief, fear) that belong to other clusters (M04, M03, M01 respectively) rather than to this one.

**External influences:**
*Social:* Network structure and relational trust between transmitter and receiver are the primary determinants of whether, how quickly, and how accurately report spreads. *Cultural:* Norms around the social role of the messenger (honoured, as in the biblical "good tidings" tradition, or historically sometimes blamed for the content they carry — "shoot the messenger") vary and shape willingness to transmit difficult news.

**Coverage assessment:** Thin but genuine, mirroring the old M41 file's own assessment of this material — communication and rumour research provide real but limited coverage, since these terms primarily describe the social vehicle of information rather than an inner-being state in their own right.

## Outside-science inventory

- **reminding as apostolic/pastoral teaching function** — the theological authority by which apostolic reminding functions as an exercise of teaching office within the church (2 Peter, the Johannine and Pauline reminder-passages) exceeds ordinary cue-dependent-retrieval psychology.
- **anamnēsis in the Lord's Supper** — the sacramental claim that this specific ritual remembrance is a form of participation in Christ's redemptive act, not merely a memory-sustaining practice, is a theological category beyond what liturgical-memory research alone addresses.
- **the report of the gospel (besorah/euangelion, the "good tidings" tradition this cluster's shemuah vocabulary anticipates)** — where tidings carry specifically salvific content, the MUM-effect and herald-effect research illuminates the human psychology of good-news reception but not the theological content or authority of the news itself.

**Observation:** This cluster's science coverage divides cleanly between a genuinely strong section (reminding, via cue-dependent retrieval research) and a genuinely thin one (news/report, largely descriptive of a social vehicle rather than a psychological state) — consistent with the old M41 file's own honest assessment of its Section 4 as its thinnest section.

## Catalogue prompt reference notes (D9.1.1 / D9.2.1 / D11.2.1 / D12.1.1)

**D9.1.1 — Physiological/neural substrate:** Reminding's substrate is the retrieval-cue and hippocampal-reactivation systems discussed under M81; news/report reception engages whatever downstream affective/somatic system the content itself triggers (joy, fear, grief circuitry), meaning this cluster's own substrate is largely the transmission channel rather than a discrete state of its own.

**D9.2.1 — Generational transmission:** Reminding practices (ritualised communal reminder, catechetical repetition) are the primary generational-transmission mechanism the programme has for sustaining shared memory across a community over time — this cluster is, in effect, part of the transmission *mechanism* for other clusters' content (especially M81) rather than a transmitted content in its own right.

**D11.2.1 — Innate endowment vs. acquired:** Both halves are acquired/practised rather than innate — reminding is a learned relational skill and report-transmission is a learned social behaviour, though both draw on innate capacities (retrieval-cue sensitivity, social-network embeddedness) that are themselves part of ordinary human cognitive and social architecture.

**D12.1.1 — Social/behavioural manifestation:** Entirely social/behavioural by definition — both reminding and report exist only as enacted communication between people, with no private, purely internal form; this cluster is one of the clearest cases in the whole programme of vocabulary whose primary referent is an interpersonal transaction.
