## 0. Standing constraints on this exploration

Accepted and carried forward from the researcher's direction:

- The inner being **does not operate in buckets**. It is a system. The 46 families are a convenience of arrangement, not a representation of the system. This exploration therefore records **presence** — does the passage address a seat, and which — and does **not** construct an ontology of parts.
- The Psalms are **one book among sixty-six**. Nothing here is offered as a correlation to other evidence, and no external source has been introduced.
- Every observation below carries a citation in the form `[theme | Psa C:V-V | #n]`, abbreviated here to `[Psa C:V-V | #n]` since the theme is constant throughout.
- References are **passage ranges** (mean ~9 verses). No observation below can be bound to a single verse or to a Hebrew lemma. This is a known and accepted limitation.
- Counts appearing below are counts of **exact formulaic strings** in the corpus. They are reported as lenses, not gates. **Measurement informs; it never decides.** No observation in this digest exists or is excluded because of a count.

**Observation of substance:** the theme named for heart, soul and spirit also contains **flesh, body and bones**. The corpus does not fence the interior off from the body. It repeatedly crosses the line:

- *"my heart and flesh sing for joy to the living God"* — recorded **twice**, once from the flesh `[Psa 84:1-12 | #1]` and once from the heart `[Psa 84:1-12 | #2]`. The corpus describes it as *"the whole embodied person, not soul alone"*.
- *"My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart"* `[Psa 73:20-28 | #1]`.
- *"my heart is glad… my flesh also dwells secure"* — *"the total self at rest"* `[Psa 16:1-11 | #3]`.
- *"no soundness in my flesh because of your indignation; no health in my bones because of my sin"* — *"the body sick with guilt"* `[Psa 38:1-20 | #2]`.

## 2. Observation — the corpus systematically declares that it does not localise

This is the single most consistent feature of the theme, and it cuts directly against the possibility of building a map of parts.

Of the 167 sections:

- **132** close with: *"The psalm does not tell us where inside a person this sits, how strong it was, or how it finally turned out."*
- **35** close with: *"The psalm does not tell us how strong it was, or how it finally turned out."*

So **every one of the 167 sections declares that intensity and outcome are unstated**, and **132 of 167 explicitly declare that the text does not say where inside a person the movement sits** — in the very theme whose subject is the seats.

That is not a small thing. The theme that names heart, soul and spirit is also the theme that most often reports that Scripture declines to say *where inside a person* anything is happening. The seat is named; the location is not given.

## 3. Observation — the same seat performs opposite movements, often within one psalm

The corpus does not assign a fixed function to any seat. It records the same seat doing contrary things, and in several places it marks the contrast itself.

**Spirit — three states in a single psalm.** The corpus explicitly enumerates them: *"first of three spirit-states in the psalm (right v10, willing v12, broken v17)"* `[Psa 51:1-19 | #2]`; the willing spirit `[#3]`; and the broken spirit as *"the third and climactic spirit-state"* `[#5]`.

**Spirit — fainting and searching, same psalm, marked as a contrast.** *"my spirit faints"* `[Psa 77:1-3 | #2]`; then *"my spirit made a diligent search"* — glossed by the corpus as *"the searching inner power (contrast the fainting spirit of v3) - grief become inquiry"* `[Psa 77:5-18 | #1]`.

**Heart — steadfast and not-steadfast.** Steadfast: `[Psa 57:1-11 | #3]`, `[Psa 108:1-8 | #1]`, *"his heart is firm, trusting in the LORD"* `[Psa 112:1-10 | #1]`. Not steadfast: *"a generation whose heart was not steadfast"* `[Psa 78:8-13 | #1]`, repeated at `[Psa 78:37-46 | #1]`, and set against *"with upright heart he shepherded them"* `[Psa 78:61-72 | #1]`.

**Heart — the same core, made clean and broken, in one psalm.** *"create in me a clean heart"* `[Psa 51:1-19 | #1]`; *"a broken and contrite heart"* — which the corpus calls *"the same core asked to be re-created clean (v10), presented in its brokenness… the two poles of the heart in one psalm"* `[Psa 51:1-19 | #4]`.

**Heart — melted and firm.** *"my heart is like wax; it is melted within my breast"* `[Psa 22:13-20 | #1]`; against the steady, unafraid heart `[Psa 112:1-10 | #2]`.

**Heart — the plotter and the wounded, mirrored.** *"war was in his heart"* — glossed as *"the dark mirror of the psalmist's own anguished heart (v4), one bleeding, the other plotting"* `[Psa 55:9-23 | #2]`, against `[Psa 55:1-5 | #1]`.

**Soul — bowed and steadfast, same psalm.** *"my soul was bowed down"* `[Psa 57:1-11 | #2]`, then *"the bowed soul rises to a steadfast heart (v7)"* `[Psa 57:1-11 | #3]`.

## 4. Observation — the seat is frequently a site, not an agent

In **14** sections the corpus states outright that *"the [seat] is not doing anything; it is simply the inner self being named or acted upon"* — e.g. `[Psa 42:1-11 | #1, #2, #3, #5, #6]`, `[Psa 44:15-26 | #2, #3]`, `[Psa 49:5-20 | #1, #2]`, `[Psa 53:1-6 | #1]`.

Beyond that formula, a large body of sections show the seat as **grammatical object** of an action performed by God or by circumstance:

- **created / renewed:** *"create in me a clean heart"* `[Psa 51:1-19 | #1]`; *"renew a right spirit within me"* `[#2]`
- **restored / revived:** *"He restores my soul"* `[Psa 23:1-6 | #1]`
- **redeemed / delivered / kept:** `[Psa 55:9-23 | #1]`, `[Psa 56:1-13 | #1]`, `[Psa 66:1-9 | #1]`, `[Psa 86:9-17 | #3]`, `[Psa 116:1-10 | #3]`
- **cut off:** *"he cuts off the spirit of princes"* `[Psa 76:7-12 | #1]`
- **turned:** *"He turned their hearts to hate his people"* `[Psa 105:24-27 | #1]`
- **bowed down:** *"he bowed their hearts down with hard labour"* `[Psa 107:4-13 | #1]`
- **given over:** *"gave them over to their stubborn hearts"* `[Psa 81:11-12 | #1]`
- **gladdened / strengthened by ordinary means:** *"wine to gladden the heart of man"* `[Psa 104:14-15 | #1]`; *"bread to strengthen man's heart"* `[#2]`
- **embittered by other people:** *"they made his spirit bitter"* — Moses `[Psa 106:22-32 | #1]`
- **cast away:** *"why do you cast my soul away?"* `[Psa 88:14-18 | #1]`

The interior is acted upon by God, by enemies, by hard labour, by bread and wine, and by grief.

## 5. Observation — the interior addresses itself

A distinct and recurring movement: the self turns on the self.

- *"Why are you cast down, O my soul?"* — *"the self in dialogue with itself… self-counsel against despair"* `[Psa 42:1-11 | #5]`, repeated `[#2]` and carried into the companion psalm `[Psa 43:1-5 | #1]`.
- *"Bless the LORD, O my soul"* `[Psa 103:1-4 | #1, #2]`, `[Psa 103:16-22 | #1]`, `[Psa 104:7-8 | #1]`, `[Psa 104:31-35 | #1]`; and *"Praise the LORD, O my soul"* — *"the will rouses the soul, praise begun by self-address… the interior turned on itself as summons"* `[Psa 146:1-10 | #1]`.
- *"Return, O my soul, to your rest"* `[Psa 116:1-10 | #2]`.
- *"in the night also my heart instructs me"* — *"the interior itself… becomes a teacher"* `[Psa 16:1-11 | #2]`.
- *"my spirit made a diligent search"* `[Psa 77:5-18 | #1]`.
- And its inverse — the self submitting the interior to an external search: *"Search me, O God, and know my heart"* — *"the self turns the searchlight inward"* `[Psa 139:21-24 | #1]`.

## 9. Interpretation — held lightly, and clearly separated from the above

Three readings the observations support. None is a conclusion.

**9.1 The corpus resists partition — and says so.** The strongest evidence is §2. In the theme most likely to produce a doctrine of parts, the corpus declares 132 times that the text does not say *where inside a person* the movement sits. Combined with §1 (flesh and bones sit inside the "inner seat" theme) and §3 (no seat has a fixed function), the material does not support a model of discrete faculties with assigned jobs. What it appears to support is closer to: **the seat names the depth at which something is happening, not the compartment in which it happens.**

**9.2 The interior is porous in both directions.** §4 and §5 together suggest the inner being is neither purely agent nor purely patient. It acts on itself (§5), it is acted upon by God, enemies, labour, food and grief (§4), and it is described as unreachable by man but transparent to God (§6). If a boundary is being drawn anywhere in this theme, it is not between spirit and body — it is between **what man can reach and what God can reach**.

**9.3 The recurring axis may be integration, not composition.** §7 — double heart against united heart, whole heart against divided — is the closest this theme comes to a structural claim about the inner being. But note what it is: not a claim that the heart *has parts*, but a claim that the heart can be *divided or whole*. That is a claim about **integrity**, not architecture.

## 10. Reflection — impact, and what this would mean if it held

**For the programme's governing question.** If §9.1 holds, then "describe the spirit–soul–body boundary" may be the wrong shape of question to put to the Psalms. This theme does not draw that boundary; it crosses it freely (§1) and declines to localise (§2). That is a finding about the evidence, not a failure of the evidence — and it is exactly the kind of finding the reset was meant to make sayable.

**For readers and traditions.** A tradition that reads the Psalms as teaching a tripartite anthropology will find the seat-vocabulary here (heart, soul, spirit) and may take it as a taxonomy. A tradition that reads holistically will find §1 and §2. Both are reading the same 167 sections. This exploration cannot adjudicate between them and does not attempt to; it can only report that the corpus, as written, names seats and simultaneously refuses to locate movements within them. Readers whose spirituality is built on interior self-examination will find §5 and §6 rich; readers for whom the Psalms are corporate and liturgical will note that several of these "inner" movements are **collective** — the nation's soul in the dust `[Psa 44:15-26 | #3]`, the people's soul kept alive `[Psa 66:1-9 | #1]`, *"our soul waits"* `[Psa 33:15-22 | #1]`.

**On application — no conclusion drawn.** The material invites application (self-address in despair, §5; the plea for an undivided heart, §7). It is not yet ready to bear it. One theme of forty-six has been read.

## 11. Referral candidates — for in-depth analysis at source level

Per the standing agreement, these are flagged, not resolved.

**11.1 A possible template tension.** 132 sections state the psalm *"does not tell us where inside a person this sits"* while the section's own subject **is** a seat (heart, soul, spirit). This may mean "the text does not localise *further*" — a coherent reading. It may also be a template default applied without discrimination. The distinction matters a great deal to §9.1, which currently rests on it. **This should be checked at source.**

**11.2 Uneven lexical identification within the theme.** **110 of 167** sections carry a transliterated term; **57 do not.** The un-transliterated sections are concentrated in the early passages (Psa 3 through roughly Psa 41): `[Psa 3:1-8 | #1]`, `[Psa 6:1-10 | #1]`, `[Psa 7:1-17 | #1, #2]`, `[Psa 10:1-8 | #1, #2]`, `[Psa 10:10-18 | #1]`, `[Psa 12:1-4 | #1]`, `[Psa 16:1-11 | #1, #2, #3]`, `[Psa 19:7-14 | #1]` and onward. The theme therefore contains at least two generations of story, written to different templates. This does not invalidate the content, but it means the sections are **not uniformly comparable**, and any future lexical work will find the early band thinner.

**11.3 Template slot-fill artefacts.** In roughly 19 sections the "The ___ is caught up in this" slot has picked up a non-seat token — e.g. *"The says is caught up in this"* `[Psa 10:1-8 | #1]`, `[Psa 10:10-18 | #1]`; *"The unfeeling is…"* `[Psa 119:66-82 | #1]`; *"The us is…"* `[Psa 124:3-7 | #1, #2]`; *"The bereft is…"* `[Psa 35:9-15 | #1]`; *"The soundness is…"* `[Psa 38:1-20 | #2]`. In two sections `[Psa 25:5-22 | #2]` and `[Psa 51:1-19 | #5]` the slot has swallowed a large run of the preceding text. **Cosmetic, but it should be cleaned before this theme is used as a lexical source.**

**11.4 A near-duplicate pair.** `[Psa 119:66-82 | #1]` and `[#2]` narrate the same clause (*"their heart is unfeeling like fat"*) twice, with different subject slots. Similarly `[Psa 101:1-8 | #2]` and `[#3]` both treat the perverse heart. Whether these are two distinct movements or one movement recorded twice is a source-level question.
