## 1. Headline observation

**`other-uncategorised` is not a bin of leftovers. It is two different things fused together, and one of them is a set of gaps in the 46-family arrangement.**

The 55 sections divide into:

- **(a) Sections that would fit an existing family.** Roughly 20 of the 55. They are not uncategorisable; they are simply unassigned. Examples: *"uphold me with a willing spirit"* `[Psa 51:1-19 | #1]` (belongs with the inner seat, and indeed the same clause **already appears** in `inner-seat-heart-soul-spirit` at `[Psa 51:1-19 | #3]`); *"do not harden your hearts"* `[Psa 95:4-11 | #1]` (rebellion; also already in inner-seat at `[Psa 95:4-11 | #2]`); *"I have stored up your word in my heart"* `[Psa 119:1-18 | #1]` (torah / keeping); *"I hasten and do not delay to keep your commandments"* `[Psa 119:42-63 | #1]` (torah); *"he who dwells in the shelter of the Most High"* `[Psa 91:1 | #1]` (trust-refuge); *"come, let us sing… let us come into his presence"* `[Psa 95:1-2 | #1]` (worship); *"our mouth was filled with laughter"* `[Psa 126:2-3 | #1]` (joy); *"they tested God in their heart"* `[Psa 78:17-22 | #1]` (desire / being-tested); *"they grumbled in their tents"* `[Psa 106:22-32 | #1]` (rebellion); the fool and his corruption `[Psa 53:1-6 | #1, #2]` (wickedness).

## 2. Observation — three coherent subjects with no family in the arrangement

### 2.1 Justice and injustice in office (~8 sections)

There is **no justice family** among the 46. Yet this theme carries a sustained treatment of it:

- *"How long will you judge unjustly?"* — God confronts the judges; the corpus reads past the verdicts to *"a settled leaning toward injustice, a crookedness of heart"* `[Psa 82:1-8 | #1]`, and then to the act it produces: *"authority using its own power to do the exact reverse of what it exists for"* `[Psa 82:1-8 | #2]`.
- *"In their hearts you devise wrongs; your hands deal out violence"* — *"as if weighing it out on scales, dispensing cruelty with the same deliberate care a judge should give to justice"* `[Psa 58:1-5 | #1]`.
- Rulers *"who frame injustice by statute"* — *"they do not break the law to do harm — they write the harm into the statutes"* `[Psa 94:7-23 | #2]`.
- *"They kill the widow and the sojourner, and murder the fatherless"* — *"a heart that holds the weak in such disregard it will destroy them"* `[Psa 94:2-5 | #1]`.
- Against these, the positive pole: the prayer for a righteous king `[Psa 72:1-5 | #1]`, and *"defend the cause of the poor… crush the oppressor"* — *"Real authority, the psalm insists, throws its weight behind the powerless"* `[Psa 72:1-5 | #2]`.
- And the character-level version: the unjust man *"not a single wrong the man commits, but the kind of person he simply is"* `[Psa 43:1-5 | #1]`.

This is a fully-formed subject with a positive and a negative pole, an inner disposition and an outward act. It has been filed under "other" because the 46 families have no slot for it.

### 2.2 Idolatry and mis-directed devotion (~5 sections)

`worship-prostration-service` exists, but it faces one way. There is **no idolatry family**, so its material sits here:

- *"they exchanged the glory of God for the image of an ox"* — *"the worst bargain imaginable, everything of worth handed over for nothing"* `[Psa 106:18-20 | #1]`.
- *"they sacrificed their sons and daughters to demons"* — *"idolatry become monstrous"* `[Psa 106:37 | #1]`.
- *"they played the whore"* — idolatry as *"unfaithfulness to the God they belonged to, like a spouse breaking the marriage bond"* `[Psa 106:38-48 | #1]`.
- And its counterpart, devotion rightly fixed: God's people *"treasure even her broken stones and feel tenderness for her very dust"* `[Psa 102:12-16 | #1]`.

### 2.3 What a human being is — dignity, mortality, limit (~4 sections)

No family covers this either:

- *"no man can ransom his brother"* — *"a leveling truth that strips away every boast"* `[Psa 49:5-20 | #2]`.

## 3. Observation — a device concentrated here that matters to the whole programme

Nine of the 55 sections carry an explicit statement that **the same inner capacity, the same word, is bent to opposite ends depending on its object.** The corpus states this itself; it is not my reading.

- *"The word for being pleased is the very one used for delighting in God's ways, which makes the twist sharper — the same power of approval that should welcome good is here bent toward theft"* `[Psa 50:13-23 | #2]`.
- *"The same ordinary word for doing and making is used elsewhere for doing no wrong at all; here it is bent to the steady manufacture of harm"* `[Psa 53:1-6 | #2]` — and the mirror: *"the same energy that some pour into wrongdoing, the blameless pour into doing right"* `[Psa 119:1-18 | #2]`.
- *"The very same word for judging is used elsewhere for judges who pervert justice; here it is turned to its true purpose"* `[Psa 72:1-5 | #2]`; and from the other side, *"the identical word, bent to the opposite end"* `[Psa 82:1-8 | #2]`.
- *"The same word can mean honest weariness or legal cruelty"* `[Psa 55:9-23 | #1]`, `[Psa 94:7-23 | #2]`.
- *"The same inner capacity for delight that others waste on flattering fools or approving thieves is here fixed where it belongs"* `[Psa 102:12-16 | #1]`.
- *"The same steady, unafraid heart that trusts God… is the heart that opens wide to the needy"* `[Psa 112:1-10 | #1]`.

**This device appears 27 times across the whole corpus — and 9 of those 27 are in this one theme of 55 sections.** It appears **zero times** in the 167 sections of `inner-seat-heart-soul-spirit`.

And one section goes further and states a general law outright — a rarity anywhere in this corpus: on the idols who have mouths and cannot speak, *"There is a law here about the inner life — we come to resemble whatever we worship… the emptiness of the idol seeps into the person who reveres it"* `[Psa 135:17-18 | #1]`.

## 5. Interpretation — held lightly

**5.1 The label is wrong.** "Uncategorised" implies the material resisted categorisation. What the reading shows is that the material was **not offered a category**. Justice, idolatry, mortality, envy, anxiety, generosity, wonder — these are not residues. They are subjects the 46-family arrangement does not have a slot for.

**5.2 This is direct confirmation of the researcher's own position on the families.** The families are *"separate for convenience sake, not a representation of the system."* This theme is where that convenience visibly runs out. It is the offcut of the arrangement, and reading the offcut tells you where the arrangement was cut.

**5.3 Section 3 may be the most consequential thing in the theme.** The "same word bent to opposite ends" device says something the movement model needs: **the movement is not intrinsically good or evil — it takes its character from its object and direction.** Delight, judgment, doing, working, steadiness — each is recorded here running both ways. If that holds across the corpus, then *direction* and *target* are not one edge among nine; they are the edge that determines what the movement **is**. That is a claim about the architecture of the model, arrived at from the residual bucket, and it feeds straight into E4.

## 6. Reflection — impact and consequence

**Consequence for the other explorations.** Any statement of the form "the Psalms have nothing to say about X" must now be checked against this bucket first. A justice-shaped, idolatry-shaped or mortality-shaped question put to the 46 families would return nothing — and the answer would be false.

**Consequence for the arrangement itself.** The families are working instruments, not claims. But an instrument with a 55-section offcut is telling you something about its own shape. Three candidate families are visible in the offcut: **justice-and-office**, **idolatry / mis-directed devotion**, and **the human condition (dignity, mortality, limit)**. Whether to form them, leave them, or dissolve the family arrangement further is a researcher decision and I make no recommendation.

**For different readers.** Sections 2.1 and 2.4 will land very differently across traditions. A reader in a context of state injustice will find `[Psa 94:7-23 | #2]` — harm written into the statute book — the sharpest thing in the corpus. A reader in comfortable circumstances may pass over it. Neither reading is available from the 46 families at all, because the material was filed under "other". That is worth sitting with.

**On application — no conclusion drawn.** Two themes of forty-six have been read.

## 7. Referral candidates

**7.1 Cross-theme duplicates.** At least two clauses appear in **both** this theme and `inner-seat-heart-soul-spirit`, written independently: the willing spirit — `[other | Psa 51:1-19 | #1]` and `[inner-seat | Psa 51:1-19 | #3]`; the hardened heart — `[other | Psa 95:4-11 | #1]` and `[inner-seat | Psa 95:4-11 | #2]`. Whether these are two distinct movements or one movement recorded twice under two families is a source-level question. **This pattern will recur across the corpus and should be checked before any counting is done anywhere.**

**7.2 Template divergence (see §4).** `other-uncategorised` and `inner-seat` were written to entirely different templates. Combined with the two-generation split already found *within* `inner-seat` (E1, §11.2), the corpus contains **at least three distinct generations of story**. Sections from different generations are **not uniformly comparable**, and any cross-theme synthesis must account for this. This is now the single largest known threat to the validity of cross-theme work.

**7.3 Four sections on one betrayal.** `[Psa 55:9-23 | #2, #3, #4, #5]` treat the same betrayal from four angles (the friend inside the defences; buttery speech; war in the heart; words softer than oil). The corpus itself calls them *"three strokes"* and *"the third image in a single verse"*. Whether these are four movements or one movement in four aspects is a source-level question, and it is a clean test case for the singleton rule.
