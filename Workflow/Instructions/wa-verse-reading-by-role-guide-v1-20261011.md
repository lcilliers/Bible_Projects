# Reading a verse, and a collection of verses, by the role of each word (v1)

**Date:** 2026-10-11 · **Escalation:** #1995 (withdrawn v1, re-proposed against this guide), from #1993 · **Audience:** Claude, when reading strand verses and weaving them. **Status:** in force on the researcher's instruction; changes go through the researcher.

**Instruction (researcher, chat 2026-10-11, verbatim):** *"proceed to withdraw and to prepare a proper guide for your self from the above findings - this is exactly how each verse and collection of verses should be considered."*

This guide defines no new concepts. It brings together terms and rulings the study already holds, each with its source, and sets them in the order in which a verse and a collection are read. Where this guide and a source differ, the source wins and this guide is corrected.

---

## 1. What the reading is for

- **The study's question:** what Scripture reveals about the **characteristics, operations and interrelationships** of the human inner being (glossary *inner being*; `cfg_prose_concept inner_being_definition`).
- **The inner being is a web of interrelated activity** (#1900). A strand is one thread of that web. A verse is where several threads cross.
- **A characteristic is not predefined.** It is known only by inference from what the inner being does. A lemma, gloss, surface word or cluster tag is **not** a characteristic (glossary *characteristic*).
- **The same word changes character with circumstance** (#1919). Each word's faces, the circumstances that differ, and why are a key discovery.
- **The focus is the human inner being (HIB).** Other beings are read for what they are and do, and for how they act with the HIB (rule 72).

So a verse is never read as "a verse about the strand's word". It is read for what the inner being is doing in it, and for how the words in it work together.

## 2. The terms used

| Term | Meaning in the study | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **M-code cluster** | A loose analytical grouping of Strong's by shared characteristics. An anchor point, not a goal. No re-tagging. | glossary *M-Code cluster*; rule 70; #1900 |
| **role** | Each word in a verse has a role **in that occurrence**: **characteristic** (it behaves as a characteristic of the inner being), **qualifier / process-element** (it binds to a characteristic and gives it value or context in place), or **standalone**. The same lemma can be a characteristic in one verse and a qualifier in another. | glossary *role*; verse-analysis method §13–14 |
| **qualifier** | Gives value to its binding pair **in place**. It gets no own account from that verse. The exception is a qualifier whose usage varies across contexts: it may earn its own reading later. | method §13b |
| **main IB operation** | The inner-being activity the verse is about. | researcher on 2Pe 2:13 (#1993) |
| **operation** | What an inner-being activity is doing and how, including to or from others. | glossary *operation* |
| **relational observation** | How two M-code words relate and operate together inside the verse. | #1704 §2 (b)(ii), decision 2 |
| **pointer observation** | Always kept separate. It points to discovery outside the verse: another cluster, another verse, a clue such as "at every point" in 2Cor 7:11 pointing back to 7:2. | #1704 decision 2; #1706 §4 item 4, §6 |
| **party-kind T-codes** | T4 adversarial, T7 divine, T8 human, T9 angelic, T11 corporate. They identify who is acting and who is acted on. | cluster table; #1704 (b)(iii) |

## 3. Before reading a collection

1. **Use what is already extracted** (rule 71). The strand folder's pulls and their columns (`other_m_words`, `narrative_refs`, `hit_words`, group) come first. The verse → M-code link is `verse_lexical.strong` → `cluster_strong` (`cluster_code LIKE 'M%'`, `deleted=0`), read with **one set-based query** over the whole set. Add the T-codes from the same join.
2. **The surface table** per Strong's, both ways (root → surfaces, surface → roots). A surface is a pointer to a verse to read, never a meaning (`feedback_surface_is_the_sensitivity_lens`).
3. **Earlier readings of the same verses.** Find what other strands, ledgers, accounts and the narrative already say about each verse. A narrative citation is verse-level: it does not mean the strand's word was read (`feedback_narrative_citation_is_verse_level_not_word_coverage`). Build on earlier readings; do not re-derive them (#1706 §1–2).
4. **The open threads register.** Check `_analytics/cross-cluster-web/open-threads-register.md` for items whose trigger this collection meets.
5. **Filters and buckets are working aids**, absorbed by the reading. They are never a standing decision item or a reason to park a verse.

## 4. Reading one verse

This follows the researcher's confirmed sequence (#1704 decision 1: *"the reading sequence matter … progressive findings drive the further discovery … llm does not pick and choose, and silently ignore what it reads; it must follow a discovery path"*). The sequence says **what to notice, and in what order**. It is **not** a frame of fixed answers to fill for every verse. The rejected M64 §2a pass did that and fragmented the meaning (`feedback_read_verse_for_inner_act_not_event_template`). What the verse says emerges from the reading.

**4.1 Read the verse in its passage and its genre.** Read the surrounding passage, not the verse alone. Name the genre and speaker: narrative, oracle, wisdom saying, poem, disputation, a party judging another's act. Explore figurative speech for what it says about the inner being; do not take it literally or merely restate it. Take paired and opposite acts together (Isa 7:15–16, *refuse the evil and choose the good*).

**4.2 Find the main IB operation, and the role of every M-coded word.** List every M-coded word in the verse. From the reading, decide:
- which word or words carry the **main IB operation** (role: **characteristic**)
- which are **qualifiers / process-elements** bound to a characteristic, and what each gives it: a measure, a strengthening, a ground, a cause, an object, a setting, the bait
- which are **standalone**

The role comes from this verse, never from the cluster tag.

**4.3 Only then read the strand's own word, in the role it has here.**
- If it is the characteristic, it is the focal point of the read. Everything else is considered in relation to it (#1704 (b)(i)).
- If it is a qualifier, it is read as the qualifier of that characteristic. It gets **no own account from this verse** (method §13b). It is carried as a face of the word ("the measure of the reveling") and as an interrelationship.

**4.4 Read how the M-code words work together.** Record a **relational observation** where the verse shows two characteristics working together, or one qualifying another. Ask what role the strand's word plays in relation to the other M-codes present (#1706 §2). Where the verse points outside itself (to another cluster, an earlier verse or a clue), record a **pointer**, separately. Do not resolve it inline.

**4.5 Read the parties.** Find who bears the inner act, who causes it, and who is acted on: God (including the Spirit, for 13.1), angels, other spirits, other people, a corporate body, nature. Note source, target, impact and purpose as the verse gives them (#1704 (b)(iii)). Route the verse A, B or C (rule 72); a verse may take A and B.

**4.6 Read the action words and the other referent words.** Read the T3 operations and their relation to each party. Read the movement and force, the negators (a refusal is an act), the connectives (cause, purpose), and the descriptive words. Read body parts, places, objects and the natural world for what they carry here (#1704 (b)(iv)). An untagged word can carry the operation (G0627 in 2Cor 7:11, #1706 §3), so read every row, not only the tagged ones.

**4.7 Note the face.** Note how the strand's word appears here, the circumstance, and why it differs from its other occurrences (#1919). Note where the sense in this verse departs from the general gloss (#1706 §4 item 5).

**4.8 Record.** For each verse, record:
- the main IB operation
- the role of each M-coded word, with what each qualifier gives
- the strand word's role and face
- the relational and pointer observations
- the parties and the route
- the home, which follows the main operation

Mark readings beyond what the verse states as **"I read"**. Join no verses into a pattern no verse states (rule 69).

## 5. Reading a collection

**5.1 Progressive, not isolated.** A verse with several M-codes is read once for each strand that reaches it. Each strand sees what earlier strands found for that verse and builds on it. The question shifts to what this strand's characteristic does in relation to the others (#1706: 2Cor 7:11 read by M67, then M03, then M18).

**5.2 Group by the main operation, not the entry word.** Gather the verses by what the inner being is doing in them. Do not gather by gloss group, cluster or literary form. Where the strand's word is the characteristic, its verses build its account. Where it is a qualifier, its verses go:
- to the account of the characteristic they qualify, with the verse's home
- to a short table in the strand word's own section, listing what it qualifies and how. An example is 10.1 *When delight is only the measure*.

**5.3 One account per phenomenon.** For each characteristic, the account answers three questions: what Scripture says it is, what forms it takes (from the words themselves), and why the forms differ. It narrates the inner workings by probing: compare, ask why, ask what lies behind it, ask whether the relationship makes a difference, and explore the figures. It never restates verses as a list (`feedback_weave_with_meaning_not_quote_lists`; narration standard, #1993). Other sections carry only what belongs to their activity, and point back to the account.

**5.4 Interrelationships and faces are findings.** Qualifier bindings and relational observations from across the collection are part of what the study is looking for. They are recorded in the reading and feed the patterns account (Ch 11 §4) and the change-of-character file. They are never thrown away because the verse's home is elsewhere.

**5.5 Faculty last.** Where the operations happen (heart, soul, spirit, mind) is reflected on once, at the end, over the collection (#1704 decision 4).

**5.6 Working disciplines.**
- Trial a few verses and have them checked before running the whole set.
- Read the verses; do not ask the researcher what a reading would answer.
- Do not hold items unnecessarily. Read them while busy with them.
- Existing narrative is not audited in a dedicated pass. Misplacements are corrected in each strand's read-back, as the verse's characteristic M-code comes into focus (#1993 ruling).
- Weave, don't fit. Every reading gets a placement; "does not fit" means the structure must change (#1918).

## 6. Worked cases

**2Pe 2:13 (#1993).** The verse came into the M18 pull through "pleasure".

| Word | M-code | Role |
|---|---|---|
| wrongdoing (G0093) | M58 | characteristic: the main IB operation is suffering the wage of wrongdoing |
| revel (G5172) | M28 | an inner-being operation acting in the verse: what they give themselves to |
| deceptions (G0539) | M14 | qualifier: strengthens the reveling |
| pleasure (G2237) | M18 | qualifier: the **measure** of the reveling ("count it pleasure") |
| count (G2233) | M72 | qualifier: the reckoning that makes it a measure; a loose tag, recorded, not re-tagged |

Researcher: *"making something of this verse with pleasure as an entry point will lead to distorted views."* The home follows the main operation. M18 carries the verse only as a measure.

**2Cor 7:11 (#1706).** Seven M-codes meet in one verse. In the M67 pass the question is what role earnestness plays in relation to the others. In the M03 pass, grief is qualified as *godly*, and earnestness becomes the measure of it. In the M18 pass, the question is what godly grief did for longing, and whether earnestness made a difference. The phrase "at every point" is a pointer back to 2Cor 7:2. *Eagerness to clear* (G0627) carried no M-code yet carried an operation.

**Psa 1:1, 1:5 (method §14).** *Counsel* in "the counsel of the wicked" and *judgment* in "stand in the judgment" are qualifiers there. *Delight*, *meditation*, *wicked* and *righteous* are characteristics. Role is per occurrence.

## 7. Checks before a reading is handed over

- Was every M-coded word, and every untagged row that carries the operation, given a role?
- Was the strand word read in the role it has in the verse, not by its tag?
- Does every verse's home follow its main operation?
- Are the qualifier bindings and relational observations recorded, and the pointers kept separate?
- Were earlier readings of the same verses used, not re-derived?
- Is every reading beyond the verse marked "I read", with no imputed pattern?
- Are the parties and the route recorded?
