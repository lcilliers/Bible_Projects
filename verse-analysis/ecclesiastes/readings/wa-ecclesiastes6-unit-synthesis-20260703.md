## ECC-06-A · Ecc 6:1–6 · [D, multi] — the un-enjoying soul; wealth without the power to enjoy it

**The reading.** This is the dark negation the refrain set up (5:19). A man is given "**wealth, possessions, and honor**, so that he **lacks nothing of all that he desires**, yet God does **not give him power to enjoy them**, but a stranger enjoys them" (v.2). The finding is **the tragedy of the un-enjoying soul** — having everything the appetite wants and being unable to enjoy any of it; the goods are present, the *capacity* absent *(stated)*. The verdict is severe: "his **soul is not satisfied** with life's good things" (v.3); a stillborn child, which "has not seen the sun or known anything, yet finds **rest**," is "better off than he" (vv.3–5). The operation is **desire fully supplied yet unable to land on enjoyment** — the worst poverty in the book is not lack but *the inability to enjoy what one has* *(inferred: this completes the never-satisfied thread's logic — 2:1–11 had everything and found it empty by *experiment*; here the man cannot even reach the enjoyment, the capacity itself withheld; the "power to enjoy" of 5:19 is revealed as the true wealth, and its absence makes abundance worse than never having lived)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *wealth* (H6239), *honor* (H3519), *desires* (H0183, *avah* — correctly `characteristic`, the desiring operation), *soul* (H5315, *nephesh* — "his soul is not satisfied," correctly seated), *finds/rest* (H5183), *good things/better off* (H2896) carried. Crucial: *he* (H5315, v.2) is *nephesh* — the soul is the subject that "lacks nothing it desires," and the same soul (v.3) "is not satisfied"; the reading rests on this soul-seating, which the lexical carries correctly. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The capacity-to-enjoy as the true wealth** — the whole unit turns on a faculty, not a possession; the inner-being finding is that *enjoyment is an ability that can be granted or withheld independent of what one owns* (5:19 gives it, 6:2 withholds it). (2) **Rest attributed to the never-born** — "finds rest" (v.5): the stillborn's *rest* is envied; the un-enjoying soul lacks not goods but *rest*, so that non-existence, which at least rests, is preferred (kin to 4:2–3, but here the cause is the withheld inner capacity, not external evil).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the un-enjoying soul (desire supplied, enjoyment withheld)* (new — the book's darkest form of dissatisfaction); *the capacity-to-enjoy as the true wealth* (new — completes 5:19); *desire (avah)* (shared w/ Ps desire cluster); *the un-satisfied nephesh* (shared vocab w/ Ps 42, 63, reframed).

## ECC-06-B · Ecc 6:7–9 · [T, multi, thread] — the never-satisfied appetite vs the sight of the eyes

**The reading.** The thread reaches its clearest statement: "all the **toil of man is for his mouth**, yet his **appetite is not satisfied**" (v.7). The finding is **labour endlessly feeding an appetite that never fills** — everything is "for the mouth," yet the *nephesh* (appetite/soul) stays empty *(stated)*. Against the roaming appetite the unit sets a small wisdom: "**better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the appetite**" (v.9). The operation is **contentment with the present over the roaming of desire** — "the sight of the eyes" (what is actually here, before you) is better than "the wandering of the appetite" (the soul's restless reaching for what is not) *(inferred: this is the thread's practical resolution — not the abolition of appetite but its being met by *what is present*; the wandering nephesh (v.9) is the same un-satisfiable soul of 6:3, and the counsel is to rest the eyes on the given rather than chase the absent)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *toil* (H5999), *appetite* (H5315, *nephesh* — twice, correctly `characteristic`/seated), *wise* (H2450), *knows* (H3045), *living* (H2416), *Better* (H2896) carried. The doubled *nephesh* (appetite v.7, wandering appetite v.9) is exactly the reading's spine — the soul as the un-filled and the wandering thing; lexical carries it correctly. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **"The sight of the eyes" as contentment's mechanism** — not a doctrine but a practice: resting attention on the present visible good rather than the absent craved one. This is the positive counterpart to the "eyes never satisfied" of 1:8 and 4:8 — the *same eyes*, differently directed; a hidden characteristic (attention as the discipline that converts the insatiable eye into a content one).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the never-satisfied appetite (nephesh feeding the mouth, never filled)* (thread climax — 1:8, 4:8, 5:10 → here); *contentment as the sight of the eyes vs the wandering soul* (new — the thread's practical resolution); *the wandering nephesh* (shared vocab, reframed).

## ECC-06-C · Ecc 6:10–12 · [D, multi] — the smallness and ignorance of man; life as a passing shadow

**The reading.** The chapter closes by sizing the self against what it cannot know or overcome: man "is **not able to dispute with one stronger** than he" (v.10); "**who knows what is good** for man while he lives the few days of his vain life, which he **passes like a shadow**? For **who can tell** man what will be after him?" (v.12). The finding is **the self dwarfed and un-knowing** — unable to argue with the stronger power, unable even to know its own good, unable to see beyond its own brief passing *(stated)*. The operation is **the humbling of the self to its limits** — three ignorances stacked: cannot dispute, cannot know its good, cannot see the future *(inferred: this is the mirror of 3:11 (eternity in the heart yet cannot find out) — the reach is real, the sight is fenced; here the fencing is felt as the self's *smallness*, "a shadow," a life too short and too blind to master its own meaning; the honest register of a creature that knows it does not know)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *dispute* (H1777), *one stronger* (H8623), *knows* (H3045, repeated), *good* (H2896), *lives/life* (H2416, H2421) carried. The knowing-verbs (and their negation — "who knows," "who can tell") carry the ignorance-finding; the reading rests on them. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Ignorance of one's own good** — "who knows what is *good for man*?" (v.12): not ignorance of facts but of the self's own true good — the deepest form of the not-knowing, since it undercuts the very project of the searching heart (1:13). The self cannot even name what would satisfy it — which is precisely why the never-satisfied thread runs: the appetite reaches, but the mind cannot say for what.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the self dwarfed and un-knowing (cannot dispute, cannot know its good, cannot see ahead)* (new); *ignorance of one's own good* (new — undercuts the searching project); *life as a passing shadow* (new — the felt brevity of the self).
