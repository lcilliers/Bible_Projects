## ECC-10-A · Ecc 10:1–3 · [D, multi] — the heart's inclination; a little folly outweighing wisdom

**The reading.** "Dead flies make the perfumer's ointment give off a **stench**; so a **little folly outweighs wisdom and honor**" (v.1) — a small inner corruption spoils a whole reputation. Then the governing image: "a **wise man's heart inclines him to the right, but a fool's heart to the left**" (v.2). The finding is **the heart as the inner lean that governs the walk** — not a decision at each step but a *standing inclination* that tilts the whole person one way or the other *(stated)*. And the fool cannot hide it: "even when the fool walks on the road, he **lacks sense**, and he **says to everyone that he is a fool**" (v.3). The operation is **the heart's inclination determining the direction of the life, and folly self-advertising** — the interior lean (right/left) is prior to conduct and shows itself involuntarily *(inferred: "inclines him" makes the heart a *tilt*, a bias the self carries, so that character is less a series of choices than a direction the heart already leans; and folly is not hidden — the fool "says to everyone" what he is simply by how he walks; the interior is legible again (cf. 8:1 the shining face), here the fool's lack broadcasting itself)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *stench* (H0887), *folly* (H5531), *honor* (H3519), *heart* (H3820, seated — the inclining heart, load-bearing), *fool* (H5530), *sense* (H3820 — "lacks sense," lit. "his heart is lacking," correctly leb) carried. Note *sense* (v.3) is *leb* — Hebrew "lacks heart" = lacks sense; the lexical correctly maps it to H3820, and the reading rests on the heart-as-inclination throughout. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The heart as *inclination*, not decision** — "inclines him to the right/left" (v.2): character modelled as a standing *lean* the self carries, prior to particular acts; a hidden characteristic (the biased heart that tilts the whole walk). (2) **The disproportion of a little folly** — "a *little* folly outweighs wisdom and honor" (v.1): the finding that the interior is *asymmetrically* vulnerable — a small corruption spoils much good, as one dead fly ruins the ointment; the self is not a ledger where good outweighs bad by volume.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the heart's inclination (the inner lean that governs the walk)* (new — character as tilt, not tally); *folly self-advertising (the interior legible)* (new — cf. 8:1); *the disproportionate cost of a little folly* (new).

## ECC-10-B · Ecc 10:4–7 · [D, multi] — calmness laying anger to rest; the disordered order observed

**The reading.** "If the **anger of the ruler rises** against you, do **not leave your place**, for **calmness will lay great offenses to rest**" (v.4). The finding is **composure that defuses wrath** — not fleeing and not matching the anger, but a steady *calmness* that "lays to rest" even great offenses *(stated)*. Then the observer notes an upside-down world: folly set in high places, the rich in low, slaves on horses and princes on foot (vv.5–7) — without the self being unsettled by it. The operation is **calmness as an active pacifying force, and composure before a disordered order** — the interior stays in its place (literally and figuratively) and thereby *quiets* the wrath that would escalate *(inferred: "calmness will lay great offenses to rest" (marpe — healing/composure) makes calm not mere passivity but a *therapeutic agent* that heals conflict; the self that does not "leave its place" under a ruler's rising anger both protects itself and de-escalates — the same un-amazed composure of 5:8, here as a response to personal wrath rather than systemic injustice)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *ruler* (H4910), *leave/rest* (H5117, *nuach* — the same "rest/settle" verb as anger "lodges" in 7:9, here "lay to rest"; note the mirror), *offenses* (H2399), *evil* (H7451), *folly* (H5529), *rich* (H6223) carried. Interesting cross-link: *nuach* (H5117) appears here as *calmness laying offenses to rest* — the same root that in 7:9 had anger *lodging* in the fool's heart; the reading notes the mirror (what settles in the fool's heart is anger; what the wise deploy is a settling calm). Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Calmness as a healing agent (marpe), not passivity** — "calmness will *lay to rest* great offenses" (v.4): the finding that composure *does work* — it heals and dissolves conflict; a hidden characteristic (the interior's active pacifying power). The *nuach* mirror is a genuine gem: anger *rests/lodges* in the fool (7:9), while the wise *rest/settle* offenses by calm (10:4) — the same verb of "settling," opposite deployments.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *calmness as an active pacifying/healing force* (new — composure that defuses wrath); *composure before a disordered order* (shared w/ 5:8); *the nuach mirror (anger lodging vs offenses laid to rest)* (new — cross-unit lexical gem).

## ECC-10-C · Ecc 10:8–11 · [C] — the self endangered by its own labour; wisdom as the edge

**The reading.** A small chain of work-hazards: "he who **digs a pit** will fall into it… he who **quarries stones is hurt** by them, and he who splits logs is endangered" (vv.8–9); "if the iron is blunt… he must use **more strength**, but **wisdom helps one to succeed**" (v.10). The finding is thin but present: **wisdom as the forethought that spares wasted strength** — the labourer without wisdom is endangered by his own work and must spend more force; wisdom is the edge that makes effort effective *(stated)*. The operation is **wisdom as applied skill/forethought conserving the self's strength** *(inferred: "wisdom helps one to succeed" (lit. "wisdom is profitable to make right/succeed") locates wisdom not in abstract knowledge but in the *practical sharpening of effort* — the dull axe forces more strength; the wise self works with a sharpened edge; a modest but real inner-being note — prudence as energy-economy)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *hurt* (H6087), *must use* (H1396), *strength* (H2428) carried; the pit/quarry hazards sit `standalone`. Modest substrate; the finding is structural (the blunt-iron/more-strength/wisdom sequence). Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Prudence as energy-economy** — "the blunt iron… he must use *more strength*, but wisdom helps one to succeed" (v.10): the inner-being finding is that wisdom *conserves* the self — it prevents the wasted force and self-inflicted hurt of unskilled labour; a quiet, practical dimension of the interior (foresight sparing effort).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *wisdom as forethought that spares wasted strength (prudence as energy-economy)* (new — modest but real); *the self endangered by its own unskilled labour* (new — cf. Pro's pit-digger).

## ECC-10-D · Ecc 10:12–15 · [D, multi, thread] — speech that reveals and consumes the fool

**The reading.** The speech-legibility thread: "the words of a wise man's mouth **win him favor**, but the **lips of a fool consume him**" (v.12); "the beginning of the words of his mouth is **foolishness**, and the end of his talk is **evil madness**" (v.13); "a fool **multiplies words**, though no man knows what is to be" (v.14). The finding is **the mouth as the fool's self-destruction and the wise man's grace** — speech *reveals* the interior and *acts back on* the speaker: it wins favour for the wise, it *consumes* the fool *(stated)*. The operation is **speech as the interior's self-disclosure that recoils on the speaker** — the fool's words do not merely reveal his folly; they *eat him* ("consume him"), escalating from foolishness to "evil madness" *(inferred: the finding is not that talk is bad but that talk is *self-implicating* — it externalises the heart's inclination (10:2) and then rebounds; the fool's multiplied words (v.14) expose his ignorance ("no man knows what is to be," yet he talks on); speech is the interior made public and made consequential)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *favor* (H2580, *chen* — the wise mouth wins grace), *foolishness* (H5531), *madness* (H1948), *fool* (H5530), *multiplies* (H7235), *knows/know* (H3045) carried. The favour/consume contrast (v.12) is the reading's spine; correctly tagged. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Speech that *consumes* its speaker** — "the lips of a fool *consume him*" (v.12): not just that the fool is judged for his words but that his words *devour* him; the finding that the mouth is not a neutral report of the interior but a *force that acts back* on the self (escalating "foolishness → evil madness"). Speech is the heart's inclination (10:2) made audible and made self-consequential.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *speech as self-disclosure that recoils on the speaker* (thread — speech-legibility, w/ Pro 10; and w/ Ecc 5:2–6); *the fool's words consuming him* (new); *the wise mouth winning grace (chen)* (new).

## ECC-10-E · Ecc 10:16–20 · [D, multi] — the inner thought that leaks; even in thought do not curse

**The reading.** After notes on a land's leadership and on sloth ("through **sloth** the roof sinks in," v.18) and pleasure ("wine **gladdens life**," v.19), the chapter ends on a gem about the *hiddenness of the interior*: "**even in your thoughts, do not curse the king**, nor in your bedroom curse the rich, for **a bird of the air will carry your voice**, or some winged creature tell the matter" (v.20). The finding is **the inner word treated as if it escapes** — the thought, the whispered curse in the private bedroom, is not truly sealed; it "will be carried" *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior as not finally private** — even unspoken malice is to be governed, because the boundary between inner and outer is porous *(inferred: this is a striking inner-being claim — the self cannot rely on the privacy of its own thoughts; "even in your *thoughts*" (madda — knowledge/mind) the curse is not safe, because it leaks; the finding governs the interior at its most hidden layer, implying that the inner life is answerable and exposed, not a sealed chamber; a sober counterpoint to the book's frequent "who knows the heart" — here the heart's secrets do not stay secret)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *strength* (H1369), *sloth* (H6103), *gladdens* (H8055 — wine gladdens life), *life* (H2416), *rich* (H6223) carried. The v.20 gem sits largely in gate-2 content ("thoughts," "curse," "bird carries voice"); the reading is taken from the verse structure, which is unambiguous. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **The porousness of the inner life** — "even in your *thoughts*… a bird will carry your voice" (v.20): the finding that the interior is not a sealed vault; the unspoken is treated as *already half-escaped*, so the self must govern even its private mind. This is the mirror of the interior-legibility findings (8:1 shining face, 10:3 fool self-advertising, 10:12 consuming speech): the inside continually becomes outside.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the inner word that leaks (the interior not finally private)* (new — Ecclesiastes gem); *governing the mind at its most hidden layer* (new); *the porousness of inner and outer* (thread w/ 8:1, 10:3, 10:12).
