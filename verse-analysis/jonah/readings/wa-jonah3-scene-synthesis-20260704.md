## JON-nineveh-repents · Jonah 3:1–10 · [D, multi] — the pagan city's repentance; God relenting

**The reading.** At the second call Jonah obeys and preaches doom; then the astonishing response: "the **people of Nineveh believed God**. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, **from the greatest of them to the least**" (v.5); the king "**arose from his throne, removed his robe**… and sat in ashes" (v.6), decreeing that all "**turn from his evil way and from the violence** that is in his hands. **Who knows? God may turn and relent**" (vv.8–9). "When God saw… how they **turned** from their evil way, **God relented** of the disaster" (v.10). The finding is **a whole pagan city's genuine repentance — belief, humbling, turning from evil — answered by God's relenting mercy** *(stated)*. The operation is **the outsider interior that *turns* wholesale, and the God who *relents* when he sees a changed heart** *(inferred: the Ninevites' repentance is complete and immediate — *belief* ("believed God", v.5), *self-humbling* (the king off his throne, into ashes), and the decisive *turning* ("turn from his evil way", shuv, v.8) — the very return Israel would not make (Hos, Zeph); the finding is the book's scandal: the *enemy* city repents at once what the covenant people resisted for centuries; and God's response — "God relented (nacham) when he saw… how they turned" (v.10) — shows a God genuinely *moved by* a changed heart, whose announced judgment is contingent on the interior's turning; "who knows? God may relent" (v.9) is the humble, un-presuming hope of true repentance (the same "perhaps" as Zeph 2:3) — they turn without guarantee, and find mercy)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the outsider's wholesale repentance (belief + humbling + turning)* (thread — the responsive outsider, w/ 1:16; the enemy repents what the covenant people would not); *God relenting when he sees a changed heart* (new — judgment contingent on the interior's turning); *the un-presuming "who knows?" of true repentance* (thread — w/ Zeph 2:3, Joel 2:14).
