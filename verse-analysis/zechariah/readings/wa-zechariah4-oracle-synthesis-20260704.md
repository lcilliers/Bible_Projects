## ZECH-not-by-might · Zechariah 4:1–14 · [D, multi] — not by might but by the Spirit; not despising the day of small things

**The reading.** Zechariah sees a golden lampstand fed continuously by two olive trees, and the interpreting word to Zerubbabel (rebuilding the temple against daunting odds): "**Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD of hosts**" (v.6); "**Who are you, O great mountain? Before Zerubbabel you shall become a plain**" (v.7). And a word for the discouraged: "**Whoever has despised the day of small things shall rejoice, and shall see the plumb line in the hand of Zerubbabel**" (v.10). The finding is **the work accomplished *"not by might… but by my Spirit"* — human strength displaced by God's Spirit — and the warning *not to despise "the day of small things"*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the reorientation of the interior's reliance from *its own force* ("might… power") to *God's Spirit* — so that overwhelming obstacles ("O great mountain") are levelled not by human effort but by the Spirit's enabling — and the correction of the heart that *despises small beginnings*, which cannot see that God works through the modest and unimpressive** *(inferred: v.6 is the book's most quoted inner-being word — "**not by might, nor by power, BUT BY MY SPIRIT**": the finding is that the interior tempted to measure a task by its own strength (and despair) is redirected to *God's Spirit* as the true agent — the work will be done not by mustering human force ("might"/chayil — army, wealth; "power"/koach — strength) but by the Spirit's quiet, continuous supply (imaged as the oil flowing to the lamp without human tending); this frees the discouraged worker from the burden of self-sufficiency — the outcome rests on God's Spirit, not the self's adequacy; and v.10's "**day of small things**" addresses a subtler interior fault — the *contempt for the modest* (the small, unimpressive beginning that seems not worth rejoicing over): the finding is that the heart which "despises the day of small things" misjudges reality, because God delights to work through the small and the weak, and the modest foundation *will* become the finished temple; the interior is taught both to rely on the Spirit rather than its strength, and to *value* the small beginnings God is pleased to use)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *"not by might but by my Spirit" (reliance reoriented from human force to God's Spirit — the true agent of the work)* (thread — a gem: the discouraged freed from self-sufficiency; the outcome resting on the Spirit, not the self's adequacy, w/ 4:6, Ps 20:7, 2 Cor 12:9, John 15:5); *the mountain made a plain (overwhelming obstacles levelled by the Spirit, not effort)* (new — w/ 4:7, Matt 17:20); *not despising the day of small things (the contempt for the modest corrected — God works through the small and weak)* (new — a gem: the small beginning misjudged, valued rightly because God uses it, w/ 4:10, 1 Cor 1:27-28, Matt 13:31-33).
