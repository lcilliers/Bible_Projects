## JOB-40-A · Job 40:1–14 · [D, multi, thread] — the hand on the mouth; first submission

**The reading.** God pauses: "**shall a faultfinder contend with the Almighty?** He who argues with God, let him answer it" (v.2). Job's first response is surrender of speech: "**behold, I am of small account; what shall I answer you? I lay my hand on my mouth**. I have spoken once, and I will not answer" (vv.4–5). But God presses further with a piercing question: "**will you even put me in the wrong? Will you condemn me that you may be in the right?**" (v.8); can Job "**abase** the proud" and "**tread down the wicked**" (vv.11–12)? The finding is **the arguing self falling silent — hand on mouth — yet pressed to see the deeper issue: it had sought its own vindication at God's expense** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self ceasing its case, and being shown the pride hidden in its self-justification** *(inferred: "I lay my hand on my mouth" (v.4) is the first great reversal — the man who would not "restrain my mouth" (7:11) now silences it; but God is not finished, because v.8 exposes the subtle fault — not that Job sinned to deserve his suffering (he did not), but that in defending himself he had *put God in the wrong* to be *in the right himself* ("will you condemn me that you may be in the right?"); the finding is precise and gentle: Job's error was never the friends' (secret gross sin) but a finer one — insisting on his own vindication at the cost of God's justice; the self-justifying impulse, even of a righteous man, can become a subtle arraignment of God; and God's challenge (can you govern the proud? v.11) shows Job he lacks the standing to run the moral universe he was critiquing)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *contend* (H7378, rib — "shall a faultfinder contend"), *question* (H7592), *condemn* (H7561, rasha — "condemn me that you may be in the right"), *right* (H6663, tsadaq), *majesty/dignity/glory/splendor* (H1347/H1363/H1935/H1926 — the divine attributes Job cannot wield), *proud* (H1343, ge'eh — twice), *abase* (H8213), *low* (H3665), *wicked* (H7563), *acknowledge* (H3034) carried. *rib* (contend) recurs (Job's lawsuit, now questioned); *rasha/tsadaq* (condemn/be-right) carries the exposed self-justification; *ge'eh* (proud, whom only God can abase) carries the challenge; correctly tagged. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** (1) **The hand on the mouth (the arguing self silenced)** — "I lay my hand on my mouth" (v.4): the finding of the great reversal — the man who refused to restrain his mouth (7:11) now stops it; the first movement of submission. (2) **The subtle pride of self-justification exposed** — "will you condemn me that you may be in the right?" (v.8): the finding that Job's real fault was never the friends' charge (gross secret sin) but a *finer* one — insisting on his own vindication at the cost of God's justice; even a righteous self's self-defence can become a subtle arraignment of God. (3) **Lacking the standing to run the universe** — "can you abase the proud?" (v.11): Job is shown he lacks the power (and thus the standing) to administer the justice he was demanding.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the hand on the mouth (the arguing self silenced)* (thread — the reversal of 7:11); *the subtle pride of self-justification (putting God in the wrong to be right)* (new — Job's finer, real fault); *lacking the standing to run the moral universe* (new).

## JOB-40-B · Job 40:15–24 · [D] — Behemoth; the creature man cannot master

**The reading.** God turns to Behemoth: "behold, Behemoth, **which I made as I made you**; he eats grass like an ox" (v.15); his strength is in his loins, and "**if the river is turbulent he is not frightened**; he is **confident** though Jordan rushes against his mouth" (v.23). The finding is **a creature of such power that man cannot subdue it — yet God made it "as I made you"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self set beside a made-thing it cannot master, both the work of one Maker** *(inferred: "which I made as I made you" (v.15) is the pointed phrase — Behemoth and Job are *fellow-creatures*, both God's handiwork; the finding is a double humbling and leveling: Job cannot master even this beast (let alone the moral order), and yet he shares createdness with it; the beast's *confidence* in the face of the turbulent river (v.23) is quietly instructive — a serene un-frightened strength that simply *rests* in what it is, which Job, thrashing in his crisis, lacks; the inner-being note is that the creature Job cannot control is nonetheless perfectly at peace, held by its Maker — a picture of the rest Job has lost)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *strength* (H3581), *power* (H0202, on), *beasts* (H2416), *confident* (H0982, batach — "he is confident though Jordan rushes") carried. *batach* (confident/trusting — the beast's serene security) is the reading's telling term; correctly tagged (the same trust-word Job's security once had, 29). Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **The creature's serene confidence as a picture of lost rest** — "if the river is turbulent he is not frightened; he is confident (batach) though Jordan rushes against his mouth" (v.23): the finding that Behemoth *rests* in what it is, un-frightened by turbulence — a serene creaturely security (batach) that Job, thrashing in his crisis, has lost. The inner-being datum: the beast Job cannot master is nonetheless perfectly at peace, held by its Maker; a quiet image of the trust-rest Job is being drawn back toward (he and the beast share one Maker, v.15).

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the fellow-creature Job cannot master ("made as I made you")* (new — the leveling of createdness); *the creature's serene confidence (batach) as a picture of lost rest* (new — the trust Job is being drawn toward).
