## JOB-20-A · Job 20:1–11 · [D, multi] — the brevity of the wicked's joy

**The reading.** Zophar, stung, answers "because of my **haste within me**" (v.2) — even he admits an inner agitation driving his speech. His theme: "the **exulting of the wicked is short**, and the **joy of the godless but for a moment**" (v.5); he "will **fly away like a dream**" (v.8). The finding is **the wicked's gladness as fleeting and unreal — a dream that vanishes** *(stated)*. The operation is **the transience of ill-founded joy** — the wicked's exultation has no root, so it evaporates *(inferred: the observation is true in itself (joy built on wickedness is unstable, cf. Ecc 7:6 the crackling thorns), but Zophar deploys it against Job with no evidence Job is wicked; the telling self-disclosure is v.2 — Zophar speaks from his own "haste/agitation within," admitting that his theology is partly a *reaction* to being provoked; the finding is that even the accuser's argument is emotionally driven — his tidy doctrine of the wicked's brief joy is fuelled by his own inner disturbance at Job's challenge)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *hear* (H8085), *insults* (H3639, kelimmah), *understanding* (H0998, binah), *exulting* (H7445, rinnah), *wicked* (H7563), *joy* (H8057, simchah — "the joy of the godless"), *favor* (H7521, ratsah), *wealth* (H0202) carried. *simchah* (joy) + *rinnah* (exulting) for the wicked's fleeting gladness; correctly tagged. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **The accuser's own agitation driving the doctrine** — "my thoughts answer me, because of my haste within me" (v.2): the finding that Zophar *admits* his speech springs from an inner disturbance — his theology of the wicked's brief joy is a *reaction*, not a calm observation. The inner-being datum: even confident moral pronouncement can be emotionally fuelled; the accuser is not the dispassionate judge he poses as, but a provoked man whose "haste within" shapes his verdict.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the transience of ill-founded joy* (thread w/ Ecc 7:6; the wicked's fleeting gladness); *the accuser's own agitation driving the doctrine (haste within)* (new — moral pronouncement as emotional reaction).

## JOB-20-B · Job 20:12–29 · [D, multi, thread] — sin sweet in the mouth turning to poison; the insatiable wicked

**The reading.** Zophar's centrepiece is a vivid image of sin's recoil: "though **evil is sweet in his mouth**, though he **hides it under his tongue**… yet his food is **turned in his stomach**; it is the **venom of cobras** within him" (vv.12–14); "he **swallows down riches** and **vomits them up** again" (v.15). The root diagnosis: "**because he knew no contentment in his belly**, he will not let anything in which he **delights** escape him" (v.20). The finding is **sin as a sweetness that turns to poison inside, driven by a belly that knows no contentment** *(stated)*. The operation is **the insatiable interior that consumes sin as sweetness and is poisoned by it** *(inferred: the psychology is acute — sin is "sweet in the mouth" (real pleasure), *hidden under the tongue* (savoured, prolonged), then turns to *venom within* (the recoil); and the root (v.20) is *insatiability* — "he knew no contentment (shalev) in his belly" — the never-satisfied appetite (the same thread as Ecc 6:7) here in its wicked form, greed that cannot stop and so ingests its own poison; the finding is true and sharp (unrestrained appetite self-poisons), though aimed unfairly at Job; it links the never-satisfied thread across the wisdom books — the belly that cannot be content ingests its own ruin)*.

**Lexical validation (read-back).** *evil* (H7451), *loath* (H2550), *riches* (H2428), *poison* (H7219, rosh — venom), *enjoyment* (H5965), *knew* (H3045), *delights* (H2530, chamad — coveted delight), *misery* (H6001), *terrors* (H0367, emah), *iniquity* (H5771), *wicked* (H7563) carried. *chamad* (covet/delight) and the venom-imagery carry the sin-recoil finding; "no contentment in his belly" sits in content. Sound.

**Hidden / less-obvious characteristics isolated.** **Insatiability as the root of sin's self-poisoning** — "because he knew no contentment in his belly… he swallows down riches and vomits them up" (vv.15,20): the finding that the *never-satisfied appetite* (the belly with no contentment) is what drives the wicked to ingest what poisons him; this ties Job's dialogue to the wisdom-books' never-satisfied thread (Ecc 5:10, 6:7) — greed that cannot be content consumes its own ruin. And the "sweet in the mouth, hidden under the tongue, venom within" (vv.12–14) is a precise anatomy of how a savoured sin recoils.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *sin savoured then turning to poison within (the sweet-then-venom recoil)* (new — a sharp anatomy); *insatiability as the root of self-poisoning (no contentment in the belly)* (thread — never-satisfied, w/ Ecc 5:10, 6:7); *coveted delight (chamad) that cannot be released* (new).
