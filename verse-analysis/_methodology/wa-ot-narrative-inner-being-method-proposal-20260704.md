## 0. Researcher corrections (2026-07-04) — these govern everything below

1. **The unit that surfaces is the span's lexical, not the scene's plot.** Each evidence verse sits in its passage; within that passage **every span (term-in-verse) gets its verse-bounded lexical**, and the **operation is read off that lexical**. Same verse-first lexical process as the poetic/prophetic corpus, now inside narrative.
2. **A passage carries *multiple* operations — individual *and* interactive.** Each span has its own operation; and spans **operate on each other** (drive / restrain / weld / reverse / transmit). The object is that **web of operations within and between spans**, not "what the scene is about." (Gen 3: ~11 individual + ~5 interactive; the interactions are the richest yield.)
3. **The story is instrumental, not the object.** The study is about the **operations**; the narrative is only the arena that lets an operation show itself more sharply. Do **not** drift into character-arc / plot as the deliverable — that was a drift in the first draft of §1–§4 below and is corrected here.
4. **Never gloss over detail; never lump.** Repeated glosses (Genesis: "to give" ×89, "to know" ×52…) are **not** one operation. For each occurrence ask *why is this different from the others* — the **difference between same-gloss spans is itself a finding** (Gen 3: promised-knowing vs shame-knowing; gift vs alibi). Premature grouping is the failure mode; distinction-preservation is the discipline (`feedback_each_chapter_first_principles_find_the_gems`).

## 1. Why narrative is genuinely different

In the poetic/prophetic corpus the inner being is **described or expressed** — the text reflects *on* the interior, so a verse or passage can be read largely on its own. In narrative the inner being is **enacted** — shown through what a person *does, decides, feels, and says inside a scene as the story is told*. Three consequences:

| | Prophets/Poetry (done) | OT Narrative (new) |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit of reading** | verse → passage / whole chapter | the **narrative scene / episode** (a coherent action-unit) |
| **Where the IB lives** | in the statement (stated/inferred off the words) | in the **action, decision, dialogue, and narrator's inside-view** |
| **New axis** | the "self" is mostly generic/corporate Israel or the prophet | the **character/agent** — the same operation recurs across *persons* |
| **Context need** | a verse can stand alone | a verse **cannot** — meaning depends on its place in the scene and the character's arc |

So the reading looks for the interior **in motion**: decisions at forks (Abraham on Moriah; Joseph and Potiphar's wife), speech that reveals the heart, the narrator's inside-view ("Cain was very angry"; "Joseph could not control himself"; "David's heart struck him"), the contrast between characters, and the consequences that expose an interior.

## 2. By book or by topic? — **By book (scene by scene). Synthesise by topic afterward.**

**Recommendation: read sequentially, book by book, scene by scene — Genesis first.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. **Narrative meaning is arc-dependent.** David's sin-and-repentance scene is unintelligible torn from the Bathsheba–Nathan–Absalom arc; Jacob's interior only makes sense across Bethel → Peniel. Reading by topic would **fragment the story and smuggle in the very imported meaning the study forbids** (`feedback_lexical_strictly_verse_bounded_no_implied_evidence`, `project_focus_points_scripture_as_data_source`).
2. **The study's core commitment is emergence, not a grid.** Since the RESET, patterns must *emerge* from reading the text as it is, never be sorted into pre-named topic bins (`project_RESET_characteristics_to_movements_changeover`, `feedback_no_forced_structure_...`). Topic-first inverts this — it presupposes the very operations we are meant to discover, *and* it presupposes we have already read everything (you cannot gather "all envy scenes" before reading the books).
3. **Character arcs are themselves inner-being data.** Reading a person's scenes in order lets the *movement* of an interior show (Joseph from pit to forgiveness; Saul's decline; Jonah's resistance). That arc is lost if scenes are shuffled by theme.
4. **Topic comparison is the synthesis layer, and it works better after.** Exactly as with the prophets: per-unit reading → then a cross-book synthesis *per operation* (Cain / Saul / Joseph's-brothers envy read together; fear-and-courage across the Judges; repentance across David, Manasseh, Nineveh-in-Jonah). We already have this two-phase pattern working (`project_cross_chapter_synthesis_per_characteristic`).

**So:** *by book* is the reading axis; *by topic/operation* is the synthesis axis, run after each book or narrative-block.

## 3. Coverage — you are right: evidence-verse-anchored, scene-scoped (not every verse)

We do **not** read every verse. Narrative is full of material with no inner-being content — genealogies, census lists, itineraries, construction specs, ritual detail, regnal formulae. The **`wa_verse_records` evidence verses are the anchors**: a verse carrying a study term (non-T2) flags where an interior is in play. But because a narrative verse cannot be read alone, we take the **whole scene the anchor sits in** as the unit — the natural pericope around the evidence verse — and read the interior *in that scene*.

- **Anchor:** each non-T2 evidence verse in `wa_verse_records`.
- **Scope:** the surrounding **scene** (its natural start/end), so the action is intelligible.
- **Skip:** scenes/blocks with **no** evidence anchor (pure genealogy/list/itinerary/ritual) — not read, not filed. This is what keeps the corpus tractable (Genesis ≈ 1,250 evidence verses, not 1,533).
- **Coverage test** (as before): every **non-T2 evidence verse** must fall inside a filed scene-unit. Genealogy-only verses legitimately fall outside — that is correct, not a gap.

This is the same `segment_unit` machinery already used for the **narrative scenes** in Jonah, Daniel 1–6, Jeremiah's Baruch chapters, and Ezekiel's sign-acts — so no new tables are needed, only a narrative-tuned reading.

## 4. The scene reading — proposed shape

Per scene-unit (adapting the prophet reading structure to enacted interior):

```
## CODE · Ref-range · [character(s)] — gist of the scene
**The scene.** what happens (brief, so the action is clear).
The interior in action is **...** *(the operation, read off the enactment)*
- shown by: **decision / speech / narrator-inside-view / consequence** (cite the verse)
The finding is **...** *(stated — where the narrator gives us the inside; or inferred — where we read it off the act)*
**Characteristics surfaced:** *phrase* (thread/new — a gem: ...)
```

New emphases vs the prophets:
- **Name the agent** whose interior it is (Cain, Abraham, Hagar, Joseph…).
- Distinguish **stated interior** (narrator's inside-view — "he was afraid", "she despised him in her heart") from **inferred interior** (read off the deed — Abraham raising the knife = a trust/obedience operation the text does not label).
- Watch **character-to-character contrast** (Cain vs Abel's acceptance; Joseph vs his brothers; Saul vs David) — narrative often teaches the interior by juxtaposition.
- Track the **arc** where a scene advances one (a chapter-level / book-level note carries the movement, as we did for the prophets).
