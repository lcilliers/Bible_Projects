## HOS-fathers-heart · Hos 11:1–12 · [D, multi] — the divine parental interior; the recoiling heart of compassion

**The reading.** The oracle opens the *heart of God* as tenderly as anywhere in Scripture. The memory: "**When Israel was a child, I loved him**, and out of Egypt I called my son" (v.1); "it was I who **taught Ephraim to walk**; I took them up by their arms… I led them with **cords of kindness, with the bands of love**… I **bent down to them and fed them**" (vv.3–4). Then the astonishing inner conflict: "**How can I give you up, O Ephraim?**… **My heart recoils within me; my compassion grows warm and tender**. I will not execute my burning anger… **for I am God and not a man**, the Holy One in your midst" (vv.8–9). The finding is **God's own interior — a father's love that taught the child to walk, and a heart that *recoils* from destroying, compassion overruling wrath** *(stated)*. The operation is **the divine heart torn between deserved judgment and unquenchable love, and love prevailing** *(inferred: this is the summit of the book's inner-being disclosure — not the human heart but *God's*; the parental images (teaching to walk, cords of kindness, bending to feed) portray a tenderness the rebellion has not extinguished; and v.8 stages an actual *inner conflict* in God — "my heart recoils (nehpak libbi, is overturned) within me; my compassion (nichumim) grows warm" — the finding is that God's love is not serene detachment but a *felt turmoil* in which compassion wins; and the ground is startling: "for I am God and not a man" (v.9) — God's *refusal* to destroy is precisely what makes him *God* rather than man, inverting the human assumption that mercy is weakness; the finding is that the deepest thing in Hosea's God is a compassion that overrules his own just wrath)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the divine parental heart (taught to walk, cords of kindness)* (new — God's tenderness toward the rebel); *God's inner conflict, compassion overruling wrath ("my heart recoils")* (new — the book's summit; the felt turmoil of divine love); *mercy as what makes God God, not weakness ("I am God and not a man")* (new — inverts the human view of mercy). **Shared:** grace-preceding-repentance (w/ 1:10, 14:4).
