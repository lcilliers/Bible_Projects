# Psalm 20 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 20:1–9 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter. Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **A royal, corporate psalm** — the people pray for the king before battle. Less introspective than the Davidic laments, but its centre is a sharp inner-being disclosure: **what you trust defines and decides you.**

## The reading
Psalm 20 is spoken *for* another — the congregation interceding — and its inner-being content lies in what it asks and where it locates confidence.

**I. Intercession — the heart's desire brought to God (vv.1–5).**
"May the LORD answer you in the day of **trouble** (H6869, M03)… May he **remember** (H2142, M41) all your offerings… May he **grant** (H5414, M12) you your **heart's desire** (H3824, the seat — M47) and **fulfil** all your **plans** (H6098, M17). May we shout for **joy** (H7442, M42) over your salvation" (vv.1–5). The inner being's deepest wishes — the *heart's desire* and *plans* — are **brought to God to be granted**, not seized by force *(inferred: the whole prayer treats desire as something entrusted for God to fulfil)*.

**II. The confession of confidence (v.6).**
"Now I **know** that the LORD **saves** his anointed; he will **answer** him… with the saving **might** (H1369, M23) of his right hand." Assurance rests on God's proven power, not the army's *(stated)*.

**III. The trust-contrast — name vs chariots (v.7).**
The centre: "Some **trust** in **chariots** and some in **horses**, but we **trust** (H2142, M41) in the **name** of the LORD our God." The Hebrew of "trust" here is *nazkir* — "we **invoke / call to mind**" — so the contrast is exact: others lean on *visible, tangible power* (chariots, horses); we *invoke the name* — God's revealed character and presence *(inferred: trust here is an act of remembering the Name, over against counting one's horses)*. **What the inner being leans on is what defines it.**

**IV. Two trusts, two ends (vv.8–9).**
"They **collapse** and **fall**, but we **rise** and **stand upright** (H5749, M11)" (v.8). The chariot-trusters *fall*; the name-invokers *stand*. The object of trust **decides the outcome** *(stated: they fall, we stand)*. The psalm closes: "O LORD, save the king! May he answer us when we **call** (H7121, M37)" (v.9).

## What Psalm 20 says about the inner being
- **What you trust defines you** (v.7): the inner being is revealed and shaped by *where it leans* — on visible power it can count (chariots, horses), or on the *name* it can only invoke. Trust is an act of *invoking* what one cannot see over against relying on what one can.
- **What you trust decides you** (v.8): the two trusts have two ends — collapse or standing. The ground of confidence is not neutral; leaning on might that fails *makes you fall*, leaning on the Name *makes you stand* (with the "never moved" of Ps 15–16).
- **The heart's desire is brought to God, not grasped** (v.4): even one's deepest wishes and plans are *entrusted* for God to grant — the inner being's proper posture toward its own longings is to lay them before God (setting up Ps 21's answer: "you have given him his heart's desire").
