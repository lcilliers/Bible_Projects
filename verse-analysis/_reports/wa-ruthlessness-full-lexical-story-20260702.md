## The story (three movements — ruthlessness is the thread)

**I. Egypt — dread hardens into ruthless enslavement (Exo 1:7-14).**
A king who *did not know* Joseph (Exo 1:8) looks on a multiplying people as **enemies** (Exo 1:10) and is seized with **dread** (Exo 1:12). Out of that dread and enmity the people are **afflicted** and **oppressed** with heavy burdens (Exo 1:11-12); they are made to **serve — ruthlessly** (Exo 1:13), and their lives are **embittered** in *all* their service, again **ruthlessly** (Exo 1:14). The inner movement runs: *dread / enmity → affliction → oppression → ruthless enslavement → the embittering of life.* Ruthlessness here is the **manner** that welds itself to the enslaving (Exo 1:13-14).

**II. The servant-law — ruthlessness forbidden, the fear of God its restraint (Lev 25:34-53).**
Against that stands the law. A brother fallen into poverty is to be **supported** (Lev 25:35), **lent to** without gain (25:37), and **not made to serve as a slave** (25:39-40). Over him you shall **not rule ruthlessly, but shall fear your God** (Lev 25:43) — and again over the redeemed brother (25:53) and even in ruling a bought servant (25:46). The one **restraint** the lexical names is exactly this: *the fear of God* (Lev 25:43, discovery: restrained-by fear-of-God). The whole unit is under prohibition — ruthlessness is condemned wherever dominion is exercised.

**III. The shepherds — ruthlessness as the failure of care (Eze 34:4-6).**
The shepherds *did not* **strengthen**, **heal**, or **seek** the flock (Eze 34:4,6); instead they **ruled** them with **force** and **harshness** (Eze 34:4) — and the flock was **scattered** (Eze 34:5). Here ruthlessness ("harshness") is the manner of a rule that withholds care; its impact is a people scattered and unsought.

**Across the three:** ruthlessness is never a standalone act — it is the **harsh manner of dominion** (welded to *enslave* and to *rule*), **driven** by dread and enmity (Exo 1), **restrained** only by the fear of God (Lev 25), and **condemned** wherever it appears — its impact the embittering of life and the scattering of the weak.

## How it came out — an honest read (for the researcher)
- **The inner-being thread is coherent and strong** where the terms are affect/vice/dominion: dread → oppression → ruthless enslavement → embitter (Exo); the fear-of-God restraint (Lev); force/harshness vs care → scattering (Eze). This reads as a real story **from the lexicals alone**.
- **"All terms" also drags in incidental machinery** that is not an inner-being operation: *know* (Joseph), *support/lend/give*, *calculate/redemption/money*, *high/search*. These surfaced because every term is written; they thin the story rather than build it. → a layer-1 story will need a **light filter** (affect/vice/dominion/faculty terms carry the story; economic/legal/narrative machinery is background) — or the reader simply skims them.
- **Three contexts, not one narrative.** The five passages are three separate settings (oppression / law / shepherds). The story is naturally **three movements** with ruthlessness as the thread — not a single continuous tale. (A single-term story that *rolls up* these into "what ruthlessness is" is the cleaner §-8.4 synthesis; this full-lexical version is the richer, noisier raw layer.)
- **The known rough edges show** (bearer picks a nearby proper noun — "Raamses"/"Egypt" as bearer of oppress; target grabs a place — "Pithom") — visible here, flagged, not yet load-bearing for the story.

**Verdict:** the underlying lexicals **can** tell the story — the inner-being spine comes through clearly across all three settings — but "all terms" makes it a **rich, noisy raw layer**; the clean per-operation story wants a light affect/vice/dominion filter over this substrate.
