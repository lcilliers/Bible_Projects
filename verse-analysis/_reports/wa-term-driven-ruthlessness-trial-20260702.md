## 2. ★ The focus point emerges (this is the study's goal working)
Reading perek across every occurrence, a consistent inner-being picture surfaces **on its own**:
> **Ruthlessness is not a standalone act — it is the harsh MANNER of exercising dominion.** In all 6 verses it is a manner-noun (`be-perek`, "with ruthlessness") welded to a verb of **ruling** (`radah`) or **enslaving** (`abad`). It is **condemned** (prohibited in Lev 25:43/53, Eze 34:4). Its **driver** can be dread (Exo 1); its **restraint** is the fear of God (Lev 25:43).

That convergence — *ruthlessness = the manner of dominion, condemned* — is exactly the kind of emergent focus point the study is after, and it came from the term-driven read, not from imposing a definition.

## 3. ★ The method flaw this exposed (self-learning in action)
**The `source` rule cannot tell a DRIVER from a RESTRAINT.** It reported `source=fear@Lev 25:36` for the ruthlessness in Lev 25:43 — but that "fear" is **fear-of-God, which RESTRAINS ruthlessness** ("you shall not rule ruthlessly **but shall fear your God**"), the opposite of the **dread that DRIVES** it in Exo 1. The rule grabbed the nearest affect regardless of role.
- **Learning:** an affect near a vice can be its **cause** *or* its **check** — the derivation must distinguish them (by polarity / by the prohibition frame / by "but"-contrast syntax), or mark it **D11: role uncertain** rather than assert `source`.
- This is precisely the "read back, evaluate for sensibility, adjust the method" loop — a real refinement discovered by working the term, not guessable a priori.
