## The reading

**I. The overwhelming, and the impulse to flee (vv.4–8).**
"My **heart** (H3820, the seat — M47) is in **anguish** (H2342, *chul*) within me; the **terrors** (H0367, M01, *emah*) of death have fallen upon me. **Fear** (H3374, M01) and **trembling** (H7461, M01, *raad*) come upon me, and **horror** (H6427, M01, *pallatsuth*) overwhelms me. And I say, 'Oh, that I had **wings like a dove**! I would **fly away** (H5774) and be at **rest** (H7931); I would **wander** (H5074, M18) far away… find a **shelter** from the raging wind'" (vv.4–8). The finding: a **fourfold flood of dread** (terrors, fear, trembling, horror — a dense M01 cluster) overwhelms the inner being *(stated)*, and its immediate impulse is **escape** — the wish for wings, to *fly away*, to *be elsewhere and at rest* *(stated)*. The operation: under extreme anguish the inner being **longs to flee its situation entirely** — an honest escape-fantasy, the desire to *not be here* *(inferred: the "oh, that I had wings… I would fly away" is the overwhelmed self's reflex toward flight)*.

**II. The deepest wound — betrayal by the intimate who shared the *sod* (vv.12–14).**
"For it is **not an enemy** who taunts me — then I could bear it… **But it is you**, a man **my equal**, **my companion** (H0441, *alluph*), my familiar friend. We used to take **sweet counsel** (H5475, M17, *sod*) together; within God's house we walked in the throng" (vv.12–14). The finding: the wound's depth is **the intimacy betrayed** — "an enemy I could bear, but *you*" *(stated)*. The gem: what was shared was the **sod** — the *confidential counsel*, the very word Ps 25:14 uses for the **friendship of the LORD** given to those who fear him *(stated)*. So the betrayal violated the **innermost circle of shared confidence** *(inferred: the sod is the intimate-confidence bond, here turned)*. The operation: **the depth of trust shared sets the depth of the wound when it turns** — betrayal by an *equal/companion* who held one's confidence cuts as an enemy's assault cannot (cf. Ps 41:9's bread-sharing friend) *(inferred)*.

**III. The war-heart behind smooth speech (v.21).**
"His speech was **smooth** (H2505, *chalaq* — the flatterer's verb, as in Ps 36:2) as butter, yet **war** (H7128) was in his **heart** (H3820, M47); his words were softer than oil, yet they were drawn swords" (v.21). The finding: the betrayer's **heart is at war while the mouth speaks peace** — smooth words masking the heart's true content *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being can be **divided against its own speech** — war within, butter without; the heart's real state (hostility) concealed by the tongue's oil (the concealing-speech mechanism of Ps 28:3, 50:19) *(inferred: the "smooth speech… yet war in his heart" names the split)*.

**IV. Casting the burden — the alternative to flight (v.22).**
"**Cast your burden** (H3053, *yehav* — your lot, what is given you) on the LORD, and **he will sustain** (H3557, *kul*) you; he will never permit the righteous to be **moved**" (v.22). The gem-resolution: against the earlier wish to **fly away** (vv.6–8), the inner being is told to **cast its burden on God**, who *sustains* *(stated)*. The finding — the psalm's key contrast: **the two responses to being overwhelmed are *escape* (fly away, vv.6–8) and *transfer* (cast the burden, be held, v.22)** — and the psalm moves from the flight-wish to the burden-casting *(inferred: v.22's "cast your burden… he will sustain" answers vv.6–8's "I would fly away")*. What the self cannot carry it **hands over** rather than **runs from** — and is *sustained* and *not moved* *(inferred)*. The close confirms the settled posture: "But I will **trust** in you" (v.23).

## What Psalm 55 says about the inner being
- **Under a flood of dread, the inner being's impulse is *flight* (vv.4–8).** A fourfold overwhelming (terrors, fear, trembling, horror) drives the honest wish to fly away, to be elsewhere and at rest — the reflex to *not be here*.
- **Betrayal by the intimate is the deepest wound (vv.12–14).** The depth of shared confidence — the *sod*, the same word for God's own friendship — sets the depth of the injury when it turns. An enemy could be borne; the equal-companion cannot.
- **The heart can be at war behind smooth speech (v.21).** The inner being divided against its own tongue — war within, butter without; the heart's true hostility concealed by the mouth's oil.
- **Casting the burden is the alternative to flight (v.22).** The two responses to being overwhelmed are *escape* and *transfer*; the inner being moves from the wish to fly away to casting its burden on God — what it cannot carry it hands over rather than runs from, and is sustained and not moved.
