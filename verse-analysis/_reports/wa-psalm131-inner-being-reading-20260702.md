- **The un-lifted heart; the soul *weaned* into stillness. A cornerstone of contentment.** Psalm 131 is three verses of profound inner peace. Its findings: the **heart that refuses to over-reach** — not lifted up, not straining after the too-great (v.1); and the **self actively *calming and quieting* its own soul, until it rests like a *weaned child*** (v.2).

## The reading

**I. The un-lifted heart that refuses to over-reach (v.1).**
"O LORD, my **heart** (H3820, M47) is **not lifted up**; my **eyes are not raised too high**; I **do not occupy myself with things too great and too marvelous** for me" (v.1). The gem-finding: the self declares a **heart *not lifted up*** — no pride of eye, no straining after matters beyond its measure *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **deliberately keeps to its *scale*** — it renounces the over-reaching of pride (the "lifted-up" heart, the "raised" eyes) and the restless ambition to grasp "things too great"; a chosen *smallness*, a refusal to be inflated *(inferred: "my heart is not lifted up… I do not occupy myself with things too great" is an active *self-limiting* — the opposite of the proud heart (Ps 101:5, 18:27) and the over-reaching that troubles the self; the inner being renounces grasping beyond its bound and so finds rest; humility as *chosen proportion*, cf. Rom 12:3 "not to think of himself more highly than he ought")*.

**II. The self *calming and quieting* its own soul — the weaned child (v.2).**
"But I have **calmed and quieted** my **soul** (H5315, M47), **like a weaned child** with its mother; like a weaned child is my soul within me" (v.2). The gem-finding — one of the tenderest inner-being images in Scripture: the self has **actively *stilled* its own soul**, and the result is a soul like a ***weaned* child** at rest with its mother *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **governs itself into *stillness*** — "I have calmed and quieted my soul" is a *deliberate act* of self-composing — and the image tells its quality: not the *nursing* child who cries and frets for the breast, but the ***weaned*** child, past the craving, content simply *to be with* the mother, wanting nothing but her presence *(inferred: "I have calmed and quieted my soul, like a weaned child" describes a self that has *outgrown restless craving* — weaning is the hard passage from demanding-what-I-want to resting-in-who-I'm-with; the soul no longer frets for gratification but is *satisfied by presence alone*; the self actively led its own soul through that weaning into peace, the stilling-register of self-address at its deepest, cf. Ps 62:5 "for God alone, O my soul, wait in silence", 116:7 "return, O my soul, to your rest", 42:5)*.

## What Psalm 131 says about the inner being
- **The heart can *refuse to over-reach* (v.1).** A chosen smallness — not lifted up, not straining after the too-great; the inner being keeps to its scale, renouncing the grasping of pride and restless ambition, and so finds rest. Humility as *chosen proportion*.
- **The self can *quiet its own soul* into contented stillness (v.2).** "I have calmed and quieted my soul, like a weaned child" — a deliberate act of self-composing whose fruit is a soul *past craving*, content simply to be with God, satisfied by presence alone. The self weaned off restless want into peace.

## Honest notes
- **A cornerstone of stillness/contentment:** Psalm 131's weaned-child is *the* image of the settled inner being — a self that has done the hard work of *weaning* itself off the fretful demand for gratification into rest in God's presence. It caps the self-quieting thread (62:5, 42:5, 116:7) with its most tender picture, and pairs the *inner* stillness (v.2) with the *renounced over-reaching* (v.1) that makes it possible. A prime datum on inner peace.
- **The two verses are one movement:** the un-lifted heart (v.1) and the quieted soul (v.2) are cause and fruit — you cannot still a soul that is still grasping "things too great"; humility of aim *enables* rest of soul.
