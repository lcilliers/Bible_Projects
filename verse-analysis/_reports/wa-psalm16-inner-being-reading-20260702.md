# Psalm 16 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 16:1–11 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter. Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **The psalm of God as the soul's supreme good.** Its own key is **portion**: the inner being that has consolidated all its good in God, and finds from that a contentment, stability, and whole-person joy that culminate in *fullness of joy in God's presence*.

## The reading
Psalm 16 is not wrung from distress like the laments; it is the settled voice of an inner being that has found where its good lies — and traces everything back to that one discovery.

**I. The founding confession — "I have no good apart from you" (vv.1–2).**
"**Preserve** (H8104, M30) me, O God, for in you I **take refuge** (H2620, M19). I say to the LORD, 'You are my Lord; I have **no good apart from you**'" (vv.1–2). This is the root: the inner being has located *all* good in God — there is no good *outside* him *(stated)*. Everything the psalm then says grows from this consolidation.

**II. Delight rightly placed; sorrow rightly seen (vv.3–4).**
Its affections follow: "as for the **saints** (H6918, M12) in the land, they are the excellent ones, in whom is all my **delight** (H2656, M04)" (v.3) — the same *delight* that Psalm 1 fixed on the law, here on God's holy people. And the contrast: those who "**run after** another god" only "**multiply** their **sorrows** (H6094, M03)" (v.4). Misplaced desire *breeds grief*; rightly placed delight does not *(inferred: the two directions of desire, as in Ps 4)*.

**III. God himself as the portion (vv.5–6).**
The heart of it: "The LORD is my **chosen portion** and my **cup**; you hold my **lot**. The **lines** have fallen for me in **pleasant places** (H5273, M22); indeed, I have a beautiful inheritance" (vv.5–6). The inner being's inheritance is not God's *gifts* but **God himself** — and because it *has* God, it is content with its lot whatever it is *(inferred: the pleasantness is in the portion being God, not the circumstances)*.

**IV. Counsel by night; presence by day (vv.7–8).**
"I **bless** (H1288, M39) the LORD who gives me **counsel** (H3289, M15); in the night also my **heart** instructs me" (v.7) — God's counsel so internalised that the heart itself teaches in the dark (against Ps 13's heart that only *churned* by night). "I have **set the LORD always before me**; because he is at my right hand, I shall **not be shaken**" (v.8) — a deliberate discipline of **keeping God in view**, and its fruit is stability *(stated)*.

**V. The whole person at rest (v.9), and beyond death (vv.10–11).**
"Therefore my **heart** (H3820, M47) is **glad** (H8055, M04), and my **whole being** (kavod) **rejoices** (H1523); my **flesh** (H1320, M47) also dwells **secure** (H0983, M19)" (v.9) — the joy is **integrated through the entire person**: heart, whole-being, and *body* all at rest *(stated: three seats, one joy)*. It reaches past death: "you will not **abandon** my **soul** (H5315, M47) to Sheol" (v.10), and climaxes: "you make known to me the **path of life** (H2416, M25); in your **presence** there is **fullness of joy** (H8057, M04); at your right hand are **pleasures forevermore**" (v.11).

## What Psalm 16 says about the inner being
- **When the inner being makes God its supreme good, everything else follows.** "I have no good apart from you" (v.2) and "the LORD is my chosen portion" (v.5) are the root; contentment, stability, and joy are the branches. The decisive inner act is *consolidating all good in God*.
- **Its inheritance is God himself, not God's gifts** (vv.5–6): having *God* as portion, it is content with any lot — the antidote to the desire that "runs after" other goods and multiplies sorrow (v.4).
- **Joy permeates the whole person** (v.9): this is not a merely spiritual gladness — heart, whole-being, and *flesh* all rejoice and rest. The God-satisfied inner being overflows into bodily security.
- **The deepest joy is presence, not possession** (v.11): the path of life ends not in things but in God's *presence*, where joy is *full* and pleasures are *forevermore*. Being *with* God is the inner being's fullness.
- **God's counsel can become the heart's own night-instruction** (v.7): internalised long enough, God's guidance teaches from within — the dark hours that churn the anxious heart (Ps 13:2) *instruct* the settled one.
