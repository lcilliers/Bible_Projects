## The reading
Psalm 4 reads the inner being as something that can be **settled from within** even while others chase after good elsewhere.

**I. Appeal to the God who hears (v.1).**
The opening stacks the grounds of confidence: "Answer me… O God of my **righteousness** (H6664, characteristic — M26); you have given me relief in **distress**; be **gracious** (H2603, characteristic — M39) to me and **hear** (H8085, characteristic — M41) my prayer." The inner posture is **appeal grounded in relationship** — God is named as the source of the psalmist's own righteousness, and as the one who *has* relieved and *does* hear *(inferred: past relief funds present confidence)*.

**II. Honor, shame, and the divide of desire (vv.2–3).**
To the assailants: "how long shall my **honor** (H3519, characteristic — M22) be turned to **shame** (M07), how long will you love vanity and **seek** (H1245, characteristic — M41) after lies?" (v.2). The contrast is between two directions of desire — some **seek lies**; the psalmist's honour rests elsewhere. And the anchor: "know that the LORD has **set apart the godly** (H2623, characteristic — M05) for himself; the LORD **hears** when I **call** (H7121, characteristic — M37)" (v.3). Security is being **set apart and heard** *(stated)*.

**III. The summons to inner self-examination (vv.4–5).**
The heart of the psalm's counsel: "**Be angry/tremble** (H7264, characteristic — M01), and do **not sin** (H2398 — negated, M10); **ponder** in your own **hearts** (H3824, the seat — M47) on your beds, and be **silent** (H1826, characteristic — M33)" (v.4). This is a whole discipline of the inner life — agitation *checked* before it becomes sin, self-examination *in the heart*, and **inner stillness** as its resolution *(inferred: the sequence tremble→ponder→be-silent is a movement toward composure)*. Then: "offer **right** (M26) sacrifices, and **put your trust** (H0982, characteristic — M19) in the LORD" (v.5) — the settling ends in **trust**.

**IV. The two inner goods — joy above plenty, peace that sleeps (vv.6–8).**
Against the many who ask "Who will show us some **good**?" and long for the light of God's face (v.6), the psalmist's answer is inward: "you have put more **joy** (H8057, characteristic — M04) in my **heart** (H3820, the seat — M47) than they have when their grain and wine abound" (v.7) — an inner joy **greater than material abundance** *(stated: more joy than… grain and wine)*. And the close, echoing Psalm 3:5: "In **peace** (H7965, characteristic — M33) I will both lie down and sleep; for you alone, O LORD, make me dwell in **safety** (M19)" (v.8) — **peace that produces rest**, security located in God alone.

## What Psalm 4 says about the inner being
- The inner being can be **settled from within** while the "many" chase good externally (v.6) — its two settled goods are **joy that outweighs plenty** (v.7) and **peace that lets one sleep in safety** (v.8).
- Psalm 4 gives a rare explicit **discipline of the inner life** (v.4): *tremble/be-angry but do not sin* (check the agitation), *ponder in your heart* (self-examine), *be silent* (still it) — a movement from disturbance to composure, resolved in **trust** (v.5).
- Desire is the divide (v.2): some **seek lies / love vanity**; the settled soul's honour and joy rest in God, who **hears** and has **set the godly apart**.
- With Psalm 3 it forms a pair — both end in **sleep granted by God** — locating the inner being's rest not in circumstances but in being heard and kept.
