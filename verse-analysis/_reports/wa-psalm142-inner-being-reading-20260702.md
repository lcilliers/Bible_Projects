## The reading

**I. Pouring out the complaint before God (v.2).**
"I **pour out my complaint** before him; I **tell my trouble** before him" (v.2). The gem-finding: the self *empties* its complaint and *tells* its trouble to God — full, unedited disclosure *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **unburdens itself entirely before God** — "pour out" figures a *complete emptying* of what is inside, holding nothing back; the self brings its trouble to God not in tidy petition but as *outpoured complaint* *(inferred: "I pour out my complaint… I tell my trouble" is the honest, total unburdening — the inner being turned out before God like a vessel emptied; cf. Ps 62:8 "pour out your heart before him", 1 Sam 1:15 Hannah "pouring out my soul")*.

**II. The fainting spirit that God still knows (v.3).**
"When my **spirit** (H7307, M47) **faints** within me, **you know my way**!" (v.3). The gem-finding: even as the *spirit faints within*, the self affirms that **God knows its way** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being at the point of *collapse* holds to **being *known* by God** — the fainting is real, but so is the confidence that God still *sees the path* the self cannot; being-known is the comfort *underneath* the fainting *(inferred: "when my spirit faints within me, you know my way" sets the self's inner collapse against God's undiminished knowledge — even fainting, the self is not lost to God, who knows the way through; the 139 datum (fully known) here as *comfort in extremity*; cf. Ps 143:4, 61:2 "when my heart is faint")*.

**III. God as refuge and portion in abandonment (vv.4–5).**
"Look to the right and see: there is **none who takes notice of me**; no refuge remains to me; **no one cares for my soul** (H5315, M47). I cry to you, O LORD; I say, '**You are my refuge** (H4268, M19), **my portion** in the land of the living'" (vv.4–5). The gem-finding: abandoned by all — "no one cares for my soul" — the self declares **God its *refuge* and its *portion*** *(stated)*. The operation: in total human abandonment the inner being **relocates its whole security and inheritance in *God alone*** — when no one takes notice and no refuge remains, the self names God as both its *shelter* (refuge) and its *allotted inheritance* (portion); the emptier the human field, the more entirely God becomes the self's all *(inferred: "no one cares for my soul… You are my refuge, my portion" makes the abandonment the very occasion for locating everything in God — refuge (safety) and portion (inheritance/possession) both found in him when all else fails; the portion-theme of 16:5, 73:26, here from the cave)*.

## What Psalm 142 says about the inner being
- **The self *pours out its complaint* before God (v.2).** A complete, unedited unburdening — the inner being emptied before God like a vessel, holding nothing back; trouble brought as outpoured complaint, not tidy petition.
- **The *fainting* spirit is still *known* (v.3).** At the point of collapse the self holds to being seen — "you know my way"; being-known by God is the comfort underneath the fainting, the path known even when the self cannot see it.
- **God is *refuge and portion* in total abandonment (vv.4–5).** When "no one cares for my soul," the inner being relocates its whole security *and* inheritance in God alone — the emptier the human field, the more entirely God becomes the self's all.
