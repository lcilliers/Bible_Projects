## The reading

**I. The soul *sated* with trouble (vv.3–4).**
"For my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) is **full** (*saba* — sated) of **troubles** (H7451, M03, *ra*), and my **life** (H2416, M25) draws near to Sheol… I am a man who has **no strength** (H0353, M23, *eyal*)" (vv.3–4). The gem-finding: the inner being is **saturated with affliction** — the soul *sated*, *filled to capacity*, with trouble *(stated)*. The operation: the very verb of *satisfaction* (*saba*, "full/sated" — the word for the soul *satisfied* with good in Ps 63:5) is here inverted — the soul is **full, but of *trouble***, drained of strength *(inferred: the soul "full of troubles" is satisfaction's dark inverse — filled to the brim, but with affliction, not good)*. The inner being is *at capacity* with suffering *(inferred)*.

**II. Prayer persists *in* the darkness — without resolution (vv.1, 9, 13).**
"O LORD, God of my salvation, **I cry out day and night** before you… my eye grows dim through **sorrow** (H6040, M24). **Every day I call** upon you… I spread out my hands to you… But I, O LORD, **cry to you**; in the morning my **prayer** comes before you" (vv.1, 9, 13). The gem-finding: **even in unrelieved darkness, the inner being keeps *crying to God*** — day and night, every day, in the morning — though **nothing turns** *(stated)*. The operation: the psalm has **no resolution** (unique among the laments — no "but you," no light), yet the self **still addresses God** *(inferred)*. The finding — profound: the inner being can **keep praying from within total abandonment** — faith here is **not a felt hope but a persistence of address** — the self holds on to *speak to God* even when it experiences only his silence and his "no" *(inferred: the sustained "I cry… I call… my prayer comes before you" *without any turn* discloses a faith that is sheer continued address, not resolved comfort)*.

**III. The questions of abandonment (v.14).**
"O LORD, **why do you cast my soul away? Why do you hide your face** from me?" (v.14). The finding: the inner being's **unanswered *why*** — questioning God's abandonment (as Ps 22:1, 77:7) — but here **left unanswered**, the questions standing open *(stated)*. The operation: the self **puts its abandonment to God as a question** and receives no reply within the psalm — the interrogation itself is an act of address to the one who seems absent *(inferred)*.

**IV. Darkness the only companion (vv.8, 18).**
"You have caused my companions to shun me… You have caused my beloved and my friend to shun me; **my companions have become darkness**" (vv.8, 18). The gem-finding: stripped of every human companion, the inner being is left with **darkness as its only intimate** — "my closest acquaintance is darkness" *(stated)*. The operation: the self's **total isolation** — all beloved and friends withdrawn, so that the only remaining *company* is the dark *(inferred: "my companions have become darkness" makes the darkness the sole intimate, the ultimate loneliness)*. **The psalm ends here** — no light follows *(stated)*.

## What Psalm 88 says about the inner being
- **The soul can be *sated with trouble* (v.3).** The very word for satisfaction is inverted — the inner being *full to capacity*, but of affliction, not good; drained of strength.
- **Faith can be *sheer persistence of address* (vv.1, 9, 13).** The inner being keeps crying to God — day and night, morning by morning — from within *unrelieved* darkness, with no turn to hope. Faith here is not felt comfort but the refusal to stop speaking to God even when it experiences only his "no."
- **Abandonment can be put to God as an open *question* (v.14).** "Why do you hide your face?" — asked and left unanswered; the interrogation itself an act of address to the seemingly-absent.
- **The self can be left with *darkness as its only companion* (v.18).** Stripped of every human intimate, the inner being's sole remaining company is the dark — and the psalm ends there, no light. That the study's data includes *this* — a genuine inner-being state without resolution — is itself the finding.
