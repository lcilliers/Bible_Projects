# Psalm 25 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **An acrostic** (each verse begins with a successive Hebrew letter). The form is not a linear argument but a **comprehensive, A-to-Z circling** of the soul's postures before God — so the reading names the *recurring postures* rather than forcing a progression. Three intertwined needs braid through it — to be **taught**, to be **forgiven**, to be **delivered** — all carried by **trust** and **waiting** (*qavah*, which brackets the psalm at vv.3, 5, 21), and all grounded in God's *chesed*, name, and goodness, never the pleader's merit.

## The reading

**I. The soul lifted to God — and held in waiting (vv.1–5).**
"To you, O LORD, I **lift up my soul** (H5375 *nasa* + H5315, the seat — M47)" (v.1). The very idiom Psalm 24:4 used *negatively* ("does **not** lift up his soul to what is false") appears here *positively*: the inner being's longing and aspiration are **directed to God** — the soul offered up in dependence rather than fixed on vanity *(inferred: nasa-nephesh is the idiom of aiming/longing the self; here the object is "to you, O LORD")*. This is coupled at once with **trust** ("in you I **trust**", H0982, M19, v.2) and its stake, **shame** ("let me not be **put to shame**", H0954, M07) — shame is again named as the antithesis of vindicated trust *(stated)*. Then the frame-posture enters: "none who **wait** (H6960, M17, *qavah*) for you shall be put to shame… for you I **wait** all the day long" (vv.3, 5). The inner being's stance is **expectant waiting** — not passive but a sustained, all-day leaning *(stated)*.

**II. The plea to be *taught* — teachability as the soul's central request (vv.4–5, 8–9, 12).**
A dense cluster of guidance-verbs: "**Make me know** (H3045) your ways… **teach** (H3925) me your paths. **Lead** (H1869) me in your **truth** (H0571, M13)" (vv.4–5). The inner being's central petition is not merely rescue but **guidance** — to be *shown* and *led* *(stated)*. And the ones God teaches are named: "He **leads the humble** (H6035, M24, *anav*) in what is right, and **teaches the humble** his way" (v.9); "Who is the man who **fears** (H3373, M01) the LORD? Him will he **instruct** in the way he should **choose** (H0977, M37)" (v.12). **Teachability has preconditions — humility and the fear of the LORD** — and its fruit is a self able to *choose* rightly because it has been taught *(inferred: the taught man is instructed "in the way he should choose")*. The inner being that lowers itself and fears is the one led.

**III. The appeal to God's memory — remember mercy, remember not my sins, remember *me* (vv.6–7, 11, 18).**
A threefold play on **remember** (H2142, M41, *zakar*): "**Remember** your **mercy** (H7356, M05, *racham*)… and your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05, *chesed*)" — "**Remember not** the **sins** (H2403, M10) of my youth or my **transgressions** (H6588, M10)" — "according to your steadfast love **remember me**" (vv.6–7). The inner being asks God to remember **selectively**: his own mercy, and the pleader — but *not* the record *(stated)*. And the ground is entirely God's: "**for the sake of your goodness**" (v.7), "**for your name's sake**" (v.11). The most striking clause is v.11: "pardon my **guilt** (H5771, M10), **for it is great**." Not *pardon despite its greatness* but **pardon because it is great** — the inner being pleads **the very size of its need as the ground of grace**, because only such pardon magnifies God's name *(inferred: the "for it is great" is offered as the reason, not the obstacle)*. Forgiveness and affliction are sought as one breath: "Consider my affliction… and **forgive all my sins**" (v.18) — deliverance from trouble and from sin are not separate requests *(stated)*.

**IV. The fear of the LORD that becomes *friendship* (vv.12–14).**
The gem at the psalm's centre: "The **friendship** (H5475, M17, *sod*) of the LORD is for those who **fear** (H3373, M01) him, and he makes **known** (H3045) to them his covenant" (v.14). *Sod* is **intimate counsel — the confidential circle, the shared secret**. The fear of the LORD issues **not in distance but in intimacy**: God lets those who fear him **into his confidence** *(stated: the sod of the LORD is for those who fear him)*. The paradox is exact — *fear* opens *friendship*; the reverent inner being is the one taken into God's counsel *(inferred)*. And the fearing soul's future is secure: "His **soul** (H5315, M47) shall **abide** in well-being" (v.13).

**V. The fixed gaze and the enlarged heart (vv.15–19).**
"My **eyes** are **ever** toward the LORD, for he will pluck my feet out of the net" (v.15). The inner being's **continual orientation** — the gaze *ever* fixed Godward, a settled attentiveness even while trapped *(stated)*. Then the raw disclosure of distress: "Turn to me and be **gracious** (H2603, M39), for I am **lonely** (*yachid* — solitary) and **afflicted** (v.16). The **troubles** (H6869, M03) of my **heart** (H3824, the seat — M47) are **enlarged** (*rachab* — made wide); bring me out of my **distresses** (H4691, M03)" (v.17). Two things for the inner being: **loneliness** named outright — the solitary self — and the heart's troubles that **widen** rather than shrink, the inner straits *growing* under pressure *(stated: the troubles of the heart are enlarged)*. The gaze stays fixed (v.15) even as the heart's distress expands (v.17) — attentiveness and anguish coexist.

**VI. Integrity, refuge, and waiting as the frame (vv.20–22).**
"Oh, **guard** (H8104, M30) my **soul** (H5315, M47), and deliver me… for I **take refuge** (H2620, M19, *chasah*) in you" (v.20) — the refuge-posture again (cf. Ps 2, 3, 11, 16). Then: "May **integrity** (H8537, M13, *tom*) and **uprightness** (H3476, M26, *yosher*) **preserve** me, for I **wait** (H6960, M17) for you" (v.21). The inner qualities are asked to **function as guardians** — integrity and uprightness *preserve* the one who has them — and the ground is once more **waiting** *(stated)*. Waiting closes the psalm as it opened it (vv.3, 5 → 21); the whole is held between the soul lifted up (v.1) and the soul that waits (v.21).

## What Psalm 25 says about the inner being
- **The soul's aim can be lifted *to God* (v.1).** The positive counterpoint to Ps 24:4 — the inner being directs its longing to the LORD rather than to vanity. Aspiration Godward is the opening posture.
- **Waiting is the sustaining stance (vv.3, 5, 21).** *Qavah* brackets the psalm — an expectant, all-day leaning that is never put to shame.
- **Teachability is the soul's central request, and it has preconditions (vv.4–5, 9, 12).** The inner being asks above all to be *taught* and *led*; humility and the fear of the LORD are what make it teachable, and the fruit is a self able to choose rightly.
- **The inner being appeals to be remembered by grace, not by record (vv.6–7).** Remember your mercy, remember *not* my sins, remember *me* by steadfast love — a selective memory pleaded on God's own goodness and name.
- **The size of the need is pleaded as the ground of grace (v.11).** "Pardon my guilt, *for it is great*" — need magnified, not minimised, because grace answers to God's name.
- **Fear of the LORD becomes *friendship* (v.14).** *Sod* — the reverent inner being is admitted to God's confidential counsel. Fear opens intimacy, not distance.
- **The gaze stays fixed while the heart's troubles widen (vv.15, 17).** "Eyes ever toward the LORD" and "the troubles of my heart are enlarged" hold together — attentiveness and anguish coexist; loneliness is named.
- **Integrity and uprightness *preserve* the inner being (v.21).** Inner qualities function as guardians, and waiting is their ground.
