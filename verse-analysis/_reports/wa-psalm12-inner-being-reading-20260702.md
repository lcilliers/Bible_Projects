# Psalm 12 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 12:1–8 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter (v.1 backfilled). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **A psalm about words.** Its own key is the contrast between the **double heart** that produces lies (v.2) and the **sevenfold-refined word** of God (v.6) — speech as the outflow of the inner being, human duplicity set against divine purity.

## The reading
Psalm 12 looks at a society where integrity has drained away, and finds the crisis located in *speech* — because speech is where the inner being shows.

**I. The vanishing of the faithful (v.1).**
"Save, O LORD, for the **godly** (H2623, M05) one is gone; the **faithful** (H0529, M13) have vanished from among the children of man." The lament is not over an enemy but over an **absence** — loyalty and faithfulness have disappeared from the common life *(stated)*.

**II. The double heart (v.2).**
The diagnosis is precise: "everyone utters **lies** to his neighbour; with flattering **lips** they **speak**, and with a **double heart** (H3820, the seat — M47)." The Hebrew is *lev va-lev* — "a heart and a heart." Corruption is not merely false words but a **split in the inner being**: the lips say one thing while the heart holds another *(inferred: the doubleness is the root; the flattery is its fruit)*. Integrity would be *singleness* of heart; its opposite is a heart divided against its own speech.

**III. The tongue as a claim to autonomy (vv.3–4).**
The boast lays the inner motive bare: "those who say, 'With our **tongue** we will **prevail** (H1396, M23); our **lips** are our own; who is **master over** us?'" (v.4). Speech here is a declaration of **self-sovereignty** — the tongue wielded to deny any lord over the self *(stated: who is master over us)*. The corrupt inner being uses words to unseat God.

**IV. God speaks — for the groaning poor (v.5).**
Into this flood of human words, one different word breaks in: "'Because the **poor** are plundered, because the **needy groan**, I will now **arise**,' says the LORD, 'I will place him in the safety for which he **longs**.'" God's word is the one that *acts* — and it is spoken **for the oppressed** *(stated: the LORD speaks)*.

**V. The refined word (vv.6–7).**
The contrast is then made total: "the words of the LORD are **pure** (H2889, M12) words, like silver refined in a furnace… **purified seven times**" (v.6). Against lips that flatter and a heart that is double, God's word is **incorruptible** — assayed metal, purest of the pure. And it is a word that **keeps**: "you, O LORD, will **keep** (H8104, M30) them; you will guard us from this generation" (v.7) *(stated)*.

## What Psalm 12 says about the inner being
- **Deceit is a divided heart, not just a false word.** The "double heart" (*lev va-lev*, v.2) names duplicity precisely: the corrupt inner being is *split* — its speech and its intent point different ways. Integrity, by contrast, is *singleness* — heart and word aligned.
- **Words are the outflow of the inner being**, and the psalm judges a whole society by its speech: lies, flattery, and the boast that "our lips are our own." What the inner being *is*, the tongue *says* (joining Ps 5:9, 10:7 — the speech-reveals-the-heart thread, here made the whole subject).
- **The tongue can be a bid for autonomy** (v.4): "who is master over us?" — speech turned into a denial of any lord, the inner being's rebellion voiced.
- **The one pure word is God's** (v.6): in a world of verbal pollution, the incorruptible thing is *God's* speech — refined seven times — and it is a word that *arises for the oppressed* (v.5). The inner being's hope in a faithless age is a God who still speaks purely and keeps his own.
