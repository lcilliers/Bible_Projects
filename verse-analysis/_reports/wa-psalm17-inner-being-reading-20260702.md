# Psalm 17 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 17:1–15 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter (vv.13–14 backfilled). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **A prayer for vindication that ends in a longing to see God.** Its own key is **two satisfactions**: the world satisfied with its earthly portion, and the inner being that will only be satisfied "with your likeness" when it awakes.

## The reading
Psalm 17 opens as a plea from a clear conscience and closes on the one thing that conscience most wants.

**I. The prayer of undeceiving lips (vv.1–2).**
"**Hear** (H8085, M41) a **just** (H6664, M26) cause, O LORD… give ear to my prayer from lips without **deceit** (H4820, M14)" (v.1). The appeal is grounded in **inner honesty** — a prayer that matches the heart *(stated: lips without deceit)*.

**II. The night-tested, purposed heart (vv.3–5).**
The confidence deepens: "you have **tried** my **heart** (H3820, the seat — M47), you have visited me by **night**, you have **tested** me, and you will find nothing; I have **purposed** that my mouth will not transgress" (v.3). Two things stand out: God **assays the inner being in the hidden hours**, and the inner being has *resolved* on integrity — it is a **deliberate purpose**, not an accident *(inferred: 'I have purposed' makes integrity an act of will)*. It is confident enough to *invite* the scrutiny (as in Ps 7), and it has "held fast" to God's paths so its "feet have not slipped" (v.5).

**III. The plea for tenderest protection (vv.6–8).**
"Wondrously show your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05), O Saviour of those who seek **refuge** (H2620, M19)… **Keep** (H8104, M30) me as the **apple of your eye**; hide me in the **shadow of your wings**" (vv.7–8). The images are the most tender in the Psalter — the *pupil*, which we guard by instinct, and the mother-bird's *wing*. The inner being asks to be held as close and as carefully as one guards one's own eye *(inferred: the images ask for God's utmost tenderness)*.

**IV. The pitiless predators (vv.9–12).**
The threat is drawn with inner detail: the wicked "have closed their hearts to **pity**; with their mouths they speak **arrogantly** (H1348, M08)" (v.10), and lurk "like a **lion eager** (H3700, M18) to tear" (v.12). The oppressor's inner state is **hardness plus arrogance** — pity shut off, self-exaltation open *(stated: closed to pity, speak arrogantly)*.

**V. Two satisfactions (vv.13–15).**
The climax turns on a single word. Of the wicked: "deliver my **soul** (H5315, M47)… from men of the world whose **portion is in this life**… they are **satisfied** with children, and leave their abundance to their infants" (v.14) — their reward is *earthly*, and they are *filled with it*. Against this: "As for me, I shall **behold your face** in **righteousness** (H6664, M26); when I **awake**, I shall be **satisfied** with your **likeness**" (v.15). The same word *satisfied* (saba) divides them: the world is *full of things*; the psalmist will be *full of God's face* *(inferred: the repeated 'satisfied' is the hinge — two fillings, two portions)*.

## What Psalm 17 says about the inner being
- **Integrity can be a deliberate purpose that welcomes testing.** "You have tested me… I have purposed that my mouth will not transgress" (v.3): the clear conscience is not passive innocence but a *resolved* inner direction, confident enough to invite God's night-assay (with Ps 7:9, 11:5 — the tested-heart thread).
- **The inner being longs to be tenderly kept** (v.8): "apple of your eye… shadow of your wings" — its deepest safety-wish is not mere rescue but to be *cherished and sheltered* with God's utmost care.
- **Two portions, two satisfactions** (vv.14–15): the world's inner being is *filled by this life* — wealth, heirs, abundance; the God-seeking inner being defers its filling to the **vision of God's face**. The decisive question is *what satisfies you* — things, or God's likeness. (This is Ps 16's "God as portion" turned toward the future.)
- **Its ultimate hope is to wake and see God** (v.15): "when I awake, I shall be satisfied with your likeness" — the inner being's telos is the *beholding* of God (with Ps 11:7, 16:11). Satisfaction is finally a matter of *sight* and *presence*, not possession.
