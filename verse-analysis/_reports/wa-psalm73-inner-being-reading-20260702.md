# Psalm 73 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 73:1–28 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter (vv.19 + others backfilled with the fixed full-text logic). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **Lens:** the finding is the inner-being **operation**, God-relation as arena (`feedback_lens_is_inner_being_process_not_god_relation`).
- **The anatomy of an inner crisis — envy, resolved by re-perception and re-valuation. A cornerstone.** Psalm 73 traces a **whole crisis of the inner being**: **envy** at the wicked's prosperity nearly *toppling* the self (vv.2–3); bitterness **reducing it to a brute** (vv.21–22); the crisis **resolved by a shift of vantage** in the sanctuary — *discerning the end* (vv.16–17); and the great pivot into desiring **God himself as the supreme good and portion** (vv.23–28). The movement is *destabilised → brutish → re-seeing → re-valuing*.

## The reading

**I. Envy nearly topples the inner being (vv.2–3).**
"But as for me, my **feet had almost stumbled** (H5186), my steps had nearly **slipped** (H8210). For I was **envious** (H7065, M02, *qana*) of the arrogant when I saw the **prosperity of the wicked**" (vv.2–3). The finding: **envy nearly overthrew the self's footing** — the feet almost slipped *(stated)*. The operation: seeing the wicked prosper set off *qana* (envy — the same passion-word as zeal, M02), and that envy **destabilised the inner being to the brink of falling** *(inferred: the "feet almost stumbled… for I was envious" makes the envy the cause of the near-collapse; the very thing Ps 37 warned against — "be not envious")*.

**II. Bitterness reduces the self to a brute (vv.13, 21–22).**
The envy fed a grievance: "**All in vain** have I kept my **heart** (H3824, the seat — M47) **clean**" (v.13). And its inner effect, seen in retrospect: "When my **soul** was **embittered** (H2556, *chamets* — soured), when I was **pricked** in **heart** (kidneys), I was **brutish** (H1198, M16, *baar*) and **ignorant**; I was **like a beast** toward you" (vv.21–22). The gem-finding: **bitterness reduced the inner being to a *beast*** — brutish, without understanding *(stated)*. The operation: the **soured soul loses its understanding** — envy-fed bitterness **strips the inner being of the very faculty (understanding) that raises it above the animals** (Ps 49:20's "man without understanding is like the beasts") *(inferred: "embittered… brutish… like a beast" chains the souring to the loss of understanding)*.

**III. The turn in the sanctuary — a shift of vantage (vv.16–17).**
"But when I **thought** how to **understand** this, it seemed a **wearisome** task, **until I went into the sanctuary** of God; then I **discerned** (H0995, M15, *bin*) their **end** (H0319, *acharith*)" (vv.16–17). The gem-finding: the crisis is **not resolved by reasoning alone** (which *wearied* him) but by **entering God's presence**, where the inner being gains a **new perception — discerning the wicked's *end*** *(stated)*. The operation: a **shift of vantage** — from the immediate view (the wicked prosper *now*) to the **end-view** (their trajectory and destruction) — and this reframing **dissolves the envy** *(inferred: the "wearisome… until I went into the sanctuary… then I discerned their end" makes the resolution a change of *where the self stands to see*, not new information reasoned out)*. Perception, reframed in God's presence, is the turn.

**IV. The great pivot — God as the supreme desire and portion (vv.23–26).**
"**Nevertheless**, I am **continually with you**; you hold my right hand. You **guide** me with your **counsel**… **Whom have I in heaven but you?** And there is **nothing on earth that I desire besides you**. My **flesh** and my **heart** (H3824, M47) may **fail**, but God is the **strength of my heart** and my **portion** forever" (vv.23–26). The gem-finding — the psalm's peak: the crisis resolves into a **re-valuation** — the inner being discovers it **desires God above everything** ("nothing on earth I desire besides you") and that **God is its portion** *(stated)*. The operation: the envy (which prized the wicked's *stuff*) is answered by a **re-ordered desire** that finds its all in God himself *(inferred: the "nothing I desire besides you" directly reverses the envy of v.3 — the object of desire is re-set)*. And even when the inner being's own resources give out — "my flesh and my **heart** may **fail**" — **God is the *strength of the heart*** beyond the heart's own failing: the self is upheld at the very point of its collapse *(stated; the heart sustained past its own end)*.

**V. "The nearness of God is my good" (v.28).**
"But for me it is **good to be near God**; I have made the Lord GOD my **refuge** (H4268, M19)" (v.28). The finding: the inner being's **supreme good is re-defined as *nearness* to God** — not prosperity (the wicked's), but *proximity* *(stated)*. The operation: the whole crisis terminates in a **redefinition of the good** — the self's *good* is being *near* God, and that nearness is where the envy finally has no more hold *(inferred: "it is good *to be near God*" names the resolved value — nearness itself is the good, over against the wicked's goods)*.

## What Psalm 73 says about the inner being
- **Envy destabilises the inner being (vv.2–3).** Seeing the wicked prosper nearly *toppled* the self — the feet almost slipped. *Qana* (envy, the M02 passion) can bring the inner being to the brink of falling.
- **Bitterness reduces the self to a *brute* (vv.21–22).** The soured soul loses its understanding — envy-fed bitterness strips the very faculty that raises the inner being above the beasts.
- **The crisis is resolved by a *shift of vantage*, not by reasoning (vv.16–17).** Entering God's presence gives a new perception — discerning the wicked's *end* — and the reframed view dissolves the envy. Perception, re-sited in the sanctuary, is the turn.
- **Resolution is a *re-ordered desire* (vv.23–26).** The envy that prized the wicked's stuff is answered by desiring *God himself* above all — "nothing on earth I desire besides you"; God is the *portion*, and the *strength of the heart* even when the heart fails.
- **The good is re-defined as *nearness* to God (v.28).** The whole crisis ends in a redefinition of the good — proximity to God, not prosperity. Where the self is near God, the envy has no hold.
