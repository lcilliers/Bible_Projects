Its inner-being findings: the **brokenhearted *healed* and their wounds bound** (v.3); and the searching datum that **God's pleasure is in the *fearing, hoping* heart — not in human strength** (vv.10–11).

## The reading

**I. The brokenhearted healed (v.3).**
"He **heals the brokenhearted** (H3820, M47) and **binds up their wounds**" (v.3). The gem-finding: God's care reaches the *broken heart* — he *heals* it and *binds up* its wounds *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's ***wounds are tended*** — the broken heart is not left to bleed but *bound up*, the healing directed precisely at the *inner* injury; God as physician of the wounded self *(inferred: "he heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds" makes the *inner* wound the object of God's tender repair — the shattered heart mended, the hurt bound like a physician dressing a wound; God's nearness to the broken, cf. Ps 34:18, 51:17, Isa 61:1)*.

**II. God's pleasure in the *fearing, hoping* heart, not in strength (vv.10–11).**
"His **delight is not in the strength of the horse**, nor his pleasure in the legs of a man, but the LORD **takes pleasure in those who fear** (H3373, M01) him, in those who **hope** (H3176, M18) in his steadfast love" (vv.10–11). The gem-finding: what *pleases* God is **not power** (horse, man's legs) but the heart that **fears him and hopes in his steadfast love** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being learns that **its *reverent fear and hope*, not its strength, are what *delight* God** — the self's value before God lies not in might or capability but in the *inward* posture of fear-and-hope; the fearing, hoping heart is the thing God *takes pleasure in* *(inferred: "his delight is not in the strength of the horse… but in those who fear him, who hope in his steadfast love" contrasts *external power* with the *inner posture* and locates God's pleasure entirely in the latter — the inner being's worth is its fear and hope, not its force; a searching datum on what God actually values in the self, cf. Ps 33:16–18, 1 Sam 16:7, Mic 6:8)*.

## What Psalm 147 says about the inner being
- **The *brokenhearted* are *healed* (v.3).** God's care reaches the inner wound — the shattered heart mended, its hurt bound up like a physician dressing a wound; the broken self not left to bleed.
- **God's pleasure is in the *fearing, hoping* heart, not in strength (vv.10–11).** What delights God is not power (horse, man's legs) but the inward posture of reverent fear and hope in his steadfast love — the inner being's worth lies in its fear and hope, not its force.
