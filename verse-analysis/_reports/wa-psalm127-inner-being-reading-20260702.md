- **The bread of anxious toil versus the sleep God gives his beloved.** Psalm 127 opens with the futility of self-reliant striving. Its inner-being finding: the contrast between the **self grinding itself in *anxious* over-work** and the **rest that God *gives* to his beloved** (vv.1–2).

## The reading

**I. Anxious toil versus God-given sleep (vv.1–2).**
"Unless the LORD builds the house, those who build it **labor in vain**… It is **in vain** that you rise up early and go late to rest, **eating the bread of anxious toil**; for he **gives to his beloved sleep**" (vv.1–2). The gem-finding: the self's frantic over-labour — early rising, late resting, "the bread of anxious toil" — is **futile**, while God *gives* his beloved **sleep** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being is offered a choice between **anxious self-exhausting striving and *received rest*** — the "bread of anxious toil" names a self grinding itself in worry-driven effort *as if the outcome depended on it*, over against the *sleep* (rest, trust, release) that comes as a *gift* to the one God loves *(inferred: "eating the bread of anxious toil… for he gives to his beloved sleep" opposes two inner postures — the anxious self that over-works because it does not trust, and the beloved self that can *sleep* because it does; the sleep is the fruit and sign of released trust, the very opposite of anxious toil; cf. Ps 4:8 "in peace I will lie down and sleep", 3:5, Matt 6:25–34 "do not be anxious")*.

## What Psalm 127 says about the inner being
- **Anxious self-reliant striving is *futile*; rest is a *gift* to the trusting (vv.1–2).** The "bread of anxious toil" is a self grinding itself in worry-driven effort as if all depended on it — over against the *sleep* God gives his beloved, the fruit and sign of released trust. The inner being can exhaust itself in anxiety or *receive* rest.
