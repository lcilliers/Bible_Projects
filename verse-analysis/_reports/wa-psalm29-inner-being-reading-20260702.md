## The reading

**I. Worship as *ascribing* — the creature's response to glory (vv.1–2).**
"**Ascribe** (H3051, *yahav* — give) to the LORD, O heavenly beings, ascribe to the LORD **glory** (H3519, M22) and **strength** (H5797, M23)… **worship** the LORD in the **splendour** (H1927, M22) of **holiness** (H6944, M22)" (vv.1–2). The proper posture of the inner being before overwhelming power is **worship — to *ascribe / give* glory** *(stated)*. The gem is that one cannot *add* to God's glory; to *ascribe* is to **acknowledge and render** what is already his — worship is **recognition and self-abasement**, not contribution *(inferred: yahav renders to God what is his, it does not augment him)*. And it is worship "in the splendour of holiness" — the inner being bows before **beauty as well as power** *(stated)*.

**II. The theophany — and the inner being as *hearer* who can only cry "Glory!" (vv.3–9).**
Seven times "the voice of the LORD" (*qol YHWH*) rolls through the psalm — over the waters, breaking the cedars, flashing flames of fire, shaking the wilderness (vv.3–9). These verses are **God's action, not the inner being's** *(honest note: the reading does not allegorise them)*. But the theophany has one human hinge: "and in his temple **all cry, 'Glory!'** (H3519, M22)" (v.9). The gem: the **only inner response the storm evokes is a single word** — *Glory!* The contemplation of God's overwhelming power **reduces the inner being to worship** — it cannot analyse or answer, only exclaim *(inferred: the cry is the sole response amid the theophany)*. This is Ps 8's movement in another key — majesty contemplated sizes the inner being rightly, and the awed heart's whole speech becomes praise *(inferred)*. And it **echoes the summons**: what vv.1–2 commanded (ascribe *glory*) the temple now spontaneously does (cries *Glory*) — the imperative has become the heart's own cry *(inferred)*.

**III. The gem — overwhelming power becomes the inner being's strength and peace (vv.10–11).**
"The LORD sits **enthroned** over the flood; the LORD sits enthroned as **king forever**. May the LORD **give** (H5414, M12) **strength** (H5797, M23) to his people! May the LORD **bless** (H1288, M39) his people with **peace** (H7965, M33, *shalom*)!" (vv.10–11). Here the psalm turns the storm **inward**. The same **strength** (*oz*) the worshippers were told to *ascribe* to God (v.1) God now **gives** to his people (v.11) — an **inclusio on strength**: ascribed in v.1, granted in v.11 *(stated)*. And the God whose voice **shatters cedars and shakes the wilderness** (vv.5, 8) **blesses his people with *peace*** *(stated)*. The gem: **the terrifying power resolves not in terror but in shalom** — the storm outside becomes **peace inside**; the inner being contemplates the overwhelming voice and receives, not dread, but **strength and settled peace** *(inferred: the theophany's payoff for the people is strength + peace, not fear)*. The God enthroned over the flood is the God who steadies the heart.

## What Psalm 29 says about the inner being
- **Before overwhelming power, the inner being's posture is worship-as-ascription (vv.1–2).** To *ascribe* glory is to acknowledge and render what is already God's — recognition and self-abasement, not contribution — and it bows before *beauty* ("splendour of holiness") as much as power.
- **Contemplation of majesty reduces the inner being to a single cry — "Glory!" (v.9).** The awed heart cannot analyse the storm, only exclaim; the imperative to *ascribe glory* (v.1) becomes the temple's spontaneous cry of *Glory* (v.9). (Ps 8's right self-sizing, in the storm's key.)
- **The overwhelming power resolves into the inner being's strength and peace (vv.10–11).** The same *strength* ascribed to God is *given* to his people (inclusio); the voice that shatters cedars blesses with *shalom*. The storm outside becomes peace inside — majesty contemplated ends not in terror but in a steadied heart.
