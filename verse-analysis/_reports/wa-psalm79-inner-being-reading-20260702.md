## The reading

**I. Met by compassion, not by the record (vv.8–9).**
"**Do not remember** (H2142, M41) against us our **former iniquities** (H5771, M10); let your **compassion** (H7356, M05, *racham*) come speedily **to meet** us, **for we are brought very low**… **atone** (H3722, M38) for our **sins** (H2403, M10)… for your name's sake" (vv.8–9). The finding: the inner being appeals to be **met by *compassion*, not remembered by its *iniquities*** — it asks God's memory to turn *from* the record *to* mercy *(stated)*. The operation: the plea is for compassion to **come out and *meet* the self** (a prevenient movement, cf. Ps 21:3's *qadam*, "meet"), and its ground is **the self's own lowness** — "for we are brought very low" *(stated)*. The finding: the inner being pleads **its lowness, not its merit** — and asks that its *sin be atoned* on God's *name's sake*, not its own standing *(inferred: "brought very low… atone… for your name's sake" makes the appeal rest on the self's need and God's name, not the self's worth; cf. Ps 25:7's "remember not the sins of my youth", 65:3's overpowering iniquities atoned)*.

## What Psalm 79 says about the inner being
- **The inner being appeals to be *met by compassion*, not by its record (vv.8–9).** It asks God's memory to turn from its iniquities to mercy — compassion coming out to *meet* it — and grounds the plea on its own *lowness* and God's *name*, not on any merit. To be brought very low is itself the ground of the appeal.
