# Psalm 23 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 23:1–6 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter. Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **The inner being at rest in the shepherd's keeping.** Six verses, one settled confidence. Read first-principles (not through its familiarity), the psalm's own key is a **movement of the inner being**: *want stilled* → *rest given and soul restored* → *fear answered by presence* → *honoured amid enemies* → *pursued by steadfast love into settled dwelling*. Its distinctive disclosures cluster on **what the shepherd does to the inner being**: he *makes* it rest, *restores* the soul, and — at the darkest point — the speech turns from *about* him to *to* him.

## The reading

**I. The wanting stilled (v.1).**
"The LORD is **my** shepherd; I shall **not want** (H2637)." The psalm opens with a statement about the inner being's *lack* — and declares it quieted. Not "I have all things" but "I shall not **want**": the *wanting itself* is stilled by the relationship *(inferred: the object of the claim is the verb "want", i.e. the inner state of lack)*. The confidence is **personal and relational** — "*my* shepherd" — not a doctrine about God but a bond *(stated)*.

**II. Rest that is *made*, and the soul *restored* (vv.2–3).**
"He **makes me lie down** (H7257) in green pastures. He **leads** me beside **still** (H4496, M33) waters. He **restores** (H7725) my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47)." Two gems here. First, the rest is not self-achieved — the shepherd *makes* the sheep lie down, and the waters are literally the waters of **rest** (*menuchah*, M33): the inner being is **led into stillness** *(stated)*. Second, and central: "he restores my **soul**" — *shub nephesh*, the bringing-back of the inner being. The soul that flags is **turned/brought back** (the same *shub* that elsewhere is repentance) — the shepherd's core work on the inner being is **restoration** *(stated)*. And the guidance in "paths of **righteousness** (H6664, M26)" is grounded not in the sheep's merit but "**for his name's sake**" — the leading rests on the shepherd's own character *(stated)*.

**III. The turn to "you" — intimacy deepens in the valley (v.4).**
"Even though I **walk through** the valley of the shadow of death, I will **fear** (H3372, M01) **no evil**, for **you** are with me; your rod and your staff, they **comfort** (H5162, M05) me." The grammar itself preaches: vv.1–3 speak *about* the shepherd ("He… He… He"); at the valley the psalm turns to speak *to* him ("**you** are with me"). **The inner being draws nearest precisely at the darkest point** *(inferred from the He→You shift landing exactly on the valley)*. And the fearlessness is not stoicism but **relational** — "I will fear no evil, **for** you are with me": fear is answered by *presence*, not by the removal of the valley (he still *walks through* it) *(stated: the ground of no-fear is "you are with me")*. The rod and staff **comfort** (*nacham*, M05) — an inner consolation, not merely protection.

**IV. Honour and abundance in the enemies' presence (v.5).**
"You **prepare** (H6186, M26 — set in order) a table before me **in the presence of my enemies**; you **anoint** (H1878, M46) my head with oil; my cup **overflows**." The inner being is **satisfied and honoured in full view of the threat** — not after the enemies are removed *(stated: the table is set "in the presence of my enemies")*. The anointing (*dashen*, M46 — to make rich/fat) and the overflowing cup are **abundance lavished amid danger** *(inferred: the overflow signals more-than-enough)*.

**V. Pursued by steadfast love into settled dwelling (v.6).**
"Surely goodness and **mercy** (H2617, M05 — *chesed*) shall **follow** (H7291) me all the days of my life, and I shall **dwell** in the house of the LORD forever." The verb *radaph* (H7291) is **pursue / chase** — the word usually used of enemies hunting the sufferer. Here the one pursued is pursued instead by **goodness and steadfast love**: the inner being is **hunted down by chesed** *(inferred: radaph inverts the pursuit — mercy chases where foes used to)*. And the destination is not merely safety but **settled belonging** — "I shall **dwell** in the house of the LORD forever." The inner being's end is *dwelling with God* *(stated)*.

## What Psalm 23 says about the inner being
- **The inner being's *wanting* is stilled by the relationship (v.1).** The opening claim is about lack — and it is quieted not by possession but by "my shepherd."
- **Rest is *made*, not achieved (v.2).** "He makes me lie down"; the soul is *led* into stillness (the waters of *menuchah*). Rest is the shepherd's gift, worked on the inner being.
- **The soul is *restored* (v.3).** *Shub nephesh* — the flagging inner being is brought back; restoration is the shepherd's central inner-work, grounded "for his name's sake," not the sheep's merit.
- **Intimacy deepens in the dark (v.4).** The speech turns from *about* him to *to* him exactly at the valley — the inner being draws nearest at its lowest.
- **Fear is answered by presence, not by rescue from the valley (v.4).** He still *walks through* it; what changes is "you are with me." The rod and staff *comfort* — an inner consolation.
- **The inner being is satisfied *amid* enemies (v.5).** Honour and abundance are given in full view of the threat, not after its removal.
- **Steadfast love *pursues* (v.6).** *Radaph* inverts the hunt — the one chased by foes is now chased by *chesed*, into settled dwelling with God.
