## The reading

**I. Trembling worship before God's holiness (vv.1, 5, 9).**
"The LORD reigns; **let the peoples tremble**!… **Worship at his footstool**! **Holy is he**!… Exalt the LORD our God, and worship at his holy mountain; for the LORD our God is **holy**!" (vv.1, 5, 9). The finding: the fitting inner response to God's holiness is **trembling worship** — bowing *at his footstool*, the lowest place *(stated)*. The operation (resonance): God's holiness draws the inner being to **awe and self-abasement** — the threefold "Holy is he" frames a worship that *trembles* and bows low *(inferred: "let the peoples tremble… worship at his footstool… holy is he" makes the holiness evoke trembling self-abasement; cf. Ps 96:9's worship in the splendour of holiness, 5:7's bowing in fear)*.

**II. Calling and being answered (vv.6, 8).**
"They **called** to the LORD, and **he answered** them… you were a **forgiving** God to them, but an **avenger** of their wrongdoings" (vv.6, 8). The finding: the pattern of the intercessors (Moses, Aaron, Samuel) — **they called, and God answered** *(stated)*. The resonance: the inner being that *calls* is *answered* — and the God who answers is both **forgiving** and **holding to account** (forgiving yet avenging wrongdoing) *(inferred: "they called… he answered… forgiving… but an avenger" pairs the answered call with a God who both pardons and holds accountable — the answer is not indulgence)*.

## What Psalm 99 says about the inner being
- **God's holiness draws the inner being to *trembling worship* (vv.1, 5, 9).** Awe and self-abasement — bowing at the footstool, the lowest place — is the fitting response to the thrice-holy God.
- **The one who *calls* is *answered* — by a God who both forgives and holds to account (vv.6, 8).** The intercessors called and were answered; the answering God is forgiving yet an avenger of wrongdoing — the answer is not indulgence.
