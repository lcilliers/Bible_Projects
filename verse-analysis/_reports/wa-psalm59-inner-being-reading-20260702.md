## The reading

**I. "I will watch for you" — vigilant Godward expectancy (v.9).**
"O my **Strength** (H5797, M23, *oz*), I will **watch** (H8104, M30, *shamar* — keep watch, guard) for you, for you, O God, are my **fortress**" (v.9). The operation: the inner being **keeps watch *for* God** — a vigilant, expectant waiting *(stated)*. The finding is sharpened by the superscription's inversion: Saul's men *watch* (*shamar*) the house *to kill* (v.1), while the psalmist *watches* (*shamar*) *for God* to deliver — **the same watching-faculty turned Godward** *(inferred: the enemy's lethal watching and the psalmist's expectant watching are the same verb, oppositely aimed)*.

**II. "O my Strength" — the self's strength located in God (vv.9, 17).**
The refrain names God as the inner being's **own strength** — "*my* Strength" — twice (vv.9, 17) *(stated)*. The finding: the self's strength is **not intrinsic but relocated** — its *oz* is God, its fortress is God *(inferred: "*my* Strength… *my* fortress" places the self's power outside itself, in God)*. The inner being draws its stability from a source it does not generate (cf. Ps 46:1's "refuge and strength").

**III. The morning-song against the evening-prowl (vv.6, 14–16).**
Twice: "Each **evening** they come back, **howling like dogs** and prowling about the city… They **wander about** for food and **growl if they do not get their fill**" (vv.6, 14–15). Against this: "But I will **sing** of your **strength**; I will **sing aloud** (H7442, M42) of your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05) **in the morning**" (v.16). The gem-finding: two **temporal rhythms of the inner being** — the wicked's **restless, never-satisfied *evening* prowling** (they *growl* when not filled), versus the psalmist's **satisfied *morning* song** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's disposition **shows in its rhythm** — the God-strengthened self *sings at dawn* (settled, satisfied), while the God-less self *prowls at dusk* (restless, unfilled) *(inferred: the evening-howl and the morning-song mark the two inner states by their hour)*.

## What Psalm 59 says about the inner being
- **The self *watches for* God with vigilant expectancy (v.9).** The same watching-faculty the enemies aim lethally, the psalmist turns Godward — expectant waiting for the fortress.
- **The self's strength is located in God (vv.9, 17).** "O my Strength" — the inner being's power is not intrinsic but relocated; it draws stability from a source it does not generate.
- **Disposition shows in the inner being's *rhythm* (vv.6, 14–16).** The wicked prowl and growl at evening, never filled; the God-strengthened self sings at morning, satisfied. The hour reveals the state.
