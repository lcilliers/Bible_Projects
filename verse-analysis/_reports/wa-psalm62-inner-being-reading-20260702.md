- **The soul's silent, exclusive waiting — held with outpouring and non-attachment.** Psalm 62's findings: the inner being's posture is a **silent, exclusive waiting for God *alone*** — and the self **commands itself** into that silence (vv.1, 5); alongside the silence, it **pours out its heart** before God (v.8); and it **governs where the heart attaches** — "set not your heart" on riches (v.10). The through-line is an **exclusive orientation** ("God alone / he only") maintained by silence, outpouring, and non-attachment.

## The reading

**I. The soul waits in silence for God *alone* — and commands itself to (vv.1, 5).**
"For God **alone** (*ak* — only) my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) **waits in silence** (H1747, *dumiyah*)… For God alone, **O my soul, wait in silence** (H1826, M33, *damam*), for my **hope** (H8615, M18) is from him" (vv.1, 5). Two findings. First, the inner being's posture is a **silent, exclusive waiting** — the *silence* here is **positive** (unlike Ps 32's guilty silence or Ps 28's dreaded divine silence): the soul's **quiet stillness** before God *(stated)*. Second, the self-government: v.5 turns to **self-address** — "*O my soul*, wait in silence" — the inner being **commands *itself*** into the stillness *(stated: the "I" tells "my soul" to be silent, cf. Ps 42, 57)*. The operation: the soul **silences itself and waits, exclusively** ("God *alone*"), and it can *direct itself* into that posture *(inferred: the repeated *ak* fixes the object as God only, and v.5's imperative makes the silence self-willed)*.

**II. Pour out the heart — outpouring held with the silence (v.8).**
"**Trust** (H0982, M19) in him at all times, O people; **pour out your heart** (H3824, the seat — M47) before him; God is a **refuge** (H4268, M19)" (v.8). The finding: alongside the *silent waiting*, the inner being **pours itself out** before God (*shaphak*-heart, cf. Ps 42:4) *(stated)*. The operation: silence and outpouring **coexist** — the soul is *quiet* toward the world's clamour (vv.3–4) yet *empties its heart* toward God; the stillness is not emptiness but an **exclusive channelling** — all the heart's content directed to the one object *(inferred: the silent-before-all yet pour-out-to-God pairing makes the silence a *focusing*, not a muteness)*.

**III. Governing the heart's attachment (v.10).**
"Put no **trust** in extortion… if **riches** (H2428, M23) increase, **set not your heart** (H3820, M47) on them" (v.10). The finding: the inner being's heart **can attach itself to things** — and the command is **not to fix it on wealth** *(stated)*. The operation: the heart has an **attachment-faculty** that must be *governed* — "set not your heart" — kept from lodging on riches, so it stays free for its exclusive object (God alone) *(inferred: the "set not your heart on them" governs where the heart's weight settles, guarding the exclusivity of vv.1–7)*.

**IV. The split blessers and the weightless self-estimate (vv.4, 9).**
Two supporting findings. The enemies "**bless with their mouths, but inwardly they curse**" (v.4) — the concealing split, outward blessing / inward cursing (cf. Ps 28:3, 55:21) *(stated)*. And the right-sizing: "those of low estate are but a **breath**; those of high estate a **delusion**… in the balances they go up; together lighter than a breath" (v.9) — man **weighed and found weightless** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **re-sizes human importance** as *breath* (*hevel*, cf. Ps 39), which is *why* the heart is not to be set on riches or high position — they weigh nothing *(inferred: the weightlessness of v.9 grounds the non-attachment of v.10)*.

## What Psalm 62 says about the inner being
- **The soul's posture is *silent, exclusive* waiting (vv.1, 5).** A positive silence — quiet stillness before God *alone* — and the inner being can *command itself* into it ("O my soul, wait in silence").
- **Silence and outpouring coexist (v.8).** The soul is quiet toward the world's clamour yet *pours out its heart* to God — the stillness is a *focusing*, an exclusive channelling of the heart's content to one object, not a muteness.
- **The heart's *attachment* must be governed (v.10).** The heart can fix itself on things; "set not your heart on riches" keeps it free for its exclusive object. The attachment-faculty is directed, not left to settle where it will.
- **Re-sizing man as *breath* grounds the non-attachment (v.9).** Human estate weighed is weightless — which is *why* the heart is not set on it.

## Honest notes
- **Two silence words:** *dumiyah* (H1747, v.1) and *damam* (H1826, M33, v.5) — both the *positive* stillness-before-God; a clean datum distinguishing this from the guilty silence (Ps 32) and the dreaded divine silence (Ps 28). Self-commanded in v.5 (self-government, cf. Ps 42, 57).
- **"pour out your heart" (v.8)** and **"set not your heart" (v.10)** are the two heart-operations; the outpouring captured via *heart* (H3824) + gate-2 *shaphak*, the attachment via *heart* (H3820) + gate-2 *sim*. Both read from the phrases.
