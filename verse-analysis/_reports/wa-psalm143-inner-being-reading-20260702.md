# Psalm 143 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **The fainting spirit that *deliberately remembers*; the thirsting soul lifted up to be taught.** Psalm 143 (the last penitential psalm) is a lament from the depths. Its inner-being findings: the self declining to plead its **own righteousness** (v.2); the **fainting, appalled spirit that *chooses to remember and meditate*** on God's works (vv.3–5); the **soul thirsting like a parched land** (v.6); and the self **lifting itself up to be taught and led** (vv.8, 10).

## The reading

**I. The self declines to plead its own righteousness (v.2).**
"**Enter not into judgment** with your servant, for **no one living is righteous** before you" (v.2). The finding: the self asks *not* to be judged on its merits, confessing that *no one living* is righteous before God *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **renounces self-justification** — it does not come pleading its own uprightness but throws itself on mercy, owning that it could not stand under strict judgment *(inferred: "enter not into judgment… no one living is righteous before you" is the self *declining to claim righteousness* — the honest ground of appeal is mercy, not merit; cf. Ps 130:3, Rom 3:20)*.

**II. The fainting spirit that deliberately remembers (vv.3–5).**
"The enemy has… made me sit in darkness… Therefore my **spirit** (H7307, M47) **faints** within me; my **heart** (H3820, M47) within me is **appalled**. **I remember** (H2142, M41) the days of old; **I meditate** on all that you have done; **I ponder** the work of your hands" (vv.3–5). The gem-finding: with spirit *fainting* and heart *appalled*, the self makes a **deliberate turn to *remembering, meditating, pondering*** God's past works *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being in darkness **actively rehearses God's deeds as the *way through* despair** — it does not wait for the faintness to lift but *chooses* to remember, meditate, ponder; recollection of what God has done is the *counter-move* to the appalled heart *(inferred: the pivot "*therefore* my spirit faints… *I remember… I meditate… I ponder*" makes memory a *deliberate discipline against despair* — the self steadies itself by rehearsing God's works when its own spirit is failing; the willed remembering of 77:11, the counter to 143:4's faintness; cf. Ps 42:6)*.

**III. The soul thirsting like a parched land (v.6).**
"I **stretch out my hands** to you; my **soul** (H5315, M47) **thirsts** for you like a **parched land**" (v.6). The gem-finding: the soul's longing for God is a *thirst* — dry ground craving rain *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **aches for God with the intensity of *drought*** — the parched-land image makes the desire a matter of *survival*, the soul cracked and dry, reaching (hands stretched) for the only water *(inferred: "my soul thirsts for you like a parched land" figures the longing as *life-and-death thirst* — the self desperate for God as dry ground for rain; cf. Ps 42:1–2, 63:1 "my soul thirsts for you… in a dry and weary land")*.

**IV. Lifting up the soul to be taught and led (vv.8, 10).**
"Let me hear in the morning of your steadfast love, for in you I **trust** (H0982, M19). Make me **know the way** I should go, for **to you I lift up my soul**… **Teach me** to do your will, for you are my God! Let your good Spirit **lead me** on level ground!" (vv.8, 10). The gem-finding: the self **lifts up its soul** to God, *trusting*, and asks to be **taught and led** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **offers itself up to God for *direction*** — "to you I lift up my soul" is a self-*presenting*, and the petitions ("make me know the way… teach me… lead me") ask God to *guide the self's steps and will*; the soul raised toward God is a soul asking to be *taught* *(inferred: "to you I lift up my soul… teach me to do your will… lead me" makes the lifted soul an act of *submission-for-guidance* — the self hands itself over to be directed, not just delivered; the desire is to be *taught God's will*, cf. Ps 25:1,4–5, 25:4 "make me know your ways")*.

## What Psalm 143 says about the inner being
- **The self *declines to plead its own righteousness* (v.2).** It throws itself on mercy, owning that no one living could stand under strict judgment — self-justification renounced.
- **The fainting spirit *deliberately remembers* (vv.3–5).** In darkness the self chooses to remember, meditate, and ponder God's past works — recollection as a willed discipline against despair, the counter-move to the appalled heart.
- **The soul *thirsts like a parched land* (v.6).** Longing for God as a matter of survival — dry, cracked ground reaching for the only water; desire at the intensity of drought.
- **The self *lifts itself up to be taught and led* (vv.8, 10).** "To you I lift up my soul… teach me to do your will… lead me" — a self-presenting for *direction*, handing itself over to be guided, not merely delivered.

## Honest notes
- **A rich lament-anatomy:** mercy-not-merit (v.2), *deliberate remembering* against despair (v.5, prime datum), *thirst* for God (v.6), and the *lifted-up soul asking to be taught* (vv.8, 10). Strong inner-being data on praying from the depths — the memory-as-discipline (v.5) especially valuable (with 77:11, 42:6).
- **Scope honesty:** the enemy's pursuit and the prayed judgment on foes (vv.3, 11–12) are the arena of the distress; the inner-being weight is the confession (v.2), the remembering (vv.3–5), the thirst (v.6), and the taught/led self (vv.8, 10).
