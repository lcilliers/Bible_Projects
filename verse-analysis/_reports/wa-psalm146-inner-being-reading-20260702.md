## The reading

**I. Trust redirected from mortal man to the enduring God (vv.3–5).**
"**Put not your trust in princes**, in a son of man, in whom there is **no salvation**. When his **breath departs**, he returns to the earth; on that very day his **plans perish**. **Blessed is he whose help is the God of Jacob, whose hope is in the LORD** his God" (vv.3–5). The gem-finding: the self is directed **not to trust mortal man** — because he *dies* and his plans *perish with him* — but to place its **hope in the LORD** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **redirects its trust by *reasoning from mortality*** — it *weighs* where to lean, sees that the strongest human helper is *breath that departs*, and so *relocates* its hope in the God who endures; trust as a *reasoned choice* informed by the transience of every human refuge *(inferred: "put not your trust in princes… his plans perish… blessed is he whose hope is in the LORD" is the self *choosing* its ground by reckoning that man is mortal and God is not — the deliberate redirection of 118:8–9, here argued from death; misplaced trust exposed by the grave, right trust anchored in the enduring God; cf. Ps 118:8, 62:9, 115:9–11)*.

## What Psalm 146 says about the inner being
