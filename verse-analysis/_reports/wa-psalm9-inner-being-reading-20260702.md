# Psalm 9 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 9:1–20 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter (v.1 backfilled). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **The first acrostic** (aleph-bet; continues into Psalm 10). Its own key is **memory and judgment**: it reads the inner being through *who is remembered and who is forgotten* — God holds the afflicted in mind while the wicked's very name is erased.

## The reading
Psalm 9 is a thanksgiving, but its deep subject is **memory** — divine and human — running in both directions.

**I. Praise as the whole heart's act of remembering (vv.1–2).**
"I will give **thanks** (H3034, M22) to the LORD with my **whole heart** (H3820, the seat — M47); I will **recount** all your wonderful deeds. I will be **glad** (H8055, M04) and exult; I will **sing praise** (H2167, M42)" (vv.1–2). Praise here is an act of **remembering back** — the inner being *recounts* God's wonders, and the whole heart, not a part, is engaged *(inferred: 'recount' makes praise a work of memory)*.

**II. The blotting-out of the wicked's memory (vv.3–6).**
God has overthrown the enemy: "you have rebuked the nations; you have made the **wicked** (H7563, M10) **perish**; you have blotted out their **name** forever and ever… the very memory of them has perished" (vv.5–6). The judgment on evil is described as **erasure from memory** — not merely defeat but *being forgotten* *(stated: their name blotted, their memory perished)*.

**III. Justice enthroned; a stronghold for the crushed (vv.4, 7–9).**
Against that, God "sits enthroned forever… he **judges** (H8199, M26) the world with **righteousness** (H6664)… in **uprightness** (H4339)" (vv.7–8), and becomes "a **stronghold** for the **oppressed**, a stronghold in times of **trouble** (H6869, M24)" (v.9). The inner being of the crushed finds its safety in a **judging God who is also a refuge** *(stated)*.

**IV. Trust that knows the Name (v.10).**
The pivot: "those who **know** your **name** put their **trust** (H0982, M19) in you, for you have not forsaken those who **seek** (H1875, M41) you" (v.10). Trust here is **not blind** — it rests on *knowing the name*, i.e. God's proven character as just and remembering *(inferred: knowing → trusting → not forsaken)*.

**V. God does not forget the afflicted (vv.12, 18).**
The counter-memory to v.6: "he who avenges blood is **mindful** (H2142, M41 — *zakar*) of them; he does not **forget** the **cry** of the **afflicted** (H6035, M24)" (v.12); and "the needy shall not always be forgotten, and the **hope** (H8615, M18) of the **poor** shall not perish forever" (v.18). Where the wicked's memory is *erased*, the afflicted are *held in mind* — and their proper inner posture is **hope** that waits on that remembering *(stated: the hope of the poor)*.

**VI. Evil snared in its own work; mortals reminded they are mortal (vv.15–20).**
The wicked "are snared in the **work of their own hands**" (v.16) — the moral boomerang of Psalm 7, now stated as a principle of divine justice. And the close: "Arise, O LORD! Let not man **prevail** (H5810, M23)… let the nations **know** that they are but **men** (enosh)" (vv.19–20) — the antidote to human self-exaltation is to be **rightly sized before God** *(inferred: 'but men' answers the nations who forget God, v.17)*.

## What Psalm 9 says about the inner being
- **To be remembered by God is salvation; to be forgotten is judgment.** The psalm's spine is memory: the wicked's name is *blotted out* (v.6), the afflicted are *not forgotten* (vv.12, 18), and human praise *recounts* God's deeds (v.1). The inner being's security is being **held in God's memory**.
- **Trust is grounded in knowing the Name** (v.10): not a leap in the dark but confidence resting on God's proven, remembered character. Knowing precedes and funds trusting.
- **Hope is the inner posture of the oppressed** (v.18): those who cannot secure justice themselves *wait* on a God who does not forget — hope as endurance under delay.
- **Right self-sizing** (v.20): the nations that forget God must learn "they are but men" — the inner being goes wrong when it forgets both God and its own mortality (the corrective echo of Psalm 8's "what is man").
