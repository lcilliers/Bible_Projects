# Psalm 13 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 13:1–6 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter (v.1 backfilled). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **The whole lament in miniature** — six verses that carry the inner being from the churning "how long?" of felt abandonment to a settled "but I have trusted," with **nothing in the circumstances changed between them**.

## The reading
Psalm 13 is the shortest complete arc of the inner life in the Psalter: complaint, petition, trust — and its gem is the *hinge* between the last two.

**I. The fourfold "how long?" (vv.1–2).**
The complaint is measured out in four beats, and the repetition *is* the anguish: "How long, O LORD? Will you **forget** me forever? How long will you **hide** your **face** from me? How long must I **take counsel** (H6098, M17) in my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) and have **sorrow** (H3015, M03) in my **heart** (H3824, M47) all the day? How long shall my **enemy** be exalted over me?" (vv.1–2). The crisis is not the trouble but its **duration** — the sense that God's silence is *permanent* ("forget me *forever*") *(inferred: 'how long' × 4 makes time itself the wound)*. And its inner signature is precise: **taking counsel in the soul** — the mind endlessly deliberating with itself, turning the problem over, while sorrow grinds "all the day." This is the loop of anxiety: self-consultation that never resolves because the One who could answer seems absent *(stated: I take counsel in my soul)*.

**II. The petition — light up my eyes (vv.3–4).**
"Consider and answer me, O LORD my God; **light up my eyes**, lest I sleep the sleep of death" (v.3). The plea is for **rekindled vitality** — dimming eyes are fading life; the inner being asks God to relight it before it slips into death's sleep *(inferred: the eyes' light = life restored)*. The stakes are the enemy's triumph: "lest my enemy say, 'I have prevailed,'… lest my foes **rejoice** (H1523, M04) because I am shaken" (v.4).

**III. The turn — "but I have trusted" (vv.5–6).**
Then, without any change in the situation, the pivot: "**But I have trusted** (H0982, M19) in your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05); my **heart** (H3820, M47) shall **rejoice** (H1523, M04) in your salvation. I will **sing** to the LORD, because he has dealt bountifully with me" (vv.5–6). The same word *rejoice* that the enemy hoped to do over the psalmist's fall (v.4) is now what the psalmist's own **heart** will do in God's salvation — the joy reassigned. And the tenses matter: trust is **past** ("I *have* trusted") — an orientation already established — while the rejoicing is **future** ("*shall* rejoice") — anticipated on the strength of that trust *(inferred: settled trust funds unseen joy)*.

## What Psalm 13 says about the inner being
- **Prolonged waiting is the sharpest trial** — worse than the trouble is its *length*. The fourfold "how long?" shows the inner being crushed not by a blow but by a *silence that feels endless* ("forget me forever," v.1).
- **Anxiety is the soul taking counsel with itself** (v.2): when God seems absent, the inner being falls into a loop of self-deliberation and daily sorrow — the mind consulting itself because the One who could answer has gone quiet.
- **The inner being can pivot from despair to joy without any change in circumstances** (vv.4→5): the hinge is not relief but **remembered steadfast love**. Trust already held ("I *have* trusted") reasserts itself, and on its ground the heart *anticipates* a joy it does not yet see.
- **Vitality and God's regard are linked** (v.3): "light up my eyes" — the inner being's very life dims under the sense of abandonment and is rekindled by God's turning toward it.
