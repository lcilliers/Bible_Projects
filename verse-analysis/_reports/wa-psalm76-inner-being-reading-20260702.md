## The reading

**I. Awed stillness — the self unable to stand (vv.7–8).**
"But you, **you are to be feared**! **Who can stand** before you when once your **anger** is roused? From the heavens you uttered judgment; the earth **feared and was still**" (vv.7–8). The finding: the fitting response to God's overwhelming power is **fear**, and before it the self **cannot stand** — "who can stand before you?" *(stated)*. The operation: God's roused judgment reduces the inner being (and the whole earth) to **fearful *stillness*** — "the earth feared and *was still*", fell silent *(stated)*. The awe is not noise but **a silenced hush** — the self stopped, unable to remain standing or speaking before the majesty *(inferred: "feared and was still" pairs the fear with a falling-silent; cf. Ps 46:10's "be still and know", here the involuntary stillness of awe)*.

**II. Man's wrath turned to God's praise (v.10).**
"Surely the **wrath of man** shall **praise** you; the remnant of wrath you will **put on like a belt**" (v.10). The gem-finding: even the inner being's **hostile passion — the *wrath of man*** — ends up **serving God's praise** *(stated)*. The operation: human wrath, the very rage that *opposes* God, is **overruled and co-opted to his glory** — and its *remnant* is **restrained** (girded on like a belt) *(inferred: "the wrath of man shall praise you… the remnant you will put on" makes the opposing passion an occasion for God's praise, its excess bound)*. The finding: the rebellious inner being's rage **does not thwart God but is turned to serve him** *(inferred)*.

## What Psalm 76 says about the inner being
- **God's roused power reduces the self to *fearful stillness* (vv.7–8).** The fitting response is fear; before God's judgment the inner being cannot *stand* — "the earth feared and was still". Awe is a silenced hush, the self stopped before the majesty.
- **Man's wrath is turned to God's *praise* (v.10).** Even the inner being's hostile passion — the wrath that opposes God — is overruled and co-opted to his glory, its remnant restrained. Rebellious rage does not thwart God but ends up serving him.
