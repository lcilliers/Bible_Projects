# Psalm 5 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 5:1–12 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter (no backfill). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **A morning prayer** (the daylight counterpart to the evening/sleep psalms 3–4). Multi-characteristic: it reads the inner being through the **moral incompatibility of God with evil**, the **inner corruption of the deceiver**, and the **refuge-joy of the one covered by favour**.

## The reading
Psalm 5 opens the day by asking to be *heard*, and reads the inner being against the character of the God who hears.

**I. Morning appeal — listening asked of God, watching practised by the psalmist (vv.1–3).**
A triple plea to be attended to: "**give ear** (H0238, M41), **consider** (H0995, M15) my **groaning** (H1901), **give attention** (H7181, M41)… **hear** (H8085, M41) my voice" (vv.1–3). And the inner posture is **expectant watching**: "in the morning I prepare a sacrifice for you and **watch**" (v.3) — prayer as a dawn discipline that then *waits* for answer *(inferred: 'watch' = looking out for the reply)*.

**II. The God who does not delight in evil (vv.4–6).**
The ground of the prayer is God's moral character, stated by what his inner being will *not* hold: "you are not a God who **delights** (H2655, M04) in wickedness; evil may not dwell with you… you **hate** (H8130, M06) all **evildoers**… the LORD **abhors** (H8581, M06) the bloodthirsty and **deceitful** (H4820, M14) man" (vv.4–6). The inner being of God is drawn by its **antipathies** — delight withheld, hatred and abhorrence directed at cruelty and lies *(stated)*.

**III. Entering by steadfast love, in fear (v.7).**
Over against the evildoers who cannot stand, the psalmist enters: "through the abundance of your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05 — chesed) I will enter your house; I will bow down… in the **fear** (H3374, M01) of you." Access is by **God's love, received**, and the answering inner posture is **reverent fear** — awe, not terror *(inferred: fear here is worshipful, paired with entering God's house)*.

**IV. The deceiver's inner corruption (vv.8–9).**
Asking to be led straight "because of my enemies" (v.8), the psalmist diagnoses their inner state through their speech-organs: "there is no **truth** in their **mouth**; their inmost self is **destruction** (H1942, M28); their **throat** is an open grave; they **flatter** with their **tongue**" (v.9). The inner being's ruin is **read off the words it produces** — the mouth/throat/tongue as the outlet of an inward *destruction* *(inferred: the organs image the inner corruption)*.

**V. The refuge-joy of the covered (vv.10–12).**
After petition that the guilty **bear their guilt** (H0816, M10) for their **transgressions** (H6588) and rebellion (v.10), the psalm turns to its close: "let all who **take refuge** (H2620, M19) in you **rejoice** (H8055, M04)… **sing for joy** (H7442)… for you **bless** (H1288, M39) the **righteous** (H6662, M26); you cover him with **favour** (H7522, M29) as with a shield" (vv.11–12). The settled inner good is again **refuge → joy**, now crowned with being **covered by God's favour** as by a shield *(stated)*.

## What Psalm 5 says about the inner being
- The inner being is read **against the character of God** — a God whose own inner life is defined by what it will *not* delight in (evil) and what it *hates* (cruelty, deceit); the human inner being is measured by whether it is at home with that God or excluded from him.
- **Inner corruption is legible in speech** (v.9): the deceiver's throat/tongue/mouth expose an inward *destruction* — words are the window on the inner being.
- Access to God is **by his steadfast love received**, answered by **reverent fear** (v.7) — awe, not cringing.
- The recurring resolution holds and deepens: **refuge → joy** (as in Ps 2, 3, 4) now becomes **being covered with favour as with a shield** (v.12) — the kept inner being.
