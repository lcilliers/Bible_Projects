# Psalm 10 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 10:1–18 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter (v.1 backfilled). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **The completion of Psalm 9's acrostic**, but in a wholly different register: where 9 celebrated the remembering Judge, **10 stares into the interior of the wicked** — and its gem is that it *quotes his heart*. The root of oppression turns out to be a **theology the heart tells itself**.

## The reading
Psalm 10 opens on God's felt absence and then does something rare: it lets us hear the oppressor think.

**I. The hidden God (v.1).**
"Why, O LORD, do you stand far away? Why do you **hide** (H5956) yourself in **times of trouble** (H6869, M24)?" The inner being's first cry is not about the enemy but about God's apparent **absence** — the sense that God has withdrawn just when he is needed *(stated)*.

**II. The wicked's heart, quoted three times (vv.2–11).**
This is the psalm's signature. The oppressor's inner life is laid open through his own **heart-speech**:
- His engine is appetite: "the wicked **boasts** (H1984, M08) of the **desires** (H8378, M28) of his **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47); greedy for gain, he **renounces** (H5006, M06) the LORD" (v.3) — craving that **spurns God** *(stated)*.
- His thinking is practical atheism: "in the **pride** (H1363, M08) of his face the wicked does not **seek** him; all his **thoughts** are, 'There is **no God**'" (v.4).
- His confidence is self-assured permanence: "he **says in his heart** (H3820, the seat — M47), 'I shall not be moved; throughout all generations I shall not meet adversity'" (v.6).
- And the root lie: "he **says in his heart**, 'God has **forgotten**, he has **hidden** his face, he will **never see** it'" (v.11).
Between these sayings he is drawn as a predator — lurking "like a **lion** in his thicket," ambushing, netting, seizing the **poor** and **helpless** (vv.8–10). The inner structure of oppression is a **conviction that God does not see** — practical atheism as the permission for cruelty *(inferred: the heart-sayings are the *cause* of the predation between them)*.

**III. The hinge — "But you do see" (vv.12–14).**
The rebuttal is aimed exactly at the wicked's core lie: "Arise, O LORD… do not **forget** the **afflicted** (H6035, M24). Why does the wicked… say… 'You will not call to account'? **But you do see** — for you note **mischief** (H5999, M03) and **vexation** (H3708, M02), that you may take it into your hands" (vv.13–14). The whole psalm turns on **God's seeing**: the oppressor bets God is blind; the psalmist knows he is not *(stated: but you do see)*.

**IV. God hears desire and strengthens the heart (vv.16–18).**
The close answers the wicked's craving with a different desire: "O LORD, you **hear** (H8085, M41) the **desire** (H8378, M28) of the **afflicted**; you will **strengthen their heart** (H3820, M47); you will **incline** your ear to do **justice** (H8199, M26) to the fatherless and the oppressed" (vv.17–18). The wicked strengthens *himself* on his soul's desire (v.3); the afflicted are **strengthened by God** when he hears *their* desire — the same word *(ta'avah)*, opposite outcomes *(inferred: the two desires frame the psalm)*.

## What Psalm 10 says about the inner being
- **The root of wickedness is a theology held in the heart.** The psalm's three heart-sayings (vv.4, 6, 11) locate evil not first in the deed but in what the inner being *tells itself about God*: "there is no God," "I shall not be moved," "he will never see." Practical atheism is the inner permission slip for cruelty.
- **The decisive question is whether God sees.** The oppressor's whole structure rests on believing God is blind and forgetful; the psalm's answer is not first "God will punish" but "**you do see**" (v.14). God's attentiveness is the hinge of the inner being's hope.
- **Two desires, two ends** (vv.3, 17): the same craving-word marks the wicked's God-renouncing appetite and the afflicted's God-heard longing. Desire itself is neutral; its object and outcome divide the two.
- **The afflicted's heart is strengthened from outside** (v.17): where the wicked is self-assured, the oppressed do not steady themselves — God strengthens their heart when he hears them.
