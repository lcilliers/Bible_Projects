## The reading

**I. The fatigue of prolonged waiting (vv.1–3).**
"Save me… the waters have come up to my **neck** (H5315, *nephesh* — throat/soul, seat=soul)… I am **weary** (H3021, *yaga*) with my crying out; my throat is parched. My **eyes grow dim** with **waiting** (H3176, M18, *yachal*) for my God" (vv.1–3). The finding: the inner being is **exhausted by the *length* of the waiting** — "my eyes grow dim with waiting" *(stated)*. The operation: the waiting itself **wears the self out** — the prolonged, unanswered cry *fatigues* the inner being (weary, parched, eyes failing) *(inferred: the eyes "grow dim *with waiting*" — the fatigue is the cost of protracted hope, not of the danger alone)*. The self is worn down not only by the flood but by **how long** it must wait for God *(inferred)*.

**II. Zeal that *consumes* and *isolates* (vv.8–9).**
"I have become a **stranger** (H2114, *zur*) to my brothers, an alien to my mother's sons. For **zeal** (H7068, M02, *qinah*) for your house has **consumed** (H0398, *akal* — eaten) me" (vv.8–9). The gem-finding: **zeal *eats* the inner being** — *qinah* (an intense heat, in the M02 passion cluster) has **consumed/devoured** the self *(stated)*. The operation: zeal is a **consuming inner fire** — a passion so intense it *eats up* the one who holds it *(inferred: akal makes zeal a devouring, the self burned through by its own intensity)*. And it has a **cost: isolation** — the zeal-consumed self becomes "a *stranger* to his brothers" *(stated)*: the very passion for God **alienates the inner being from its own kin** *(inferred: the "stranger to my brothers… for zeal has consumed me" links the alienation to the zeal — the fire isolates)*.

**III. The heart broken by reproach — despair with no comforter (v.20).**
"**Reproaches** (H2781, M06, *cherpah*) have **broken** (H7665, *shabar*) my **heart** (H3820, the seat — M47), so that I am in **despair** (H5136, M24). I looked for **pity**, but there was none, and for **comforters**, but I found none" (v.20). The gem-finding: the heart **broken by reproach → despair**, and — the aggravation — the search for **pity and a comforter that *fails*** *(stated)*. The operation: the wound is **doubled** — the reproach breaks the heart, *and* the absence of any comforter compounds it *(inferred)*. The finding is the **wound of un-comforted suffering**: the inner being, already broken, **looks outward for pity and finds none** — the loneliness of the unshared break is itself part of the injury *(inferred: "broken my heart… I looked for comforters but found none" makes the missing comforter a second wound on the first)*.

**IV. The revived heart of the humble (vv.29–32).**
"But I am **afflicted** and in pain… I will **praise** the name of God with a song… When the **humble** see it they will be glad; you who **seek** God, **let your hearts revive** (H2421, *chayah* — live)" (vv.29–32). The finding: against the *broken* heart of v.20, the seeking/humble inner being's heart is **revived** — brought back to life *(stated)*. The operation: the heart can **move from broken to revived** — the same organ shattered by reproach (v.20) is *made to live again* in the seeking humble (v.32) *(inferred: "broken my heart" v.20 and "let your hearts revive" v.32 are the two poles — the light-rekindled/heart-revived thread, cf. Ps 22:26)*.

## What Psalm 69 says about the inner being
- **Prolonged waiting *fatigues* the inner being (vv.1–3).** The self is worn out not only by the flood but by *how long* it must wait — "eyes grow dim with waiting." The cost of protracted, unanswered hope is exhaustion of the very faculties of seeing and crying.
- **Zeal can *consume* and *isolate* the self (vv.8–9).** *Qinah* eats the inner being — a passion so intense it devours the one who holds it — and it alienates: the very zeal for God makes the self a stranger to its own kin.
- **The broken heart is doubled by the absent comforter (v.20).** Reproach breaks the heart into despair; the search for pity and a comforter that *fails* is a second wound — the loneliness of un-comforted suffering.
- **The heart can move from *broken* to *revived* (vv.20, 32).** The organ shattered by reproach is made to live again in the seeking humble — broken-heart and revived-heart are the two poles.
