# Psalm 15 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 15:1–5 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter. Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **An entrance liturgy.** It asks who may dwell with God and answers with a **portrait of integrity** — its own key is that the qualification is not ritual but a whole inner being that is *true all the way through*, and therefore **unmovable**.

## The reading
Psalm 15 poses a question at the sanctuary gate and answers it by describing a *person*, not a rite.

**I. The question (v.1).**
"O LORD, who shall **sojourn** in your **tent**? Who shall **dwell** on your **holy** hill?" (v.1). The issue is who may live in God's presence — and the answer that follows is entirely about **character** *(inferred: no sacrifice is named; the reply is moral)*.

**II. Truth in the heart (v.2).**
The foundational qualification: "he who **walks blamelessly** (H8549, M12) and does what is **right** (H6664, M26) and **speaks truth** (H0571, M13) **in his heart** (H3824, the seat — M47)." The deepest mark is not honest *speech* but a **heart that is true** — inner truthfulness, so that the words merely follow what the heart already is *(inferred: 'truth in the heart' locates integrity below the tongue)*. It is the exact opposite of Psalm 12's *double heart*.

**III. Integrity toward the neighbour, and rightly ordered esteem (vv.3–4a).**
Outward, it shows as restraint: "who does not slander with his **tongue** and does no **evil** (H7451, M27) to his neighbour, nor takes up a **reproach** (H2781, M06) against his friend" (v.3). And it shows as **rightly ordered love**: "in whose eyes a vile person is despised, but who **honours** those who **fear** (H3373, M01) the LORD" (v.4a). What the inner being *esteems* reveals it — it prizes the God-fearer, not the powerful *(stated: honours those who fear the LORD)*.

**IV. The oath kept to one's own hurt (v.4b).**
The most searching mark: "who **swears to his own hurt** and does not **change** (H4171)." Integrity here is tested by **self-interest and holds** — the will that binds itself with a promise and refuses to wriggle free even when the promise proves *costly* *(stated: to his own hurt, and does not change)*. This is the hinge: integrity is proven precisely where keeping faith *costs* something.

**V. Clean hands with money (v.5a), and the reward — immovability (v.5b).**
"who does not put out his **money** at interest and does not take a **bribe** against the **innocent** (H5355, M12)" (v.5a). And the crown: "He who does these things shall **never be moved**" (v.5b). The integrated inner being is **stable** — unshakeable *(stated)*.

## What Psalm 15 says about the inner being
- **The qualification for God's presence is an integrated inner being, not a ritual.** The dweller-with-God is described entirely by character — heart, word, esteem, promises, money — a life that is *true all the way through*.
- **Integrity begins as truth in the heart** (v.2): honest speech is the fruit; the root is a heart honest with itself. This is the antidote to the *double heart* of Psalm 12 — singleness where that was division.
- **Integrity is proven where it costs** (v.4): "swears to his own hurt and does not change" — the decisive mark is a self-binding will that holds faith *against its own advantage*. Cheap consistency proves nothing; costly fidelity proves the inner being.
- **What the inner being honours reveals it** (v.4a): esteeming the God-fearer over the vile is a disclosure of the heart's true loyalties.
- **Integrity produces stability** (v.5): the truthful, promise-keeping inner being "shall never be moved." Where the wicked only *claimed* "I shall not be moved" (Ps 10:6) on the ground of self-assertion, here immovability is the real fruit of a life aligned with God.
