- **The cry from the depths; forgiveness that *makes God feared*; the soul waiting more than watchmen for the morning. A cornerstone.** Psalm 130 (De Profundis) is one of the Psalter's great inner-being texts. Its findings: the **cry from the lowest place** (vv.1–2); the profound insight that **forgiveness — not judgment — is what makes God *feared*** (vv.3–4); and the **whole soul *waiting and hoping*, straining toward God like a watchman for dawn** (vv.5–6).

## The reading

**I. The cry from the depths (vv.1–2).**
"**Out of the depths** I cry to you, O LORD! O LORD, **hear my voice**! Let your ears be **attentive** to the voice of my **pleas for mercy**!" (vv.1–2). The gem-finding: the self cries **from *the depths*** — the lowest place, drowning — and still reaches upward for a hearing *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being at its **absolute nadir *still cries*** — "the depths" (deep waters, the place of sinking) is not too low for the cry to rise; from the very bottom the self flings its voice up for mercy *(inferred: "out of the depths I cry… let your ears be attentive" makes even the lowest point a place from which prayer ascends — the self is submerged but not silenced; the depths are the *starting-place*, not the end; cf. Ps 69:1–2 "the waters have come up to my neck", Jonah 2:2 "out of the belly of Sheol")*.

**II. Forgiveness that *makes God feared* (vv.3–4).**
"If you, O LORD, should **mark iniquities**, O LORD, **who could stand?** But **with you there is forgiveness**, that you may be **feared** (H3372, M01)" (vv.3–4). The gem-finding — one of the most searching in the Psalter: the self first sees that *no one could stand* under a God who kept accounts — and then grasps that **forgiveness is precisely what makes God *feared*** *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being discovers that ***grace*, not judgment, generates reverent awe** — "there is forgiveness, *that you may be feared*"; a God who merely condemned would be *fled from*, but a God who *forgives* is *revered*; mercy does not cheapen awe but *creates* it, binding the pardoned self to God in grateful fear *(inferred: "with you there is forgiveness, that you may be feared" inverts the natural expectation — forgiveness might seem to *reduce* fear, but the psalm says it *produces* it; the self that has been spared what it could not survive is drawn into a deeper, love-shot reverence; the fear of the LORD here is the awe of the *forgiven*, cf. Ps 130:7, 1 Kgs 8:39–40, Rom 2:4 "God's kindness leads to repentance")*.

**III. The whole soul waiting more than watchmen for the morning (vv.5–6).**
"**I wait** (H6960, M18) for the LORD, **my soul** (H5315, M47) **waits**, and in his **word** I **hope**; my soul waits for the LORD **more than watchmen for the morning**, more than watchmen for the morning" (vv.5–6). The gem-finding: the **whole soul *waits and hopes*** — grounded in God's word — with a longing **more intense than a night-watchman's for dawn**, the phrase *repeated* to press the ache *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **strains toward God in *expectant, hoping waiting*** — not passive delay but the taut, watchful longing of one who scans the dark for the first light, *sure it will come*; the self's whole weight leaned forward on God's word, waiting *(inferred: "my soul waits… more than watchmen for the morning, more than watchmen for the morning" fuses certainty and yearning — the watchman *knows* dawn comes yet *aches* for it; the doubled line enacts the straining; the waiting is anchored "in his word" (hope with a ground) and involves the *whole soul*, not a corner of it; cf. Ps 27:14, 40:1, 62:5 "for God alone, O my soul, wait in silence")*.

## What Psalm 130 says about the inner being
- **The self cries *from the depths* (vv.1–2).** No place is too low for prayer to rise — submerged but not silenced, the self flings its voice up from the very bottom; the depths are the *starting-place* of the cry, not its end.
- **Forgiveness *makes God feared* (vv.3–4).** The searching inversion: grace, not judgment, generates reverent awe. A God who only condemned would be fled; a God who *forgives* is *revered* — the fear of the LORD here is the awe of the *forgiven*, bound to God in grateful reverence.
- **The whole soul *waits and hopes*, straining for God (vv.5–6).** Expectant, watchful longing — "more than watchmen for the morning" — certainty and yearning fused, the self's whole weight leaned forward on God's word; waiting that *knows* the dawn comes yet aches for it.

## Honest notes
- **A cornerstone triad:** the *cry from the depths* (the lowest place still prays), *forgiveness generating fear* (grace produces awe — a profound anatomy of the forgiven heart), and the *watchman-waiting soul* (expectant hope with a ground). All three are prime inner-being data; v.4 in particular is a rare and deep insight into how *mercy shapes reverence* — foregrounded.
- **The waiting/hope thread** (vv.5–6) is a capstone of the Psalter's wait-on-the-LORD motif (with 27:14, 40:1, 62:5, 33:20) — here at its most intense (doubled watchman-line) and most grounded ("in his word I hope").
