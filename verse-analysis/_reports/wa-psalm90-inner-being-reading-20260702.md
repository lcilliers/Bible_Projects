## The reading

**I. Secret sins in the light of God's presence (v.8).**
"You have set our **iniquities** (H5771, M10) before you, our **secret sins** (H5956, M14, *alum* — the *concealed*) in the **light** of your **presence** (H6440)" (v.8). The finding: the inner being's **hidden, concealed sins are fully exposed** in God's presence-light — nothing is out of view *(stated)*. The operation: the *concealed* self (the *alum*, the same root as the dissemblers of Ps 26:4) is **illuminated by God's presence** — what the inner being hides even from itself stands in the light *(inferred: "secret/concealed sins in the light of your presence" makes the hidden fully seen; cf. Ps 19:12's hidden faults, 44:21's secrets of the heart)*.

**II. The life as a *sigh* (vv.9–10).**
"For all our days pass away under your wrath; we **bring our years to an end like a sigh** (H1899, *hegeh* — a murmur/moan)… their span is but toil and trouble; they are soon gone, and **we fly away**" (vv.9–10). The finding: the inner being's whole life is figured as a single **breathed-out sigh** — brief, exhaled, gone *(stated)*. The operation: the self grasps its life as **momentary and toilsome** — a sigh, soon flown *(inferred: "years to an end like a sigh… soon gone… fly away" compresses the whole life to a breath; cf. Ps 39:5's breath, 144:4)*.

**III. Numbering the days → a heart of wisdom (v.12).**
"So **teach us to number** (H4487, *manah* — count) our **days** that we may **get a heart of wisdom** (H3824, the seat — M47 + H2451, *chokmah*)" (v.12). The gem-finding: the inner being asks to **learn to *count* its days** — to grasp its brevity — **so that it may gain a heart of wisdom** *(stated)*. The operation: **wisdom is the *fruit* of rightly grasping finitude** — the inner being that *numbers its days* (feels how few they are) becomes *wise* *(inferred: "number our days *that we may get* a heart of wisdom" makes the counting the means, the wise heart the end; cf. Ps 39's finitude-grasped)*. And crucially, **God must teach the counting** — the self cannot rightly number its own days without instruction; the grasp of finitude is a God-given lesson, not a self-achievement *(inferred: "*teach us* to number" hands the counting to God)*.

**IV. Morning satisfaction against the brief life (v.14).**
"**Satisfy** us in the **morning** with your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05), that we may **rejoice** (H7442, M42) and be **glad** (H8055, M04) **all our days**" (v.14). The gem-finding: against the brief, toilsome life (vv.9–10), the inner being pleads to be **satisfied with God's *chesed*** — a satisfaction that makes the *few* days **glad** *(stated)*. The operation: the short span is **redeemed into gladness** not by lengthening it but by **filling it with God's steadfast love** — "satisfy us… that we may be glad *all our days*" *(inferred: the plea does not ask for more days but for the days to be *satisfied with chesed*, turning toil to gladness)*.

## What Psalm 90 says about the inner being
- **The concealed self is exposed in God's light (v.8).** The inner being's secret, hidden sins stand fully in the light of God's presence — what it conceals even from itself is seen.
- **The whole life is a *sigh* (vv.9–10).** The inner being grasps its span as a single breathed-out sigh — brief, toilsome, soon flown.
- **Wisdom is the fruit of *numbering the days* (v.12).** Rightly grasping one's brevity yields a *heart of wisdom* — and the counting must be *taught by God*; the grasp of finitude is a God-given lesson, not a self-achievement.
- **The short life is redeemed by *morning satisfaction* in God's love (v.14).** Not by more days, but by being *satisfied with chesed* — the plea turns the few, toilsome days to gladness "all our days."
