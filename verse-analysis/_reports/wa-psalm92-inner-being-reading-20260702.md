## The reading

**I. Gladness from contemplating God's works (vv.4–5).**
"For you, O LORD, have made me **glad** (H8055, M04) by your **work**; at the works of your hands I **sing for joy** (H7442, M42). How great are your works, O LORD! Your **thoughts** are very **deep**!" (vv.4–5). The finding: the inner being's gladness **arises from *beholding* what God has done** — the works and thoughts of God *(stated)*. The operation: **contemplation of God's works → joy** — the self is *made glad* by attending to God's deeds, and drawn to the recognition that God's thoughts are **unfathomably deep** *(inferred: "you have made me glad by your work… your thoughts are very deep" makes the joy a fruit of contemplating God's works, and opens onto their unsearchable depth; cf. Ps 143:5)*.

**II. The God-planted self flourishing into old age (vv.12–14).**
"The **righteous** (H6662, M26) **flourish** (H6524, *parach*) like the **palm tree**… **planted** in the house of the LORD; they flourish in the courts of our God. They still **bear fruit** (H5107) in **old age**; they are ever full of **sap** and **green**" (vv.12–14). The gem-finding: the God-planted inner being's fruitfulness **continues into old age** — it does not wither but stays *green*, *full of sap*, *bearing fruit* *(stated)*. The operation: the self **rooted *in God's house*** flourishes **continuously** — its fruitfulness *increases with age* rather than fading, the opposite of the grass that withers (Ps 90:5–6) *(inferred: "planted in the house of the LORD… still bear fruit in old age… ever green" makes the rooting-in-God the source of a flourishing that does not decline; cf. Ps 1's tree by the water, 84's strength-to-strength, 71's old-age hope)*. The rooting determines the flourishing.

## What Psalm 92 says about the inner being
- **Contemplating God's works makes the inner being *glad* (vv.4–5).** Joy arises from beholding what God has done; the self is made glad by attending to God's deeds, and drawn to their unfathomable depth.
- **The God-planted self flourishes *into old age* (vv.12–14).** Rooted in God's house, the inner being stays green, full of sap, and fruitful even in age — its fruitfulness continues rather than fading, the opposite of the withering grass. The rooting determines the flourishing.
