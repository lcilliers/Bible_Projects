## AMOS-no-escape-restore · Amos 9:1–15 · [D, multi] — no hiding from God's presence; the sieve; the fallen booth raised

**The reading.** The final vision shows the LORD by the altar, and a judgment from which there is no flight: "**If they dig into Sheol, from there shall my hand take them; if they climb up to heaven, from there I will bring them down. If they hide themselves on the top of Carmel, from there I will search them out… and if they hide from my sight at the bottom of the sea, there I will command the serpent**" (vv.2–3). Yet the judgment is *discriminating*: "**I will shake the house of Israel among all the nations as one shakes with a sieve, but no pebble shall fall to the earth**" (v.9). And the book turns, at the last, to hope: "**In that day I will raise up the booth of David that is fallen… I will restore the fortunes of my people Israel… and they shall never again be uprooted**" (vv.11–15). The finding is **the *inescapable presence* of God ("if they climb to heaven… if they dig into Sheol") — here in judgment — with a *sieve* that loses no true grain, resolving into the *restoration* of the fallen and a people "never again uprooted"** *(stated)*. The operation is **God's omnipresence experienced by the guilty as *the impossibility of hiding* — the same reach that is comfort to the psalmist (Ps 139) is here inescapable exposure — tempered by the *precision* of the sieve (judgment that separates, losing no genuine grain) and crowned by the raising of what had fallen** *(inferred: vv.2–3 are the dark counterpart of Psalm 139 — "**if I ascend to heaven… if I make my bed in Sheol, you are there**": the finding is that God's *omnipresence* is felt by the unrepentant interior as *the impossibility of escape* — there is no corner of the cosmos (height, depth, mountaintop, sea-floor) outside his reach; what is refuge to the trusting is exposure to the fleeing — the same nearness, opposite experience (as with the day of the LORD, Joel 3:16); and the *sieve* (v.9) qualifies the judgment as *discriminating* — "no pebble [or: no true grain] shall fall to the earth": the finding is that God's shaking is not indiscriminate destruction but a *sifting* that distinguishes and preserves the genuine; and the book that has thundered judgment for nine chapters ends in *restoration* — "**I will RAISE UP the booth of David that is fallen**" (v.11), "**restore the fortunes**", "**never again be uprooted**" (vv.14–15): the finding is that the last word is not the inescapable hand of judgment but the raising of the fallen — the God from whom none can hide is also the God who rebuilds the ruin, so the interior's final horizon under this God is not annihilation but *restoration*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the inescapable presence of God ("if they climb to heaven… if they dig into Sheol… I will search them out" — omnipresence as the impossibility of hiding)* (thread — a gem: the dark counterpart of Ps 139; the same nearness that comforts the trusting exposes the fleeing, w/ 9:2-3, Ps 139:7-12, Jer 23:24); *the discriminating sieve (judgment that sifts and preserves the genuine, losing no true grain)* (new — a gem: shaking that distinguishes, not indiscriminate destruction, w/ 9:9, Luke 22:31, Mal 3:17-18); *restoration as the last word ("I will raise up the booth of David that is fallen… never again uprooted" — the God from whom none can hide rebuilds the ruin)* (thread — a gem: the final horizon is restoration, not annihilation, w/ 9:11-15, Acts 15:16-17).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the inescapable presence of God; the discriminating sieve; restoration as the last word. Chapter 9 closes Amos with the God from whom none can flee — felt as inescapable judgment by the guilty, yet sifting to preserve the genuine, and raising at the last the fallen booth of David.
- **Book-level note (Amos):** Amos measures the interior almost entirely by *justice and compassion toward the weak*. Its findings: cruelty as a *nursed disposition* (1–2); *intimacy raising accountability* ("you only have I known; therefore I will punish", 3:2); the heart *impervious to correction* ("yet you did not return", 4); *life as seeking God*, inseparable from loving good and letting "justice roll down like waters" (5:24), against a *ritual worship God hates* for its justice-lessness; *complacent ease* that is "not grieved over the ruin of Joseph" (6:6); the prophet's *intercession* and *plumb-line* standard and *courage under threat* (7); greed that *resents the Sabbath* and the *famine of hearing God's word* (8:11); and the *inescapable God* who nonetheless *raises the fallen* (9). Master-findings: **the treatment of the poor as the true index of the heart**; **"let justice roll down like waters"**; **worship without justice is anti-worship**; and **the un-grieving, comfort-anaesthetised heart**.
- **Threads (book-wide):** justice/the poor (2:6, 5:24, 8:4); seek-and-live (5:4); the day of the LORD as darkness (5:18); God's inescapable presence (9:2, w/ Ps 139).
