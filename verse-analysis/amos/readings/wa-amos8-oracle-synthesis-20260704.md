## AMOS-famine-of-word · Amos 8:1–14 · [D, multi] — greed that resents the Sabbath; the famine of hearing God's word

**The reading.** A basket of summer fruit signals "**the end has come upon my people Israel**" (v.2). The merchants' interior is laid bare: they "**trample on the needy**" and chafe at every pause in trade — "**When will the new moon be over, that we may sell grain? And the Sabbath, that we may offer wheat for sale?**" — scheming to "**make the ephah small and the shekel great and deal deceitfully with false balances… buy the poor for silver**" (vv.4–6). Then the strangest judgment of all: "**Behold, the days are coming… when I will send a famine on the land — not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD. They shall wander… to seek the word of the LORD, but they shall not find it**" (vv.11–12). The finding is **a greed that *resents the Sabbath* as an interruption of profit ("when will the Sabbath be over?") — and the terrible judgment of a *famine of hearing God's word*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior so captured by acquisitiveness that even sacred rest is an *obstacle* to be endured ("when will it be over, that we may sell?") — the heart present at worship but *impatient for it to end* so trade can resume — answered by a judgment fitted to it: the *withdrawal of God's word*, so that the people who could not be bothered to hear it will one day *wander desperate to find it and cannot*** *(inferred: vv.4–6 expose an interior in which *greed has colonised even the holy* — the merchants keep the new moon and Sabbath outwardly but *inwardly* count the hours ("when will it be over, that we may sell?"), their hearts on the scales they will rig ("make the ephah small and the shekel great"); the finding is that acquisitiveness can hollow out worship from within — the body rests while the mind trades, resenting the pause that keeps it from gain (the precise inverse of "call the Sabbath a delight", Isa 58:13); and the judgment (vv.11–12) is exquisitely fitted — "**a FAMINE… of HEARING the words of the LORD**": the finding is that the word *neglected* is eventually *withdrawn* — those who would not listen when God spoke will "wander from sea to sea… to seek the word of the LORD, but shall not find it"; the most terrible poverty is not of bread but of access to God's voice, and it comes as the ripened fruit of *not wanting it* — the interior that resented the Sabbath's interruption is left with an unbroken silence it now craves; the presence of the word is not guaranteed forever to those who despise it)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *greed that resents the sacred pause ("when will the Sabbath be over, that we may sell?" — the heart present at worship but impatient for it to end)* (new — a gem: acquisitiveness hollowing worship from within, w/ 8:5, Isa 58:13 inverted, Mal 1:13); *deceit woven into commerce ("make the ephah small and the shekel great… false balances… buy the poor for silver")* (thread — the oppression of the poor through rigged trade, w/ 8:5-6, 2:6, Pro 11:1, Mic 6:11); *the famine of hearing God's word ("not a famine of bread… but of hearing the words of the LORD" — the word neglected eventually withdrawn)* (new — a gem: the most terrible poverty is the withdrawal of God's voice, come as the fruit of not wanting it, w/ 8:11-12, 1 Sam 3:1, Ps 74:9); *the word not guaranteed to those who despise it (they will seek and not find)* (thread — w/ 8:12, Pro 1:28).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** greed that resents the sacred pause; deceit woven into commerce; the famine of hearing God's word; the word not guaranteed to those who despise it. Chapter 8 pairs a heart so greedy it resents the Sabbath with a judgment fitted to it — the withdrawal of the very word it could not be bothered to hear.
- **Threads:** greed / oppression of the poor (← 2:6, 5:11; 8:4-6); Sabbath as delight vs burden (← Isa 58:13); the word withdrawn (w/ 1 Sam 3:1, Ps 74:9).
