## AMOS-at-ease-not-grieved · Amos 6:1–14 · [D, multi] — complacent ease; the heart un-grieved by others' ruin; self-strength

**The reading.** The woe falls on comfortable religion: "**Woe to those who are at ease in Zion, and to those who feel secure on the mountain of Samaria**" (v.1); who "**lie on beds of ivory… eat lambs from the flock… drink wine in bowls and anoint themselves with the finest oils**" (vv.4–6). The indictment lands on a single interior failure: "**but are not grieved over the ruin of Joseph!**" (v.6). God "**abhors the pride of Jacob**" (v.8), and exposes their self-congratulation: "**Have we not by our own strength captured Karnaim for ourselves?**" (v.13). The finding is **luxurious *complacency* ("at ease… secure") whose defining sin is a heart *un-grieved by others' ruin* ("not grieved over the ruin of Joseph") — joined to a *self-crediting pride* ("by our own strength")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the anaesthesia of comfort: the well-fed, well-oiled interior has become *incapable of grief* over the suffering around it — the indulgence has deadened the compassion — and this *callous ease* is named as the culminating sin, worse in some sense than the luxury itself; the same self is puffed with taking credit ("our own strength")** *(inferred: the structure of the woe is telling — a long catalogue of luxury (ivory beds, choice meat, idle songs, bowls of wine, finest oils, vv.4–6) climaxes not in "and they are gluttons" but in "**BUT ARE NOT GRIEVED over the ruin of Joseph**" (v.6): the finding is that the deepest indictment of comfortable ease is what it does to the *capacity for compassion* — the anaesthesia of indulgence renders the interior *unable to feel* the pain of others ("the ruin of Joseph" = the nation's suffering, to which the luxurious are numb); the sin is not merely enjoying good things but *feeling nothing* for those who suffer while one enjoys them — a callousness bred by comfort; "at ease" (sha'anan — complacent, untroubled) names an interior that has insulated itself from the world's pain; and the self-security is doubled by *self-credit* — "by our **own strength**" (v.13, and "the pride of Jacob", v.8): the finding is that complacent ease and self-crediting pride travel together — the heart insulated from others' pain is also inflated about its own achievement; the un-grieving heart is the proud heart)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *complacent ease as anaesthesia ("at ease in Zion… secure" — comfort insulating the interior from the world's pain)* (thread — a gem: the deadening of feeling by indulgence, w/ 6:1,4-6, Luke 16:19-25, Rev 3:17); *the heart un-grieved by others' ruin ("but are not grieved over the ruin of Joseph" — the culminating sin: feeling nothing for the suffering around one)* (new — a gem: comfort's destruction of compassion, w/ 6:6, Ezek 16:49); *self-crediting pride ("by our own strength… the pride of Jacob" — the inflated heart that takes the credit)* (thread — a gem: complacency and self-credit travel together, w/ 6:8,13, Deut 8:17, Isa 10:13); *God abhorring pride ("I abhor the pride of Jacob")* (thread — w/ 6:8, Pro 8:13).
