## DAN-constant-prayer · Daniel 6:1–28 · [D, multi] — unwavering devotion under threat; the God served continually

**The reading.** Daniel's excellence is such that his rivals "**could find no ground for complaint or any fault, because he was faithful**" (v.4), and conclude they must attack him "**in connection with the law of his God**" (v.5). They engineer a decree against praying to anyone but the king. Daniel's response is the still centre of the story: "**When Daniel knew that the document had been signed, he went to his house… and he got down on his knees three times a day and prayed and gave thanks before his God, as he had done previously**" (v.10). In the den, "**My God sent his angel and shut the lions' mouths… because I was found blameless before him**" (v.22); he was unharmed "**because he had trusted in his God**" (v.23) — the God "**whom you serve continually**" (vv.16,20). The finding is **a devotion so *constant* that a death-decree does not alter it — Daniel prays "**as he had done previously**", neither defiantly louder nor secretly quieter — grounded in a life of *continual* service and integrity** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior whose devotion is a *settled habit* impervious to circumstance: the lethal decree produces *no change* in Daniel's practice ("as he had done previously") — he does not begin to defy provocatively nor retreat into concealment, because his prayer was never *for* anyone's watching; and this constancy rests on a life already *whole* ("no fault… faithful", "blameless before him") and a service that is "**continual**"** *(inferred: v.10's "**as he had done previously**" is the inner-being gem — the finding is that Daniel's response to the threat is *continuity*, not reaction: he neither escalates (praying ostentatiously to make a point) nor hides (praying secretly to survive) but simply *continues* what he always did — because his devotion was never a performance calibrated to an audience; the finding is that the truest test of the interior is what a threat *does not* change — a devotion that bends to danger was shallow, while one that continues "as previously" was real; and this is possible only because of what precedes it — a life of *integrity* ("no error or fault", v.4) and *continual* service (the word recurs, vv.16,20): the finding is that constancy under crisis is the *fruit of ordinary faithfulness* — the habit built in safety is what holds under threat (he "had done previously"); the open windows "toward Jerusalem" (v.10) show the devotion was *directed and unhidden* but not *staged* — the same before and during the danger; and the ground is named simply — "because he had **trusted** in his God" (v.23) — the constancy was trust in action)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *devotion unchanged by threat ("he prayed… as he had done previously" — neither defiant escalation nor secret retreat; the truest test is what danger does not alter)* (thread — a gem: constancy as continuity, devotion never calibrated to an audience, w/ 6:10, Ps 55:17, Acts 4:19-20); *constancy as the fruit of ordinary faithfulness (the continual habit built in safety holds under threat — "as he had done previously")* (new — a gem: crisis-faithfulness grown from everyday faithfulness, w/ 6:4,16,20, Luke 16:10); *integrity that gives the enemy no handle ("no error or fault… because he was faithful")* (thread — the whole life leaving nothing to accuse, w/ 6:4-5, 1 Pet 2:12); *trust as the ground of it all ("because he had trusted in his God")* (thread — w/ 6:23).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** devotion unchanged by threat; constancy as the fruit of ordinary faithfulness; integrity that gives the enemy no handle; trust as the ground. Chapter 6 closes Daniel's narrative half with the portrait of *unwavering* devotion — prayer that a death-decree does not alter, grown from a life of continual faithfulness and trust.
- **Book-level note (Daniel 1–6, the narrative):** the six scenes form a gallery of the *tested interior* under pagan power: the **resolved heart** that draws its line in advance (1); the **God-dependent** interior that seeks mercy, worships, and disclaims credit (2); the **"but if not" faith** that obeys regardless of rescue (3); **pride humbled** and sanity restored by the Godward gaze (4); the heart that **knew but would not humble itself**, weighed and found wanting (5); and **unwavering devotion** that danger does not alter (6). Master-findings: **integrity is settled before the crisis**; **faith trusts God's power and submits to his freedom**; **pride's danger-point is success, and its cure is looking up**; **constancy under threat is the fruit of ordinary faithfulness.**
- **Threads:** the settled/resolved heart (1:8, 3:16, 6:10); pride humbled vs the humble self (4, 5); trust (3:28, 6:23).
