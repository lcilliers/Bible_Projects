## DAN-mystery-revealed · Daniel 2:1–49 · [D, multi] — dependence on God for wisdom; humility about the source; the doxology of trust

**The reading.** Under a death-decree to reveal the king's undisclosed dream, Daniel's first move is *interior and Godward*: he asks his companions "**to seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery**" (v.18). When the mystery is revealed, he does not rush to the king but *worships*: "**Blessed be the name of God forever… he reveals deep and hidden things; he knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with him**" (vv.20–22). Before the king he disclaims all credit: "**this mystery has been revealed to me, not because of any wisdom that I have more than all the living**" (v.30). The finding is **the interior that, facing the impossible, *seeks mercy* rather than scrambling — *worships* before it acts — and *refuses the credit*, referring its wisdom wholly to God** *(stated)*. The operation is **the reflex of a God-dependent interior: (1) under threat it turns *first* to God ("seek mercy… concerning this mystery"); (2) on receiving help it *stops to bless* before using the gift; (3) it holds the gift with *humility about the source* ("not because of any wisdom that I have") — so that competence never becomes self-credit** *(inferred: three inner-being reflexes stand out. First, the *direction of the first move* — faced with death and an impossible demand, Daniel neither panics nor bluffs but "sought mercy from the God of heaven" (v.18): the finding is that the God-dependent interior's *instinct under crisis* is to turn Godward before turning to its own resources. Second, the *pause to worship* — the moment the answer comes, "then Daniel **blessed** the God of heaven" (v.19) *before* going to save his life: the finding is that gratitude precedes exploitation of the gift — the interior stops to praise the Giver before spending the gift. Third, the *disclaiming of credit* — "**not because of any wisdom that I have**" (v.30): the finding is that the healthy interior holds even its genuine competence as *received*, refusing the self-credit that Nebuchadnezzar will later fall by (4:30) and Belshazzar (5:23); the doxology "he reveals deep and hidden things, he knows what is in the darkness" (v.22) names *why* — all insight is God's to give, so the one who receives it worships rather than boasts)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *turning to God first under crisis ("seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery" — the God-dependent interior's instinct before its own resources)* (thread — a gem: the first move Godward, w/ 2:18, 6:10, Ps 50:15); *worship before using the gift ("then Daniel blessed the God of heaven" — gratitude preceding the exploitation of the answer)* (new — a gem: the pause to praise the Giver, w/ 2:19-23); *humility about the source ("not because of any wisdom that I have" — competence held as received, refusing self-credit)* (thread — a gem: the reflex opposite to Nebuchadnezzar's and Belshazzar's pride, w/ 2:30, 4:30, 5:23, 1 Cor 4:7).
