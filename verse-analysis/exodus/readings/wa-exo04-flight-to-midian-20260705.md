## EXO-04-flight-to-midian · Exodus 2:11–22 · [the deliverer's premature violence, rejection, and fearful flight] — Moses strikes, is rebuffed, and becomes a sojourner

**The passage.** "When Moses had **grown up** (gadal), he went out… looked on their burdens… saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew" (2:11); he struck the Egyptian; the next day two Hebrews **struggling** (natsah); "why do you strike your companion?" — to the one **in the wrong** (rasha, 2:13); "who made you a **judge** (shaphat)? will you kill me as you killed the Egyptian?" — Moses was **afraid** (yare): "the thing is **known** (yada)" (2:14); Pharaoh **heard** (shama), **sought** (baqash) to kill him; Moses fled to Midian (2:15); he saved the daughters at the well; Reuel: "**call** (qara) him to eat" (2:20); Moses was **content** (yaal) to dwell; Zipporah; Gershom, "a sojourner in a foreign land" (2:22).

### The operations, span by span (individual)

- **gadal (grown up) — the deliverer *come of age to act* (2:11).** "when Moses had **grown up** (gadal), he went out to his people and looked on their burdens" *(stated)* — gadal *distinct from 2:10* (the child's growth): now the *grown man*, who *goes out*, *looks on the burdens*, and *identifies with "his people"*. The gem is the *seeing* — Moses *looks on the affliction* (as God will *see* it, 3:7) — a deliverer's *solidarity awakening*; but his interior *moves straight from seeing to striking* *(inferred: gadal = the grown deliverer whose solidarity awakens — and acts)*.
- **natsah (struggling) — the *internal strife* of the oppressed (2:13).** "two Hebrews were **struggling** (natsah) together" *(stated)* — natsah, *struggle/quarrel*: the gem is *who* fights — *Hebrew against Hebrew*; the oppressed *turned on each other*; the interior of a people *divided among itself* even under a common bondage (the deliverer's task complicated by the people's own strife) *(inferred: natsah = the internal strife of the oppressed)*.
- **rasha (in the wrong) — the *guilty aggressor* named (2:13b).** "he said to the one **in the wrong** (rasha), 'why do you strike your companion?'" *(stated)* — rasha, *wicked/guilty*: Moses *names the aggressor* and *intervenes as arbiter* — the interior *drawn to judge and set right*; but his authority is *self-appointed* *(inferred: rasha = the guilty one Moses moves to judge)*.
- **shaphat (judge) — the *rejected authority* (2:14a).** "who made you a prince and a **judge** (shaphat) over us?" *(stated)* — the gem: **shaphat** — the aggressor *rejects Moses' self-appointed authority* — *"who made you judge?"*. The irony runs deep: Moses *will* be made Israel's deliverer/judge — *but by God, not by self-appointment*; here the interior's *premature, self-authorised* deliverance is *rebuffed by the very people it would help* *(inferred: shaphat = the rejected self-appointed authority — the deliverer un-authorised)*.
- **yare (afraid) + yada (known) — the deliverer's *fear and exposure* (2:14b).** "Moses was **afraid** (yare): 'surely the thing is **known** (yada)'" *(stated)*. **yare** — the gem: the man who will *confront Pharaoh* is here *afraid* — a *fear-of-exposure* (distinct from the midwives' *fear-of-God* 1:17; this is *self-preserving* fear, like the sister-lie fear of Genesis); **yada** — the deed *known*, the secret out. The interior's *courage collapses into fear* the moment his violence is *exposed* — the deliverer *not yet ready* *(inferred: yare/yada = the deliverer's self-preserving fear at his exposure)*.
- **shama (heard) + baqash (sought) — the *adversary's murderous pursuit* (2:15a).** "Pharaoh **heard** (shama) and **sought** (baqash) to kill Moses" *(stated)*. **shama** — Pharaoh *hears*; **baqash** — *seek* (to kill): the adversary's interior *hunts the deliverer's life* (→ 4:19 "all who **sought** your life are dead"). The pursuit that *drives the exile* *(inferred: shama/baqash = the adversary's murderous seeking)*.
- **qara (call him) — Midian's *hospitality* (2:20).** "why have you left the man? **Call** (qara) him, that he may eat bread" *(stated)* — qara *distinct from the summons-to-account* (1:18): here a *welcome to table* (as Laban welcomed, Gen 29:13) — the interior of *hospitality* receiving the fugitive; the exile *taken in* *(inferred: qara = the hospitable summons to table)*.
- **yaal (content) — the deliverer *at rest in exile* (2:21).** "Moses was **content** (yaal) to dwell with the man" *(stated)* — yaal, *be willing/content/undertake*: the gem is the *settling* — the would-be deliverer, *rejected and afraid*, is now *content to dwell* in Midian, marry, shepherd; the interior *at rest in a foreign land* — the long un-making before the call (Gershom, "sojourner") *(inferred: yaal = the deliverer's contentment to dwell in exile)*.

### The web (interactive)

1. **solidarity that strikes (gadal 2:11 → the killing 2:12):** the grown Moses *sees his people's burdens* and *identifies* — but his interior *moves from seeing to violence*, striking the Egyptian in secret.
2. **the arbiter rejected (natsah 2:13 → rasha/shaphat 2:14a):** intervening in *Hebrew-on-Hebrew* strife, he is *rebuffed* — *"who made you judge?"* — his *self-appointed* deliverance *rejected by the oppressed themselves*.
3. **fear and flight (yare/yada 2:14b → shama/baqash 2:15a):** exposed, the deliverer *collapses into fear*; Pharaoh *hears and hunts him*; he *flees* — the premature deliverance *ends in exile*.
4. **welcome and rest (qara 2:20 → yaal 2:21):** Midian *takes him in*; he is *content to dwell*, a *sojourner* — the deliverer *un-made and at rest* before God will *make* him at the bush.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced (kept distinct):** gadal (the *grown* deliverer whose solidarity *awakens and acts* — vs the child's growth 2:10); natsah (the *internal strife* of the oppressed — Hebrew against Hebrew); rasha (the *guilty aggressor* Moses judges); **shaphat (the *rejected self-appointed authority* — "who made you judge?", the deliverer un-authorised)**; **yare/yada (the deliverer's *self-preserving fear* at exposure — vs the midwives' fear-of-God)**; shama/baqash (the *adversary's murderous pursuit*); qara (Midian's *hospitable* welcome); yaal (the deliverer *content to dwell in exile*).
- **Whose interior (multi-interior):** **Moses** — awakening solidarity that *acts by violence*, *rejected*, *afraid*, and finally *at rest in exile* (the un-making of the self-made deliverer); the **wicked Hebrew** — *rejecting* the would-be deliverer and *exposing* him (the people not ready); the **adversary** (Pharaoh) — *hunting* the deliverer's life; **Midian** (Reuel) — *hospitality*. God *unnamed* here — the deliverer must be *emptied* (rejected, exiled, shepherd) before he is *called*.
- **Threads:** the *seeing* of affliction (Moses "looked on their burdens" 2:11 → God "I have **seen** the affliction" 3:7 — the deliverer's seeing answered by God's); the *rejected authority* (shaphat 2:14 → God *makes* him "like God to Pharaoh" 7:1 — self-appointment vs divine commission); the deliverer's *fear* (yare 2:14 → his reluctance at the bush 3:11; 4:1,10,13 — the fear God must overcome); the murderous *seeking* (baqash 2:15 → 4:19); *sojourner* (Gershom → Israel as sojourners, the exodus toward a land); the *content dwelling* before the call (yaal 2:21 → the bush, 3:1, "Moses was keeping the flock").
- **Guarded against:** reading Moses' killing as simple heroism *or* simple murder — the text gives *awakened solidarity acting prematurely and by violence*, then *rejected and afraid* (the un-making is the finding); merging the interiors (Moses / the rejecting Hebrew / Pharaoh / Reuel); importing the NT "by faith Moses… chose to suffer" (Heb 11) over the *fear and flight* the passage narrates; over-reading yaal (2:21) as mere acquiescence when it marks the deliverer's *rest in exile* — the necessary emptying before the call.
