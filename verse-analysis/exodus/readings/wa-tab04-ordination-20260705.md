## TAB-04-ordination · Exodus 29:1–46 · [atonement, the pleasing aroma, and God dwelling and known] — the seven-day ordination, and "I will dwell among them… and they shall know"

**The passage.** **Consecrate** (qadash) them; the rams **without blemish** (tamim, 29:1); the **holy** (qodesh) crown (29:6); the bull's **flesh** (basar) burned — a **sin offering** (chattat, 29:14); the ram a **pleasing aroma** (nichoach, 29:18); the blood on ear/thumb/toe, garments made **holy** (qadash, 29:21); the ordination portions burned, a **pleasing aroma** (nichoach, 29:25); **consecrate** (qadash) the wave-breast, the **contribution** (rum, 29:27); the **holy** (qodesh) garments passed on (29:29); the ram's **flesh** (basar) boiled in a **holy** (qadosh) place (29:31), eaten (basar, 29:32); the food by which **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) was made, holy (29:33); remnant **flesh** (basar) burned, holy (29:34); daily a **sin offering** (chattat) for **atonement** (kipper/kaphar/kippurim), the altar **purified** (chata) and **consecrated** (qadash, 29:36); seven days' **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) makes the altar **most holy** (qodesh, 29:37); the daily lamb a **pleasing aroma** (nichoach, 29:41); the tent **sanctified by my glory** (kavod/qadash, 29:43); "I will **consecrate** (qadash) the tent… I will **dwell** among Israel… and they shall **know** that I am the LORD their God" (29:44–46).

### The operations (the genuine inner-being content)

- **tamim (without blemish) — the *unblemished offering* (29:1).** "'take one bull and two rams **without blemish** (tamim)'" *(stated)* — tamim, *whole/unblemished*: the faint gem is that *what is offered to God must be whole* — *perfection required in the gift*; the interior-context (of the worshipper) that *God is given the unflawed*, not the leftover *(inferred: tamim = the unblemished offering — the whole given to God)*.
- **nichoach (pleasing aroma) — *God's pleasure in the offering* (29:18, 29:25, 29:41).** "'it is a **pleasing aroma** (nichoach) to the LORD'" — of the burnt ram (29:18), the ordination portions (29:25), the daily lamb (29:41) *(stated)*. **nichoach** — *soothing/restful* (from *nuach*, rest): the gem, though a *recurring ritual formula*, carries a genuine note about **God's interior** — the offering is *soothing / restful / pleasing* to him; the anthropopathic picture of *God met and pleased* by the offering. The three occurrences are the *same note* on three occasions (the ram, the ordination, the perpetual daily lamb) — I do not invent three meanings; it is *God's settled pleasure* in the rightly-given offering, sounded repeatedly *(inferred: nichoach = God's pleasure in the offering — the offering that soothes/pleases him)*.
- **chattat (sin offering) + kipper / kaphar / kippurim (atonement) + chata (purify) — the *atonement that fits for the holy* (29:14, 29:33, 29:36, 29:37).** "'it is a **sin offering** (chattat)'" (29:14); "'the food by which **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) was made'" (29:33); "'a sin offering for **atonement** (kippurim); **purify** (chata) the altar'" (29:36); "'seven days you shall make **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) for the altar'" (29:37) *(stated)*. **chattat** — *sin offering*; **kipper/kaphar** — *atone/cover*; **kippurim** — *atonement*; **chata** (in the *purify* sense) — *de-sin*: the gem is *atonement as covering / cleansing* — even the *altar* must be *atoned for and purified* before it is fit; the interior-context (of the whole system) that *approach to God requires guilt covered and uncleanness removed* — *nothing unatoned may stand in the holy*. Here it is *ritual mechanics*, but the concept is genuine: **atonement makes fit for God's presence** *(inferred: chattat/kipper/kaphar/chata = the atonement that covers and cleanses — making fit for the holy)*.
- **kavod (glory) + qadash (consecrate/dwell) — the *climax: God sanctifying, dwelling, and known* (29:43–46).** "'the tent shall be **sanctified by my glory** (kavod)… I will **consecrate** (qadash) it… I will **dwell** among the people of Israel and will be their God, and they shall **know** that I am the LORD their God, who brought them out… that I might **dwell** among them'" *(stated)*. **kavod** — *glory*; **qadash** — *make holy*: the gem is *THE purpose of the whole block* — all the construction, garments, and ordination are *for this*: **God will dwell among them and be known.** The *glory* sanctifies the tent; God *consecrates* it; and the goal is *relational* — *"I will be their God"* and *"they shall know that I am the LORD their God who brought them out."* The interior in view is *the covenant bond consummated in indwelling* — the exodus refrain (*that they may know*, ← 6:7; 10:2; answering Pharaoh's "I do not know the LORD", 5:2) reaches its rest: **God dwells, God is known, the deliverance was FOR this nearness** *(inferred: kavod/qadash/dwell/know = the climax — God sanctifying, dwelling among them, and known; the deliverance's relational goal)*.

### Ritual detail (surfaced honestly, not read as inner-being)

The bulk of the chapter is *ordination procedure* — captured so nothing is skipped, **not** inner-being operations: the **holy** (qodesh) crown/garments/place (29:6, 29:29, 29:31, 29:33, 29:34 — the *holiness designation* of objects and space); the sacrificial **flesh** (basar 29:14, 29:31, 29:32, 29:34 — the meat of the offerings, burned or eaten); the **contribution** (rum 29:27 — the wave/heave portion "lifted" for the priests); the repeated **consecrate** (qadash) of persons, garments, and altar (the setting-apart running through the rite). These are the *machinery* of ordination; the theology they serve is real, but the verses give *instruction*, not interior.

### The web (interactive)

The chapter's genuine movement is simple and worth stating plainly: **atonement fits, the offering pleases, and God comes to dwell.** The *unblemished* offering is given; *atonement* covers and cleanses (even the altar); the offering is a *pleasing aroma* — *God is met and pleased*; and the whole apparatus terminates in the *climax*: **God's glory sanctifies the tent, God dwells among Israel, and Israel knows him.** The ritual is the *means*; the *end* is the nearness and the knowing. Nothing more is claimed than the text gives — but the end it gives is the covenant's heart.

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** **tamim (the *unblemished* offering — the whole given to God)**; **nichoach ×3 (*God's pleasure* in the offering — the same note on three occasions, not three operations)**; **chattat/kipper/kaphar/kippurim/chata (the *atonement* that covers and cleanses — making fit for the holy)**; **kavod + qadash + dwell + know (the *climax* — God sanctifying, dwelling among them, and known: the deliverance's relational goal)**. Ritual/designation terms (qodesh "holy" ×many, basar "flesh" ×4, rum "contribution", qadash "consecrate" ×many) noted, not read.
- **Whose interior (multi-interior, only at the climax):** **God** — *pleased* by the offering (nichoach), *sanctifying* the tent by his glory, *dwelling* among the people, *making himself known*; **Israel** — the people God will *dwell among* and who will *know* him. Elsewhere the chapter is procedure, honestly.
- **Threads:** **that they may know** (29:46 ← "you shall know that I am the LORD" 6:7; 10:2; answering Pharaoh's un-knowing 5:2 → the knowing-God refrain — the deliverance's catechetical goal); **God dwelling** (29:45 ← 25:8 "that I may dwell among them" → the glory filling the tent 40:34; "the Word dwelt among us" Jn 1:14 — the indwelling thread); **atonement** (kipper 29:33,36,37 → the Day of Atonement Lev 16; "without shedding of blood there is no forgiveness" Heb 9:22 — atonement as fitting-for-the-holy); **the pleasing aroma** (nichoach 29:18 ← Noah's offering Gen 8:21 "the LORD smelled the pleasing aroma" → "a fragrant offering" Eph 5:2 — God's pleasure in the given); **the unblemished** (tamim 29:1 → the lamb "without blemish" 12:5; 1 Pet 1:19 — the whole given).
- **Guarded against:** over-reading the *blood on ear/thumb/toe*, the *fat and kidneys*, the *wave offering*, or the *seven-day sequence* into interior meaning when they are ritual procedure; inflating the three *nichoach* into three distinct operations (it is one note — God's pleasure — on three occasions); treating the *atonement* mechanics here as a developed doctrine when the text gives the *rite* (the doctrine belongs at Lev 16 / Heb); mistaking the many *qadash/qodesh* designations for interior states when they mark *ritual holiness* of objects; **letting the climax (29:45–46) be the finding it is — the relational goal — without importing the later theology onto the machinery that serves it.**
