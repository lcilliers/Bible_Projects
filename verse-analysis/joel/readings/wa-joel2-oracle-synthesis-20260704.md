## JOEL-rend-hearts · Joel 2:1–17 · [D, multi] — the day of the LORD; rend the heart, not the garment

**The reading.** The trumpet sounds and the terrible day approaches — "**a day of darkness and gloom**" (v.2), an unstoppable army before which "**all faces grow pale**" (v.6), until "**the day of the LORD is great and very awesome; who can endure it?**" (v.11). Then the pivot on which the book turns: "**'Yet even now,' declares the LORD, 'return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning; and rend your hearts and not your garments.' Return to the LORD your God, for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love; and he relents over disaster**" (vv.12–13). The finding is **the summons to *whole-hearted, interior* repentance — "**rend your hearts and not your garments**" — grounded in God's *gracious, relenting* character** *(stated)*. The operation is **the redirection of repentance from the *outward sign* (the torn robe, the visible fast) to the *inward reality* (the torn heart, "**all your heart**") — the interior itself broken open in return — and the assurance that such a turn is met by a God whose nature is "gracious and merciful… and he relents"** *(inferred: v.13 is the book's inner-being gem — "**rend your HEARTS and not your GARMENTS**": the finding is that true repentance is an *interior* act, not a performance — the tearing of the robe (the conventional gesture of grief) is worthless unless the *heart* is what is torn; God asks for "**all your heart**" (v.12), the undivided interior turned back, expressed in fasting/weeping/mourning but *located in the heart*, not the display (the exact opposite of the "lips near, heart far" of Isa 29:13); and the summons rests entirely on *who God is* — "**for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love, and he relents**" (v.13, the great self-revelation of Exod 34:6): the finding is that the ground of return is not the penitent's worthiness but God's *character* — because he "relents over disaster", the torn heart has somewhere to turn; and the tentative "**who knows whether he will not turn and relent?**" (v.14) preserves the freedom of grace — repentance does not coerce God but casts itself on his known mercy)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *interior repentance ("rend your hearts and not your garments" — the heart torn, not the robe; repentance an inward act not a performance)* (thread — a gem: true return located in the heart, not the display, w/ 2:12-13, Isa 29:13 inverted, Ps 51:16-17, Matt 6:16-18); *the whole heart returned ("return to me with all your heart" — the undivided interior)* (thread — w/ 2:12, Deut 6:5, Jer 29:13); *return grounded in God's character ("for he is gracious and merciful… and he relents" — the ground of repentance is who God is, not the penitent's worthiness)* (thread — a gem: mercy as the basis of return, w/ 2:13, Exod 34:6, Jonah 4:2); *the freedom of grace ("who knows whether he will relent?" — repentance casts itself on mercy, does not coerce it)* (new — w/ 2:14, Jonah 3:9).

## JOEL-restore-spirit · Joel 2:18–32 · [D, multi] — the years restored; satisfied and unashamed; the Spirit poured on all flesh

**The reading.** God answers the returning people: "**Fear not, O land; be glad and rejoice, for the LORD has done great things!**" (v.21); "**I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten**" (v.25); "**You shall eat in plenty and be satisfied, and praise the name of the LORD… And my people shall never again be put to shame**" (v.26). Then the great promise: "**I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy… Even on the male and female servants… I will pour out my Spirit**" (vv.28–29); "**everyone who calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved**" (v.32). The finding is **the returning interior met with *restoration* ("the years the locust ate"), *satisfaction*, and freedom from *shame* — and the outpoured *Spirit* on "all flesh", with salvation for "everyone who calls"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the reversal of chapter 1's emptying: the stripped, ashamed interior *refilled and satisfied* — and, beyond material restoration, given *God's own Spirit* poured out indiscriminately (sons, daughters, servants), so that the relationship is not merely repaired but *deepened* into direct, Spirit-borne knowing** *(inferred: the answer matches the loss point for point — the locust took "gladness" (1:12), God restores "be glad and rejoice" (v.21); the harvest failed (1:10), God gives "plenty… satisfied" (v.26); the deepest reversal is *shame* — "**my people shall never again be put to shame**" (vv.26–27, twice): the finding is that the returning interior is not merely given back its goods but relieved of its *shame*, restored to confident belonging ("I am in the midst of Israel"); and the climax exceeds restoration — "**I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh**" (v.28): the finding is that the fruit of the return is not just renewed provision but *renewed presence* — God's Spirit poured out *democratically* (across age, gender, and status — "even on the servants"), so that the knowing of God becomes direct and universal; and the door is thrown wide — "**everyone who calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved**" (v.32): the finding is that the whole movement from locust-stripped despair ends in an open invitation grounded in *calling* on God)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the emptied interior refilled and satisfied ("I will restore the years the locust has eaten… you shall be satisfied")* (thread — a gem: loss reversed, the stripped self refilled, w/ 2:25-26, 1:12 reversed); *shame lifted ("my people shall never again be put to shame" — restored to confident belonging)* (thread — a gem: the returning interior relieved of shame, w/ 2:26-27, Isa 54:4, 45:17); *the Spirit poured on all flesh (renewed presence, not just provision — God's Spirit given democratically)* (thread — a gem: the fruit of return is direct Spirit-borne knowing across age/gender/status, w/ 2:28-29, Isa 32:15, 44:3, Acts 2:17); *salvation for everyone who calls ("everyone who calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved" — the open door grounded in calling)* (thread — w/ 2:32, Rom 10:13).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** interior repentance; the whole heart returned; return grounded in God's character; the emptied interior refilled; shame lifted; the Spirit poured out; salvation for all who call. Chapter 2 is Joel's heart — the day of the LORD answered by the summons to *rend the heart* (not the garment), and the returning people met with restoration, satisfaction, un-shaming, and the outpoured Spirit.
- **Threads:** interior repentance (← 1:14; w/ Ps 51:17); God gracious and relenting (w/ Exod 34:6, Jonah 4:2); loss reversed / shame lifted (← 1:12; Isa 54:4); the Spirit poured (w/ Isa 44:3; → Acts 2).
