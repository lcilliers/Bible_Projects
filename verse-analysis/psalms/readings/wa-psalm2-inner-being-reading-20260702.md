- **Multi-characteristic**, and structured as a **four-voice drama** — the nations, the LORD, the Anointed, and the psalmist's closing counsel — each disclosing a different movement of the inner being: **defiant desire, holy wrath, derision, and the fear/refuge that is the only safety.**

## The reading
Psalm 2 stages the inner being through **who speaks**, and reads it as a collision between human self-will and divine sovereignty.

**I. The nations — defiant desire that schemes (vv.1–3).**
The peoples **rage** and **plot** (H1897, characteristic — M42) "in vain" (v.1) — an inner scheming, the same lexical operation that is godly *meditation* in Psa 1:2, here turned to **conspiracy** *(inferred: valence from context; the operation is identical, the object opposite)*. The kings **set themselves** and take **counsel together** against the LORD and his Anointed (v.2). Their inner posture is then spoken aloud (v.3, backfilled): "let us **burst** their bonds and **cast** away their cords" — the desire underneath the plotting is **the throwing-off of restraint** *(stated: burst/cast the bonds/cords)*. The inner being here is **autonomy as appetite** — rule experienced as bondage, freedom imagined as revolt.

**II. The LORD — derision, then holy wrath (vv.4–6).**
The answer is first **laughter**: the One enthroned in the heavens **laughs**, holds them in **derision** (v.4, backfilled) — the rebellion is not met with alarm but with **contempt for its futility** *(inferred: derision as the measure of the threat)*. Then the mood turns: he will **speak** to them in his wrath and **terrify** them (H0926, characteristic — M01) in his **fury** (H2740, qualifier — M02) (v.5) — dread deliberately induced. And he asserts the settled fact they revolted against: "I have **set** my King on Zion, my **holy** hill" (v.6). *(holy here qualifies the place, not a human inner state — sanity-checked to standalone.)*

**III. The Anointed — sonship and the asking (vv.7–9).**
The King recites the **decree**: "You are my Son; today I have begotten you" (v.7) — an identity **received, not seized** *(inferred, against the nations' seizing)*. And the mode of his rule is **petition**: "**Ask** of me (H7592, characteristic — M21), and I will make the nations your heritage" (v.8) — dominion comes by **asking the Father**, the exact inverse of the kings who take counsel to grasp it. The rule itself is unbreakable (v.9, the iron rod).

**IV. The counsel — be wise; serve with fear; take refuge (vv.10–12).**
The psalmist turns to the rebels with an inner-being summons: "be **wise** (H7919, characteristic — M15), be warned" (v.10) *(rulers/judges here = a vocative address, sanity-checked to standalone).* The prescribed inner posture is **Serve the LORD** (H5647, characteristic — M36) **with fear** (H3374, qualifier — M01), and **rejoice** (H1523, characteristic — M04) **with trembling** (H7461, qualifier — M01) (v.11) — a striking weld: joy and dread held together, worship that is glad *and* awed. The alternative is his **anger** (H0599, characteristic — M02) kindled (v.12). The psalm closes where Psalm 1 began: "**Blessed** are all who **take refuge** (H2620, characteristic — M19) in him" — the inner being's only safety is **sheltering trust**, the opposite of the opening revolt.

## What Psalm 2 says about the inner being
- The chapter reads the inner being as a **contest of wills**, disclosed through four voices: **defiant desire** (the nations — autonomy felt as the throwing-off of restraint), **holy wrath and derision** (the LORD — the rebellion is contemptible, then dreadful), **received sonship that asks rather than seizes** (the Anointed), and the counselled posture of **fear-filled worship and sheltering refuge** (the close).
- Its sharpest disclosure is the **weld of opposites** in v.11: *serve with fear*, *rejoice with trembling* — the right inner orientation to God is **glad awe**, joy and dread inseparable.
- **Refuge/trust** (v.12) is named as the inner being's safety — and it stands as the exact inverse of the opening **revolt** (v.3): the whole psalm moves from "burst his bonds" to "take refuge in him."
- The **same operation carries opposite value by context**: *hagah* is *meditation* (Psa 1:2) and *plotting* (Psa 2:1) — the inner being's activity is morally weighted by its object, not its mechanism.
