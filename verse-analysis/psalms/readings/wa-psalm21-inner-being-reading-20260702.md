# Psalm 21 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 21:1–13 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked). Complete chapter. Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **A royal thanksgiving** — the companion and answer to Psalm 20. Where Ps 20 *asked* ("may he grant you your heart's desire", 20:4), Ps 21 *reports the giving* ("you have given him his heart's desire", 21:2). Its inner-being disclosure is not petition but **reception**: the trusting heart is a **receiving** heart, and everything it holds — joy, honour, life, security — it holds *derivatively*, from God.

## The reading
Psalm 21 divides cleanly: the king's joy in what God has **given** (vv.1–7), the rout of the enemy (vv.8–12, largely the external pole), and a closing doxology (v.13). The inner-being weight is almost all in the first movement, and it turns on one word in v.7.

**I. Joy that is wholly derivative (v.1).**
"O LORD, in **your strength** (H5797) the king **rejoices** (H8055, M04), and in **your salvation** how greatly he **exults** (H1523, M04)!" Two joy-verbs, intensified ("how greatly") — but note *where* the joy is located: **in your strength**, **in your salvation**. The lexical marks *strength* and *salvation* as process-qualifiers binding the rejoicing, not the king's own possessions. The inner being's gladness here has an **external ground**: it is joy *in* God's power and rescue, not joy in the self or the victory as the king's own achievement *(inferred from the "in your…" framing of both joy-verbs)*.

**II. The heart's desire — named and granted (v.2).**
"You have **given** (H5414, M12) him his **heart's** (H3820, the seat — M47) **desire** (H8378, M28) and have not **withheld** the **request** of his **lips**." The gem: the inner longing is not condemned, not merely tolerated, but **answered**. The *desire of the heart* (the seat's own wanting) and the *request of the lips* (its outward voicing) are set in parallel — inner longing and its expression — and **both are met** *(stated: given… not withheld)*. This is the exact hinge back to Ps 20:4's petition: what was asked *for* the king is here reported as **done** *(inferred from the verbal echo; not asserted as the psalm's own claim)*.

**III. Blessing that comes to meet him (v.3).**
"For you **meet** (H6923) him with **rich blessings** (H1293, M22); you **set** a **crown** of fine gold upon his head." The verb *qadam* (H6923) is "to come before, to anticipate, to go to meet." God does not merely respond when reached — he **comes out to meet** the king with good, ahead of the asking *(inferred: qadam carries the sense of anticipation)*. Blessing is **prevenient** — it arrives before it is earned or even fully sought.

**IV. Asked one thing, given more (v.4).**
"He **asked** (H7592, M21) **life** (H2416, M25) of you; you **gave** it to him, length of days forever and ever." He asked for *life*; he received **life extended past its own measure** — "forever and ever." The giving **exceeds the request** *(stated: he asked life; the answer is length of days forever)*. The inner being's petition is not merely granted but **overflowed**.

**V. Glory that is conferred, not self-generated (v.5).**
"His **glory** (H3519, M22) is great **through your salvation**; **splendor** (H1935, M22) and **majesty** (H1926, M22) you **bestow** on him." Three honour-words — glory, splendor, majesty — and every one is **derivative**: his glory is great *through your salvation*, splendor and majesty are what *you bestow*. The king's honour is real but **received**, never manufactured from within *(stated: "you bestow"; the glory is "through your salvation")*. (Cf. Ps 8 — worth is conferred by God's action, not intrinsic to the creature.)

**VI. The culmination is joy in the presence, not the gifts (v.6).**
"For you make him most **blessed** (H1293, M22) forever; you make him **glad** (H2302, M04) with the **joy** (H8057, M04) of your **presence** (H6440 — your *face*)." The movement of gifts (desire, crown, life, glory) does **not** terminate in the gifts — it terminates in **gladness in God's face**. The lexical binds *joy* and *presence* as the manner of the gladness: the deepest thing given is **joy in the presence itself** *(inferred: "the joy of your presence" makes the presence, not the benefits, the ground of the gladness)*. This is the same note struck in Ps 16:11 ("fullness of joy in your presence") and Ps 17:15 ("satisfied with your likeness") — a cross-psalm thread.

**VII. The hinge — WHY all of this: the king trusts (v.7).**
"For the king **trusts** (H0982, M19) in the LORD, and through the **steadfast love** (H2617, M05) of the Most High he shall **not be moved** (H4131)." The whole psalm turns here — *ki*, "for/because." The reason for the joy, the giving, the prevenient blessing, the conferred glory is **the king's trust**, met by **God's steadfast love**, issuing in "**not be moved**." The structure is exact: the **inner posture** (trust, *batach*) → **God's response** (steadfast love, *chesed*) → **the outcome** (immovability, *bal yimmot*). "Not be moved" is the same word as Ps 15:5 and 16:8 — the security of the one whose confidence is rightly placed *(stated: the king trusts; through steadfast love he is not moved)*. **The receiving of the first six verses rests entirely on the trusting of the seventh.**

**VIII. The enemy's inner scheming — and its futility (vv.8–12).**
The second movement turns outward to the enemies (largely the **external pole** — judgment, wrath, fire — not the inner being). But one inner-being note surfaces: "Though they **plan** evil (H7451, M27) against you, though they **devise** (H2803, M15) **mischief**, they will **not succeed**" (v.11). The enemies have an *inner life* too — they **plan** and **devise** (*chashab*, the same faculty of purposing/reckoning that is a good when stayed on God) — but here it is bent to **evil**, and its verdict is **"they will not succeed"** *(stated)*. The inner devising of the wicked against God **recoils into futility** *(inferred: the plan is real but comes to nothing)*.

**IX. The frame closes in praise (v.13).**
"Be **exalted**, O LORD, in **your strength**! We will sing and **praise** (H2167, M42) your **power** (H1369, M23)." The psalm opened with the king rejoicing *in your strength* (v.1) and closes with the community praising *your strength / power* (v.13) — an **inclusio on God's strength**. The inner being's final act, having received everything, is **praise of the Giver's power** *(stated)*. Reception completes itself in worship.

## What Psalm 21 says about the inner being
- **The trusting heart is a receiving heart (v.7 → vv.1–6).** All the psalm's giving is grounded, by its own *ki* in v.7, in the king's **trust** met by God's **steadfast love**. The inner posture that receives is trust; the security it gains ("not moved") is the same immovability as Ps 15–16.
- **Joy is wholly derivative (vv.1, 5, 6).** The inner being's gladness is located *in your strength*, *through your salvation*, in *the joy of your presence* — never in the self or the gift-as-possession. Honour too is **conferred** ("you bestow"), not self-generated.
- **The heart's desire is answered, not denied (v.2).** The seat's own longing and its voiced request are both met — the inner life's deepest wants, when brought to God (Ps 20), are **granted** and even **exceeded** (v.4, "length of days forever").
- **Blessing is prevenient (v.3).** God *comes to meet* (qadam) the inner being with good ahead of the asking — grace anticipates.
- **The gifts terminate in the Presence (v.6).** The chain of benefits resolves not in the benefits but in **joy in God's face** — the same thread as Ps 16:11 and 17:15.
- **The wicked's inner devising is futile (v.11).** The enemy's *planning* and *devising* (M15, the purposing faculty) bent to evil against God **does not succeed** — inner scheming against God recoils into nothing.
