# Psalm 113 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 113:1–9 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked; aheb-fix build). Complete chapter. Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **Lens:** the finding is the inner-being **operation**, God-relation as arena (`feedback_lens_is_inner_being_process_not_god_relation`).
- **Scope honesty up front:** Psalm 113 (opening the Hallel) is a hymn to the LORD who is *exalted above all* yet *stoops to the lowly* — the whole psalm describes *God's* character and action (the arena). Its one inner-being touch-point is the transformed condition of the lifted: the **barren made a *joyous* mother** (v.9), and the lowly raised to *sit with princes* (vv.7–8).

## The reading

**I. The lowly lifted; the barren made a *joyous* mother (vv.7–9).**
"He **raises the poor from the dust** and **lifts the needy from the ash heap**, to make them **sit with princes**… He gives the **barren woman a home**, making her the **joyous** (H8056, M04) **mother of children**" (vv.7–9). The finding: God's lifting of the lowly issues in the barren woman's **joy** — from desolation to a *joyous* motherhood *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's condition is **utterly reversed** — the self that was in the dust / on the ash heap / barren is raised to honour and *gladness*; the outward reversal *terminates in inner joy* ("joyous mother") *(inferred: "raises the poor from the dust… making her the joyous mother" traces God's condescension all the way into the lifted one's *inner state* — the reversal is felt as joy; the lowly self does not merely change place but is *gladdened*; cf. Ps 107:41, 1 Sam 2:8 Hannah's song)*.

## What Psalm 113 says about the inner being
- **God's lifting of the lowly *terminates in inner joy* (vv.7–9).** The self raised from dust, ash heap, barrenness is not merely relocated but *gladdened* — the barren becomes a *joyous* mother; the outward reversal is felt inwardly as joy.

## Honest notes
- **Scope honesty:** this is overwhelmingly a hymn about *God* — his name blessed, his exaltation above the heavens, his stooping to look on the lowly (vv.1–6). The inner-being weight is only the *joyous* mother of v.9 (the felt reversal). I have *not* inflated the God-description into inner-being findings.
- **The high-yet-stooping pattern** (vv.5–7) is a datum about *God's* posture toward human lowliness — the arena in which many inner-being reversals across the Psalter occur (with Ps 138:6, 34:18, 147:6).

- **Characteristics (sanity-checked):** *joyous* (H8056, M04, v.9, the sole inner-being datum). The M22/M08/M07 terms (praise, high, brought-down) describe God's exaltation and condescension — the arena.
