## The reading

**I. "God doesn't see" — self-deception refuted by the Maker of eye and ear (vv.7–9).**
"And they say, '**The LORD does not see**; the God of Jacob does not **perceive** (H0995, M15, *bin*).' Understand, O dullest of the people!… **He who planted the ear, does he not hear? He who formed the eye, does he not see?**" (vv.7–9). The gem-finding: the wicked inner being grants itself license by telling itself **God is blind and deaf** to its evil — "the LORD does not see" *(stated)*. The refutation strikes at the root: **the One who *formed* the faculties of perception cannot lack them** — the Maker of the eye *sees*, the Maker of the ear *hears* *(stated)*. The operation: the wicked's **self-deception about God's perception** (the license "he doesn't see") is exposed by God-as-Maker-of-perception *(inferred: "he who formed the eye, does he not see?" turns the very existence of the self's faculties into evidence that their Maker perceives; cf. Ps 10:11, 50:21's projected/like-me God)*.

**II. Discipline received as blessing (vv.11–13).**
"The LORD knows the **thoughts** of man, that they are but a **breath**… **Blessed** is the man whom you **discipline** (H3256, *yasar*), O LORD, and whom you **teach** (H3925) out of your law" (vv.11–13). Two findings. God **knows the inner thoughts**, and they are *breath* (transient, fully seen) *(stated)*. And, counter-intuitively, God's **discipline is a *blessing*** — "*blessed* is the man whom you discipline" *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **counts God's correction as a good** — the disciplined-and-taught self is *blessed*, not merely chastened *(inferred: "blessed… whom you discipline… teach" makes correction a benefit; the opposite of the flight-from-discipline, Ps 39:13)*.

**III. The anxious soul consoled — cares answered (vv.17–19).**
"If the LORD had not been my help, my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) would soon have lived in the land of **silence**. When I thought, '**My foot slips**,' your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05) **held me up**. When the **cares** (*sarappim* — anxious, divided thoughts) of my **heart** (H3820, M47) are **many**, your **consolations** (*tanchumim*) **cheer** my **soul**" (vv.17–19). The gem-finding: when the inner being's **anxious cares *multiply*** — the heart full of divided, worrying thoughts — God's **consolations *cheer* the soul** *(stated)*. The operation: the **many inner cares** are met and **answered by God's comforts** — the anxious, care-crowded heart is *cheered/delighted* by consolation *(inferred: "the cares of my heart are many → your consolations cheer my soul" pairs the multiplication of anxiety with the consolation that lifts it)*. And at the point of *slipping* ("my foot slips"), it is **steadfast love that *holds up*** the self — catching the inner being precisely as it gives way *(stated)*.

## What Psalm 94 says about the inner being
- **The wicked license themselves by "God doesn't see" — refuted by the Maker of the faculties (vv.7–9).** The self-deception that God is blind to its evil is exposed: the One who *formed* the eye and ear surely perceives. The existence of the self's own faculties is evidence their Maker sees.
- **God's discipline is received as *blessing* (vv.12–13).** The inner being counts correction as a good — "blessed is the man whom you discipline" — the taught, corrected self, not merely chastened.
- **Multiplied cares are answered by God's *consolations* (vv.18–19).** When the heart's anxious thoughts multiply, God's comforts *cheer* the soul; and at the point of slipping, steadfast love *holds up* the self — catching it as it gives way.
