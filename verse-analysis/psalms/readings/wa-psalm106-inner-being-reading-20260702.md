# Psalm 106 — what it tells us about the inner being (Phase-2 chapter reading)

- **Source:** the `lexical-model-2026` Phase-1 lexicals for Psa 106:1–48 (chapter-driven poetic build; roles sanity-checked; aheb-fix build). Complete chapter (7 vv. backfilled with the fixed full-text logic). Lexicals only; cited; grounded; inferences tagged *stated* | *inferred*.
- **Lens:** the finding is the inner-being **operation**, God-relation as arena (`feedback_lens_is_inner_being_process_not_god_relation`).
- **The anatomy of a forgetting, craving, impatient heart — set against a God who remembers.** Psalm 106 is a **confession of Israel's rebellions**, and — unlike a bare recital — it is unusually rich on the inner being, because it *diagnoses* the failures. Its findings are operations of a **failing inner life**: the **failure of memory that breeds rebellion** (vv.7, 13); the **collapse of belief into forgetting** (vv.12–13); the **refusal to *wait* for counsel** (v.13); the **wanton craving that tests God — and is answered to its ruin** (vv.14–15); and the **despising of the gift through unbelief** (v.24).

## The reading

**I. The failure of memory that breeds rebellion (vv.7, 13).**
"Our fathers… **did not consider** (H7919, M15) your wondrous works; **they did not remember** (H2142, M41) the abundance of your steadfast love, but **rebelled** by the sea" (v.7); "But they soon **forgot** his works" (v.13). The gem-finding: the root of the rebellion is a **failure of memory** — *not considering, not remembering* God's works and love — *out of which* the rebellion grows *(stated; "did not remember… but rebelled")*. The operation: the inner being's **lapse of memory is the *seedbed of rebellion*** — when the recollection of God's works and steadfast love goes dark, the self turns to revolt; memory-failure *precedes and enables* the sin *(inferred: the "did not remember… but rebelled" structure makes forgetting the *cause*, not a side-effect — the whole psalm reads rebellion as un-remembering; cf. Ps 78:11 "they forgot his works")*.

**II. The collapse of belief into forgetting (vv.12–13).**
"Then they **believed** (H0539, M13) his words; they sang his praise. **But they soon forgot** his works" (vv.12–13). The gem-finding: faith and praise are **real but short-lived** — belief and song at the Red Sea collapse into forgetting *"soon"* after *(stated)*. The operation: the aroused inner being is **transient** — a genuine belief-and-praise that does not *hold*, dissolving almost at once into forgetfulness *(inferred: "they believed… they sang… but they soon forgot" names the tragic brevity of the roused heart — the problem is not the absence of faith but its *failure to endure*; cf. the stony ground of later Scripture, and Ps 78:36–37 "their heart was not steadfast")*.

**III. The refusal to *wait* for counsel (v.13).**
"they **did not wait** (H2442, M18) for his counsel" (v.13). The finding: the inner being **would not *wait*** for God's counsel *(stated)*. The operation: **impatience** — the self runs *ahead* of God's guidance, unwilling to hold still for the counsel that would order its steps *(inferred: "did not wait for his counsel" makes the failure a refusal to *wait* — the impatient heart that will not defer to God's timing or direction; the negative of the wait-on-the-LORD of Ps 27:14, 40:1)*.

**IV. The wanton craving that tests God — answered to its own ruin (vv.14–15).**
"But they had a **wanton craving** (H8378 / H0183, M28) in the wilderness, and **put God to the test**… he **gave them what they asked**, but sent a **wasting** disease among them" (vv.14–15). The gem-finding: the inner being's **craving** was so intense it **tested God** — *demanding* — and the answer is terrible: *the craving was granted, and it hollowed them out* ("he gave them what they asked, but sent a wasting disease") *(stated)*. The operation: **disordered craving is self-destructive even when satisfied** — the appetite that demands and tests God is *answered*, but the granting itself becomes the judgment, the fulfilled craving wasting the very self that craved *(inferred: "he gave them what they asked, but sent a wasting" welds the *granting* to the *wasting* — the got-what-it-wanted craving is the ruin; cf. Ps 78:29–31, the KJV "leanness into their soul")*.

**V. Despising the gift through unbelief (v.24).**
"Then they **despised the pleasant** (H2532, M28) land, **having no faith** (H0539, M13) in his promise" (v.24). The finding: the inner being **despised the good gift** — the pleasant land — *because it had no faith* in the promise *(stated)*. The operation: **unbelief turns the good gift *contemptible*** — the self, not trusting the promise, *despises* the very thing promised; faith and desire are linked, and where faith fails, the desirable becomes despised *(inferred: "despised the pleasant land, having no faith" makes the unbelief the ground of the contempt — the heart that will not trust cannot value the gift; cf. Num 14)*.

## What Psalm 106 says about the inner being
- **Forgetting is the *seedbed of rebellion* (vv.7, 13).** When the memory of God's works and steadfast love goes dark, the self turns to revolt — the psalm reads rebellion as fundamentally *un-remembering*.
- **The roused inner being is *transient* (vv.12–13).** Belief and praise are real but do not hold — they collapse "soon" into forgetting; the problem is faith's *failure to endure*, not its absence.
- **Impatience refuses to *wait* for counsel (v.13).** The self runs ahead of God's guidance, unwilling to hold still for the counsel that would order its steps.
- **Disordered craving is *self-destructive even when satisfied* (vv.14–15).** The appetite that demands and tests God is answered — and the granting itself becomes the judgment; the fulfilled craving wastes the craver.
- **Unbelief turns the good gift *contemptible* (v.24).** The heart that will not trust the promise despises the very thing promised — faith and valuing are linked.

## Honest notes
- **This closing psalm is *rich*, not thin** — because it is a *diagnostic* confession (not a bare recital like 105). It names the inner mechanisms of failure: un-remembering, transient faith, impatience, craving, unbelief. A cornerstone catalogue of the *failing* inner being (the shadow-side counterpart to the seeking/blessing heart of 103–105).
- **The frame — a God who remembers against a people who forget:** the human failure is un-remembering (vv.7, 13, 21); the resolution is that "he **remembered** his covenant, and relented according to the abundance of his steadfast love" (v.45). The contrast (human forgetting vs divine remembering) is the psalm's architecture — God's memory is the arena/hope against which the inner-being findings are set.
