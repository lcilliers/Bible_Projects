## The reading

**I. Urgency — the inner being at the limit (vv.1, 5).**
"**Make haste** (H2363, *chush*), O God, to deliver me! O Lord, **make haste** to help me!… **hasten** to me, O God!… O Lord, **do not delay**!" (vv.1, 5). The finding: the inner being's need is **time-sensitive** — the *haste*-word tolls three times, closing on "do not delay" *(stated)*. The operation: the self is **at the limit of what it can bear**, pressing not merely for help but for **speed** — the urgency itself discloses an inner being that **can wait no longer** *(inferred: the repeated "make haste… do not delay" makes the *timing* the burden, the self unable to sustain further delay)*. (Notably, where Ps 40 framed this same plea with an opening "I *waited patiently*", Ps 70 is the plea **stripped to its urgency** — the same words at a different inner moment.)

**II. Acknowledged neediness (v.5).**
"But I am **poor** (H6041, *ani*) and **needy** (H0034, *evyon*); … You are my **help** and my **deliverer**" (v.5). The finding: the inner being **names its own poverty** — *poor and needy* — as the ground of its appeal *(stated)*. The operation: the self approaches God **not from strength but from acknowledged lack** — its neediness is the very basis on which it asks *(inferred: "I am poor and needy… you are my help" makes the confessed poverty the reason God is appealed to; cf. Ps 40:17, 34:6)*.

## What Psalm 70 says about the inner being
- **Urgency itself is a disclosure (vv.1, 5).** The threefold "make haste… do not delay" reveals an inner being at the limit — pressing for *speed*, unable to sustain further delay. The *timing* is the burden. (The same plea Ps 40 framed with patient waiting, here stripped to its urgency.)
- **The self appeals from acknowledged *lack* (v.5).** "I am poor and needy" — the inner being approaches God not from strength but from confessed poverty, which is the very ground of its asking.
