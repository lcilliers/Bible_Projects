- **Fearlessness and stillness grounded in an indwelling.** Psalm 46's inner-being findings are two operations: a **fearlessness decoupled from the world's stability** — the self unafraid *even when the world is unmade* (vv.1–3), *not moved* while the mountains move (v.5); and the command to **cease striving** — "be still" (*raphah*, let go / drop the hands) — as the **precondition of knowing God** (v.10). ## The reading

**I. Fearlessness decoupled from the world's stability (vv.1–3).**
"God is our **refuge** (H4268, M19) and **strength** (H5797, M23)… **Therefore we will not fear** (H3372, M01) though the earth give way, though the mountains be **moved** (H4131) into the heart of the sea, though its waters roar" (vv.1–3). The operation: the inner being's fearlessness is a **consequence** — "*therefore* we will not fear" — grounded in God-as-refuge *(stated)*. The finding: the not-fearing extends **even to total cosmic collapse** — the earth dissolving, the mountains falling into the sea — because the self's **ground is God, not the world** *(inferred: the fear is refused precisely at the point where every solid thing gives way, so the stability cannot be the world's)*. The inner being's steadiness is **decoupled from the stability of its surroundings**: even if everything solid is unmade, it does not fear *(inferred)*.

**II. The God-indwelt self is *not moved* while the mountains move (v.5).**
"God is **in the midst** of her; she shall **not be moved** (H4131, *mot*); God will help her when morning dawns" (v.5). The finding, in exact contrast to v.2: the mountains **are moved** (*mot*, v.2), but the God-indwelt self is **not moved** (*mot*, v.5) *(stated: the same verb, opposite outcome)*. The operation: stability comes **from an indwelling presence** ("God is in the midst"), not from external solidity — what steadies the inner being is *who is within it*, while the world outside shakes *(inferred: the not-moved is predicated on "God is in the midst")*.

**III. "Be still" — cease striving as the precondition of knowing (v.10).**
"**Be still** (H7503, M24, *raphah* — let go, drop the hands, cease), and **know** (H3045) that I am God" (v.10). The gem-operation: *raphah* is not merely "be quiet" but **let go / stop striving** — the same verb as Ps 37:8's "*refrain* from anger" — a **dropping of the self's own frantic effort** *(stated)*. And it is joined to *knowing*: "be still, **and** know." The finding: **the cessation is the *condition* of the knowledge** — the inner being must **stop its own struggling** for the knowledge of God to arrive *(inferred: the imperative pairs the ceasing and the knowing — one enables the other; striving must drop for knowing to happen)*. That *raphah* sits in the humility cluster (M24) fits: the ceasing is a **self-lowering**, a release of one's own grip *(inferred)*.

## What Psalm 46 says about the inner being
- **Fearlessness can be *decoupled from the world's stability* (vv.1–3).** The self does not fear *even when the world is unmade*, because its ground is God, not its surroundings. The not-fearing holds precisely where every solid thing gives way.
- **Stability comes from an *indwelling*, not from external solidity (v.5).** The mountains are moved; the God-indwelt self is *not* moved. What steadies the inner being is who is within it, while the world outside shakes.
- **Ceasing is the precondition of knowing (v.10).** "Be still" is *raphah* — let go, drop the hands, stop striving — and it is the condition of knowing God. The inner being must stop its own struggling for the knowledge of God to arrive; the ceasing is a self-lowering release of its grip.
