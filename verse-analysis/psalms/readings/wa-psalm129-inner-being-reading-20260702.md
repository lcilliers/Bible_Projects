## The reading

**I. Afflicted from youth, yet not prevailed against (vv.1–3).**
"Greatly have they **afflicted** (H6887, M24) me **from my youth**… yet **they have not prevailed** against me. The **plowers plowed upon my back**; they made long their furrows" (vv.1–3). The gem-finding: the self testifies to **lifelong affliction** — "from my youth," the back *plowed* into furrows — *and* to **not having been overcome** — "yet they have not prevailed" *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being holds together **long, scarring suffering and *unbroken survival*** — it does not deny the wounds (the plowed back) but declares its endurance: the affliction was *great* and *early* and *lasting*, yet it failed to *defeat* the self *(inferred: "greatly afflicted from my youth… yet they have not prevailed… plowers plowed upon my back" is the self's *defiant resilience* — the honest owning of deep, lifelong wounds *combined with* the testimony that they did not win; the scarred-but-standing self, cf. Ps 124:1–2, 2 Cor 4:8–9 "afflicted… but not crushed")*.

## What Psalm 129 says about the inner being
- **The self can be *lifelong-afflicted yet unconquered* (vv.1–3).** It owns the deep, early, lasting wounds — "the plowers plowed upon my back" — and *at the same time* testifies "they have not prevailed"; scarred, but standing. Endurance that neither denies the suffering nor is defeated by it.

## Honest notes
- **A resilience datum:** the psalm's inner-being value is the *combination* — honest acknowledgement of severe, lifelong affliction held together with unbroken survival (with Ps 124, 2 Cor 4:8–9). Not triumphalism (the wounds are real) but endurance.
