## The reading

**I. Trust performed *in* the fear (vv.3–4, 10–11).**
"When I am **afraid** (H3372, M01), I put my **trust** (H0982, M19) in you. In God… I **trust**; I shall **not be afraid**. What can **flesh** (H1320, seat=flesh — mortal weakness) do to me?" (vv.3–4; repeated vv.10–11). The gem-finding: the inner being **does not wait for the fear to pass before trusting — it trusts *when* afraid** *(stated: "when I am afraid, I put my trust in you")*. The operation, with its mechanism: **afraid → trust → not afraid** — trust is what the self *does with* the fear, and the doing dissolves the fear's grip *(inferred: the sequence runs from "when I am afraid, I trust" to "I shall not be afraid")*. Fear is the **occasion** for trust, not its obstacle; the two coexist and the trusting is the resolving act *(inferred)*.

**II. Trust re-sizes the threat (vv.4, 11).**
"What can **flesh** (H1320) do to me?… What can **man** do to me?" (vv.4, 11). The finding: the trusting inner being **re-measures its attackers** — they are mere *flesh* (basar, the word of mortal frailty) *(stated)*. The operation: trust **shrinks the threat to its true size** — what looked overwhelming (v.1, "man tramples on me all day long") is re-seen as *flesh*, unable to reach the self that trusts God *(inferred: the rhetorical "what can flesh do?" follows the trusting, re-sizing the same attackers of v.1)*.

**III. The tenderly-recorded grief — tossings counted, tears bottled (v.8).**
"You have kept **count** of my **tossings** (H5112, *nod* — restless turnings/wanderings); put my **tears** (H1832, M03, *dimah*) in your **bottle** (*nod* — a wordplay). Are they not in your **book** (H5612)?" (v.8). The gem-finding: the inner being's suffering is **not ignored but preciously recorded** — every sleepless *tossing* numbered, every *tear* stored in a bottle, all written in a book *(stated)*. The operation: the self's grief is **seen and treasured** by God with tender precision — the restless nights counted, the tears kept as something of value *(inferred: counting the tossings and bottling the tears makes the grief precious data, not overlooked pain)*. The Hebrew wordplay (*nod* = wandering / *nod* = skin-bottle) binds the two: **the wanderings counted, the tears kept in the wanderer's flask** *(inferred)*.

**IV. The delivered soul walks in the light of life (v.13).**
"For you have delivered my **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) from **death**… my feet from falling, that I may **walk** before God in the **light of life** (H0216 + H2416)" (v.13). The finding: the deliverance's **end is not mere survival but a *walk before God in the light of life*** — the restored soul lives *in God's presence and light* *(stated)*. The operation: rescue is **for** relationship — the soul brought out of death to *walk before God*, in the light (the light-of-life, cf. Ps 36:9) *(inferred: the "delivered… that I may walk before God" makes the presence-walk the purpose of the rescue)*.

## What Psalm 56 says about the inner being
- **Trust is performed *in* the fear, not after it (vv.3–4).** "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you" — the inner being trusts *while* afraid; the trusting is what it does with the fear, and the doing dissolves the fear's grip. Fear is the occasion for trust, not its obstacle.
- **Trust re-sizes the threat (vv.4, 11).** The attackers who "trample all day long" are re-seen as mere *flesh* — trust shrinks the threat to its true size, unable to reach the God-trusting self.
- **Grief is precisely counted and treasured (v.8).** Every restless tossing numbered, every tear bottled, all booked — the inner being's suffering is seen and kept as something of value, not overlooked.
- **Rescue is *for* the presence-walk (v.13).** The soul delivered from death is delivered *to* walk before God in the light of life — survival's end is relationship, life in God's presence and light.
