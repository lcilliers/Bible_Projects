- **Joy so great it feels *dreamlike*; tears *sown* that reap joy.** Psalm 126 is a pilgrim song of restoration. Its inner-being findings: the **overwhelming, dreamlike joy** of deliverance (vv.1–3); and the profound principle of **sorrow *sown* in tears that reaps a harvest of joy** (vv.5–6).

## The reading

**I. Joy so great it feels *dreamlike* (vv.1–3).**
"When the LORD restored the fortunes of Zion, **we were like those who dream**. Then our **mouth was filled with laughter**, and our tongue with **shouts of joy**… The LORD has done great things for us; **we are glad** (H8056, M04)" (vv.1–3). The gem-finding: the deliverance is so great that the joy feels ***unreal* — "like those who dream"** — overflowing into laughter and shouting *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's joy at a great restoration is **so overwhelming it borders on *disbelief*** — the self can hardly credit it ("like those who dream"), and the gladness spills past containment into laughter and shouts; a joy too big for the self to fully take in *(inferred: "we were like those who dream… our mouth filled with laughter" figures a delight that exceeds the self's capacity to believe it — the stunned, laughing joy of the too-good-to-be-true; cf. Gen 21:6 Sarah's laughter, Luke 24:41 "disbelieved for joy")*.

**II. Tears *sown* that reap joy (vv.5–6).**
"Those who **sow in tears** (H1832, M03) shall **reap with shouts of joy**! He who goes out **weeping**, bearing the seed for sowing, shall come home with **shouts of joy**, bringing his sheaves with him" (vv.5–6). The gem-finding — one of the Psalter's deepest: **sorrow *sown* becomes joy *reaped*** — the weeping that carries the seed returns as harvest-gladness *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's **grief, borne faithfully *through*, is *transformed* into future joy** — tears are not wasted but *sown*, an investment whose harvest is gladness; the self weeps *now* in hope of reaping *then*, and the sorrow itself is the seed of the coming joy *(inferred: "sow in tears… reap with shouts of joy; goes out weeping… comes home with shouts of joy" makes present sorrow a *seed* — grief endured in faithfulness yields a harvest of joy; the tears are not the end but the planting; a profound reframing of suffering as *fruitful*, cf. John 16:20–21, Ps 30:5 "weeping may tarry for the night, but joy comes with the morning")*.

## What Psalm 126 says about the inner being
- **Great restoration produces a *dreamlike, disbelieving joy* (vv.1–3).** A delight so overwhelming the self can hardly credit it — "like those who dream" — spilling past containment into laughter and shouts; joy too big to fully take in.
- **Sorrow *sown* is joy *reaped* (vv.5–6).** Grief borne faithfully *through* is transformed into future gladness — tears are not wasted but *planted*, an investment whose harvest is joy; the self weeps now in hope of reaping then.
