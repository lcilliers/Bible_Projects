- **The first penitential psalm** — a lament that reads the inner being in **anguish**, then in the **sudden turn to confidence that God has heard**.

## The reading
Psalm 6 lets the inner being come apart under distress, and then names the single fact that reassembles it.

**I. The dread of God's anger (v.1).**
The opening is not guilt so much as **dread of chastening**: "O LORD, rebuke me not in your anger, nor discipline me in your **wrath** (H2534, M02)." The inner being's first movement is fear of the God it appeals to *(inferred: the negated plea shows the fear)*.

**II. The whole self undone (vv.2–3, 6–7).**
The anguish is **total and embodied**: "be **gracious** (H2603, M39) to me… my bones are **troubled** (H0926, M01). My **soul** (H5315, the seat — M47) is **greatly** troubled" (vv.2–3) — the intensity marker *very* attaches to the soul's turmoil *(stated)*. It floods the body: "I am weary with my **moaning** (H0585, M03); every night I flood my bed with **weeping** (H1832, M03)… my eye wastes away because of **grief** (H3708, M02)" (vv.6–7). The inner being's distress is read across **bones, soul, and eye** — a person dissolving in tears, night after night *(inferred: the physical images carry the inner state)*. Note the same verb *bahal* ("troubled") that God aims at the nations in Psa 2:5 is here turned inward on the psalmist's own soul.

**III. The appeal — for grace, healing, rescue, for steadfast love's sake (vv.4–5).**
"Turn, O LORD, **deliver** my **life** (H5315, M47); **save** me for the sake of your **steadfast love** (H2617, M05)" (v.4). The plea is grounded not in merit but in **God's chesed**, and sharpened by the loss death would bring: in Sheol "who will **give you praise** (H3034, M22)?" (v.5) — the inner being pleads to go on *knowing and praising God* *(inferred)*.

**IV. The turn — the LORD has heard (vv.8–10).**
Then, abruptly, the mood inverts on a single fact: "Depart from me, all you workers of **evil** (H0205, M10), for the LORD has **heard** (H8085, M41) the sound of my **weeping** (H1065, M03). The LORD has **heard** my **plea** (H8467, M21); the LORD accepts my prayer" (vv.8–9). What was begged (to be heard) is now **grasped as accomplished** — and the same *trouble* that gripped the psalmist's soul is turned on the enemies: "all my **enemies** shall be **ashamed** (H0954, M07) and greatly **troubled** (H0926, M01)" (v.10).

## What Psalm 6 says about the inner being
- The inner being can be **wholly undone** — bones, soul, and eye giving way, tears night after night — and Scripture lets that anguish be voiced without resolution for most of the psalm.
- Its rescue turns on **one fact grasped**: *the LORD has heard*. The mood inverts not because circumstances change but because being-heard is seized as certain (vv.8–9) — the counterpart to the pleas of Psalm 5.
- The appeal rests on **God's steadfast love** (v.4), not the sufferer's worth — and even fears death chiefly as the *end of praising God* (v.5): the inner being wants to go on *knowing* him.
- The distress verb *bahal* ("troubled") moves across the psalm from the psalmist's **soul** (v.3) to his **enemies** (v.10) — the same inner turmoil, reassigned once God has heard.
