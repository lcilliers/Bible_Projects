- **Love born of being *heard*; the self calling itself back to *rest*; faith held while afflicted.** Psalm 116 is a thanksgiving for deliverance from death. Its inner-being findings: **love arising *because* God heard** (vv.1–2); the self **commanding its soul to *return to its rest*** (v.7); **faith held even in the midst of affliction** (vv.10–11); and the **grateful self seeking what to *render*** (v.12).

## The reading

**I. Love born of being heard (vv.1–2).**
"**I love** (H0157, *aheb*, M05) the LORD, because he has **heard my voice** and my **pleas for mercy**. Because he **inclined his ear** to me, therefore I will **call** on him as long as I live" (vv.1–2). The gem-finding: the self's **love for God *arises from* having been *heard*** — "I love… *because* he has heard" *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being's love is **evoked by God's *attentiveness*** — love as a *response* to being listened to; because God inclined his ear, the self both *loves* and resolves to *keep calling* — the answered prayer *generates ongoing devotion* *(inferred: "I love the LORD because he has heard… therefore I will call" makes the love reactive-and-generative — born of being heard, and issuing in lifelong calling; love here is not abstract but the grateful bond of one who has been *attended to*; cf. 1 John 4:19 "we love because he first loved")*.

**II. The self calling itself back to its rest (v.7).**
"**Return, O my soul** (H5315, M47), to your **rest**; for the LORD has dealt **bountifully** with you" (v.7). The gem-finding: the self **addresses its own soul, commanding it to *return to rest*** — on the ground that God has dealt bountifully *(stated)*. The operation: the inner being **governs itself back into *settledness*** — after the turmoil of near-death (vv.3, 8), the self *recalls its soul* from agitation to its "rest," grounding the command in God's proven bounty *(inferred: "Return, O my soul, to your rest… he has dealt bountifully" is self-address in the mode of *stilling* — the self leading its own soul back from distress to repose, the counterpart to the self-rousing of Ps 103:1; the settledness is *argued* from remembered goodness; cf. Ps 62:5 "for God alone, O my soul, wait in silence", 42:5)*.

**III. Faith held even in affliction; the alarmed judgment (vv.10–11).**
"**I believed** (H0539, M13), even when I spoke: 'I am greatly **afflicted** (H6031, M24)'; I said in my **alarm**, 'All mankind are liars'" (vv.10–11). The gem-finding: the self **kept believing *while* declaring itself greatly afflicted** — faith and honest complaint coexisting — and, in *alarm*, made a sweeping bitter judgment *(stated)*. The operation: **faith persists *through* affliction, not by denying it** — "I believed, *even when* I said 'I am greatly afflicted'"; the inner being holds trust and honest anguish *together*; and it notes its own *alarmed overstatement* ("all mankind are liars") as the distortion panic works *(inferred: "I believed, even when I spoke… I said in my alarm" shows faith surviving alongside voiced distress, and a self honest enough to flag its own panic-driven judgment as hasty; cf. Ps 31:22 "I had said in my alarm, I am cut off")*.

**IV. The grateful self seeking what to render (v.12).**
"**What shall I render to the LORD** for all his **benefits** to me?" (v.12). The finding: the delivered self asks what it can **give back** for all God's benefits *(stated)*. The operation: gratitude issuing in the impulse to **respond / reciprocate** — the inner being, overwhelmed by benefit, *searches for a fitting return* (the cup of salvation, the vows, the thanksgiving sacrifice, vv.13–17) *(inferred: "what shall I render… for all his benefits" is gratitude become active — the self not passive in receiving but moved to answer; cf. Ps 103:2 "forget not all his benefits", here the benefits provoke a *response*)*.

## What Psalm 116 says about the inner being
- **Love is *born of being heard* (vv.1–2).** The self loves God *because* he inclined his ear — love as the grateful response of one attended to, issuing in lifelong calling.
- **The self *calls its own soul back to rest* (v.7).** Self-address in the mode of stilling — leading the soul from near-death agitation back to its repose, grounded in God's proven bounty. (The counterpart to the self-*rousing* of 103:1.)
- **Faith *persists through* affliction (vv.10–11).** "I believed, even when I said 'I am greatly afflicted'" — trust and honest anguish held together; and the self flags its own alarmed overstatement as panic's distortion.
- **Gratitude seeks *what to render* (v.12).** The delivered self is not passive in receiving but moved to *answer* — searching for a fitting return for all God's benefits.
