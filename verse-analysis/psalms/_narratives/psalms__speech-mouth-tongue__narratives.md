**tongue** — Psa 5:9  ·  `H3956:tongu#1`

_Story_

This psalm turns its gaze on the psalmist's enemies and looks straight past their words into what they are inside. Their mouths, it says, hold no truth; their throat is like an open grave; their tongue flatters. The picture is of people whose whole inner life is dishonest - not a slip or a bad mood, but a settled, thought-out falseness. The smoothness of their speech is exactly the problem: the flattery is the disguise that hides something deadly underneath. The passage does not explain what made them this way or what they hope to gain; it simply reports the rot as fact. Their pleasant words and their inner cruelty are one thing, and it lives deep in them, a permanent condition rather than a passing act. It is the picture of a person who speaks sweetly and means death.

**prevail** — Psa 12:4  ·  `H1396:prevail#1`

_Story_

Here the psalm listens to the arrogant talk of people who trust in their own words. With our tongue, they say, we will win; our lips belong to us - who is lord over us? It is a deliberate boast, a chosen stance, not a slip of temper. What they are really claiming is total ownership of their own speech: they will say what they like, answer to no one, and expect their words alone to carry the day. The pride sits deep inside them, and it takes shape precisely in the mouth. The passage does not soften this or look for an excuse behind it; it lets the boast stand in its ugliness - people so sure of their own tongues that they recognise no master above them at all. It is pride that has made a god of its own voice.

**tell** — Psa 22:22  ·  `H5608:tell#1`

_Story_

This is the great turning-point of a psalm that began in utter abandonment. The same voice that cried out feeling forsaken now makes a promise: I will tell your name to my brothers; in the middle of the gathered people I will praise you. Something has shifted. The despairing cry becomes a vow to speak - to make God's name known to others. It is a decision, a resolve of the will, and its content is God himself: his name is what will be told. The passage does not explain exactly how the change came; it simply lets the turn happen, from a man crushed to a man determined to testify. That resolve now lives inside him as settled purpose. It is the picture of someone who has come through the dark and cannot keep quiet about it - who must gather others and tell them what God is.

_Reading_

Seat (104) is none: the telling is not organ-lodged; the whole self resolves, not a faculty. *Why this reading differs:* This is the first of seven distinct readings of H5608 (tell/recount, saphar) in the family, and together they map how testimony works. Here at Psa 22:22 the telling is the dramatic HINGE of a lament - dereliction turning into proclamation, a willed vow located as an inner state (internal:ib-state). The passage does not add motive beyond the greatness being celebrated; it simply shows worship spilling over into speech. It is the picture of a person so full of God's greatness that he has to announce it - taking his place in a long line of voices, each declaring to the next how mighty and awesome God is.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is declaring God's awesome deeds - the self proclaiming his greatness. Type (102) is volition: an act of the will, a resolve to declare, not a feeling. Source (103) is absent: no interior spring is booked; the declaring overflows from praise but its origin is unfiled. Seat (104) is none: the declaring is not organ-lodged; the whole self declares. Bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred): the interior of the one extolling God the King. Operation (106) is the event - the self declares God's mighty, awesome deeds: proclamation of his greatness. Target (107) is proclamation (inferred): the aim is the declaring of God's greatness. Manner (108) is none - no mode is coloured. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: no degree, no narrowing, no booked result. Coupling (112) is declare-awesome: the declaring is welded to the awesome deeds it names. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114) names it: the interior overflows into public telling. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state: the impulse to declare is a condition of the inner being. The full process: from no named source and no organ-seat, the psalmist declares God's mighty, awesome deeds - the declaring welded to those deeds as its content, aimed at proclaiming his greatness, held within as a disposition of the worshipping interior: one generation speaking of God's works to another, praise so full it cannot stay private but overflows into public telling of how great and awesome God is.

*Why this reading differs:* See the variation note at Psa 96:3 (H5608:declar#1). This third saphar-declare reading foregrounds OVERFLOW ACROSS GENERATIONS: the psalmist declares God's greatness as one generation commends his works to another, praise too full to stay private. Where Psa 96:3 faces the nations and Psa 102:21 gathers in Zion, this reading sets the declaring within the succession of generations extolling God the King.

## Psa 145:15-21

> Psa 145:15 The eyes of all look to you, and you give them their food in due season . Psa 145:16 You open your hand ; you satisfy the desire of every living thing . Psa 145:17 The Lord is righteous in all his ways and kind in all his works . Psa 145:18 The Lord is near to all who call on him, to all who call on him in truth . Psa 145:19 He fulfills the desire of those who fear him; he also hears their cry and saves them . Psa 145:20 The Lord preserves all who love him, but all the wicked he will destroy . Psa 145:21 My mouth will speak the praise of the Lord , and let all flesh bless his holy name forever and ever .

**mouth** — Psa 145:21  ·  `H6310:mouth#2`

_Story_

The long alphabet-song of praise ends by opening its arms as wide as they will go. My mouth will speak the praise of the Lord, the psalmist says - and let all flesh bless his holy name forever and ever. He begins with his own single voice and then widens it to take in every living thing, for all time. The praise wells up out of him with real feeling and refuses to stay contained; one mouth becomes the seed of a worship meant to fill the whole of creation. The passage does not add motive beyond the goodness of the God being praised; it simply lets the song swell outward without end. And it is worth setting beside that other prayer about the mouth, where the psalmist asked for a guard over his lips: here the very same mouth is flung wide open - not watched against harm now, but pouring out a praise it hopes every creature everywhere will one day join.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is all flesh blessing God's name - the self's praise widened to all creation. Type (102) is affect: the closing resolve is charged with feeling, praise welling up, not a bare deed or cold report. Source (103) is absent: no interior spring is booked; the resolve simply crowns the acrostic. Seat (104) is none: though the mouth is named, the operation is not organ-seated - the whole self praises, the mouth its outlet not its seat. Bearer (105) is the psalmist (inferred): the interior of the one who has extolled God through the whole psalm. Operation (106) is the closing event - the self's mouth speaks praise, and all flesh will bless God's holy name forever. Target (107) is universal-praise (inferred): the aim is praise widened to every creature. Manner (108) is none - no mode is coloured, though 'forever' colours the sense. Intensity (109), specifier (110) and effect (111) are absent: no degree, no narrowing, no booked result. Coupling (112) is all-flesh-bless: the self's praise is welded to the blessing of all flesh, one voice opening into all. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114) names it: the acrostic closes by widening the self's praise to all creation. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state: the impulse to praise is a condition of the inner being that opens outward to all. The full process: from no named source and no organ-seat, the psalmist's mouth speaks the praise of the LORD and calls all flesh to bless his holy name forever - the self's praise welded to the praise of all creation, aimed at a universal and unending worship, rising from within and opening outward: the single mouth become the seed of a praise meant to fill every creature for ever. Set against Psa 141:3, where the mouth was placed under guard, here the same organ is thrown wide open in praise.

*Why this reading differs:* See the variation note at Psa 141:3 (H6310:mouth#1). This second reading of 'mouth' is the OPEN pole against the guarded pole of Psa 141:3: there the mouth is sentried as a danger; here it is thrown wide in praise and widened to all flesh blessing God's name forever. The same organ is restrained in one reading and made the fountain of universal, unending praise in the other - the two extremes the mouth can occupy in the inner life.
