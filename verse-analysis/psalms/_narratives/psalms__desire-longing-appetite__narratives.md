## Psa 1:1-6

> Psa 1:1 Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of the wicked , nor stands in the way of sinners , nor sits in the seat of scoffers ; Psa 1:2 but his delight is in the law of the Lord , and on his law he meditates day and night . Psa 1:3 He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season , and its leaf does not wither . In all that he does , he prospers . Psa 1:4 The wicked are not so , but are like chaff that the wind drives away. Psa 1:5 Therefore the wicked will not stand in the judgment , nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous ; Psa 1:6 for the Lord knows the way of the righteous , but the way of the wicked will perish .

**delight** — Psa 1:2  ·  `H2656:delight#1`

_Story_

In this opening psalm the truly happy man is described first by what he refuses - he keeps no company with the mocking and the corrupt - and then, more deeply, by what he loves: God's instruction is his joy. His pleasure has simply moved house, away from evil and onto the good. The poem does not tell us how this love began or where in him it sits; it does not measure how strong it is or spell out what it produces. It only says the love is his, and real, and that it is this delight - not grim duty - that keeps him poring over God's words all day and all night. What the man wants has been quietly retrained toward the right thing.

_Reading_

The item is delight (chephets), read as sense 'delight in the law' and typed as affect (101/102): an inclination of feeling, not an act of will. Its bearer is the blessed man of v1 (105), and the operation (106) is the positive counterpart to the three refusals that open the psalm - he does not walk, stand, or sit with the wicked; instead his delight is fixed on the LORD's law, desire relocated to the good. The target is delight itself, the settled object of the affection (107), welded to it by the coupling 'delight-in-law' (112). No source is recorded (103 absent): the psalm does not say what kindled this love, only that it is his. No seat is given (104 none) - the delight is not lodged in a named organ but simply owned by the man; no manner is marked (108 none) - it is delight plainly. Intensity, specifier, effect, and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113): the verse states the affection and its object but not its degree, its precise shade, its downstream fruit, or any forbidding of it - the fruit-image of v3 lies outside this row. The discovery (114) fixes the finding: the interior does not merely avoid evil, it positively loves the law, and this affection fuels the day-and-night meditation. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* This reading is delight fixed on God's law as a standing affection of the righteous man; #2 (16:3) turns the same delight toward the godly community, and #3 (111:2) toward God's works studied - the object of the pleasure shifts from law to people to deeds.

## Psa 4:1-5

> To the choirmaster : with stringed instruments . A Psalm of David . Psa 4:1 Answer me when I call , O God of my righteousness ! You have given me relief when I was in distress . Be gracious to me and hear my prayer ! Psa 4:2 O men , how long shall my honor be turned into shame ? How long will you love vain words and seek after lies ? Selah Psa 4:3 But know that the Lord has set apart the godly for himself; the Lord hears when I call to him . Psa 4:4 Be angry , and do not sin ; ponder in your own hearts on your beds , and be silent . Selah Psa 4:5 Offer right sacrifices , and put your trust in the Lord . Psa 4:6 There are many who say, “Who will show us some good? Lift up the light of your face upon us, O Lord!”

**good** — Psa 4:6  ·  `H2896:good#1`

_Story_

Around the praying psalmist stands a restless crowd, and their inner life shows in one weary question: who will show us anything good? It is a hunger that reaches out but lands on nothing in particular - a craving for benefit with no address. The poem does not say where this longing comes from or how fierce it is, and it never tells us whether it is ever satisfied; it simply leaves the many wanting. That empty, drifting appetite is set against the psalmist himself, whose own joy has found its home in the light of God's face. The difference is the point: one hunger wanders, the other has come to rest.

_Reading_

Here 'good' (tob) is read not as a quality but as a craving after benefit: sense 'the many who crave some good', typed as volition (101/102) - an act of wanting rather than a feeling. The bearer is 'the many' (105), the anonymous crowd of v6, and the operation (106) is their restless question, 'Who will show us any good?' - a diffuse, unanchored reaching for benefit. The target is that very restless-craving (107), bound by the coupling 'who-shows-good' (112). No source is named (103 absent): the crowd's hunger has no stated origin, which is itself telling - it floats free, attached to no object. No seat (104 none): the craving is ascribed to the group as such, not organ-located; no manner (108 none): it is bare wanting. Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition all absent (109/110/111/113) - the verse gives the appetite but not its measure or its issue. The discovery (114) draws the governing contrast: the interior of the many is a vague, unanchored hunger for good, set over against the psalmist's own located joy in the light of God's face. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* This reading takes tob as an aimless craving for 'some good' in the restless crowd; the other tob readings are settled convictions - good found in God alone (16:2), nearness to God as one's good (73:28), one's own good repaid with evil (109:5), the good who receive God's good (125:4), the goodness of brothers in unity (133:1). Here alone 'good' names a hunger rather than a possession.

**straight** — Psa 5:8  ·  `H3474:straight#1`

_Story_

Hemmed in by watching enemies, the psalmist asks God for something more particular than escape: he wants the road ahead made plain and level. His longing is not just to be saved but to see clearly where to walk, to have no ambiguity under his feet while foes surround him. The poem does not explain what stirred this desire or how intense it burns, and it stops before telling us whether the smooth path was granted. It simply shows a man whose deepest wish, under pressure, is for a straight way - he craves guidance as much as rescue.

_Reading_

The item is 'straight' (yashar), read as sense 'longing for the straightened way' and typed as volition (101/102) - a wanting, here the desire for a clear path. The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is his petition that God lead him in righteousness and make the way straight before him amid his enemies. The target is that desire-for-guidance (107), coupled 'make-way-straight' (112). No source is recorded (103 absent) - the longing arises from his exposed situation, unattributed. No seat (104 none): the desire is not placed in a bodily organ; no manner (108 none): it is asked plainly. Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition all absent (109/110/111/113) - the verse voices the want without grading it or reporting its fulfilment. The discovery (114) sharpens it: the interior longs not merely for rescue but for a level, unambiguous path through the enemies' terrain - it wants clarity, not only safety. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).

**desire** — Psa 10:17  ·  `H8378:desir#1`

_Story_

The psalm has just exposed the inner voice of the oppressor, who tells himself God has forgotten and will never call him to account. Against that, it lifts up the inner life of the crushed and the helpless - and their longing is the one God actually stoops to hear. He listens to their desire, steadies their trembling hearts, and turns his ear toward them. The poem does not say what first kindled this longing or how strong it runs, and it does not spell out every result; it fixes on the single fact that God attends to it. The oppressor's heart talks itself into safety; the sufferer's heart is heard.

_Reading_

'Desire' (taavah) here is read as sense 'the afflicted's desire heard' and typed as affect (101/102). The bearer is the afflicted (105) - the crushed and helpless of the psalm - and the operation (106) is that the LORD hears their desire, strengthens their heart, and inclines his ear: the longing of the crushed is answered. The target is that answered-longing (107), coupled 'desire-heard' (112). No source is named (103 absent): the desire wells up from affliction itself, its origin unstated. No seat (104 none): though v17 speaks of the heart being strengthened, the desire-row itself assigns no organ-seat to the desire; no manner (108 none). Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113) - the verse reports the desire and its hearing but does not grade the longing or detail its result beyond the strengthening named in the operation. The discovery (114) sets the finding against the psalm's other interior: over against the wicked's inner life (who says in his heart 'God has forgotten'), the afflicted's inner longing is the one God bends down to and steadies. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* This reading is the afflicted's God-heard longing that God answers; #2 (21:2) is the king's heart's-desire granted, and #3 (112:10) is the wicked's desire that perishes unfulfilled - the same word runs from answered longing, to satisfied longing, to frustrated longing depending on whose it is.

## Psa 16:1-11

**good** — Psa 16:2  ·  `H2896:good#2`

_Story_

The psalmist turns to God and says something total: apart from you I have nothing good. It is not a burst of feeling but a considered conviction - a decision about where his whole welfare lies. Everything he would call good he has relocated onto God alone, so that nothing outside God still counts as benefit to him. The poem does not say what brought him to this settled mind, where in him it rests, or how forcefully he holds it; it simply states the confession. Where an anxious crowd elsewhere merely wonders who will show them any good, this man already knows: only God.

_Reading_

'Good' (tob) here is read as sense 'no good apart from you' and typed as cognition (101/102) - not a craving but a confession, an act of the knowing mind. The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is his declaration to the LORD that he has no good apart from him: all his welfare is located in God alone. The target is total-dependence (107), coupled 'no-good-apart' (112). No source is recorded (103 absent). No seat (104 none): the confession is not lodged in a named organ; no manner (108 none). Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113). The discovery (114) names the operation as a radical relocation of the good: nothing outside God counts as the self's benefit. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116). The type is worth marking - unlike the crowd's craving-good of 4:6 (volition), this is cognition, a settled judgement rather than a restless want.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the tob readings this one is a reasoned confession that God is the only good (cognition), distinct from the crowd's aimless craving (4:6, volition) and from the later settled reckoning that nearness to God is best (73:28). Here 'good' is a claim about where all benefit is located.

**delight** — Psa 16:3  ·  `H2656:delight#2`

_Story_

The psalmist tells us where his heart's pleasure goes: to the good and faithful people around him, the ones he calls excellent. They are the company he delights in, and his choice of them is shaped by their holiness. The poem does not explain how this affection arose or how deep it runs; it simply names the object of his joy. And it sets that joy in sharp relief - for the very next breath speaks of those who chase after other gods and only multiply their own griefs. His delight has picked its people.

_Reading_

'Delight' (chephets) here is read as sense 'delight in the saints' and typed as affect (101/102). The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is that the saints in the land, the excellent ones, are those in whom is all his delight - joy taken in godly company. The target is holy-fellowship (107), coupled 'delight-in-saints' (112). No source (103 absent), no seat (104 none), no manner (108 none). Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113). The discovery (114): the interior's pleasure is fixed on the godly; its chosen company of joy is selected by holiness - which the very next verse contrasts with the multiplied sorrows of those who run after another god. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* Same word as 1:2 and 111:2, but here delight rests on the godly community rather than on God's law (1:2) or God's works (111:2) - the affection keeps its shape while its object moves from text, to people, to deeds.

**lines** — Psa 16:6  ·  `H2256:lin#1`

_Story_

Using the picture of a surveyor's cord marking out a plot of land, the psalmist says his measured-out portion has landed in lovely places - and he is glad of it. This is contentment with the actual shape of his life, the very circumstances handed to him, received as beautiful rather than resented. The poem does not say what produced this settled gladness or how strongly he feels it. It is worth noticing this is a slightly different thing from calling God his portion a verse earlier: here he is happy not only with God but with the plain facts of the life God has allotted him.

_Reading_

'Lines' (chebel, the measuring-line and so the allotted portion) is read as sense 'a pleasant lot gladly owned' and typed as affect (101/102). The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is that the boundary lines have fallen for him in pleasant places, and he owns his inheritance as beautiful - contentment with what he has been given. The target is contentment (107), coupled 'pleasant-lines' (112). No source (103 absent), no seat (104 none), no manner (108 none). Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113). The discovery (114) draws a fine distinction: this is not the same as taking God as one's portion (v5) - it is the glad acceptance of the actual circumstances of one's life, the concrete lot itself received as good. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).

**desired** — Psa 19:10  ·  `H2530:desir#1`

_Story_

The psalmist does something with God's commands that we usually do only with money: he prices them. Set beside the finest gold, God's rules are worth more to him; set beside honey fresh from the comb, they taste sweeter. His inner life here is an act of weighing and choosing - and the word wins. The poem does not tell us where in him this desire sits or how it first grew, and it puts no number on its strength beyond the comparison itself. But the verdict is clear: given the choice between treasure and truth, he wants the truth more.

_Reading_

'Desired' (chamad) is read as sense 'desire the law above gold' and typed as affect (101/102). The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is that the rules of the LORD are more to be desired than much fine gold and sweeter than honey - a valuing that outbids treasure. Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113) - notably no intensity marker, though the comparison itself ('than much fine gold') carries the weight the intensity-row leaves blank. The discovery (114) names the operation as appraisal: the interior weighs the word against wealth and prefers it. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).

_Story_

The king had a deepest wish, and God gave it to him - the very thing his heart wanted, the very request his lips made, held back in no part. Here the inner longing is not thwarted or merely heard but wholly satisfied. The poem does not say what the desire was made of, or where it lived in him, or how it burned; it simply reports that it was met, in full and without reservation. _Reading_

'Desire' (taavah) here is read as sense 'the heart's desire granted' and typed as affect (101/102). The bearer is the king (105); the operation (106) is that God has given the king his heart's desire and not withheld the request of his lips - a longing fully met. ## Psa 23:1-6

> A Psalm of David . Psa 23:1 The Lord is my shepherd ; I shall not want . Psa 23:2 He makes me lie down in green pastures . He leads me beside still waters . Psa 23:3 He restores my soul . He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name’s sake. Psa 23:5 You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies ; you anoint my head with oil ; my cup overflows . Psa 23:6 Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life , and I shall dwell in the house of the Lord forever .

**want** — Psa 23:1  ·  `H2637:want#1`

_Story_

In a collection of psalms full of hunger and longing, this one names the opposite: I shall not want. Because the LORD is his shepherd, the psalmist's inner life is emptied of craving - no gnawing lack, no anxious reaching for what he needs, because he is being led to pasture and water and simply has enough. The poem does not measure this peace or say exactly how it took hold; it states it as a settled fact of the shepherded life. Where others in these psalms thirst and pant, this heart rests. Being cared for has quieted the appetite.

_Reading_

'Want' (chaser, to lack) is read as sense 'I shall not want' and typed as affect (101/102) - here the affect of settled sufficiency, the felt absence of craving. The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is that because the LORD shepherds him, the self lacks nothing - a contentment that rests in being led and fed. The target is contentment (107), coupled 'shall-not-want' (112). No source (103 absent), no seat (104 none), no manner (108 none). Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113). The discovery (114): the operation is settled sufficiency - the interior, under a shepherd, is freed from craving and anxiety about need. Within a family of desire and longing, this reading is a striking inversion: the item is the stilling of desire. Role = characteristic (115); locus = internal:ib-state (116).
