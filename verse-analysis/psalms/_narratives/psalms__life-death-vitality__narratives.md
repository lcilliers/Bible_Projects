**life** — Psa 49:8  ·  `H5315:life#1`

_Story_

This song stares hard at what a human life is worth, and the answer is disquieting: it cannot be bought. No sum of money - not even the great wealth the boastful put their trust in - can buy a single person out of the grave. The life here is simply something every person holds, a possession lodged deep in the self, and the poem pointedly refuses to say it comes from anywhere human hands can reach. It does nothing and reaches for nothing; it just waits, the one thing money was supposed to be able to fix and never can. The price is impossibly high, and the bargain never closes - and that unclosed bargain, that silence where a rescue should be, is the whole message. Even the rich fool's life is beyond his own price.

_Reading_

At Psa 49:8 the inner-being characteristic is life (H5315), read from 'the ransom of their life is costly and can never suffice' - nefesh, the soul/life (sense 101, 7651041). Its type is status (102, 7651042): here life is not an act but a standing, a possessed condition-of-being. Uniquely within this whole family the reading records a seat: the soul as the self (104, 7651043), a self-referential span (279406->279406), matched by locus internal:seat (116, 7651049) - life is lodged in the person as such, the interior seat of the being. The bearer is any man, his life unransomable (105, 7651044): not the psalmist alone but the generic human, and pointedly the wealthy fool of vv6-13 who trusts his riches (directive 5 - the descent is read at full weight: it is precisely the rich boaster whose priceless life money cannot buy). Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the psalm never names where this life originates - the silence fits the argument, that no human agency, least of all wealth, produces or secures it. Operation is none (106, 7651045): the verse assigns the life no act - it does nothing; it is acted upon (or rather, fails to be acted upon), and that passivity before the pit is the point. Target is none (107, 7651046): the life reaches toward nothing; it is the object of a failed transaction, not an agent. Manner is costly, can never suffice (108, 7651047) - the single qualitative note, and a heavy one: the price of this nefesh lies beyond all payment. Intensity, specifier and effect are all present:false (109/110/111): no degree is measured, no delimiter added, and - tellingly - no outcome logged, because the ransom never completes; the empty effect-slot is itself the finding, the deal that never closes. Coupling is none (112, 7651048): this life is welded to no other inner movement here - it stands isolated in its unbuyable-ness, coupled to nothing. Prohibition is absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7651050) names it: the priceless, unbuyable soul - nefesh no wealth can redeem from the pit, beyond all human price. Role is characteristic (115, 7651051).

*Why this reading differs:* Of the eight life-readings this is the outlier: no enemy hunts it and no God rescues it. It is the generic human life (any man, the rich fool of vv6-13 included), the ONLY reading carrying a recorded seat (soul-as-self) and locus internal:seat, and the ONLY one whose manner is 'costly / can never suffice.' Where 54:3/56:6/59:3 make life the hunted object and 54:4/86:2/121:7 make it the kept object, here it is the un-transactable status - beyond price, coupled to nothing, acting on nothing.

**life** — Psa 54:3  ·  `H5315:life#2`

_Story_

Here the singer is a hunted man: ruthless people are after his very life. That life is the core of who he is, the target of their pursuit, and the poem does not dress it up as anything more than his own threatened self. It does nothing here - it is the thing being chased, not the one doing the chasing. Yet the poem quietly links this endangered life to the next breath of the song, where God is named as the one who holds that same life up. The self under threat and the self about to be sustained are one and the same person - the danger and the rescue meet in a single life.

_Reading_

At Psa 54:3 the characteristic is life (H5315), read from 'ruthless men seek my life' - nefesh, life/soul (sense 101, 7653300). Type is status (102, 7653301): life as a standing, the contested centre of the self, not an act it performs. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the passage does not name where this life comes from; it is simply presupposed as the psalmist's own. Seat is none (104, 7653302): the endangered life is not lodged in any inner organ - it reads as the whole self at risk, not a heart or bones. The bearer is the psalmist (105, 7653303). The operation is sought - by the enemies (106, 7653304): the life is the object the ruthless men hunt; the movement runs from the enemies inward onto the self, the psalmist's nefesh the prize of the pursuit (God and the enemies are the arena the self stands within). Target is none (107, 7653305): the life itself reaches toward nothing - it is the hunted, not the hunter, an object not an agent. Manner is none (108, 7653306): no qualifying mode of the life is recorded. Intensity, specifier, effect all present:false (109/110/111): no degree, no delimiter, no downstream result logged at this point - the threat is stated but its outcome is deferred. Prohibition absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7653309): the psalmist's very life, the contested centre - what the enemies hunt is the same nefesh God upholds in v4, the self endangered and then sustained. Role characteristic (115, 7653310). Locus internal:ib-state (116, 7653308): a state of the inner self, not an outward performance.

*Why this reading differs:* This is the endangered pole of the pair within Psalm 54: life as the hunted object (operation 'sought'), coupled FORWARD to v4 where the same nefesh is upheld. It differs from its twin 54:4 (which is the same life now sustained, with God recorded as source and operation 'upheld'), and from the status-with-seat reading of 49:8 in that here no seat is recorded - the whole self, not a located organ, is at risk.

**life** — Psa 54:4  ·  `H5315:life#3`

_Story_

A breath after naming his hunters, the singer turns and declares it: God is the one holding his life up. This is the same life the enemies were chasing, but now the poem traces where its steadiness comes from - not from himself, but from God, who props him up from outside. His life here does not strive or reach; it simply rests, held. The whole self is being carried, not any single part of him, and the poem says nothing of how or how much - it just states the security as plain fact. The threatened self of a moment ago has become the confidently kept self: 'God is my helper.'

**life** — Psa 56:6  ·  `H5315:life#4`

_Story_

The singer is being stalked: his enemies lurk and watch his every step, lying in wait for his very life. That life is the prize they are patient to seize - the whole of his threatened self, not just some part of him, and it does nothing here but be hunted. The poem leaves the danger open for a while, saying nothing yet of how it ends. But it ties this cornered life to the song's closing line, where God pulls that same soul back from death. The self the enemies waited to destroy is the self God delivers - one life, endangered and then rescued.

**life** — Psa 59:3  ·  `H5315:life#5`

_Story_

Once again the singer is ambushed: fierce men lie in wait for his life, and he insists he has done nothing to deserve it. His life is simply the target of their trap - his whole threatened self, doing nothing but standing in danger. And here the poem binds that cornered life to a desperate cry: he begs God to wake up, come, and see what is happening to him. The endangered self and the plea for God to look are one movement - a hunted man calling on heaven to open its eyes.

**life** — Psa 86:2  ·  `H5315:life#6`

_Story_

This prayer hands the singer's whole life over to God for safekeeping: 'Keep me safe, for I am devoted to you.' Unlike the songs where life is a hunted prize, here it is the living, faithful self actively placing itself in God's care. The life does not guard itself; it asks to be guarded, and it looks to God alone to do the guarding. And the plea leans on his devotion - his loyalty to God and his request to be kept are tied together, the one offered as the reason for the other. The prayer simply asks and waits, never saying whether or how the keeping comes.

_Reading_

At Psa 86:2 the characteristic is life (H5315), read from 'Preserve my life, for I am godly' - nefesh, life/soul (sense 101, 7667071). Here the type shifts to faculty (102, 7667072): the life is read not merely as a standing but as the functioning self committed to God for keeping - the whole active person entrusted. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the passage does not name the life's origin. Seat is none (104, 7667073): the life to be preserved is not organ-located; it is the whole self. The bearer is the psalmist (105, 7667074). The operation is be preserved (106, 7667075): the movement is a requested keeping - the self asks to be guarded; it is the recipient of a preservation it does not perform. The target is by God (107, 7667076): the preservation is directed to come from God - the arena the self turns toward for its keeping. Manner is none (108, 7667077). Intensity, specifier, effect all present:false (109/110/111): no degree, delimiter, or logged outcome - the plea stands as request, its result unstated. The coupling: paired with being godly (112, 7667078) - the request to be preserved is welded to the ground 'for I am godly'; the self's devotion and its plea for keeping are bound together, the one offered as warrant for the other. Prohibition absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7667080): v2 'PRESERVE my life, for I am godly' - the self committed to God for keeping. Role characteristic (115, 7667081). Locus internal:ib-state (116, 7667079).

*Why this reading differs:* The first of three faculty-typed life-readings (with 119:109 and 121:7), a shift from the status typing of 49:8/54:3/54:4/56:6/59:3. Here life is the committed self asking to 'be preserved' with the target explicitly 'by God' - not the hunted object of the enemy-psalms but the devoted self entrusting itself, its keeping grounded in 'for I am godly.' It differs from 121:7 (where God's keeping is declared as promise) by being a petition, and from 119:109 (life held in constant peril) by its settled confidence.

**live** — Psa 89:48  ·  `H2421:live#1`

_Story_

In the darkest stretch of this song the singer asks the question everyone eventually faces: is there anyone alive who will never die? The answer, unspoken but obvious, is no. This is living stripped to its plainest fact - the shared condition of every human being, and its shadow is that death waits for all of us. To be alive is, here, to be walking toward an end no one can dodge; and the twin question drives it home - no one can rescue his own self from the grave. The poem asks and lets the silence answer, and that silence is the point: mortality, faced without comfort.

_Reading_

At Psa 89:48 the characteristic is live (H2421, chayah), read from 'What man can live and never see death?' (sense 101, 7668257). Type is state (102, 7668258): living as a condition, the mortal state of being alive. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the passage does not name a giver of this life within the lexical - the question is posed bare, the origin unstated even as the whole psalm laments a God who seemed to give and then withdraw. Seat is none (104, 7668259): living is not organ-located; it is the condition of the whole man. The bearer is any man (105, 7668260): not the psalmist alone but the universal human - 'What man...' - the mortality of everyone. The operation is live (106, 7668261): the plain act/condition of being alive. But the target is the hard turn: yet not escape death (107, 7668262) - living is directed toward, and bounded by, a death no one evades; the very act of living runs up against its inescapable end (directive 5 - the descent is read at full weight: this is mortality without softening). Manner is none (108, 7668263). Intensity, specifier, effect all present:false (109/110/111): no degree, delimiter, or logged outcome - the rhetorical question hangs unanswered, and the empty effect-slot mirrors the futility it voices. The coupling is recorded as internal:ib-state (112, 7668264): the living is bound into an inner-being state - here welded to the companion question, 'Who can deliver his soul from the power of Sheol?' Prohibition absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7668266): v48 'What man can LIVE and never see death?' - the reflection on mortality; no one delivers his own soul from the grave. Role characteristic (115, 7668267). Locus: paired with the soul unable to escape Sheol (116, 7668265) - living is set beside the soul that cannot free itself from the grave's power.

*Why this reading differs:* The DESCENT reading of the two 'live' (chayah) anchors, mirror-opposite to 118:17. Both share sense 'live' and type 'state,' but here the bearer is 'any man' (universal mortality) and the target is 'yet not escape death' - living defined by the death it cannot outrun, paired with the soul unable to escape Sheol. Where 118:17 is one man's spared life turned to praise (ascent), 89:48 is every man's bounded life turned to lament (descent). The same verb, opposite movement - this contrast is itself the finding.

> Psa 118:14 The Lord is my strength and my song ; he has become my salvation . Psa 118:15 Glad songs of salvation are in the tents of the righteous : “The right hand of the Lord does valiantly , Psa 118:16 the right hand of the Lord exalts , the right hand of the Lord does valiantly !” Psa 118:17 I shall not die , but I shall live , and recount the deeds of the Lord . Psa 118:18 The Lord has disciplined me severely , but he has not given me over to death . Psa 118:19 Open to me the gates of righteousness , that I may enter through them and give thanks to the Lord . Psa 118:20 This is the gate of the Lord ; the righteous shall enter through it . Psa 118:21 I thank you that you have answered me and have become my salvation . Psa 118:22 The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.

**live** — Psa 118:17  ·  `H2421:live#2`

_Story_

Rescued from the edge of death, the singer makes a ringing declaration: 'I will not die - I will live!' This is life claimed back and defended, one man's survival snatched from the grave. But he does not want to live merely to live; he lives for a reason - to tell everyone what God has done. His spared life comes with a job: to recount God's deeds, to turn survival into praise. Living and testifying are bound together here; the breath he keeps is breath he means to spend proclaiming what he has seen.

_Reading_

At Psa 118:17 the characteristic is live (H2421, chayah), read from 'I shall not die, but I shall live, and recount the deeds of the Lord' (sense 101, 7679754). Type is state (102, 7679755): living as a condition - but here a granted, defended condition rather than a bare one. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the lexical does not tag a giver, though the surrounding verses credit the LORD ('he has not given me over to death,' v18) - at the dimension level the origin is left unstated. Seat is none (104, 7679756): living is not organ-located; the whole self lives. The bearer is the psalmist (105, 7679757): here one man, not humanity at large. The operation is live and not die (106, 7679758): the movement is a decisive survival, life asserted against death ('I shall not die, but I shall live'). The target is to recount God's deeds (107, 7679759): living is directed toward a purpose - the spared life exists in order to testify; survival is not an end but a commission to praise. Manner is none (108, 7679760). Intensity, specifier, effect all present:false (109/110/111): no degree, delimiter, or separately-logged outcome - the resolve stands, its purpose being the nearest thing to an effect and carried by the target. The coupling is recorded as internal:ib-state (112, 7679761): the living is bound into an inner-being state, welded to the resolve to testify. Prohibition absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7679763): v17 'I shall not die, but I shall LIVE, and recount the deeds of the LORD' - the resolve to live for testimony, life spared for praise. Role characteristic (115, 7679764). Locus: paired with recounting his deeds (116, 7679762) - living is set beside the telling of God's works, its very reason for being.

*Why this reading differs:* The ASCENT reading of the two 'live' (chayah) anchors, mirror-opposite to 89:48. Both are sense 'live,' type 'state,' seatless, sourceless-at-the-dimension-level. But here the bearer is 'the psalmist' (one spared man, not 'any man'), and the target is purposive - 'to recount God's deeds' - living defended against death and commissioned for praise, paired with recounting his deeds. Against 89:48's universal mortality bounded by inescapable death, this is personal deliverance bounded by testimony. Same verb, opposite trajectory.

**life** — Psa 119:109  ·  `H5315:life#7`

_Story_

The singer describes living with his life permanently in his own hands - an image of constant danger, as if he could lose it at any moment. Yet the striking thing is where his attention stays fixed: even carrying his life on the edge like this, he refuses to forget God's teaching. The threatened self does not turn inward on its fear; it keeps its bearing on God's word. This continual holding-on is part of the whole song's steady pull - a soul clinging to what God has said, no matter how exposed it feels.

_Reading_

At Psa 119:109 the characteristic is life (H5315), read from 'I hold my life in my hand continually' - nefesh, soul (sense 101, 7682778). Type is faculty (102, 7682779): the life is the functioning self, actively held and directed, not a mere status. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the passage does not name where this life originates. Seat is none (104, 7682780): the soul-in-peril is not organ-located; it is the whole self carried in the hand. The bearer is the psalmist (105, 7682781). The operation is long / cling / keep with the soul (106, 7682782): the movement is the self's continual holding of its own life in constant exposure - 'in my hand' means at perpetual risk, yet actively kept. The target is toward God's word (107, 7682783): even as the life is held in jeopardy, its orientation is toward the law - 'yet I do not forget your law'; the endangered self keeps its bearing fixed on God's word (the arena it holds itself toward). Manner is none (108, 7682784). Intensity, specifier, effect all present:false (109/110/111): no measured degree, delimiter, or logged outcome - the tension of held-life-under-threat is stated, not resolved. The coupling: paired with the whole soul-arc of the psalm (112, 7682785) - this held life is welded to Psalm 119's larger movement of the soul clinging to God's word. Prohibition absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7682787): v109 'I hold my SOUL in my hand continually, yet I do not forget your law' - the self held in constant peril, yet the law unforgotten. Role characteristic (115, 7682788). Locus internal:ib-state (116, 7682786).

*Why this reading differs:* The second faculty-typed life-reading (with 86:2 and 121:7). Distinctively, the self here is not hunted by enemies nor simply entrusted for keeping but ACTIVELY holds its own life 'in my hand continually' - peril made a settled condition - while its target is 'toward God's word' (not 'by God,' as in 86:2/121:7). It couples not to a specific rescue but to 'the whole soul-arc of the psalm.' Against 86:2's petition and 121:7's assurance, this is endangered life keeping its bearing on the law - risk held in tension with fidelity.

## Psa 121:7-8

> Psa 121:7 The Lord will keep you from all evil ; he will keep your life . Psa 121:8 The Lord will keep your going out and your coming in from this time forth and forevermore .

**life** — Psa 121:7  ·  `H5315:life#8`

_Story_

This traveller's song gives a plain, unshakeable promise: God will guard your life. It is spoken not as a request but as a certainty over anyone on the road - your whole self, kept safe. The life here does nothing to protect itself; it is simply held, and the one doing the holding is God. There is no measuring of how much or how strongly - just the flat assurance that it will happen. And it sits right beside the larger promise that God guards the traveller from every evil, coming and going, now and always. The self, entirely, is in safe hands.

_Reading_

At Psa 121:7 the characteristic is life (H5315), read from 'he will keep your life' - nefesh, life/soul (sense 101, 7684533). Type is faculty (102, 7684534): the life is the functioning self, here the object of a promised keeping. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the passage does not name the life's origin - only its keeper. Seat is none (104, 7684535): the kept life is not organ-located; it is the whole self. The bearer is the pilgrim (105, 7684536) - notably not 'the psalmist' but the pilgrim, the traveller addressed by this song of ascents ('your life,' 'your going out and your coming in'). The operation is be kept (106, 7684537): the movement is a guarding the self receives - the life is preserved, not self-preserving. The target is by the LORD (107, 7684538): the keeping is sourced in and directed from the LORD - the arena the self is held within. Manner is none (108, 7684539). Intensity, specifier, effect all present:false (109/110/111): no measured degree, delimiter, or separately-logged outcome - the promise is stated as flat certainty, its keeping asserted rather than quantified. The coupling is recorded as internal:ib-state (112, 7684540): the kept life is bound into an inner-being state, welded to the assurance. Prohibition absent (113 present:false). The discovery note (114, 7684542): v7 'he will keep your LIFE' - the very self kept by God, the soul preserved from all evil. Role characteristic (115, 7684543). Locus: paired with God keeping from all evil (116, 7684541) - the kept life is set beside the promise 'The Lord will keep you from all evil.'

*Why this reading differs:* The third faculty-typed life-reading (with 86:2 and 119:109). It stands apart by voice and mood: the bearer is 'the pilgrim' (not the psalmist), and the keeping is stated as settled PROMISE ('he will keep your life,' operation 'be kept,' target 'by the LORD'), coupled to 'God keeping from all evil.' Against 86:2 (a petition to be preserved) and 119:109 (life held in continual peril), this is life declared secure - assurance rather than plea or tension - the calmest point on the family's endangered-to-kept spectrum.
