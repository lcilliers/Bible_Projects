## Psa 17:12-15

> Psa 17:12 He is like a lion eager to tear , as a young lion lurking in ambush . Psa 17:13 Arise , O Lord ! Confront him , subdue him! Deliver my soul from the wicked by your sword , Psa 17:14 from men by your hand , O Lord , from men of the world whose portion is in this life . You fill their womb with treasure ; they are satisfied with children , and they leave their abundance to their infants . Psa 17:15 As for me , I shall behold your face in righteousness ; when I awake , I shall be satisfied with your likeness .

**satisfied** — Psa 17:14  ·  `H7646:satisf#1`

_Story_

This passage looks inside people who have everything the present world can give. Their inner sense of being full and content is real, but it is fastened entirely to what this life holds — money in the treasury, children around the table, an inheritance handed on to the little ones. Scripture does not say where this contentment comes from or where it leads; it simply notes that it settles nowhere deep and produces nothing lasting. It is a fullness with a lid on it, one that stops at the grave. Set beside the writer's own longing to wake and be satisfied with God himself, this satisfied heart is shown to be complete in its own terms yet closed to anything greater.

_Reading_

The sense (101, 7693800) reads this as 'the worldly satisfied with this life', and the type (102, 7693801) files it as an affect — a felt inner state, not an act done. Its locus (116, 7693808) is internal:ib-state and its role (115, 7693810) is characteristic: this is genuinely an interior condition of a human being, not merely an external circumstance. The seat (104, 7693802) is 'none' — the satisfaction is not lodged in a named organ (heart, soul), which fits a contentment that is diffuse and appetitive rather than seated in the deep self. The source (103, present:false) is not recorded: the passage does not say what produces this fullness beyond the circumstance itself. The bearer (105, 7693803) is inferred as 'men of the world', and the operation (106, 7693804) traces the whole movement — men whose portion is in this life are filled with treasure, satisfied with children, and leave their abundance to their infants: a contentment bounded by the present age. The target (107, 7693805) is 'this-life-satisfaction' and the coupling (112, 7693807) is 'worldly-satisfied' — the satisfaction points at, and binds to, only what the present world affords. Manner (108, 7693806) is 'none': no qualifying mode is added; the satisfaction is stated flatly. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111) and prohibition (113) are all present:false — the reader recorded no measured degree, no narrowing qualifier, no downstream consequence, and no restriction. The silence on effect is itself telling: a full interior that produces nothing beyond itself, terminating in the infants who inherit the goods. The discovery (114, 7693809) names the finding plainly: the interior of the worldly is filled and content, but only with what this life holds — a satisfaction that stops at the grave. Read against directive 5, this dark or bounded satisfaction is given full weight: it is a real, complete inner fullness, only cut off from anything beyond the present, and the passage sets it deliberately beside the psalmist's own hope in v15 to be satisfied with God's likeness on waking.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the three readings of 'satisfied' (saba), this is the only one whose bearer is 'men of the world' and whose target/coupling is bounded to 'this life'. Where #2 (Psa 63:5) and #3 (Psa 65:4) read the soul filled BY God, this reads a fullness closed off from God — the same word carrying a descent rather than an ascent, which the passage itself contrasts with v15.

**reviving** — Psa 19:7  ·  `H7725:reviv#1`

_Story_

Here a tired, drooping inner life is brought back to life by God's word. Scripture compares it to what food does for someone who has fainted — the law of the Lord reaches the soul and revives it. It is not something the person achieves; it is a state they are restored into, touching the whole self rather than one corner of it. The writer does not spell out degrees or after-effects; the point is simply the coming-back-to-life. The perfect word arrives from outside and the weary interior is quickened.

_Reading_

The sense (101, 7694496) is 'soul revived by the law', and the type (102, 7694497) is state — a condition the inner being is brought into, not an action it performs. Locus (116, 7694504) is internal:ib-state and role (115, 7694506) is characteristic, so this is a true interior alteration. The seat (104, 7694498) is 'none': though the operation names the soul, the reader recorded no organ-seat for the revival itself, marking it as a whole-self restoration rather than something localised. Source (103, present:false) is not recorded as a discrete row, yet the operation (106, 7694500) supplies the agent implicitly: the perfect law of the LORD revives/restores the soul — the interior brought back to life by the word. The bearer (105, 7694499) is the psalmist. The target (107, 7694501) is 'revival' and the coupling (112, 7694503) is 'soul-revived': the movement runs from the word acting upon, and binding to, the soul that is renewed. Manner (108, 7694502) is 'none' — no mode is specified beyond the plain fact of restoration. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), prohibition (113) are all present:false: the reader logged no degree, no qualifier, no consequence beyond the state itself, and no prohibition. The discovery (114, 7694505) crystallises it: the word acts on the soul the way food acts on a faint body — inner restoration as the operation. Per directive 4, the full process is short but complete: an external, perfect word touches the interior and the faint self is brought back to life; the passage locates the whole cluster of v7-9 benefits (wisdom, joy, light) around this first act of reviving.

**help** — Psa 22:11  ·  `H5826:help#1`

_Story_

This is the inner experience of having no one. Trouble is closing in, and the writer's cry is that God would not stay far away, because there is no one at all to help. The word 'help' appears here only as something absent — every human support has fallen away. It is not an action but a state: the bare, exposed feeling of being utterly alone. And it is out of that emptiness, with only God left to appeal to, that the plea for nearness rises.

_Reading_

Locus (116, 7695108) is internal:ib-state, role (115, 7695110) characteristic: the helplessness is treated as a real interior condition of the psalmist. Seat (104, 7695102) is 'none' — no organ houses this; it is the whole self's exposure. Source (103, present:false) is unrecorded. The bearer (105, 7695103) is the psalmist, and the operation (106, 7695104) traces the movement: the self pleads that God not be far, for trouble is near and there is none to help — utter isolation. The target (107, 7695105) is 'helplessness' and the coupling (112, 7695107) is 'none-to-help': the whole inner movement points at, and binds to, the emptiness where a helper should be. Manner (108, 7695106) is 'none'. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), prohibition (113) all present:false — no degree, qualifier, consequence, or restriction is logged; the bareness of the record matches the bareness the verse describes. The discovery (114, 7695109) reads it: the operation names the aloneness — every human support has failed, leaving only God to appeal to. Under directive 5 this is a genuinely dark interior state and is read at full weight: 'help' appears here only in its negation, and that negation is the finding — the inner being stripped of every prop, its plea for nearness rising precisely because no one is left to help.

**comforted** — Psa 23:4  ·  `H5162:comfort#1`

_Story_

Walking through the darkest valley, the writer finds his inner fear replaced by comfort. What comforts him is the shepherd's rod and staff — the very tools used to guide and to guard. God's protecting nearness, felt through those instruments, soothes him. The danger is not removed; rather, he is consoled because he is not alone in it. It is a settled inner ease that comes simply from the presence of one who guides and defends.

_Reading_

The sense (101, 7695478) is 'your rod and staff comfort me', and the type (102, 7695479) is affect — a felt consolation. Locus (116, 7695486) is internal:ib-state, role (115, 7695488) characteristic: a genuine inner state. Seat (104, 7695480) is 'none' — the comfort is not organ-located but pervades the self as it passes through the valley. Source (103, present:false) is not a separate row, yet the operation (106, 7695482) makes the agent plain: the shepherd's rod and staff comfort the self — a felt consolation from God's guiding, protecting nearness. The bearer (105, 7695481) is the psalmist. The target (107, 7695483) is 'consolation' and the coupling (112, 7695485) is 'comforted': the movement runs from the instruments of the shepherd, through the self, to the state of being consoled. Manner (108, 7695484) is 'none' — no mode is added beyond the bare consolation. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), prohibition (113) are present:false: no measured degree, qualifier, downstream effect, or restriction is recorded. The discovery (114, 7695487) sharpens the finding: the very instruments of guidance and defence — the rod and staff — become a source of comfort; the interior is soothed by what also disciplines and protects. Per directive 4 the process is complete though compact: God's near, guarding presence, felt through its tools, produces consolation in a self walking through the shadow of death — fear displaced not by the removal of danger but by accompaniment ('for you are with me').

*Why this reading differs:* Of the three readings of 'comforted' (nacham), this one is comfort RECEIVED and felt, sourced in God's guiding nearness (rod and staff). It differs from #2 (Psa 77:2), where the same comfort is REFUSED by the soul, and from #3 (Psa 86:17), where comfort is God's answer to a lament and is paired with being helped. Here consolation is present and accepted mid-danger.

**renew** — Psa 51:10  ·  `H2318:renew#1`

_Story_

After his sin, the writer asks God to make his inner life new again — to set a steadfast spirit right inside him. This is not a feeling that comes over him but something he begs God to do: to rebuild the will and disposition that had collapsed. It happens deep within, in the spirit, and it stands alongside his other plea, that God would create in him a clean heart. Two pictures — making new and creating fresh — describe one act of inner re-founding. The self that had broken down is to be re-established, firm and turned back toward God.

_Reading_

The sense (101, 7652488) is 'renew / make new (chadash — a right spirit)', and the type (102, 7652489) is action — unlike the state-readings elsewhere in this family, renewal here is an act (petitioned of God). The seat (104, 7652490) is recorded as a span-resolved pair 'within me (the spirit)' (from_span 279648 to_span 279650): the renewal is precisely seated within, in the spirit, making this the most explicitly located reading in the family. Source (103, present:false) is not a separate row, but the address 'O God' makes God the implied agent of the making-new. The bearer (105, 7652491) is 'the penitent'. The operation (106, 7652492) is 'be renewed'. The target (107, 7652493) is a span-pair 'a right / steadfast spirit' (279648 to 279650): the inner disposition is what is remade. Manner (108, 7652494) is 'within me' — an interior mode, reinforcing that this happens inside the self, not at the surface. The coupling (112, 7652495) is a span-pair 'paired with the created clean heart' (279648 to 279645): renewal (chadash) is twinned with creation (bara) of the clean heart — two verbs for one interior re-founding. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), prohibition (113) are present:false: no degree, qualifier, consequence, or restriction logged. The discovery (114, 7652497) reads the whole: the inner disposition made new and firm; a steadfast spirit set right within, the will re-established toward God after collapse. Per directives 4 and 8 the process is a re-founding after moral ruin — the penitent asks God to remake the interior itself, the spirit made steadfast, distinct from yet paired with the clean-heart creation, so that the difference between renewing and creating is preserved as two facets of one restoration.

**satisfied** — Psa 63:5  ·  `H7646:satisf#2`

_Story_

The writer began this psalm thirsting for God like a man in a waterless desert. Now he says his inner self will be satisfied as if seated at a rich feast. The deep longing of the soul is answered, not by any particular thing, but by being filled to fullness in God himself. And that fullness does not stop there — it spills over into joyful praise on his lips. The dry, fainting self of the opening has become a soul richly fed and singing.

_Reading_

The sense (101, 7656467) is 'be satisfied / filled (saba — my soul will be satisfied as with fat)', and the type (102, 7656468) is status — a settled condition. Locus (116, 7656475) is internal:ib-state, role (115, 7656477) characteristic. Uniquely among the satisfaction-readings, the seat (104, 7656469) is a span-pair 'the soul' (from_span 281062 to_span 281061): this satisfaction is seated in the nephesh, the deep self that in v1 thirsted and fainted. The bearer (105, 7656470) is also a span-pair 'the psalmist (his soul)' (281062 to 281061) — bearer and seat converge on the soul. Source (103, present:false) is unrecorded as a row, but the surrounding verses make God the one who fills. The operation (106, 7656471) is 'be satisfied / filled' (inferred, to_span 281062). The target (107, 7656472) is 'none' — strikingly, no object is named: the soul is simply filled, and the absence of a target says the fullness is not fixed on a thing but is a state of the self. Manner (108, 7656473) is 'as with fat and rich food' — the mode is banquet-richness, answering the desert of v1. The coupling (112, 7656474) is a span-pair 'issues in joyful praise' (281062 to 281066): the satisfaction does not terminate in itself but overflows into praise with joyful lips. Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), prohibition (113) present:false. The discovery (114, 7656476) reads the arc: the thirst of v1 answered with the fullness of a feast — desert-longing turned to banquet-satisfaction, the richest fare found in God himself. Per directive 4 the full movement runs from a fainting, thirsting soul in a dry land to a soul sated as at a feast, and onward into joyful praise — an ascent whose coupling to praise is the distinguishing note.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the three 'satisfied' readings this is the individual soul (nephesh) filled BY God after desert-thirst, seated in the soul and overflowing into praise. It differs from #1 (Psa 17:14), a worldly fullness bounded to this life, and from #3 (Psa 65:4), a corporate 'we' satisfied with the goodness of God's house — here the bearer is one longing soul and the coupling is to joyful praise.

Per directives 4 and 8 the process runs from being chosen and drawn near, to dwelling in the courts, to a satisfaction whose very content is God's goodness and holiness; the corporate bearer and the explicit target/manner distinguish it from the individual soul of #2.

*Why this reading differs:* This 'satisfied' reading has a corporate bearer ('the worshippers', a 'we') and no seat, with the fullness explicitly qualified by the house's goodness and the temple's holiness and coupled to being brought near. It differs from #2 (Psa 63:5), an individual soul seated in the nephesh overflowing into praise, and from #1 (Psa 17:14), a worldly fullness closed to God. **revive** — Psa 69:32  ·  `H2421:reviv#1`

_Story_

To people who seek God but whose courage has drooped, the psalm says: let your hearts come alive again. Watching God act on behalf of the needy and afflicted, their fainting inner life is quickened once more. It is centred in the heart — a coming-back-to-life with no object beyond the reviving itself. This is not one person's private restoration but an invitation to a whole company of seekers. Seeing that God cares for the lowly, their hearts revive.

_Reading_

The sense (101, 7660220) is 'revive / live (chayah — let your hearts revive)', and the type (102, 7660221) is status — a condition the seekers are summoned into. Locus (116, 7660228) is internal:heart and role (115, 7660230) characteristic. The seat (104, 7660222) is a span-pair 'the heart' (from_span 281845 to_span 281844): the reviving is explicitly heart-seated, distinct from the soul-revival of Psa 19:7. Source (103, present:false) is not a separate row, yet the context — the LORD who hears the needy (v33) — supplies the implied ground. The bearer (105, 7660223) is inferred as 'those who seek God'. The operation (106, 7660224) is 'revive / come alive' (inferred, to_span 281845). The target (107, 7660225) is 'none' — no object beyond the coming-alive itself; the absence marks revival as an intransitive quickening of the self. Manner (108, 7660226) is a span-pair 'in the heart' (281845 to 281844) — the mode is interior, in the heart. The coupling (112, 7660227) is a span-pair 'the reviving of the seekers' hearts' (281845 to 281841): the reviving is bound to the seeing of God's help ('when the humble see it they will be glad'). Intensity (109), specifier (110), effect (111), prohibition (113) present:false. **help** — Psa 119:147  ·  `H7768:help#1`

_Story_

Before the sun is up, the writer is already awake and crying out to God for help. This 'help' is not something he passively receives but something he actively reaches for — an urgent appeal driven by hope. What underwrites the cry is his confidence in God's word; he calls out because he trusts what God has promised. Where elsewhere a sufferer feels utterly without a helper, here the same longing becomes early-morning prayer full of expectation. He rises in the dark and cries out, hoping in God's words.

_Reading_

The sense (101, 7683680) is 'cry for help (shava)', and the type (102, 7683681) is action — this 'help' is not a state of being helped but the act of crying out for it. Notably the locus (116, 7683688) is external:god: the cry is directed outward, toward God, unlike the internal-state loci of most of the family. Role (115, 7683690) is characteristic.
