## Psa 2:1-2

> Psa 2:1 Why do the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain ? Psa 2:2 The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together , against the Lord and against his Anointed , saying,

**counsel** — Psa 2:2  ·  `H3245:counsel#1`

_Story_

The psalm opens on the world's rulers with their heads together. Kings and princes are pooling their minds and laying plans - but the plan is a plot, and the plot is against the Lord and the king he has anointed. This is the mind's power to think and confer, here bent to rebellion: many rulers reasoning as one, their scheming fused into a single conspiracy. The passage never tells us what is driving them, whether fear or ambition; it simply shows the shared deliberation and where it is aimed. It is a settled inner resolve that shows itself in their banding together. Nothing here spells out the verdict, but the picture is unmistakable - good sense, the gift of taking counsel, turned into organised revolt.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'the rulers take counsel together' - deliberation, minds pooled into a single plan. Type (102) is cognition: this is a thinking act, planning and conferring, not a felt state nor a bare deed. Source (103) is absent: no origin is booked, so the reading does not trace what drives the plotting - not fear, not ambition - it reports the deliberation without naming its spring. Seat (104) resolves to none: the counsel is not organ-located but the persons' concerted planning, held by the powerful as such, not the working of an inner faculty. Bearer (105) is the rulers - the kings of the earth, whose collective interior is read here. Operation (106), the core event, is that the kings and rulers conspire in concert against the LORD and his Anointed - organised rebellion, cognition turned into coordinated revolt. Target (107) is 'conspiracy': the deliberation aims at a plot, the joint scheme itself. Manner (108) is none - no mode marked, the conferring reported flatly. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are all absent: no degree of the plotting is scored, nothing narrows it, and no downstream outcome is booked - the LORD's answering derision (v4) is not filed as this operation's effect. Coupling (112) is 'take-counsel': the reading is welded to the very act of counselling together, plural minds bound in one aim. Prohibition (113) is absent - not forbidden in this row, though the whole psalm indicts the revolt. Discovery (114) names it: the plot organises into a conspiracy of the powerful, deliberation turned to coordinated revolt. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - a shared inner posture held within, even as it issues in outward alliance. The full process: from no named source and no seat, the rulers' collective mind pools into an organised conspiracy against the LORD and his Anointed - cognition welded to concerted counsel, an inward resolve of the powerful that turns thought into revolt. Read at full weight, this is the mind's gift of deliberation bent to rebellion.

**wise** — Psa 2:10  ·  `H7919:wise#1`

_Story_

The same kings who were plotting against God and his anointed are now, at the end of the psalm, told plainly: be wise, be warned. Wisdom here is a command aimed straight at rebels - a call to come to their senses. The passage does not say where the wisdom is to come from; it simply holds it out as the way back from ruin. It is paired with a warning, and it is left open whether they will take it. In short, good sense is offered as an off-ramp to minds set on revolt - the chance to reconsider before it is too late.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'be wise, be warned' - wisdom issued as an imperative to rebel kings. Type (102) is cognition: an act of the understanding, being brought to sober sense. Source (103) is absent: the reading books no origin for the summons behind the verse - it reports the call, not who supplies the wisdom. Seat (104) is none: not organ-located; the wisdom urged is the persons' own reconsidering, not an inner organ switched on. Bearer (105) is the kings - the same rebellious rulers of v2, now addressed. Operation (106) is the turn: the kings are summoned to prudence, to be warned - the interior called back from revolt to sober sense. Target (107) is 'summons-to-prudence': what the operation aims at is the rousing of good sense in the rebel, wisdom offered as reconsideration. Manner (108) is none - the charge stands plainly. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: no degree is scored, nothing narrows the wisdom, and no outcome is booked - whether the kings heed is left open. Coupling (112) is 'be-wise': welded to the paired imperative to be warned. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114): the psalm calls the rebellious interior to reconsider; wisdom offered as the off-ramp from ruin. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - a condition the will is charged to enter. The full process: from no booked source and no seat, the psalm commands the rebel kings' interior to become wise and take warning - a cognition-turn, wisdom held out to the very minds that had pooled into conspiracy, the sober sense that would pull them back from destruction.

*Why this reading differs:* The same word for being wise recurs at Psa 94:8 (reading H7919:wise#2), and the difference is a finding. Here at Psa 2:10 the wisdom is read as a cognition summoned as an imperative to rebel kings - the sober sense that would turn them from an organised revolt already under way, an off-ramp from ruin. At Psa 94:8 it is read as a disposition the fools are urged toward - the discernment lacked by those who imagine God does not see. Both are calls to the senseless to become wise, but this reading foregrounds the sharp turn demanded of the powerful mid-rebellion, while the other foregrounds a settled discernment the dull are pressed to acquire.

**counsel** — Psa 13:2  ·  `H6098:counsel#1`

_Story_

The psalmist takes counsel in his soul, turning his case over and over inside himself, all day long, with sorrow sitting in his heart the whole time. The thinking has no outward aim; it just circles back on itself and finds no answer. And it is bound tightly to grief - every round of self-consultation renews the sorrow. The passage does not say what first set this churning going, only that it will not stop and will not rest. It is the mind's good power to deliberate, here turned into an exhausting inner round that only deepens the ache.

The full process: from no named source and no seat, the psalmist's mind turns over its own trouble all day long, taking counsel with itself and finding no answer, the deliberation welded to a sorrow that renews with every round - an inner churn that is cognition without relief, wisdom's faculty of counsel turned into a wearying self-consultation.

## Psa 14:1-7

**fool** — Psa 14:1  ·  `H5036:fool#1`

_Story_

The fool of this psalm is not a stupid man but a morally reckless one, and his folly is a sentence he passes inside himself: there is no God. It is not a worked-out argument; it is a lived assumption, God quietly ruled out of the picture. The passage does not say what led him there, only that once the verdict is in, the way is clear for corrupt and abominable deeds - the denial acts as a licence. It is carried in the heart, and from that inner premise everything foul flows. This is folly at its deepest: not a lack of information but a heart that has cleared God out of its accounts.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'the fool says no God' - nabal, the moral fool. Type (102) is cognition: this is an interior verdict, a thing said in the heart, not merely a deed. Source (103) is absent: no driver is booked behind the denial - the reading gives the verdict without naming what produced it. Seat (104) is none: the operation is not organ-located; though it is said 'in his heart', heart here reads as the whole inner person, not a faculty-organ. Bearer (105) is the fool - his interior read from within. Operation (106) is the core event: the fool says in his heart there is no God - a moral-practical denial that issues in corrupt deeds. Target (107) is 'practical-atheism': not a philosophy but a lived premise, God ruled out in practice. Manner (108) is none. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: no degree is scored, nothing narrows the denial, and the abominable deeds are read as what the denial issues in rather than filed as a separate booked effect. Coupling (112) is 'no-God-in-heart': the verdict is welded to its inward seat, a premise carried in the heart. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114): the operation is an interior verdict (not a philosophy) that clears the way for abominable deeds - denial as licence. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - a condition of the inner life. The full process: from no booked source and no organ-seat, the fool's interior pronounces there is no God - a practical, not intellectual, denial welded to the heart that carries it, and this inward verdict licenses the corruption and abominable deeds that follow. This is folly at its root: not ignorance of facts but a heart that has cleared God out of its reckoning.

*Why this reading differs:* The same figure recurs at Psa 53:1 (reading H5036:fool#2), in what is nearly a twin psalm, and the coding difference is a finding. Here at Psa 14:1 the fool is read as a cognition - an interior verdict, the denial itself, with no seat named (the heart standing for the whole person). At Psa 53:1 the same words are read as a status, with an explicit seat (the heart), a manner (inwardly), and the denial coupled as what issues in corruption and abominable deeds. Both name practical atheism, the same premise from which corruption flows; but this reading foregrounds the act of the denial, while the other foregrounds the fool's settled condition and its lodging in the heart.

And notice the direction - the person does not achieve this wisdom by effort; it is given, worked in from outside by the word, and it becomes a settled new condition of the inner life. Wisdom here is received, not earned: the word opens a mind that had nothing.

**wounds** — Psa 38:5  ·  `H2250:wound#1`

_Story_

In the middle of a psalm heavy with sickness and sin, one line traces the rot to its root: my wounds stink and fester because of my foolishness. _Story_

Hunted and worn down, his spirit failing, the psalmist prays for something deeper than escape: teach me to do your will, for you are my God. He is not just asking for directions out of trouble; he is asking to be taught obedience itself, to have his will shaped so that he actually does what God wants, led on level ground by God's good Spirit. This is an act of longing - a heart reaching out to be instructed and conformed. What he wants is a teachable, obedient inner life, not merely a safe way through his enemies. It is striking that, in the middle of real danger, his deepest request is to be made the kind of person who does God's will. Here the hunger to be taught comes from the inside, from someone who wants God not only to rescue him but to remake how he lives.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'desire to be taught God's will' - a longing for instruction, read off 'teach me to do your will.' Type (102) is volition: an act of the will, the interior wanting to be shaped. Source (103) is absent: no origin is booked behind the longing. Seat (104) is none: not organ-located; the desire is the whole self's, not an organ's. Bearer (105) is the psalmist - hunted, fainting in spirit, turning to God. Operation (106) is the core event: the self asks to be taught to do God's will, wanting the good Spirit to lead it on level ground. Target (107) is 'teachability' (inferred): what the operation aims at is a taught, conformed interior - not mere rescue but obedience learned. Manner (108) is none. Intensity (109), specifier (110), and effect (111) are absent: no degree is scored, nothing narrows it, no outcome booked. Coupling (112) is 'teach-me-your-will' (inferred): the volition is welded to its very content, the plea to be taught God's will. Prohibition (113) is absent. Discovery (114): beyond guidance-for-safety - the interior wants conformity, to be led on level ground; not just direction but a taught will. Role (115) is characteristic. Locus (116) is internal:ib-state - a condition of the inner life. The full process: with no booked source and no seat, the psalmist's will reaches out and asks to be taught to do God's will, wanting the good Spirit to lead it level - a volition welded to that plea, aimed at a teachable, conformed interior, held within. The finding is the depth of the request: pressed hard by enemies, he asks not merely to be shown a safe path but to be taught obedience itself - the inner life wanting to be conformed to God's will, not just guided around danger. Here teaching is longed for from the receiving side, the hunger of a heart that wants to be made obedient.

*Why this reading differs:* The same verb for teaching (lamad) appears at Psa 34:11 (reading H3925:teach#1), and the direction reverses - the finding. There the psalmist is the teacher, willing to hand the fear of the LORD to children (outward, giving). Here at Psa 143:10 he is the pupil, asking God to teach him to do his will (inward, receiving) - not guidance for safety but conformity of the will, the hunger to be made obedient. Both are acts of the will about instruction, but this reading foregrounds the longing to be taught oneself, while the other foregrounds the desire to teach others.
