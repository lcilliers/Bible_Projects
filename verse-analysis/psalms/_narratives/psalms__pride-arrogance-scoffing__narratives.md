## Psa 10:1-8

**arrogance** — Psa 10:2  ·  `H1346:arroganc#1`

_Story_

The psalm opens with a man so full of himself that his pride cannot sit still - it turns into a hunt. Puffed up, he chases down the poor and weak like quarry. The poem never says what made him this way; his arrogance is simply there, the whole set of the man rather than something in one corner of him. Pride and predation have fused into a single motion: to be this proud is to prey. The verse stops before telling us how the hunt ends, leaving the outcome hanging - it shows us the swollen self reaching out to seize the helpless, and lets that stand.

_Reading_

The characteristic is 'arrogance' typed as a disposition (D102 type=disposition), a settled inner posture rather than a single act. Its sense (D101) is 'arrogant hot pursuit': self-exaltation that does not stay interior but discharges as a chase. No source is recorded (D103 present:false) - the passage does not trace where the arrogance comes from; it is presented as the given condition of 'the wicked'. The seat (D104) is 'none': this pride is not lodged in a named organ or faculty, it is a whole-person stance. The bearer (D105) is the wicked (inferred); the operation (D106) is the interior turned outward - 'in arrogance the wicked hotly pursue the poor', pride expressed as predatory chase. The target (D107) reads back as the arrogance itself / the poor who are hunted; manner (D108) is 'none' - no modality is specified beyond the hot pursuit. Intensity, specifier and effect are all absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false): the verse marks no degree, no further qualifier, and does not yet record the outcome of the hunt - the consequence is left open. The coupling (D112) is 'arrogant-pursuit': pride and the hunting of the weak are welded into one movement, not two. Prohibition is absent (D113 present:false) - this is described, not forbidden here. The discovery (D114) fixes the finding: the interior driving the hunt is pride, self-exaltation converted into the chase of the weak. Role (D115) is characteristic; locus (D116) internal:ib-state - it is read as a state of the inner being, its arena being the poor it pursues. Read on the inner-being lens, arrogance here is not mere self-regard but an engine: the swollen self reaches out to prey.

**boast** — Psa 10:3  ·  `H1984:boast#1`

_Story_

Here the same wicked man opens his mouth, and what comes out is bragging about the things he craves. He is proud of his own appetite, glorying in his greed as if it were something to celebrate. In the same breath he curses and turns his back on God - the two go together, so that praising himself and dismissing God are really one movement of the heart. The poem does not say what his boasting will cost him; it simply shows a man whose desires have become his creed. The outcome is left unsaid; the picture is complete without it.

_Reading_

The characteristic is 'boast' (halal) typed as a disposition (D102 type=disposition) - here a habitual bent of the self, not an isolated speech-act. The sense (D101) is 'boasts of his soul's desire': the vaunting is of one's own cravings. No source is recorded (D103 present:false) - the boasting is not derived from anything; it stands as the native reflex of the wicked. Seat (D104) is 'none' - not organ-located; it is the whole man's stance. Bearer (D105) is the wicked (inferred); the operation (D106) is the full movement - 'the wicked boasts of the cravings of his soul, and the greedy man curses and renounces the LORD'. Target (D107) is 'self-glorying-greed': the boast aims at nothing outside the self; it circles its own appetite. Manner (D108) is 'none' - unqualified. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false): no degree, no qualifier, and the consequence of the boast is not recorded here. Coupling (D112) is 'boast-of-desire' - the boasting is bonded to appetite; the two cannot be separated. Prohibition absent (D113 present:false). The discovery (D114) states the finding: the interior parades its own appetites as its glory, greed become a creed that dismisses God. Role (D115) characteristic; locus (D116) internal:ib-state. On the inner-being lens this is pride's speech-face: the self makes its wants its boast and, in the same breath, renounces the LORD - self-glory and God-denial are one interior act.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the five 'boast' (halal) readings this is the only one typed as a settled disposition aimed at 'self-glorying-greed' with God actively renounced; #2 (Psa 44:8) is boasting IN God, #3 (Psa 49:6) in riches, #4 (Psa 52:1) in evil, #5 (Psa 97:7) in idols. This #1 is the appetite-boast - the self glorying in its own cravings, the most inward and God-defiant of the set.

**arrogantly** — Psa 17:10  ·  `H1348:arrogantly#1`

_Story_

The psalmist's enemies have hearts bolted shut against pity, and out of those closed hearts comes proud, swaggering talk. The two belong together: their mouths brag because their hearts feel nothing. This pride is not a small flaw tucked away somewhere; it is the whole set of the men, who stalk the psalmist like a hungry lion ready to tear. The poem does not explain how they got so hard, nor does it tell us whether the hunt succeeds - it simply shows arrogance and cruelty as one and the same inner state, a self so swollen it has no room left for mercy.

_Reading_

The characteristic is 'arrogantly' typed as a disposition (D102 type=disposition). Its sense (D101) is 'enemies closed to pity, arrogant': the arrogance is bound to a shut-off compassion. Source is absent (D103 present:false) - the passage does not say what produced this hardness; it is simply the enemies' condition. Seat (D104) is 'none' - though v10 speaks of hearts closing, the analysis records no organ-seat for the arrogance itself; it is a whole-person disposition. Bearer (D105) is the enemies (inferred); the operation (D106) is a compound movement - 'the enemies close their hearts to pity and speak arrogantly, tracking the self down like a lion eager to tear'. Target (D107) is 'merciless-arrogance'; manner (D108) is 'none' - unqualified. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false): no degree marked, no qualifier, and the outcome (whether the hunt succeeds) is not recorded. Coupling (D112) is 'closed-and-arrogant': pitilessness and arrogant speech are welded together - the mouth's pride and the heart's closure are one thing. Prohibition absent (D113 present:false). The discovery (D114): the enemies' interior is shut against mercy and swollen with arrogance, a pitilessness that hunts. Role (D115) characteristic; locus (D116) internal:ib-state. On the inner-being lens the finding is that arrogance and mercilessness co-occur as a single inner condition: the self so exalted that it feels no pity, its pride audible in the mouth while the heart is barred - and that state reaches outward to hunt the psalmist like a lion.

**presumptuous** — Psa 19:13  ·  `H2086:presumptuou#1`

_Story_

Here the proud, defiant sin is not out in an enemy - it is a threat the psalmist fears inside himself. He prays to be kept from the bold, willful wrongs that he knows could take him over and rule him. He treats these differently from the small hidden mistakes he cannot even see: presumptuous sin is the deliberate, high-handed kind, and his dread is that it might become his master. The prayer is a matter of the will - a man asking to be guarded before such pride ever takes the throne of his heart. The poem leaves the answer to the prayer unspoken, resting simply in the asking.

_Reading_

This reading is distinctive within the family: 'presumptuous' is typed not as disposition or action but as volition (D102 type=volition) - a matter of the will. Its sense (D101) is 'kept from presumptuous sin'. Uniquely, the bearer (D105) is the psalmist himself (inferred), not the wicked: the arrogance-word appears from the inside, as a danger the speaker fears in his own interior. Source is absent (D103 present:false) - the passage does not name where presumptuous sin arises. Seat (D104) is 'none' - not organ-located. The operation (D106) is a plea: 'the self asks to be kept from presumptuous sins and not let them have dominion - guarding against willful, ruling sin'. Target (D107) is 'guarding-against-dominion'; manner (D108) 'none'. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false): no degree, no qualifier, and the outcome of the plea is not recorded within the span. Coupling (D112) is 'keep-from-presumption'. Prohibition absent (D113 present:false) - notably, though the sin is deprecated, no formal prohibition is tagged; the guarding is carried as petition, not command. The discovery (D114) sharpens it: distinct from hidden faults, this is the deliberate sin the interior fears could master it; the plea is against being ruled. Role (D115) characteristic; locus (D116) internal:ib-state. On the inner-being lens this is the family's reflexive pole - presumption (defiant, high-handed sin) seen not in an enemy but as a possible tyrant over the speaker's own soul, so that the movement is a will asking to be kept from a pride that would seize dominion over it.

**taunt** — Psa 42:10  ·  `H2778:taunt#1`

_Story_

The psalmist's enemies jeer at him all day long, and their favourite taunt is a question: 'Where is your God?' It is not just an insult he can shrug off - he feels it like a killing blow deep in his bones. The poem does not say why they hate him; the mockery is simply there, aimed straight at him. And the sting is not to his reputation but to his faith: the jeer is designed to make him doubt that God is there at all. The words come from outside, but the wound lands deep inside, on the very thing that holds him up.

_Reading_

The characteristic is 'taunt / reproach' (charaph) typed as an action (D102 type=action) - a deed done to the speaker, not a standing state. Sense (D101): 'taunt / reproach (charaph)'. Source is absent (D103 present:false) - no cause is given for the adversaries' scorn. Seat (D104) is 'none' - not organ-located. Bearer (D105) is the adversaries (inferred); the operation (D106) is simply 'taunt'; the target (D107) is David, the speaker himself. Distinctively, this taunt carries a manner (D108): 'like a deadly wound in the bones' (inferred) - the reproach is felt as bodily, mortal injury, not mere words. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false): no numeric degree, no qualifier, and the resulting inner collapse, though present in the surrounding lament, is not tagged as an effect here. Coupling (D112) is 'none' - this taunt is not welded to another act; it stands alone. Prohibition absent (D113 present:false). The discovery (D114): the reproach wounds like a mortal blow to the bones, the foes' 'Where is your God?' assaulting the very ground of faith. Role (D115) characteristic; locus (D116) internal:ib-state - though the taunt comes from outside, it registers as an inner-being wound. On the inner-being lens the finding is that hostile mockery is not experienced merely as insult but as an attack on faith itself: the jeer 'Where is your God?' penetrates to the bones, so the arena of the taunt is the sufferer's confidence in God.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the four 'taunt' (charaph) readings this one alone carries a manner - the reproach felt 'like a deadly wound in the bones' - and its whole force is the faith-assault 'Where is your God?'; #2 (Psa 55:12) is the endurable taunt of a mere enemy contrasted with a friend's betrayal, #3 (Psa 102:8) is the sufferer taunted all day as a passive state, #4 (Psa 119:42) is the taunter answered by the psalmist's trust. This #1 is the taunt as bodily, faith-piercing wound.

## Psa 44:1-10

> To the choirmaster . A Maskil of the Sons of Korah . Psa 44:1 O God , we have heard with our ears , our fathers have told us, what deeds you performed in their days , in the days of old : Psa 44:2 you with your own hand drove out the nations , but them you planted ; you afflicted the peoples , but them you set free ; Psa 44:3 for not by their own sword did they win the land , nor did their own arm save them, but your right hand and your arm , and the light of your face , for you delighted in them . Psa 44:4 You are my King , O God ; ordain salvation for Jacob ! Psa 44:5 Through you we push down our foes ; through your name we tread down those who rise up against us . Psa 44:6 For not in my bow do I trust , nor can my sword save me . Psa 44:7 But you have saved us from our foes and have put to shame those who hate us. Psa 44:8 In God we have boasted continually , and we will give thanks to your name forever . Selah Psa 44:9 But you have rejected us and disgraced us and have not gone out with our armies . Psa 44:10 You have made us turn back from the foe , and those who hate us have gotten spoil .

**boast** — Psa 44:8  ·  `H1984:boast#2`

_Story_

Here the very same act of boasting appears, but turned the right way round. The nation glories 'in God' - not in itself - and it does so all the time, boasting bound up with thanksgiving. This bragging has a real foundation: it rests on the rescues God worked for their fathers, driving out nations and saving them. So the same glorying that elsewhere is arrogance becomes, when it points to God, simply praise. The poignancy is that they say this even as they feel rejected - their confident boast in God stands against the sense that God has turned away, and they hold both together.

_Reading_

This is the family's counter-instance: 'boast / glory' (halal) typed as an action (D102 type=action), but here directed rightly. Sense (D101): 'boast / glory (halal)'. Uniquely for a boast in this set, a source is recorded (D103, pair, span): the boasting is 'grounded in God's past saving deeds (v7)' - it is not self-generated pride but a response to what God has done. Seat (D104) is 'none' - not organ-located. Bearer (D105) is the nation (inferred); operation (D106) 'boast / glory'; and the target (D107) is 'in God' - the vaunting aims away from the self, toward God. Manner (D108) is 'continually' (inferred): habitual, unceasing. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false): no degree, no qualifier, no tagged outcome. Coupling (D112) is a pair: 'paired with giving thanks' - the boast is welded to thanksgiving, not to appetite or evil. Prohibition absent (D113 present:false). The discovery (D114): the nation's habitual glorying in God, grounded in his past deeds, the confidence now tested by rejection. Role (D115) characteristic; locus (D116) external:god - alone among the readings here, the locus is God himself, not an inner state. On the inner-being lens the finding is that the very act (halal) that elsewhere in this family is pride's speech is here redeemed: the same movement of glorying, when its source is God's deeds and its target is God, becomes praise - the interior directed outward and upward rather than curved on itself. That this confident boast is set against v9's rejection is the tension the passage holds.

*Why this reading differs:* This is the only 'boast' (halal) reading whose target is 'in God' and whose locus is external:god, and the only one with a recorded source (God's past deeds) and a positive coupling (thanksgiving). Against #1 (self/greed), #3 (riches), #4 (evil), #5 (idols), #2 shows the identical verb redeemed: the difference in TARGET and SOURCE flips the same movement from pride to praise - decisive evidence that halal's meaning is set by what it is boasted in, not by the act itself.

## Psa 44:12-13

> Psa 44:12 You have sold your people for a trifle , demanding no high price for them. Psa 44:13 You have made us the taunt of our neighbors , the derision and scorn of those around us .

**taunt** — Psa 44:13  ·  `H2781:taunt#1`

_Story_

The nation that once boasted proudly in God is now the joke of the neighbourhood. God has, as it were, sold them off cheap, and the surrounding peoples sneer and scorn them. Here the people do nothing - they are not the ones mocking; they are the ones mocked, turned into a byword. That is the whole weight of it: they have become a thing others laugh at. The same people who gloried in God a few lines earlier now sit in disgrace, their proud confidence turned inside out into public shame.

_Reading_

The characteristic is 'reproach / taunt' (cherpah) typed not as an action but as a status (D102 type=status) - a condition the sufferer is put into, not a deed he does. Sense (D101): 'reproach / taunt (cherpah)'. Source is absent (D103 present:false) - no cause named for the disgrace. Seat (D104) is 'none' - not organ-located. Bearer (D105) is the nation, 'made a taunt' (inferred) - the people are the object, reduced to a byword. Distinctively, the operation (D106) is 'none' (resolution none): the nation performs no act; it is acted upon - a striking silence where in most readings an operation is present. Likewise the target (D107) is 'none': there is no one the nation taunts, because the nation IS the taunt. Manner (D108) is a pair: 'derision and scorn of neighbours' - the disgrace is qualified by the surrounding peoples' contempt. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false). Coupling (D112) is 'none'. Prohibition absent (D113 present:false). The discovery (D114): the nation reduced to an object of reproach, the once-boasting people derided and scorned by those around. Role (D115) characteristic; locus (D116) internal:ib-state. On the inner-being lens the finding is the underside of #2 (Psa 44:8): the people who boasted in God are now themselves made a public shame. The empty operation and empty target are the point - this taunt is a passive humiliation, a state of being scorned, the interior collapse of a people whose glory has become disgrace.

*Why this reading differs:* This is the family's only H2781 (cherpah) reading typed as a status with an empty operation and empty target - the sufferer is the taunt, not the taunter. It is the deliberate inverse of the same passage's #2 (Psa 44:8) boast in God: proud glorying and public disgrace bracket one psalm, and the reversal is itself the finding.

**boast** — Psa 49:6  ·  `H1984:boast#3`

_Story_

These are people who trust their money and then brag about how much of it they have. Their boasting and their trusting are the same thing: they lean on their wealth and can't help showing it off. But the poem sets this against a hard fact - no amount of riches can buy anyone out of death. The bragging that feels so solid is really folly, and death will expose it. Where the good kind of boasting points to God, this one points to a bank balance that cannot save its owner.

_Reading_

The characteristic is 'boast' (halal) typed as an action (D102 type=action). Sense (D101): 'boast (halal)'. Source absent (D103 present:false) - the passage does not derive the boasting from a prior cause. Seat (D104) 'none' - not organ-located. Bearer (D105) is the rich (inferred); operation (D106) 'boast'; target (D107) is 'the abundance of their riches' - the vaunting aims at wealth. Manner (D108) 'none'. Intensity, specifier, effect absent (D109/D110/D111 present:false): no degree, no qualifier, and the outcome (death exposing the folly) belongs to the wider passage but is not tagged here. Coupling (D112) is a pair: 'paired with trusting in wealth' - the boast is welded to trust; to brag of riches is to lean on them. Prohibition absent (D113 present:false). The discovery (D114): the fool's self-glory in riches, the vaunting that death exposes as folly. Role (D115) characteristic; locus (D116) internal:ib-state. On the inner-being lens the finding is that this boast is misplaced trust made audible: the interior rests its security on wealth and then broadcasts it, so that pride and trust are the same motion pointed at a thing that cannot ransom a life. Against the family's God-directed boast (#2), here the target is riches; the same act curves onto possessions rather than God.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the five 'boast' (halal) readings this one alone targets 'the abundance of riches' and is coupled to 'trusting in wealth' - pride as misplaced security. It differs from #2 (boast in God), #1 (in one's own greed), #4 (in evil) and #5 (in idols): here the object is wealth, and the finding is that boasting and trusting fuse, both aimed at what death nullifies.
