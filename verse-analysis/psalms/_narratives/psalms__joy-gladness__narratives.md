## Psa 2:5-12

> Psa 2:4 He who sits in the heavens laughs; the Lord holds them in derision. Psa 2:5 Then he will speak to them in his wrath , and terrify them in his fury , saying, Psa 2:6 “As for me , I have set my King on Zion , my holy hill .” Psa 2:7 I will tell of the decree : The Lord said to me, “You are my Son ; today I have begotten you . Psa 2:8 Ask of me, and I will make the nations your heritage , and the ends of the earth your possession . Psa 2:9 You shall break them with a rod of iron and dash them in pieces like a potter’s vessel .” Psa 2:10 Now therefore, O kings , be wise ; be warned , O rulers of the earth . Psa 2:11 Serve the Lord with fear , and rejoice with trembling . Psa 2:12 Kiss the Son , lest he be angry , and you perish in the way , for his wrath is quickly kindled . Blessed are all who take refuge in him .

**rejoice** — Psa 2:11  ·  `H1523:rejoic#1`

_Story_

Rulers are told to serve the Lord and, in the same breath, to rejoice while they tremble. This is not fear cancelling joy or joy cancelling fear; the two are fused into a single response - a gladness that never loses its awe. Nothing is said about what triggers it, how strong it is, or what it leads to; it is simply the right way for the powerful to stand before the enthroned King. It is a joy that keeps its reverence, felt deep in the person even as it looks upward.

_Reading_

The sense is 'rejoice with trembling' (101) and the type is affect (102): a felt movement of the interior, not an act performed. The bearer is the kings and rulers of the earth (105), summoned to serve. The operation (106) is the paradoxical joy of gladness held together with dread, and the target (107) is 'awed-joy' - the coupling (112) welds rejoice to trembling so neither cancels the other. Source is unmarked (103 present:false): no separate cause is named, the joy is simply the fitting response to the enthroned Son. No seat (104 'none') - not organ-located but a whole-self stance. No manner beyond the pairing (108 'none'); intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all unrecorded (109/110/111/113 present:false): the joy is not graded, not narrowed, not made a means to a further end, and never forbidden. The discovery (114) reads it as a submission that is glad yet never casual. Role is characteristic (115); locus internal:ib-state (116) - the movement lives inside the person even as it faces the King.

*Why this reading differs:* Uniquely among the eleven gil readings, this one welds joy to trembling (awed-joy) and gives it an unusual bearer - the kings and rulers of the earth summoned to submit - where every other gil reading grounds joy in salvation, trust, restoration, God's judgments, a festal day, or relation to God. Here the distinctive is the fusion of gladness with dread.

## Psa 4:7-8

> Psa 4:7 You have put more joy in my heart than they have when their grain and wine abound . Psa 4:8 In peace I will both lie down and sleep ; for you alone , O Lord , make me dwell in safety .

**joy** — Psa 4:7  ·  `H8057:joy#1`

_Story_

The psalmist says God has put more joy in his heart than others feel at a bumper harvest of grain and wine. The gladness is a gift - God placed it there - and it simply outweighs the happiest material plenty. There is no measuring of how great it is or what it produces next; it stands on its own as an inner fullness. The point is stark: the best that abundance can give is surpassed by a joy that comes from God.

_Reading_

The sense is 'joy greater than harvest' (101), an affect (102) placed in the heart. The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is God having put more joy in the heart than the abundance of grain and wine gives, and the target (107) is 'surpassing-joy', coupled (112) as joy-over-harvest. Source is unmarked (103 present:false) at the row level, yet the operation makes God the giver - the joy is not self-generated but received. No seat (104 'none'): though the verse says 'heart', the reading treats the joy as a whole-self state. No manner (108); intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113 present:false) - the gladness is not measured, not qualified, not instrumental, not interdicted; it is terminal. The discovery (114) locates it in God and notes it outweighs the best material happiness. Role characteristic (115); locus internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* This simchah reading places joy in the heart as a present possession outweighing material abundance, where #2 orients joy to its future destination (fullness in God's presence), #3 makes it the gladness filling the righteous' jubilation, #4 pictures it as seed sown for the upright, and #5 subordinates it beneath the memory of Jerusalem in exile. Here the distinctive is joy already given, surpassing plenty.

_Story_

**glad** — Psa 9:2  ·  `H8055:glad#1`

_Story_

The psalmist declares he will be glad and exult in God, singing praise to the name of the Most High. This is joy reaching upward, fastened to God at his highest - not just thanking him for things, but delighting in who he is. The text does not measure the gladness or say what it produces; it simply lifts toward its greatest object. Gladness here is an upward leap of the whole person toward the Most High.

_Reading_

The sense is 'glad and exult in the Most High' (101), an affect (102). The bearer is the psalmist (105); the operation (106) is the self being glad and exulting while singing praise to the name of the Most High, target (107) 'exultation', coupled (112) glad-and-exult. Source unmarked (103 present:false) - no separate trigger; the object itself, the Most High, draws the joy upward. No seat (104 'none'), whole-self; no manner (108). Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition all absent (109/110/111/113 present:false): the gladness is ungraded, unqualified, terminal, and never interdicted. The discovery (114) marks it distinct from the thanks-recital as an upward-leaping joy fixed on the Most High. Role characteristic (115); locus internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* One of twenty readings of samach ('glad'). Here the bearer is the singing psalmist and the joy leaps upward to 'the Most High', paired with exultation - distinct from the many communal or restoration-grounded gladness readings, and from the darker instances (Egypt's relief, the enemies' malice) where the same word carries no praise.

_Story_

*Why this reading differs:* This gil reading uniquely marks joy as the sequel to trust - the heart that first chose to rely on God then rejoices - where #1 fuses joy with trembling and #2 grounds it publicly at Zion's gates. The movement here is inward and sequential: sorrow, then trust, then joy.

**rejoice** — Psa 14:7  ·  `H1523:rejoic#4`

_Story_

At the very end of a psalm about human corruption, the hope breaks through: when God restores his people's fortunes, Jacob will rejoice and Israel will be glad. This is a shared, forward-looking joy - not one person's feeling but a whole people longing for the day God acts. The gladness is still future, reached for across the darkness of the psalm. It is the community's answer to corruption: a joy held in hope of restoration.

_Reading_

The sense is 'gladness of restoration' (101), an affect (102). The bearer is Jacob / Israel (105) - the joy is communal, not individual. The operation (106) is that when the LORD restores the fortunes of his people, Jacob rejoices and Israel is glad; the target (107) is 'restoration-joy', coupled (112) rejoice-at-restoring. Source unmarked (103 present:false): the restoring act is the ground, but no prior cause is named. No seat (104 'none'), whole-people state; no manner (108). Intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113 present:false). The discovery (114) sets this joy against the whole psalm's portrait of corruption: the interior reaches forward to a communal gladness for when God finally acts. Role characteristic (115); locus internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* Unlike the individual gil readings (#1-#3), the bearer here is the whole people (Jacob/Israel) and the joy is anticipated, not present - reaching forward to God's restoring act. It is nearly identical to #8 (Psa 53:6), a doublet psalm; the difference is only its setting inside Psalm 14's portrait of universal corruption.

**joy** — Psa 137:6  ·  `H8057:joy#5`

_Story_

Let my tongue stick to the roof of my mouth if I do not set Jerusalem above my highest joy. Here, uniquely, joy is not celebrated but ranked - the exile swears to place the memory of Jerusalem above his greatest gladness. His deepest joy is deliberately subordinated to his love for the ruined city. In exile, joy itself is measured and ordered: nothing, not even his highest happiness, may outrank Jerusalem.

_Reading_

The sense is 'joy (simchah)' (101), type state (102). Bearer is the psalmist (105); operation (106) 'rank Jerusalem above his highest joy'; target (107) 'in exile'. The coupling (112) is booked 'internal:ib-state'; the locus (116) is a descriptive pair 'paired with remembering the city'. Source, intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (103/109/110/111/113 present:false); seat 'none' (104); manner 'none' (108). The discovery (114) reads it as the highest gladness itself made subordinate to the memory of Jerusalem - love that orders every joy beneath the city. Role characteristic (115).

*Why this reading differs:* This simchah reading is the family's counter-note: rather than being expressed or sought, joy is here subordinated - the exile makes his highest gladness rank beneath the memory of Jerusalem. Where every other simchah reading celebrates or longs for joy, this one holds joy under judgment, refusing it first place.

**rejoice** — Psa 149:2  ·  `H1523:rejoic#11`

_Story_

Let Israel be glad in his Maker; let the children of Zion rejoice in their King. The joy is grounded in relationship - gladness in God as the one who made them, rejoicing in God as the one who rules them. It is fixed not on a gift but on who God is to his people: their Maker and their King. Here joy flows from belonging to God in these two ways.

_Reading_

The sense is 'glad in Maker, rejoice in King' (101), type affect (102). Bearer is Israel / the godly (105); operation (106) is Israel being glad in its Maker and the children of Zion rejoicing in their King - joy grounded in relation - target (107) 'relational-joy', coupled (112) glad-in-Maker. Source unmarked (103 present:false): no separate cause, the relationship itself is the ground. No seat (104 'none'), whole-people; manner 'none' (108); intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113 present:false). The discovery (114) reads it as joy located precisely in who God is to them: maker and king. Role characteristic (115); locus internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* This gil reading grounds joy in relationship - God as Maker and King - a summary, relational note distinct from the salvation-, trust-, presence-, judgment-, or festal-day objects of the other gil readings. It names the ground of joy as who God is to his people.

**exult** — Psa 149:5  ·  `H5937:exult#4`

_Story_

Let the godly exult in glory; let them sing for joy on their beds. The joy does not stop when the worship service ends - it follows the faithful home, into the solitary hours of night, so that even lying on their beds they still exult and sing. Here joy is unbroken and private as well as public: the gladness of God's people spills past the assembly into the quiet of rest.

_Reading_

The sense is 'the godly exult on their beds' (101), type affect (102). Bearer is the godly (105); operation (106) is the faithful exulting in glory and singing for joy even on their beds - praise carried into private night - target (107) 'exultation', coupled (112) exult-on-beds. Source unmarked (103 present:false): no separate trigger, the joy of God's favour carries over. No seat (104 'none'), whole-self; manner 'none' (108); intensity, specifier, effect, prohibition absent (109/110/111/113 present:false). The discovery (114) reads it as praise carried past the assembly into the solitary hours of rest. Role characteristic (115); locus internal:ib-state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* This alats/exult reading carries joy into the private night - the godly exulting on their beds - where #1 turns the heart's exulting into public thanksgiving, #2 exults before God's revealed name in worship, and #3 (the wicked) exults in cruel triumph. The distinctive here is joy in solitude, praise continuing beyond the assembly.
