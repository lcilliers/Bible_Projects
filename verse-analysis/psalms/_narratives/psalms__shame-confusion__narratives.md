**reproach** — Psa 31:11  ·  `H2781:reproach#1`

_Story_

David describes a specific kind of pain: he has become someone people are ashamed to be near. Because of those attacking him, his neighbours dread him and his old friends look away; when people see him in the street they hurry off in the other direction. He has not done anything here — this is something that has happened to him, a condition he has sunk into rather than a choice he made. The hurt is not in any one part of his body but sits deep inside, the ache of watching everyone withdraw. It is the loneliness of a person who feels he has become repellent to the very community he belonged to.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'a reproach, dreaded, fled from', and the type (102) is state — cherpah here is not an act the self performs but a condition it falls into. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the operation (106) traces the movement in full: 'because of adversaries the self has become a reproach, an object of dread to acquaintances, fled from in the street — the pain of social rejection.' The target (107) is social-shame: the reproach is directed outward into the arena of neighbours, acquaintances and passers-by, and it is there that the interior is wounded. Coupling (112) welds it to being shunned ('reproach-shunned') — the shame and the withdrawal of others are one linked event. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): the passage names the adversaries as occasion but registers no originating cause inside the item itself; the reproach simply lands. Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) is internal:ib-state — this is not lodged in an organ but is a state of the whole self, felt inwardly even as its cause is outward. Manner is 'none' (108): no mode is described. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are all absent (109/110/111/113 present:false): the reader measures no degree of the shame, adds no qualifier, traces no downstream consequence, and does not frame it as forbidden — the state is reported, not graded or resolved. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) fixes it: 'I have become a reproach... an object of dread to my acquaintances; those who see me in the street flee from me' — the interior suffers the withdrawal of everyone. Read for the inner being, reproach here is the wound of being made repellent to one's own community — the self experiences its own diminishment through the eyes of those who flee it.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the three reproach (cherpah) readings this is the only one where the reproach is a purely social wound turned inward (target social-shame, locus internal:ib-state) with no reference to God: the self becomes repellent to its community. By contrast #2 (69:7) and #3 (69:9) read the reproach as borne for God's sake with locus external:god — shame suffered on God's account rather than the pain of communal rejection.

**radiant** — Psa 34:5  ·  `H5102:radiant#1`

_Story_

In the middle of a family of poems all about shame, this one line shines the other way. People who turn their eyes toward God light up inside, and that inner light shows on their faces so that they are never left disgraced. It is not that God rescues them from shame after it strikes — it is that the simple act of looking to him fills them and keeps shame from ever settling. The looking and the shining are the same motion. Where the others describe faces covered and heads bowed, here the face is lifted and glowing.

_Reading_

This is the family's single ascent reading, and the sense (101) is 'those who look to him are radiant'. The type (102) is affect — a felt, luminous interior state, not an act or a mere status. The bearer (105) is 'those who look to him', a plural of the trusting, and the operation (106) is transformation by gaze: 'those who look to God are radiant, their faces never ashamed — the interior lit up by looking to him.' The target (107) is 'radiance' itself: the movement issues in an inner light that surfaces on the face. Coupling (112) welds the two halves into a single mechanism, 'look-and-be-radiant' — the looking and the shining are one linked motion. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): no external agent is named as igniting the radiance; it arises from the act of looking, which the reading treats as target/operation rather than as an outside source. Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) is internal:ib-state — this is not organ-lodged but a condition of the whole self. Manner is 'none' (108): no mode described. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (109/110/111/113 present:false): the reader records no degree of brightness, no qualifier, no separately traced consequence, and no prohibition — the shining is simply stated. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) crystallises it: 'transformation by gaze: the interior that turns to God is lit and kept from shame.' Read for the inner being, radiance is the exact inverse of the family's shame movements — where reproach and disgrace darken and cover the face, here the outward-turned gaze floods the interior with light and the face is guaranteed against shame. Shame is here defeated not by rescue after the fact but by the direction of attention.

*Why this reading differs:* The only reading in the family; also its only positive movement. Where every shame item traces a covering, wrapping or bowing of the face, radiant reverses the vector: the outward gaze toward God produces inner light (type affect, target radiance) and pre-empts shame ('their faces shall never be ashamed'), making it the structural counterpoint to the whole shame-confusion set.

**shame** — Psa 44:15  ·  `H1322:sham#1`

_Story_

This is a whole community's shame, not one person's. All day long a sense of disgrace hangs over them, and shame is drawn across their faces like a veil, hiding the dignity a face is meant to show. It is not aimed at anyone — it just settles down over them and stays there. What brings it on is the sound of taunts and the sight of gloating enemies, whose mockery makes the covering fall. The people do nothing back; they simply carry the humiliation inwardly, faces hidden, under the jeering of those around them.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'shame (bosheth — covers my face)', and the type (102) is status — a settled condition the sufferer inhabits. The bearer (105) is the nation: this is a communal shame, 'us', not one man. The operation (106) is a pair (spans 278724->...) 'cover the face' — shame behaves like a veil drawn over the countenance, the visible seat of dignity. The manner (108) is a pair (278724->278730): the shame is felt 'at the taunter and avenger', i.e. in the presence of mocking and hostile onlookers, which supply its occasion. Coupling (112) is a pair (278724->278722) binding shame to disgrace ('paired with disgrace'), linking this reading to the kelimmah item alongside it in the same verse. Target is 'none' (107, resolution none): the shame does not aim outward at anyone — it settles on the self, a purely inward covering with no object. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): though the wider psalm names God's rejection (v9) as the deep cause, this bosheth item records no source, leaving the shame's origin unstated at the item level. Seat is 'none' (104) yet the operation and discovery both locate it on the face; locus (116) is internal:ib-state — the covering is an inner condition surfacing outwardly, not an organ. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (109/110/111/113): no degree, qualifier, downstream consequence or prohibition is registered. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) reads it as 'shame as a veil over the countenance... the disgrace that covers the face at the sound of the taunter and reviler.' For the inner being, shame here is a communal covering — a whole people's dignity screened out by humiliation, felt inwardly with no outward target, simply endured before the jeering of enemies.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the two bosheth ('shame') readings this one is self-suffered: the nation's own shame covers its face, with no target (107 none) and no bearer-as-enemy. The other, #2 (109:29), reverses it — shame is invoked upon the accusers, who are 'wrapped in shame as in a cloak', bearer = the accusers. Here shame descends on the sufferer; there it is called down on the foe.

**disgrace** — Psa 44:15  ·  `H3639:disgrac#1`

_Story_

Alongside the veil of shame in the same verse sits a heavier thing: disgrace that never moves and never leaves. It does not act or reach out; it just stands there, in front of the people every waking hour, all day long. And the poem does something startling — it traces this humiliation not only to the mocking enemies but back to God, who has cast his people off. The taunters are only the occasion; the real source is a God who seems to have turned away. The disgrace simply sits before them, a constant weight on the mind, with no relief in sight.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'disgrace / humiliation (kelimmah)', and the type (102) is status. Uniquely in this family, source IS recorded (103): a pair (spans 278722->278857) names 'God's rejecting and disgracing (v9)' — the deep origin of the humiliation is traced back to God's own act of casting off, an uncomfortable finding the reading does not soften. The bearer (105) is the nation ('my disgrace'). The operation (106) is 'none' (resolution none): notably, no verb-action is attached — the disgrace does not do anything or move; it simply stands. This is confirmed by target (107) 'none': it aims nowhere. Instead the manner (108) carries the weight: 'all day long before me' — the humiliation's mode is relentless, ceaseless presence in the sufferer's sight. Coupling (112) is a pair (278722->278724) 'paired with shame covering the face', binding it to the bosheth item beside it. Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) internal:ib-state — an inner condition, not organ-lodged. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (109/110/111/113): the disgrace is neither graded, qualified, given a consequence, nor forbidden. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) reads it as 'the humiliation constantly in view; kelimmah the shame the nation carries, its source God's rejecting and disgracing (v9 qualifier), the taunter and reviler its occasion.' For the inner being, disgrace here is a static, ever-present interior weight whose ultimate author, the reading dares to say, is God himself — the community's shame is not merely the enemies' doing but the felt outcome of divine rejection, held constantly before the mind's eye with no movement and no relief.

*Why this reading differs:* The only kelimmah 'disgrace' reading; the sole item in the family to record a source (103), and it names God's own rejecting and disgracing (v9) as origin. It is also distinctive for having no operation and no target (both 'none') — a static, motionless humiliation whose whole force is carried by its manner, 'all day long before me'. Distinguished from the paired bosheth (#1) in the same verse: shame covers the face (an act), disgrace merely stands in view (a state).

**put them to shame** — Psa 53:5  ·  `H0954:putthemtosham#1`

_Story_

The poem watches what happens inside people who told themselves there is no God. Their swagger falls apart. Sudden terror grips them even where nothing frightening is present, and with it comes disgrace — they are exposed and defeated from the inside. And the reason given is stark: God has rejected them, and that rejection is the ground of their collapse. The confidence they built on denying God simply caves in, leaving panic and shame where certainty used to be. It is the undoing of a self-assured heart that had no room for God.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'be put to shame (bosh — you put them to shame)', and the type (102) is status. Source IS recorded (103): a pair (spans 279936->279939) names 'because God has rejected them (v5)' — the shame of the godless is grounded in God's prior rejection. The bearer (105) is 'the evildoers' — this is a descent reading, the interior of the wicked, read (per directive 5) with the same weight as any sufferer's. The operation (106) is 'be put to shame' (inferred), a passive undergoing: the fools who said 'there is no God' are made to bear disgrace. Target is 'none' (107, resolution none): the shame does not reach outward but collapses inward on them. Coupling (112) is a pair (279936->279928) 'paired with their terror' — the shame is welded to the great dread that seizes them 'where there is no terror', so exposure and panic are one event. Seat is 'none' (104) and locus (116) internal:ib-state — the ruin is interior, not organ-lodged. Manner is 'none' (108). Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (109/110/111/113): no degree, qualifier, traced consequence or prohibition. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) reads it as 'the disgrace of the godless when their confidence collapses... the inner ruin of exposure and defeat, grounded in God's rejection of them.' For the inner being, this is shame as the interior undoing of the self-assured wicked: the ones who denied God are stripped of their false confidence, their bravado dissolving into terror and disgrace precisely because God has rejected them — the collapse of a godless interior from the inside out.

*Why this reading differs:* This bosh reading (char_key putthemtosham, distinct from puttosham) is a descent reading focused on the interior collapse of the wicked, and it records a source (103): 'because God has rejected them'. Its coupling to terror (112) marks it out — shame here is fused with panic, the inner ruin of exposed godlessness, rather than the vindication-shame invoked on enemies elsewhere in the family.

## Psa 69:3-7

> To the choirmaster : according to Lilies . Of David . Psa 69:1 Save me, O God ! For the waters have come up to my neck . Psa 69:3 I am weary with my crying out ; my throat is parched . My eyes grow dim with waiting for my God . Psa 69:4 More in number than the hairs of my head are those who hate me without cause ; mighty are those who would destroy me, those who attack me with lies . What I did not steal must I now restore ? Psa 69:5 O God , you know my folly ; the wrongs I have done are not hidden from you. Psa 69:7 For it is for your sake that I have borne reproach , that dishonor has covered my face .

**reproach** — Psa 69:7  ·  `H2781:reproach#2`

_Story_

Here the psalmist owns his shame in a different way: he carries it deliberately, and he carries it because of God. The insults heaped on him are not random misfortune but the cost of belonging to God, and he shoulders them the way a man carries a heavy load he has agreed to bear. The disgrace shows on him like a covering over his face, and it is knotted together with the wider scorn that falls on him for God's sake. What could have crushed him he instead takes up as loyalty. His humiliation points away from himself and toward the God whose honour he shares.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'reproach (cherpah — for your sake I have borne reproach)', and the type (102) is status — a standing the psalmist occupies rather than a passing feeling. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the operation (106) is 'borne / carried': crucially this is an active endurance, the self shouldering a load, not merely sinking into a condition. The target (107) is 'for God's sake' — the whole vector of the reproach points toward God as its reason, which is why the locus (116) is external:god: the shame reaches outside the self and is bound up with God's own honour. Manner (108) is a pair (spans 281896->281897): the reproach is borne with 'dishonor covering his face', shame's outward mark. Coupling (112) is a pair (281896->281905) tying it to 'the reproaches that fell on him for God (v9)' — this reading anticipates and links to reading #3. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): no cause internal to the item is named; the reproach's origin lies in his devotion, stated as target rather than source. Seat is 'none' (104): not organ-located. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (109/110/111/113): no degree, qualifier, consequence or prohibition is registered — the endurance is stated without being measured or resolved. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) reads it as 'shame suffered on God's account... his disgrace bound up with God's.' For the inner being, reproach here is a chosen, borne shame — the self willingly carries humiliation because its life is fastened to God, transfiguring disgrace into an act of loyalty.

*Why this reading differs:* Differs from #1 (31:11) in that the reproach is not the inward pain of social rejection but a status actively borne for God (target 'for God's sake', locus external:god, operation 'borne/carried'). It differs from #3 (69:9) in direction: here the psalmist carries the reproach as his own devotional burden, whereas #3 reads reproaches originally aimed at God falling onto him — endurance chosen vs. scorn received.

**dishonor** — Psa 69:7  ·  `H3639:dishonor#1`

_Story_

The shame the psalmist carries for God's sake does not stay hidden — it shows on his face. Dishonour has come down over his features like a cloth pulled across them, the visible sign of everything he is bearing within. It does not lash out at anyone; it simply settles on him, resting on the very part of a person where dignity usually shows. This covering of the face is the outward mark of the reproach he shoulders because he belongs to God. What he suffers inside has surfaced for everyone to see.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'dishonor / shame (kelimmah — dishonor has covered my face)', and the type (102) is status. The bearer (105) is the psalmist. Distinctively, seat IS recorded (104): 'the face' — this reading lodges the dishonor on a specific bodily site, the countenance, the visible register of a person's standing. The operation (106) is 'cover his face' (inferred): the shame behaves like a garment drawn over the face. Target is 'none' (107): the dishonor does not aim outward but settles inward. Coupling (112) is a pair (spans 281897->281896) 'paired with the reproach borne for God', binding this item to the cherpah of the same verse (reading #2) — dishonor is the visible mark of the reproach. Source is not recorded (103 present:false): no separate origin is named at the item level; the covering is stated as fact. Manner is 'none' (108). Locus (116) is internal:ib-state: though it rests on the face, the dishonor is an inner condition surfacing outwardly. Intensity, specifier, effect and prohibition are absent (109/110/111/113): no degree, qualifier, traced consequence or prohibition. Role (115) is characteristic. The discovery (114) reads it as 'humiliation veiling the psalmist like a garment... the visible mark of the reproach borne for God's sake.' For the inner being, dishonor here is the outward face of an inwardly borne shame: what the psalmist carries within (the reproach for God) shows itself as a covering over his face — the interior humiliation made visible, seated on the countenance for all to see.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the two kelimmah 'dishonor' readings this one is self-suffered and, uniquely, seats the shame on 'the face' (104) — the psalmist's own humiliation made visible as the outward mark of reproach borne for God. The other, #2 (109:29), reverses the bearer: dishonor is invoked upon the accusers, 'clothed with dishonor', with seat 'none'. Here the face wears shame; there enemies are dressed in it as a called-down garment.

## Psa 69:9-10

> Psa 69:9 For zeal for your house has consumed me, and the reproaches of those who reproach you have fallen on me . Psa 69:10 When I wept and humbled my soul with fasting , it became my reproach .

**reproach** — Psa 69:9  ·  `H2781:reproach#3`

_Story_

The mockery here was never really aimed at the psalmist first — it was aimed at God, and it simply lands on him because he stands so close to God. Insults thrown at heaven fall on the man beneath. He does not go looking to carry them; they come down on him because he and God have become inseparable in the eyes of the scoffers. When he wept and fasted, even that was turned into more scorn. His identification with God is so complete that God's disgrace becomes his own, absorbed straight into his heart.

_Reading_

The sense (101) is 'reproaches (cherpah — the reproaches of those who reproach you fell on me)', and the type (102) is status. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, but the operation (106) is distinctive: 'fall on him' (a pair, spans 281905->281907) — the reproach is received, landing on the self from elsewhere rather than actively shouldered. The target (107) is the key finding: 'originally aimed at God' — the scorn was launched at God and lands on his servant, so the movement runs God -> reviler -> psalmist. Accordingly the locus (116) is external:god: the reproach belongs to God's sphere and only secondarily settles on the man. Coupling (112) is a pair (281905->281896) binding it back to 'the reproach borne for God (v7)', linking this reading to #2.
