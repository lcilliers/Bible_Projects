## Psa 1:1-6

> Psa 1:1 Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of the wicked , nor stands in the way of sinners , nor sits in the seat of scoffers ; Psa 1:2 but his delight is in the law of the Lord , and on his law he meditates day and night . Psa 1:3 He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season , and its leaf does not wither . In all that he does , he prospers . Psa 1:4 The wicked are not so , but are like chaff that the wind drives away. Psa 1:5 Therefore the wicked will not stand in the judgment , nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous ; Psa 1:6 for the Lord knows the way of the righteous , but the way of the wicked will perish .

**Blessed** — Psa 1:1  ·  `H0835:bless#1`

The psalter's opening word weighs a whole human life and pronounces it happy (101 'blessed is the man'; 102 affect): beatitude is not a feeling that flares but the settled well-being (106) carried by the blessed man himself (105) — the bearer who walks not in the counsel of the wicked, delights in the law, and prospers like a watered tree. The movement is inward and terminal: its locus is an interior state (116 internal:ib-state), its target simply the beatitude it names (107), and its coupling is blessedness as such (112). The silences are eloquent — no source is recorded (103 absent), no seat (104 none), no manner, no intensity, no effect, no prohibition: this happiness is not lodged in a bodily organ, is not graded up or down, produces no further downstream act, and is forbidden to no one. It is read as a role-bearing characteristic (115), a state the whole shaped life simply is rather than does.

*Why this reading differs:* The Psalter's opening esher-beatitude and the archetype of the whole item: among the H0835 readings this one grounds happiness in a whole way of life (avoidance of the wicked's counsel joined to delight in the law), not in a single footing like forgiveness (32:1), trust (40:4), the fear of God (112:1), discipline (94:12), or prosperity (144:15) — the foundational, comprehensive beatitude the later readings each narrow.

## Psa 3:1-8

Many are rising against me ; Psa 3:2 many are saying of my soul , "There is no salvation for him in God ." Psa 3:7 Arise , O Lord ! Save me, O my God ! For you strike all my enemies on the cheek ; you break the teeth of the wicked . Psa 3:8 Salvation belongs to the Lord ; your blessing be on your people ! Selah

**Salvation** — Psa 3:8  ·  `H3444:salvation#1`

Against the taunt hurled at the psalmist's very soul in v2 ('there is no salvation for him in God'), the interior lands, at the close, on the opposite settled conviction: salvation belongs to the LORD (101; 102 cognition). This is a movement of the mind — the self (105) settling its own theology of rescue (106), confessing (107) what it now holds as certain, the confession coupled to the conviction that salvation is God's alone (112). The absences frame it: no source is named (103), no seat (104 none) — the conviction is not organ-lodged; no manner, intensity, effect or prohibition. That effect-silence matters — the confession is offered as an end in itself, the interior at rest in what it knows, not as a lever to a further outcome. Locus is internal (116): the drama is entirely a change of mind inside the man, God the arena it now trusts.

**bless** — Psa 16:7  ·  `H1288:bles#1`

Here blessing runs outward from the human interior toward God: 'I bless the LORD who gives me counsel' (101; 102 affect). The self (105) performs the operation of blessing the God who guides it (106), the act a grateful-blessing (107) specifically coupled to the counsel received (112 bless-for-counsel) — gratitude aimed not at rescue but at being advised, with the night-heart instructing him as the arena of that guidance. The effect-silence reads as devotion offered for its own sake. The lens stays on the inner being: the movement is the self's answering thanks, and its locus is named an interior state (116).

*Why this reading differs:* This is barak directed at God in thanks, not the esher-state; and among the bless-God readings this one is narrowly occasioned — blessing specifically for divine counsel/guidance, not for rescue, hearing, or in the assembly.

**bless** — Psa 26:12  ·  `H1288:bles#2`

The vindicated interior, having pleaded its integrity through the whole psalm, closes by turning to public praise: 'in the great assembly I will bless the LORD' (101; 102 affect). *Why this reading differs:* Distinct from the other bless-God readings by its setting: this blessing is explicitly public, resolved 'in the great assembly', the endpoint of a plea for vindication rather than gratitude for a specific rescue or counsel.
