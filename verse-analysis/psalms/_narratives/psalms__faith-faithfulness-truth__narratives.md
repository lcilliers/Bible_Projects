## Psa 12:1-4

> To the choirmaster : according to The Sheminith . A Psalm of David . Psa 12:1 Save , O Lord , for the godly one is gone ; for the faithful have vanished from among the children of man . Psa 12:2 Everyone utters lies to his neighbor ; with flattering lips and a double heart they speak . Psa 12:3 May the Lord cut off all flattering lips , the tongue that makes great boasts, Psa 12:4 those who say , “ With our tongue we will prevail , our lips are with us; who is master over us ?” Psa 12:5 “Because the poor are plundered, because the needy groan, I will now arise,” says the Lord; “I will place him in the safety for which he longs.”

**godly** — Psa 12:1  ·  `H2623:godly#1`

_Story_

The psalmist looks around for someone he can trust and finds no one. He cries out that the good and faithful people have simply disappeared from among ordinary men. This is not a calm observation but raw grief — an ache spread through his whole being rather than a single passing feeling. He does not explain how it came to this or blame a particular cause; he just states the bare, heavy fact and lets it stand. There is no measuring of how bad it is and no talk of what will come next — the loss itself is the whole weight. What the passage shows is a person whose inner world has been emptied of trustworthy company, and who feels that emptiness as a wound.

_Reading_

Here the godly (chasid) is read not as a present quality but as an absence grieved: sense (101) = 'the godly have vanished'. Its type (102) is affect, not status — the item registers in the interior as felt grief. There is no source (103 present:false): the psalmist does not name what produced the loss, so the reading rests on the fact of it, not its cause. Seat (104) = none — the grief is not organ-located; it is a whole-self lament, not a movement of a named faculty. The bearer (105) is the psalmist, and the operation (106) is his lament that the godly one is gone and the faithful have disappeared from among men — grief at the collapse of faithfulness itself. Target (107) is lament-over-loss: the movement turns not outward against enemies but inward-and-back upon a vanished good. Manner (108) is none, and intensity, specifier and effect are all absent (109-111 present:false) — the psalm states the loss flatly, without measuring its degree, qualifying it, or naming a downstream result; the silence says the fact alone is the weight. Coupling (112) = godly-vanished, binding the item to its own disappearance — the characteristic is coupled to its own negation. No prohibition (113 present:false) is in view. Discovery (114): the interior grieves a social collapse — it can no longer find a trustworthy person. Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = internal:ib-state — this is an inner condition, the felt state of a self that scans its world and finds no one faithful. Directive 5 requires reading descent as fully as ascent: this is the faith-family in its failure mode, faithfulness experienced as loss.

*Why this reading differs:* Of the three 'godly' readings, this one alone reads the characteristic as ABSENCE and as affect (grief), not status: the godly are gone, and the item lives in the interior as lament (locus internal:ib-state, bearer the psalmist). It is the descent pole of the item, against Psa 52:9 (the godly as an external company he waits among) and Psa 86:2 (the godly as the psalmist's own claimed devotion).

## Psa 27:11-14

> Psa 27:11 Teach me your way , O Lord , and lead me on a level path because of my enemies . Psa 27:12 Give me not up to the will of my adversaries ; for false witnesses have risen against me, and they breathe out violence . Psa 27:13 I believe that I shall look upon the goodness of the Lord in the land of the living ! Psa 27:14 Wait for the Lord ; be strong , and let your heart take courage ; wait for the Lord !

**believe** — Psa 27:13  ·  `H0539:believ#1`

_Story_

Hemmed in by enemies and lying witnesses, the psalmist makes a quiet, load-bearing declaration: he believes he will still see good things from the Lord while he is alive. This is more than an idea in his head — it is a feeling that props him up and keeps him from caving in to despair. He does not argue for it or say where the confidence came from; it is simply the stance his heart takes. And he does not measure how strong it is or spell out what it will lead to — the point is that this expectation of coming good is exactly what holds him together. His believing reaches forward toward a day when he will see the Lord's goodness, and that forward reach is what keeps his inner self from collapsing.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = 'I believe I shall see his goodness'; the item is belief as a forward-leaning conviction about what the future holds. Type (102) is affect — here believing is not a bare cognitive assent but a felt steadying of the self. No source (103 present:false) is named: the psalm does not derive the belief from a prior cause but presents it as the interior's own act. Seat (104) = none — belief is not lodged in a named organ; it is the whole self's posture. Bearer (105) is the psalmist; operation (106) = the self believes it will look upon the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living, a faith that steadies against despair. Target (107) = sustaining-faith: the movement's endpoint is the holding-up of the self. Manner (108) is none; intensity, specifier and effect are all absent (109-111 present:false) — the text does not grade the belief's strength, qualify it, or trace a result beyond the steadying itself. Coupling (112) = believe-to-see: belief is bound to a coming sight, faith reaching toward future vision. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the operation is the faith that keeps the interior from collapse — belief that it will yet see good is what holds it, framed against the false witnesses and violence of v12. Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = internal:ib-state — an inner condition that props the self up under threat. God is the arena toward which the belief looks; the inner-being process is the self holding itself steady by expecting good.

*Why this reading differs:* Among the six 'believe' readings this is the only one typed as affect and read as ascent from the psalmist's own mouth: belief as a felt anchor against despair (target sustaining-faith, coupling believe-to-see). It contrasts with the disposition-typed failures of Psa 78:22/78:32/106:24 and the held-belief of Psa 116:10 and 119:66.

**truth** — Psa 45:4  ·  `H0571:truth#1`

_Story_

In this royal wedding song the king is pictured riding out in majesty to fight for a cause, and the first thing named in that cause is truth — set alongside gentleness and doing right. Truth here is not a hidden honesty tucked inside a person; it is a public banner, a standing value the king openly champions and works to establish in his realm. The song does not say where this truth comes from or picture it stirring anything inside him; it simply names it as the thing he stands for. It is bound together with meekness and righteousness, three virtues advanced as one. So truth appears less as something moving within a heart and more as a worthy cause a good ruler carries — a value he embodies rather than a feeling he experiences.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = truth (emeth) as 'the cause of truth' — truth here is not a private inner honesty but a public moral cause the king rides out to establish. Type (102) is status: truth names a standing condition, a value the king embodies and advances, not an event that happens. No source (103 present:false): the psalm does not trace where this truth arises; it is simply the cause he champions. Seat (104) = none — truth is not located in a named organ; it is a value carried, not an organ-movement. Bearer (105) is the king (his cause). Operation (106) = none and target (107) = none: strikingly, this truth performs no interior operation and reaches no interior target — the silence is evidence that here emeth functions as a standing banner, not a working movement within a self; it is what the king rides FOR, not something his inner being does. Manner (108) = none; intensity, specifier and effect are absent (109-111 present:false) — no degree, qualifier, or result is recorded, consistent with truth-as-status rather than truth-as-act. Coupling (112) is a pair: truth is bound with meekness and righteousness — the first of a triad of virtues the king advances together. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): 'ride out victoriously for the cause of truth and meekness and righteousness' — emeth is the moral cause the king's rule advances. Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = internal:ib-state — though its bearer is the royal cause, the item is still filed as an inner-being condition: truth as a value seated in the person of the king. The inner-being reading is thin here by design (directive 7): truth appears as a championed standing, with its operation and target explicitly empty.

*Why this reading differs:* The two 'truth' readings split outward vs inward. Here truth is a PUBLIC moral cause borne by the king (bearer = the king's cause; operation and target both 'none'; paired with meekness and righteousness) — truth as championed standing. Psa 51:6 reads the same emeth as INNER truthfulness in the inward being (seat present, bearer the penitent, locus internal:heart). The shift in bearer and seat is the finding: the same word names both a public cause and the honesty of the hidden self.

## Psa 50:3-7

> A Psalm of Asaph . Psa 50:1 The Mighty One , God the Lord , speaks and summons the earth from the rising of the sun to its setting . Psa 50:3 Our God comes ; he does not keep silence ; before him is a devouring fire , around him a mighty tempest . Psa 50:4 He calls to the heavens above and to the earth , that he may judge his people : Psa 50:5 “ Gather to me my faithful ones , who made a covenant with me by sacrifice !” Psa 50:6 The heavens declare his righteousness , for God himself is judge ! Selah Psa 50:7 “ Hear , O my people , and I will speak ; O Israel , I will testify against you. I am God , your God .

**faithful ones** — Psa 50:5  ·  `H2623:faithfulon#1`

_Story_

As God arrives to judge, wrapped in fire and storm, he issues a command: gather my faithful ones to me — the people who bound themselves to him by covenant and sacrifice. What defines these faithful ones here is not something they are actively doing but the fact that they are being called; their identity is set by God's summons. The passage does not show them stirring with any particular feeling or reaching for anything; they simply stand as those who belong to God and are ready to be gathered. Nothing else is tied to them in the moment — they stand alone before their Judge. Their faithfulness is a steady loyalty, rooted in covenant, that answers when God calls, and it marks them off from the wicked he will later confront.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = the godly / faithful ones (chasidim). Type (102) is status — a standing identity, the covenant-loyal, not an act. Uniquely in this family, source (103) is present: the faithful ones are summoned by God who comes to judge (v1,3-4) — a pair binding their gathering to God's advent. This is evidence: their standing is defined by being called, by the summons rather than by an inner initiative of their own. Seat (104) = none — no organ locates the identity; it is a whole-person belonging. Bearer (105) = the faithful / godly ones (chasidim). Operation (106) = none and target (107) = none: the reading records no interior movement and no interior target — the silence says these faithful ones are, at this moment, objects of a summons, not agents performing an operation; their inner-being contribution is their standing readiness to be gathered, not an act. Manner (108) = none; intensity, specifier, effect absent (109-111 present:false). Coupling (112) = none — no other item is welded to this one here; the faithful ones stand alone before the Judge. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): 'Gather to me my faithful ones, who made a covenant with me by sacrifice' — the covenant-loyal summoned before the Judge, the true worshippers distinguished from the wicked (v16). Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = external:god — the item's frame of reference is God who gathers, the arena toward which their loyalty is defined. The inner-being process is a standing loyalty that answers a summons: identity constituted by covenant and call rather than by any recorded interior act.

## Psa 51:1-19

> To the choirmaster . A Psalm of David , when Nathan the prophet went to him, after he had gone in to Bathsheba . Psa 51:1 Have mercy on me, O God , according to your steadfast love ; according to your abundant mercy blot out my transgressions . Psa 51:2 Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity , and cleanse me from my sin ! Psa 51:3 For I know my transgressions , and my sin is ever before me. Psa 51:4 Against you, you only , have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight , so that you may be justified in your words and blameless in your judgment . Psa 51:5 Behold , I was brought forth in iniquity , and in sin did my mother conceive me. Psa 51:6 Behold , you delight in truth in the inward being , and you teach me wisdom in the secret heart . Psa 51:7 Purge me with hyssop , and I shall be clean ; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow . Psa 51:8 Let me hear joy and gladness ; let the bones that you have broken rejoice . Psa 51:9 Hide your face from my sins , and blot out all my iniquities . Psa 51:10 Create in me a clean heart , O God , and renew a right spirit within me. Psa 51:11 Cast me not away from your presence , and take not your Holy Spirit from me . Psa 51:12 Restore to me the joy of your salvation , and uphold me with a willing spirit . Psa 51:13 Then I will teach transgressors your ways , and sinners will return to you. Psa 51:14 Deliver me from bloodguiltiness , O God , O God of my salvation , and my tongue will sing aloud of your righteousness . Psa 51:15 O Lord , open my lips , and my mouth will declare your praise . Psa 51:16 For you will not delight in sacrifice , or I would give it; you will not be pleased with a burnt offering . Psa 51:17 The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit ; a broken and contrite heart , O God , you will not despise . Psa 51:18 Do good to Zion in your good pleasure ; build up the walls of Jerusalem ; Psa 51:19 then will you delight in right sacrifices , in burnt offerings and whole burnt offerings; then bulls will be offered on your altar .

**truth** — Psa 51:6  ·  `H0571:truth#2`

_Story_

In the middle of his confession, David realises what God is really after: not outward ceremony but honesty deep down, truth in the hidden, innermost self. This is the one place in these psalms where such truth is pictured as living in a specific inner room of a person — the secret heart rather than out in public. It is described as something God delights in and longs to find there, a settled condition of the self rather than an action the self performs. And it comes paired with wisdom: the same secret place where God wants truthfulness is where he teaches wisdom. So the picture is of God reaching into a person's most hidden depths, wanting real honesty there, and meeting that honesty with wisdom he himself imparts.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = truth / faithfulness (emeth) 'in the inward being' — here emeth is inner truthfulness, the honesty of the hidden self, not an outward cause. Type (102) is status: a standing condition God desires within. Source (103) is present as a pair: truth is what God delights in / desires (v6) — its origin-frame is the divine delight that seeks it. Seat (104) is present, and this is the family's one located reading: 'in the inward being' — a pair binding truth to the deep interior (tuchoth), the hidden self. That the seat is named here (against 'none' everywhere else) is itself the finding: this is truth precisely as an interior organ-condition. Bearer (105) = the penitent. Operation (106) = none and target (107) = none — no discrete interior act or object is recorded; truth is a state God wants IN the self, not an operation the self performs. Manner (108) = none; intensity, specifier, effect absent (109-111 present:false) — no degree or result is graded; the point is the presence or absence of the condition itself. Coupling (112) is a pair: truth is bound with wisdom taught in the secret heart — the interior honesty God desires is answered by wisdom imparted in the hidden place. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the integrity God desires deep within; inner truthfulness, not outward conformity — the honesty of the hidden self, what God's delight seeks, answered by wisdom taught in the secret heart. Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = internal:heart. The inner-being process: God's desire reaches into the deepest interior and seeks truthfulness there, and that desired honesty is coupled to a wisdom taught in the secret heart — truth as the moral condition of the hidden self, with God as the arena that both wants it and supplies its companion, wisdom.

*Why this reading differs:* This reading turns truth INWARD against Psa 45:4's outward cause. Only here is a seat recorded ('in the inward being') and the locus is internal:heart, with the bearer the penitent rather than the king; source and coupling are both present (what God desires / paired with wisdom in the secret heart). The move from a public championed truth to the honesty of the hidden self, and from 'no seat' to a located seat, is the finding: emeth ranges from banner to buried integrity.

## Psa 52:1-9

> To the choirmaster . A Maskil of David , when Doeg , the Edomite , came and told Saul , “ David has come to the house of Ahimelech .” Psa 52:1 Why do you boast of evil , O mighty man ? **godly** — Psa 52:9  ·  `H2623:godly#2`

_Story_

After denouncing a violent, boastful man who trusted in his riches, the psalmist places himself in very different company: he waits and gives thanks in the presence of the godly. These faithful people are not shown doing anything in particular; their role is simply to be the good and trustworthy circle he belongs to. Being among them is set right alongside his waiting on God and his thankfulness — this fellowship is the setting in which those acts happen. It is the exact opposite of the treacherous man he condemned. Belonging to this faithful community is what steadies him and confirms his hope; his own faith is held up by the trustworthy people around him.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = godly / faithful (chasid), read 'in the presence of the godly'. Type (102) is status — a standing identity, here that of a community. No source (103 present:false): the psalm does not derive this godly company from a stated cause; it is simply the fellowship the psalmist inhabits. Seat (104) = none — no organ locates it; belonging is not an organ-movement. Bearer (105) = the godly, the psalmist's company. Operation (106) = none and target (107) = none — the godly perform no discrete interior act here; the silence says their function is to BE the setting, the trustworthy fellowship in whose midst the psalmist acts. Manner (108) = none; intensity, specifier, effect absent (109-111 present:false). Coupling (112) is a pair: the godly are the community in whose presence he waits and gives thanks — the item is bound to his acts of waiting and thanksgiving. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): the faithful community, the psalmist's true company — the covenant-loyal among whom he waits and gives thanks, the opposite fellowship to the tyrant, a belonging that steadies hope. Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = external:person. The inner-being process: over against the boastful, deceit-loving tyrant of vv1-4, the psalmist locates himself among the godly, and that belonging is what steadies his waiting and thanks — faithfulness here is a communal arena, other people, that holds and confirms the self's hope.

*Why this reading differs:* Here 'godly' is an external COMMUNITY (locus external:person; bearer the psalmist's company; coupled with his waiting and thanks) — belonging that steadies hope. It differs from Psa 12:1, where the godly are grieved as VANISHED (affect, internal loss), and from Psa 86:2, where the psalmist claims godliness as HIS OWN devotion (internal:ib-state, bearer the psalmist). The shift from lost-company to present-company to self-claim is the finding.

## Psa 78:8-13

> Psa 78:8 and that they should not be like their fathers , a stubborn and rebellious generation , a generation whose heart was not steadfast , whose spirit was not faithful to God . Psa 78:9 The Ephraimites , armed with the bow , turned back on the day of battle . Psa 78:10 They did not keep God’s covenant , but refused to walk according to his law . Psa 78:11 They forgot his works and the wonders that he had shown them . Psa 78:12 In the sight of their fathers he performed wonders in the land of Egypt , in the fields of Zoan . Psa 78:13 He divided the sea and let them pass through it, and made the waters stand like a heap .

**faithful** — Psa 78:8  ·  `H0539:faithful#1`

_Story_

The psalm looks back at an earlier generation and describes them as stubborn and rebellious, with a spirit that would not stay loyal to God. This is not one bad moment but a settled inward tendency — a heart that refused to hold steady and a loyalty that kept slipping. The passage does not explain what made them this way; it simply names them as a people bent away from faithfulness. Their failure to stay true is aimed squarely at God, the one they owed loyalty to, and it is tied to a heart that was never firm in the first place. The picture is of unfaithfulness as a deep-seated trait: an unsteady inner core that would not commit itself to the God who had done wonders for them.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = be faithful (aman), negated — the item appears in its failure: a spirit that was NOT faithful. Type (102) is disposition: not a single act but a settled bent of the inner self, here a bent away from fidelity. No source (103 present:false): the psalm does not name a cause behind the unfaithfulness; it is presented as the generation's own condition. Seat (104) = none — no organ is named, though the verse speaks of heart and spirit generally; the reading declines to lodge the failure in a single faculty. Bearer (105) = the fathers, the stubborn and rebellious generation. Operation (106) = fail to be faithful — the interior movement is a non-movement, a withholding of fidelity. Target (107) = to God: the failed faithfulness is directed (negatively) toward God. Manner (108) = none; intensity, specifier, effect absent (109-111 present:false) — no degree or downstream result is graded here, though the surrounding psalm supplies consequences; at the item itself the failure is simply stated. Coupling (112) = paired with the unsteady heart — the unfaithful spirit is welded to a heart that was not steadfast (v8). No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): 'whose spirit was not faithful to God' — the fidelity of heart the fathers lacked toward God. Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = external:god — God is the one toward whom fidelity was owed and withheld. The inner-being process (directive 5, descent read fully): a whole generation's settled disposition is a heart that will not hold firm and a spirit that will not stay loyal — faithlessness as an ingrained bent, coupled to inner instability, aimed away from the God it should have trusted.

*Why this reading differs:* This 'faithful' reading is a NEGATED failure of the fathers (disposition; operation 'fail to be faithful'; target to God; coupled with the unsteady heart) — the descent pole. Psa 78:37 repeats the failure but aims it 'to his covenant'; Psa 101:6 flips the item to ascent, the faithful in the land honoured with nearness (status). The variation is target (God / covenant) and valence (failure vs reward).

## Psa 78:17-22

> Psa 78:17 Yet they sinned still more against him, rebelling against the Most High in the desert . Psa 78:18 They tested God in their heart by demanding the food they craved . Psa 78:19 They spoke against God , saying , “ Can God spread a table in the wilderness ? Psa 78:20 He struck the rock so that water gushed out and streams overflowed . Can he also give bread or provide meat for his people ?” Psa 78:21 Therefore , when the Lord heard , he was full of wrath ; a fire was kindled against Jacob ; his anger rose against Israel , Psa 78:22 because they did not believe in God and did not trust his saving power .

**believe** — Psa 78:22  ·  `H0539:believ#2`

_Story_

The people in the wilderness grumbled and doubted, demanding food and questioning whether God could really provide. The passage names the root under all that complaining: they did not believe in God and did not trust his power to save. This was not a one-off doubt but a settled inward refusal to lean on God. Their disbelief was aimed straight at God himself, not just at their hungry circumstances, and it went hand in hand with a refusal to trust that he could rescue them. Even after seeing water gush from a rock, they would not put their weight on him. The heart of their failure was this deep unwillingness to trust the God who kept proving himself.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = believe (aman), negated — belief in its absence: they did not believe. Type (102) is disposition, a settled inner bent (here toward distrust), not a passing thought. No source (103 present:false): no cause is named behind the unbelief; it is the people's own condition. Seat (104) = none — the psalm speaks of testing God 'in their heart' (v18) but the item is not lodged in a named organ; the failure is a whole-self posture. Bearer (105) = 'they', the wilderness generation. Operation (106) = fail to believe — the interior act is a refusal of trust. Target (107) = in God: the unbelief is aimed at God himself, not merely at circumstances. The passage does not tidy this away: it plainly presents the faithful experiencing the carrying out of God's judgment, violence included, as an honour — obedience felt as glory rather than as something troubling.

_Reading_

Sense (101) = the honour of executing judgment — the item names not the deed as such but the DIGNITY felt in carrying out the decreed judgment. Type (102) is affect: this is a felt state, honour experienced, not the volitional act itself. No source (103 present:false): the honour is not traced to a stated cause. Seat (104) = none — not organ-located. Bearer (105) = the godly. Operation (106) = to carry out the written judgment is the honour of all God's saints — dignity in obedience. Target (107) = dignified-obedience: the endpoint is the sense of dignity in obeying. Manner (108) = none; intensity, specifier, effect absent (109-111 present:false). Coupling (112) = honour-in-judgment — the felt honour welded to the act of judgment. No prohibition (113). Discovery (114): 'to execute on them the judgment written! This is honour for all his godly ones' — the operation is the sense of dignity in performing the decreed judgment; obedience felt as honour. Role (115) = characteristic; locus (116) = internal:ib-state. The inner-being process, read without softening (directives 5-6): the godly experience carrying out God's written judgment — including vengeance and punishment on nations and kings (vv7-8) — as HONOUR, a dignity that fills the interior. This is faithfulness felt as elevation: the self esteems its own obedience as glory. The uncomfortable finding is that the interior here binds violence-as-obedience to a sense of dignity and glory ('let the godly exult in glory', v5); the faith-family surfaces an affect in which executing decreed judgment is felt as honourable, not troubling.

*Why this reading differs:* The two 'execute' readings split the same act into two inner registers. This one is the AFFECT: the FELT honour and dignity of carrying out the decreed judgment (sense 'the honour of executing judgment'; target dignified-obedience; coupling honour-in-judgment). Psa 149:7 is the VOLITION: the will/agency actually to execute the vengeance. Variation = type (affect vs volition) — the felt glory of obeying vs the resolve to act.
