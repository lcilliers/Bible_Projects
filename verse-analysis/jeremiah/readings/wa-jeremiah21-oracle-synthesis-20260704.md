## JER-kings-know-me · Jeremiah 21:1–22:30 · [D, multi] — justice to the poor as knowing God; prosperity deafness

**The reading.** To the house of David God's demand is *justice*: "**Execute justice in the morning, and deliver from the hand of the oppressor him who has been robbed**" (21:12); "**Do justice and righteousness… do no wrong or violence to the resident alien, the fatherless, and the widow**" (22:3). Then the decisive inner-being equation, contrasting good king Josiah with his gain-obsessed son: "**Did not your father eat and drink and do justice and righteousness?… He judged the cause of the poor and needy; then it was well. Is not this to know me? declares the LORD. But you have eyes and heart only for your dishonest gain**" (22:15–17). And the diagnosis of a heart deafened by ease: "**I spoke to you in your prosperity, but you said, 'I will not listen.' This has been your way from your youth**" (22:21). The finding is **the astonishing identification: *doing justice to the poor* is *"to know me"* — while *dishonest gain* has "eyes and heart" only for itself — and *prosperity* deafens the interior ("I will not listen")** *(stated)*. The operation is **the collapse of the distance between *knowing God* and *doing justice to the weak*: God says of Josiah's care for the poor, "**is not this to know me?**" — so that *knowledge of God is not a private mysticism but is enacted in justice to the vulnerable*; and the counter-interior is one whose "eyes and heart" are captured by "dishonest gain" (the whole attention absorbed by acquisition); and *prosperity* is named as a *deafening* condition — the comfortable heart that "will not listen"** *(inferred: 22:16 makes one of Scripture's most striking inner-being identifications — Josiah "**judged the cause of the poor and needy… IS NOT THIS TO KNOW ME?**": the finding is that *to know God* (the one legitimate boast, 9:24) is *not separable from doing justice to the weak* — the two are the same reality; a claim to know God that does not issue in care for the poor is empty, and conversely the doing of justice to the vulnerable *is itself* a knowing of God (because it enacts his own character — "steadfast love, justice, righteousness", 9:24); the finding demolishes any spirituality divorced from justice; and the contrast interior is precise — Jehoiakim has "**eyes and heart only for… dishonest gain**" (22:17): the finding is that the acquisitive self's *attention* (eyes) and *affection* (heart) are wholly *captured by gain*, leaving nothing for the poor or for God — a total absorption; and 22:21 adds the enabling condition — "**I spoke to you in your PROSPERITY, but you said, I will not listen**": the finding is that *ease deafens* — prosperity is the state in which the interior most easily stops listening to God (having no felt need), so that comfort itself becomes a spiritual danger, the "way from your youth" of a heart never made to listen)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *justice to the poor as knowing God ("he judged the cause of the poor… is not this to know me?" — knowledge of God enacted in justice to the weak, not a private mysticism)* (thread — a gem: to know God and to do justice to the vulnerable are the same reality, w/ 22:16, 9:24, Hos 6:6, 1 John 3:17, Matt 25:40); *the acquisitive heart's total capture ("eyes and heart only for dishonest gain" — attention and affection wholly absorbed by acquisition)* (new — a gem: the self so captured by gain that nothing is left for God or the poor, w/ 22:17, Luke 12:34, Col 3:5); *prosperity as a deafening condition ("I spoke to you in your prosperity, but you said, I will not listen" — ease dulling the interior's hearing)* (thread — a gem: comfort as a spiritual danger, the un-needy heart that stops listening, w/ 22:21, Deut 8:11-14, Prov 30:9, Rev 3:17).
