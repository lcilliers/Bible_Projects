## JER-jeremiah-on-trial · Jeremiah 26:1–24 · [D, multi] — the prophet's composure under the threat of death; relenting still possible

**The reading.** Commanded to preach the temple sermon and "**not hold back a word**" — with the hope, "**it may be they will listen, and every one turn from his evil way, that I may relent**" (vv.2–3) — Jeremiah is seized by priests and prophets crying "**You shall die!**" (v.8). His response under the death-threat is remarkable composure: he repeats the call to "**mend your ways… and the LORD will relent**" (v.13), then commits himself: "**As for me, behold, I am in your hands. Do with me as seems good and right to you. Only know for certain that if you put me to death, you will bring innocent blood upon yourselves**" (vv.14–15). The elders recall Hezekiah, who "**feared the LORD and entreated the favour of the LORD, and… the LORD relented**" (v.19); yet another prophet, Uriah, is hunted down and killed (vv.20–23). The finding is **the prophet's *composure* under the threat of death — "**I am in your hands; do with me as seems good**" — joined to the steady hope that even now God "**may relent**" if they turn** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior that, threatened with death for the message, *neither retracts nor panics* — it *entrusts itself* ("I am in your hands") while *warning* them of the innocent blood, and keeps holding out the door of *relenting*; the calm rests not on confidence of survival (Uriah's fate shows the risk was real) but on having spoken truly and left the outcome to God** *(inferred: Jeremiah's poise (vv.14–15) is the inner-being center — under a capital charge he does not recant the word, nor rage, nor grovel, but says "**I am IN YOUR HANDS; do with me as seems good and right to you**": the finding is that the interior secured by having *spoken truly* can face even death with *composure* — Jeremiah's peace is not the assurance of rescue (the parallel Uriah *was* killed, v.23) but the settledness of one who has done what God sent him to do and *entrusts the rest*; and he still, at the trial, holds out hope for *them* — "mend your ways… and the LORD will relent" (v.13): the finding is that the threatened prophet's concern remains *their repentance*, not his own vindication — even facing death he offers his accusers the door; the Hezekiah precedent (v.19) grounds it — God "relented" when a king "feared the LORD and entreated his favour", so relenting is *always possible* while there is turning; the sober Uriah note (vv.20–23) keeps it honest — faithfulness is genuinely dangerous, and God's protection of Jeremiah (through Ahikam, v.24) is not guaranteed to every faithful witness)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *composure under the threat of death ("I am in your hands; do with me as seems good" — neither retracting nor panicking, entrusting the outcome)* (thread — a gem: the peace of one who has spoken truly, resting not on assured rescue but on having obeyed, w/ 26:14-15, Dan 3:16-18, Acts 21:13, Phil 1:20-21); *holding out the door of relenting even to one's accusers ("mend your ways… and the LORD will relent" — the threatened prophet's concern still their repentance)* (new — a gem: offering the door to those who threaten you, w/ 26:13, Luke 23:34, Acts 7:60); *relenting always possible while there is turning (the Hezekiah precedent — "he entreated the LORD, and the LORD relented")* (thread — w/ 26:19, 18:8, Jonah 3:10); *faithfulness genuinely dangerous (Uriah killed — God's protection not guaranteed to every witness)* (new — the honest cost, w/ 26:20-23, Heb 11:36-38).
