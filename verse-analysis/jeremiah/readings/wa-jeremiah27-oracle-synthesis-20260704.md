## JER-yoke-trust-a-lie · Jeremiah 27:1–28:17 · [D, multi] — trusting a comforting lie; the test of true vs false prophecy; the honest "Amen"

**The reading.** Jeremiah wears a yoke to enact God's hard word: submit to Babylon "**and live**" (27:12); "**Do not listen to the words of the prophets who are saying… 'You shall not serve the king of Babylon,' for it is a lie**" (27:14). The false prophet Hananiah announces the opposite — the yoke broken "**within two years**" (28:3). Jeremiah's response is striking: "**Amen! May the LORD do so; may the LORD make the words that you have prophesied come true**" (28:6) — *he wishes the good news were true* — yet he holds the test: "**As for the prophet who prophesies peace, when the word of that prophet comes to pass, then it will be known that the LORD has truly sent the prophet**" (28:9). When Hananiah breaks the wooden yoke, God replaces it with iron (28:13), and Jeremiah names the real harm: "**the LORD has not sent you, and you have made this people trust in a lie**" (28:15). The finding is **the interior's peril of *trusting a comforting lie* ("made this people trust in a lie") — the *test* of prophecy by fulfilment — and Jeremiah's *honest "Amen"*, genuinely wishing the false comfort were true even while knowing it is not** *(stated)*. The operation is **the exposure of a lie's *appeal* — it tells the anxious what they want ("the yoke will break in two years") and so wins their *trust* against the truth; and Jeremiah's remarkable interior — he *sincerely wishes* the happy prophecy were real ("Amen! may the LORD do so"), harbouring no delight in the hard word, yet he *will not let his wish override the truth* ("but hear now this word") — the union of *tender-heartedness* and *un-flinching honesty*** *(inferred: two inner-being findings. First, the *seduction of the comforting lie* — Hananiah's false "peace in two years" is believed because it is what the captives *long* to hear, and its real damage is that it makes them "**trust in a lie**" (28:15): the finding is that a lie's power over the interior is its *congeniality* — it aligns with the heart's wishes, so the heart *trusts* it against harder truth (the same as 5:31, "my people love to have it so"); the deadly thing is not merely being deceived but *investing trust* in the deception. Second, Jeremiah's "**Amen! may the LORD do so**" (28:6) is a jewel of the honest interior — the finding is that a truthful person can *genuinely wish* the pleasant prophecy were true (Jeremiah takes no pleasure in announcing the yoke; his heart is *with* his people's hope) *and yet* refuse to let that wish bend his testimony ("**but hear now this word**", v.7): the union of a *sympathetic heart* (he wants the comfort to be real) and an *honest tongue* (he will not pretend it is) — the truthful witness is not cold to the longing for good news; he shares it, but he will not *lie* to satisfy it; the test he applies (v.9, fulfilment) locates truth *outside* both his and their wishes, in what actually "comes to pass")*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the seduction of the comforting lie ("you have made this people trust in a lie" — a lie's power is its congeniality; the heart trusts what aligns with its wishes)* (thread — a gem: the deadly thing is investing trust in a deception one wants to believe, w/ 28:15, 5:31, 2 Thess 2:10-11, 2 Tim 4:3); *the honest "Amen" ("may the LORD make your words come true… but hear now this word" — genuinely wishing the good news true while refusing to bend the truth to the wish)* (new — a gem: the union of a sympathetic heart and an honest tongue; the truthful witness shares the longing yet will not lie to satisfy it, w/ 28:6-9); *truth located outside one's wishes (the test of fulfilment — what actually "comes to pass" decides, not what either party longs for)* (new — a gem: the criterion set outside the interior's desires, w/ 28:9, Deut 18:22); *the hard word borne without relish (no delight in announcing the yoke)* (thread — w/ 28:6, 17:16).
