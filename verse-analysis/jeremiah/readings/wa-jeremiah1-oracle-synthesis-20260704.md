## JER-call · Jeremiah 1:4–19 · [D, multi] — known and consecrated before birth; reluctance met with presence; the fortified prophet

**The reading.** The call reaches back before Jeremiah existed: "**Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations**" (v.5). His response is shrinking reluctance: "**Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth**" (v.6). God overrides the fear with *presence and provision*: "**Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you**" (v.8); "**I have put my words in your mouth**" (v.9); and a promise of fortification: "**I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls… they will fight against you, but they shall not prevail**" (vv.18–19). The finding is **an *identity and calling given before the self existed* ("I knew you… I consecrated you… before you were born") — and a *reluctance* met not by argument but by *God's presence and equipping*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the self's *worth and vocation grounded outside itself*, in God's prior knowing and setting-apart — so that identity is *received*, not achieved — and the interior's *inadequacy-fear* ("I am only a youth… I do not know how to speak") answered not by boosting the self's confidence but by relocating the ground: "**I am with you**", "**I have put my words in your mouth**"** *(inferred: v.5 anchors the whole book's understanding of the person — "**before I formed you… I KNEW you**": the finding is that the self's deepest identity is *conferred by God before the self can earn or even exist to earn it* — Jeremiah is *known* and *consecrated* prior to any achievement, so his worth and calling are pure gift, not self-made; and the *fear* of inadequacy (v.6, "I am only a youth") is a genuine interior state the passage takes seriously — the finding is that God does not answer the fear by inflating Jeremiah's self-estimate ("no, you *are* capable") but by *shifting the ground of confidence off the self entirely*: "do not be afraid, **for I am with you**" (v.8) — the antidote to inadequacy-fear is not self-confidence but *God-presence*; and the "words in your mouth" (v.9) means even the competence required is *given*, not summoned; the "fortified city, iron pillar" (v.18) is the paradox — the reluctant youth is made *unbreakable*, not by becoming strong in himself but by being *made* so by the God who calls: the interior's security in a hostile vocation rests on being known, sent, accompanied, and fortified from outside)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *identity and calling given before the self existed ("before I formed you… I knew you… I consecrated you" — worth conferred by God, received not achieved)* (thread — a gem: the self known and set apart prior to any achievement, w/ 1:5, Ps 139:13-16, Gal 1:15, Isa 49:1); *inadequacy-fear answered by presence, not self-confidence ("I am only a youth"… "do not be afraid, for I am with you")* (new — a gem: the ground of confidence shifted off the self onto God-presence, w/ 1:6-8, Exod 3:11-12, 4:10-12); *even the competence given ("I have put my words in your mouth" — the equipping received, not summoned)* (thread — w/ 1:9, Exod 4:12); *the reluctant self made unbreakable ("a fortified city, an iron pillar… they shall not prevail" — security in a hostile calling made by God, not by the self)* (new — w/ 1:18-19, 15:20).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** identity given before the self existed; inadequacy-fear answered by presence; even the competence given; the reluctant self made unbreakable.
