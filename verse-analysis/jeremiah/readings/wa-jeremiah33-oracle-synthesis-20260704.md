## JER-call-to-me · Jeremiah 33:1–26 · [D, multi] — call to me and I will answer; cleansing and forgiveness; the covenant as fixed as day and night

**The reading.** Still imprisoned (v.1), Jeremiah receives an open invitation: "**Call to me and I will answer you, and will tell you great and hidden things that you have not known**" (v.3). God promises healing and cleansing: "**I will bring to it health and healing… I will cleanse them from all the guilt of their sin against me, and I will forgive all the guilt of their sin and rebellion**" (vv.6,8). Joy returns to the desolate place, with the refrain: "**Give thanks to the LORD of hosts, for the LORD is good, for his steadfast love endures forever!**" (v.11). And the covenant is anchored to creation itself: "**If you can break my covenant with the day and my covenant with the night… then also my covenant with David my servant may be broken**" (vv.20–21). The finding is **an *open invitation to call and be answered* ("call to me and I will answer"), a promise of *cleansing and forgiveness*, and a covenant *as unbreakable as day and night*** *(stated)*. The operation is **God *inviting* the interior to *call* — with the promise not merely of an answer but of "**great and hidden things you have not known**" (revelation given to the one who asks); the offer of *cleansing* that reaches "all the guilt of their sin"; and the anchoring of the covenant's *reliability* to the *fixed order of creation* — so the interior's security rests on something as immovable as the succession of day and night** *(inferred: v.3's "**Call to me and I will answer you, and will tell you great and hidden things**" is a striking inner-being invitation, spoken *to a prisoner* — the finding is that God *invites* the constrained, waiting interior to *call*, and promises not just relief but *deeper knowledge* ("great and hidden things you have not known") — prayer is the doorway to a knowing otherwise closed; the God who is findable by whole-hearted seeking (29:13) here *asks to be called upon*; and the cleansing (v.8) is comprehensive — "**cleanse them from ALL the guilt**… forgive all" (the same completeness as 31:34); and the covenant-anchor (vv.20–21) grounds the interior's confidence not in its own steadiness but in the *fixity of creation's order* — "if you can break my covenant with the day and the night, then also my covenant may be broken": the finding is that God attaches the reliability of his promise to the most *immovable regularity* the interior knows (the sun rising, the night falling), so that doubting his faithfulness would require doubting that day will follow night — the security is placed beyond the reach of the interior's fluctuating feelings, on the bedrock of the created order the God who keeps it also keeps his word)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the invitation to call and be answered ("call to me and I will answer… great and hidden things you have not known" — God inviting the constrained interior to call, prayer the doorway to deeper knowing)* (thread — a gem: God asking to be called upon, promising revelation to the one who asks, w/ 33:3, 29:12-13, Ps 50:15, Matt 7:7); *comprehensive cleansing and forgiveness ("cleanse them from all the guilt… forgive all their sin and rebellion")* (thread — a gem: forgiveness reaching all the guilt, w/ 33:8, 31:34, 1 John 1:9); *"the LORD is good, his steadfast love endures forever" (the refrain of confident thanksgiving)* (thread — w/ 33:11, Ps 136); *the covenant as fixed as day and night (the interior's security anchored to the immovable order of creation, beyond fluctuating feelings)* (new — a gem: God attaching his promise's reliability to the sun's rising, security placed on bedrock, w/ 33:20-21,25-26, 31:35-37, Gen 8:22).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the invitation to call and be answered; comprehensive cleansing and forgiveness; "the LORD is good, his steadfast love endures forever"; the covenant as fixed as day and night. Chapter 33 closes the Consolation by *inviting* the imprisoned interior to call and receive great hidden things, promising full cleansing, and anchoring the covenant's reliability to the immovable order of day and night.
- **Book-level note (Jeremiah batch 6, chs 30–33, the Book of Consolation):** the inner-being peak of the book. Its findings: the *humanly-incurable wound healed by God*, whose care is aroused by the sufferer's very abandonment (30); *everlasting love* as the root of faithfulness, a *yearning father-heart*, and *mourning turned to joy* (31:1-26); the *new covenant* — the law *written on the heart*, the knowledge of God *universal*, sin *remembered no more* (31:27-40); *enacted hope* against the evidence and God *putting his own fear within* so the heart cannot turn, *rejoicing* whole-heartedly to do good (32); and the *invitation to call* and the covenant *fixed as day and night* (33). Master-findings: **God's whole dealing is rooted in an everlasting, yearning love**; **the deceitful heart is answered by a law written within and a fear God himself implants**; **forgiveness is so complete God remembers sin no more**; and **the interior's security is anchored to a faithfulness as immovable as the created order.**
- **Threads carried forward:** the new heart / written law (← 17:9, 24:7; the answer completed here); everlasting love (31:3); call to me / seek and find (← 29:13); the incurable wound healed (← 8:22, 30:17).
