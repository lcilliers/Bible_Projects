## EZE-watchman-turn-and-live · Ezekiel 33:1–33 · [D, multi] — the watchman re-commissioned; despair answered by God's no-pleasure in death; turn and live

**The reading.** As judgment arrives, Ezekiel is re-commissioned as *watchman*: "**If you do not speak to warn the wicked… his blood I will require at your hand. But if you warn… you will have delivered your soul**" (vv.8–9). Then a despairing voice rises from the people: "**Surely our transgressions and our sins are upon us, and we rot away because of them. How then can we live?**" (v.10). God's answer is one of the tenderest in Scripture: "**As I live, declares the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way and live; turn back, turn back from your evil ways, for why will you die, O house of Israel?**" (v.11). And the future is open both ways — the righteous who "**trusts in his righteousness and does injustice**" dies; the wicked who "**turns from his sin**" lives, "**none of the sins that he has committed shall be remembered against him**" (vv.12–16). The finding is **the *despair* of those crushed by guilt ("we rot away… how can we live?") answered by God's *no pleasure in death* and the double summons "**turn back, turn back… why will you die?**"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the *guilt-crushed* interior — feeling its sins "upon it", rotting, unable to see how it could "live" — met not with condemnation but with God's *own longing for its life*: "**I have NO PLEASURE in the death of the wicked**… turn, and live"; and the *open future* — the past sins "not remembered" against the one who turns — so the despairing "how can we live?" is answered by "you can, by turning"** *(inferred: v.10's despair is a real inner-being state — "**we ROT AWAY** because of [our sins]; **HOW THEN CAN WE LIVE?**": the finding is the paralysis of *guilt without hope* — the sense that one's sins are so "upon" one that life is impossible, a rotting despair that sees no way forward; and God's answer (v.11) is aimed straight at it — "**I have NO PLEASURE in the death of the wicked… TURN BACK, turn back… why will you die?**": the finding is that God's disposition toward the guilt-crushed sinner is not the eagerness for death they fear but a *yearning for their life* — he *pleads* ("turn back, turn back", the doubled urgency) and asks "**why will you die?**" as one grieved by their perishing (as 18:23,32); the finding is that the despairing "how can we live?" is met by "you *can* — turn"; and the *open future* (vv.12–16) removes the two false fixities — the righteous is not *safe* by past goodness ("trusts in his righteousness" and falls), and the wicked is not *doomed* by past sin ("none remembered against him" when he turns): the finding is that *no accumulated record binds the present* — the guilt-crushed are not sealed in death, and the self-assured are not sealed in life; what saves is the *present turning*, and to the one who turns, the past is *un-remembered*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the paralysis of guilt without hope ("we rot away because of [our sins]; how then can we live?" — the despair that sees no way forward)* (new — a gem: guilt-crushed despair, the sense that one's sins make life impossible, w/ 33:10, Ps 38:3-4, 40:12, Rom 7:24); *God's yearning for the sinner's life ("I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked… turn back, turn back… why will you die?" — the pleading God grieved by the sinner's perishing)* (thread — a gem: God's disposition not eagerness for death but longing for life, w/ 33:11, 18:23,32, Luke 15:20, 2 Pet 3:9); *the open future / the past un-remembered ("none of the sins he has committed shall be remembered… when he turns" — no accumulated record binds the present)* (thread — a gem: the guilt-crushed not sealed in death, the self-assured not sealed in life; the present turning saves, w/ 33:12-16, 18:21-24, Isa 43:25); *the watchman's charge to warn (his soul delivered by faithfulness)* (thread — w/ 33:7-9, 3:17-21).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the paralysis of guilt without hope; God's yearning for the sinner's life; the open future / the past un-remembered; the watchman's charge. Chapter 33 answers the guilt-crushed "how can we live?" with God's own "I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked… turn back, turn back… why will you die?" — the past un-remembered for the one who turns.
