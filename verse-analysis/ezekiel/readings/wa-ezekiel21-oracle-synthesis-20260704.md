## EZE-stand-in-the-breach · Ezekiel 21:1–22:31 · [D, multi] — the groaning prophet; the dross in the furnace; the missing intercessor

**The reading.** The sword-song is announced, and the prophet must *feel* it: "**groan; with breaking heart and bitter grief, groan before their eyes**" (21:6). The city's sins are catalogued — bloodshed, defiled holy things, prophets like "**a roaring lion tearing the prey**", priests who "**made no distinction between the holy and the common**" (22:25–26); "**the house of Israel has become dross to me… in the furnace**" (22:18). And the chapter ends on one of Scripture's most poignant absences: "**I sought for a man among them who should build up the wall and stand in the breach before me for the land, that I should not destroy it, but I found none**" (22:30). The finding is **the prophet's *groaning heart* made a sign, the people become worthless *dross*, and the tragic *absence of an intercessor* — "I sought… but I found none"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure that God *seeks* someone to "**stand in the breach**" — an *intercessor* to stand between him and the judgment, pleading and repairing on the land's behalf — and *finds none*; the inner-being weight is that God *desires to be stayed*, looking for one who will stand in the gap, and the tragedy is not that judgment is eager but that *no one will intercede*** *(inferred: 22:30 is the chapter's heart — "**I sought for a man… to STAND IN THE BREACH before me… that I should not destroy it, but I FOUND NONE**": the finding is astonishing about God's own disposition — he *wants* to be interceded with, *seeks* someone who will "stand in the breach" (the gap in the wall through which judgment would pour) and *plead* for the land, so that he "should not destroy it"; the finding is that God *desires a reason to relent* — the intercessor is one who stands *between* God and the people, and God actively *looks for* such a one (as Moses stood in the breach, Ps 106:23; as Abraham pleaded for Sodom); the tragedy here is *not* that God is bloodthirsty but that *no one would intercede* — "I found none": the absence of a single person willing to *stand in the gap* on others' behalf is what leaves judgment unstayed; the inner-being note is the *rarity and preciousness of the intercessor* — the interior that will *stand between* God and the deserving, pleading for mercy it does not itself need, is what God seeks and too rarely finds; and the prophet's own "**groaning with breaking heart**" (21:6) shows what such an interior looks like — one who *feels* the coming judgment and grieves it, the very opposite of the numb; the calling to intercede is a calling to *care enough to stand in the breach*)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *God seeking an intercessor to stand in the breach ("I sought for a man… to stand in the breach before me… but I found none" — God desiring to be stayed, looking for one to plead)* (thread — a gem: God actively seeks a reason to relent, an intercessor to stand in the gap, w/ 22:30, Ps 106:23, Gen 18:22-33, Isa 59:16, 1 Tim 2:1); *the tragedy of no intercessor (judgment unstayed not by God's eagerness but by the absence of anyone willing to plead)* (new — a gem: the rarity and preciousness of the one who stands between God and the deserving, w/ 22:30, Ezek 13:5); *the groaning heart that feels the judgment ("groan with breaking heart and bitter grief" — the interceding interior grieves the coming ruin, the opposite of the numb)* (thread — a gem: caring enough to grieve, w/ 21:6, 9:4, Jer 9:1); *the people become dross (worthlessness fit only for the furnace)* (thread — w/ 22:18, Isa 1:22).
