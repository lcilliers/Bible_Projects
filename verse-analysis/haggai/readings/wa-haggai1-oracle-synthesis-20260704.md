## HAGGAI-consider-your-ways · Haggai 1:1–15 · [D, multi] — self-examination; misplaced priorities; the unsatisfying labour

**The reading.** The people say "**the time has not yet come to rebuild the house of the LORD**" (v.2), yet live in "**paneled houses**" of their own while God's house "**lies in ruins**" (v.4). Twice God calls: "**Consider your ways**" (vv.5,7). The symptom he points to is a haunting *un-satisfaction*: "**You have sown much, and harvested little. You eat, but you never have enough… you clothe yourselves, but no one is warm. And he who earns wages does so to put them into a bag with holes**" (v.6). The diagnosis: "**Because of my house that lies in ruins, while each of you busies himself with his own house**" (v.9). The people then "**obeyed… and the people feared the LORD**" (v.12), and God said, "**I am with you**" (v.13), stirring up their spirit (v.14). The finding is **the call to *"consider your ways"* — self-examination — exposing a life of *unsatisfying labour* ("a bag with holes") rooted in *misplaced priorities* (one's own house before God's)** *(stated)*. The operation is **the summons to *stop and examine the actual trajectory of one's life* ("consider your ways" — set your heart on where you are really going), which reveals that the chronic *dissatisfaction* (much sown, little reaped; eating without fullness) is not random but the *fruit of a disordered priority* — the self absorbed in "his own house" while God's is neglected — so that reordering the priority is the cure** *(inferred: "**consider your ways**" (v.5,7, literally *set your heart upon your ways*) is the inner-being key — the finding is that the interior needs periodic *honest self-examination*, a deliberate setting of the heart on where one's life is actually tending (not where one assumes it is); and what such examination here reveals is a *disproportion between effort and satisfaction* — "sown much, harvested little… wages into a bag with holes" (v.6): the finding is that labour can be *strangely unfruitful of satisfaction* — not that nothing is produced, but that it never *satisfies* (the never-full curse, as in Mic 6:14) — and this dissatisfaction is *diagnostic*, pointing to a deeper disorder; the disorder named is *priority* — "each busies himself with **his own house**" while "**my house lies in ruins**" (v.9): the finding is that when God is displaced from first place (the self's comfort put before God's honour), even legitimate labour leaks its satisfaction away ("a bag with holes"); the cure is not more effort but *re-ordering* — putting God's house first — and the chapter's happy turn is that when the people "obeyed" and "feared the LORD" (v.12), the immediate word is "**I am with you**" (v.13) and their spirit is "stirred up" (v.14): reordered priority restores both God's presence and the interior's energy)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *"consider your ways" (the discipline of honest self-examination — setting the heart on where one's life is actually tending)* (thread — a gem: periodic examination of the real trajectory, w/ 1:5,7, 2 Cor 13:5, Ps 139:23-24, Lam 3:40); *unsatisfying labour as diagnostic ("sown much, harvested little… wages into a bag with holes" — dissatisfaction pointing to a deeper disorder)* (new — a gem: labour that produces without satisfying, a symptom to be read, w/ 1:6, Mic 6:14, Ecc 5:10); *misplaced priorities ("each busies himself with his own house" while God's lies in ruins — God displaced from first place)* (thread — a gem: legitimate labour leaking its satisfaction when God is put second, w/ 1:9, Matt 6:33); *reordered priority restoring presence and energy ("I am with you" + the stirred spirit follow obedience)* (new — w/ 1:12-14).
