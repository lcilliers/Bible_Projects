## ISA-new-heavens · Isa 65:1–25 · [D, multi] — the God who says "Here I am" to the unseeking; servants vs forsakers; the new creation

**The reading.** God answers chapter 64's longing with a disclosure of his own *seeking*: "**I was ready to be sought by those who did not ask for me; I was ready to be found by those who did not seek me. I said, 'Here I am, here I am,' to a nation that was not called by my name**" (v.1). He indicts a proud, self-sanctifying separatism — "**'Keep to yourself, do not come near me, for I am too holy for you'**" (v.5) — and divides two destinies: "**my servants shall eat, but you shall be hungry… my servants shall sing for gladness of heart, but you shall cry out for pain of heart**" (vv.13–14). Then the new creation: "**Behold, I create new heavens and a new earth… be glad and rejoice forever in that which I create… no more shall be heard in it the sound of weeping**" (vv.17–19); "**Before they call I will answer; while they are yet speaking I will hear**" (v.24). The finding is **God *stretching out his hands* to a people who "did not seek" him ("Here I am, here I am") — the *servants* (glad, satisfied) parted from the *forsakers* (hungry, wailing) by disposition — and a *new creation* where "**before they call I will answer**"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the reversal of the seeking: it is not first the interior that seeks God but *God who seeks the unseeking* ("Here I am" to those who did not ask) — the initiative wholly his; and the two destinies turn on *response* (servants who answer vs forsakers who "did not listen"), while the consummation is an intimacy so complete that God answers *before the prayer is finished*** *(inferred: v.1 answers 64's ache ("rend the heavens and come down") with a startling reversal — the God longed-for reveals that *he* has been the seeker all along: "**I was ready to be sought by those who did not ask… I said, Here I am, here I am, to a nation not called by my name**"; the finding is that the divine initiative *precedes* the human search — God stretches out his hands "all the day to a rebellious people" (v.2), the "Here I am" (the same words Isaiah spoke to God in 6:8) now spoken *by God to man*; and the division of "servants" and "forsakers" (vv.13–15) is drawn along the line of *responsiveness* — "**when I called, you did not answer; when I spoke, you did not listen**" (v.12): the finding is that the two destinies (eating/hungry, singing/wailing) are not arbitrary but flow from whether the interior *answered* the seeking God; a special foil is the self-righteous separatist — "**I am too holy for you**" (v.5) — the pride that keeps others at arm's length in the name of holiness, "a smoke in my nostrils"; and the consummation reverses even the delay of prayer — "**before they call I will answer, while they are yet speaking I will hear**" (v.24): the finding is that the new-creation intimacy is one of *anticipated* communion, God so near and attentive that the answer precedes the finished request — the opposite of the "hidden face" (59:2) that sin had caused)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *God seeking the unseeking ("Here I am, here I am, to a nation that did not seek me" — the divine initiative preceding the human search)* (thread — a gem: God the seeker before the interior seeks him, w/ 65:1-2, 6:8 reversed, Rom 10:20-21, Luke 19:10); *the two destinies drawn along responsiveness ("when I called, you did not answer" — servants who answered vs forsakers who did not)* (new — a gem: destiny flowing from whether the interior answered the seeking God, w/ 65:12-15); *self-righteous separatism as offence ("I am too holy for you… a smoke in my nostrils" — pride that keeps others off in holiness's name)* (new — a gem: exclusivist self-sanctity that God finds offensive, w/ 65:5, Luke 18:11); *anticipated communion ("before they call I will answer, while they are yet speaking I will hear")* (thread — a gem: intimacy so near the answer precedes the finished prayer, w/ 65:24, 58:9, contrast 59:2).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** God seeking the unseeking; the two destinies drawn along responsiveness; self-righteous separatism as offence; anticipated communion. Chapter 65 answers the longing of chapter 64 by revealing God as the *seeker* ("Here I am" to those who did not ask), divides servants from forsakers by their answering, and promises a new creation of anticipated communion — "before they call I will answer."
- **Threads:** God the seeker / "Here I am" (← 6:8; 55:6 reversed; → Rom 10:20-21); responsiveness / answering God (← 50:2, 66:4); the new heavens (→ 66:22); communion restored (← 59:2, the separation; 65:24).
