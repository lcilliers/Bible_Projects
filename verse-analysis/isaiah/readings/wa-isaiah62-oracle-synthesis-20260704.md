## ISA-new-name-delight · Isa 62:1–12 · [D, multi] — the new name; God's delight; rejoicing over as a bridegroom

**The reading.** God will not rest until Zion's vindication shines (v.1), and he renames her: "**You shall be called by a new name that the mouth of the LORD will give… You shall no more be termed Forsaken, and your land shall no more be termed Desolate, but you shall be called My Delight Is in Her, and your land Married; for the LORD delights in you**" (vv.2–4). And the astonishing image of God's joy: "**As the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you**" (v.5). The finding is **the *Forsaken, Desolate* self *renamed* "**My Delight Is in Her**" — because "**the LORD delights in you**" and *rejoices over you as a bridegroom over a bride*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's *identity* rewritten by God — the names it had internalised ("Forsaken", "Desolate") replaced with a name expressing *God's delight in it* — and the relation reframed from abandonment to *being rejoiced over*, God himself the glad bridegroom** *(inferred: the renaming (vv.2–4) works on the interior's deepest self-conception — a person or people who had come to *bear the names* "Forsaken" (azuvah) and "Desolate" (shemamah), names that had become their identity; the finding is that God *gives a new name* that reverses the self-understanding: not merely "no longer forsaken" but positively "**My Delight Is in Her**" (Hephzibah) — the interior redefined by *God's pleasure in it*; and the emotional register is startling — "**as the bridegroom REJOICES OVER the bride, so shall your God REJOICE OVER YOU**" (v.5): the finding is that God's disposition toward the restored self is not grudging acceptance but *bridegroom-joy* — the exultant, delighting gladness of a wedding; the interior that felt "forsaken" is told it is not merely tolerated but *delighted in*, *rejoiced over*, the object of God's glad affection; this is perhaps the tenderest reversal in the book — the self's inmost name changed from abandonment to belovedness, and God's face toward it that of a rejoicing bridegroom)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the self renamed from Forsaken to Delighted-in ("no more Forsaken… but My Delight Is in Her" — identity rewritten by God's pleasure)* (thread — a gem: the interior's deepest self-conception reversed, w/ 62:2-4, 54:6, Hos 2:23, 1 Pet 2:10); *God's bridegroom-joy over his people ("as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you" — delight, not grudging acceptance)* (new — a gem: God's exultant gladness over the restored self, w/ 62:5, Zeph 3:17, Luke 15:7); *the new name given by God (identity conferred, not self-made)* (thread — w/ 62:2, 56:5, 65:15).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the self renamed from Forsaken to Delighted-in; God's bridegroom-joy; the new name given by God. Chapter 62 rewrites the forsaken self's very name to "My Delight Is in Her" — the interior redefined not by its abandonment but by God's rejoicing, bridegroom delight in it.
- **Threads:** the new name / belonging (← 56:5, 43:1; 62:2); the deserted wife rejoiced over (← 54:5-6; w/ Zeph 3:17); delight/joy (→ 65:18-19, 66:10).
