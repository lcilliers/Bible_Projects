## ISA-afflicted-with-them · Isa 63:1–19 · [D, multi] — the God afflicted in their affliction; the grieved Spirit; the Father appealed to

**The reading.** After the lone warrior treading the winepress (vv.1–6), the chapter turns to a recounting of "**the steadfast love of the LORD**" (v.7), at whose centre is a stunning statement of divine empathy: "**In all their affliction he was afflicted, and the angel of his presence saved them; in his love and in his pity he redeemed them; he lifted them up and carried them all the days of old**" (v.9). Yet "**they rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit**" (v.10). The chapter ends in appeal: "**You are our Father, though Abraham does not know us… O LORD, why do you make us wander from your ways and harden our heart, so that we fear you not? Return for the sake of your servants**" (vv.16–17). The finding is **a God who is *afflicted in his people's affliction* — who "**carried them all the days of old**" — whose *Holy Spirit can be grieved*, and who is appealed to as *Father*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the disclosure of God's own *interior* toward his people — not detached but *co-suffering* ("in all their affliction he was afflicted"), *carrying* them, capable of being *grieved* ("they grieved his Holy Spirit") — so that the relation is intimate enough to wound; and the human response, when estranged, is to appeal to that intimacy: "**you are our Father… return**"** *(inferred: v.9 is one of the most tender inner-being disclosures *about God* in the corpus — "**in all their affliction HE WAS AFFLICTED**" (the divine interior *entering into* the suffering of his people, not observing it from outside); the finding is that God's relation to his people is *empathetic* — he does not merely help from a distance but is *himself afflicted* in their affliction, and "**carried them**" (as in 40:11, 46:4, the recurring bearing); and v.10's counterpart is equally intimate — "**they grieved his Holy Spirit**": the finding is that this closeness is *vulnerable* — a relationship intimate enough that rebellion *grieves* God, wounds his Spirit (only love can be grieved; indifference cannot); and the prayer that follows leans on exactly this intimacy — "**you are our Father**" (v.16, twice) and even the raw complaint "**why do you make us wander… harden our heart?**" (v.17): the finding is that the estranged interior, knowing God's fatherly closeness, appeals *back to it* — the very intimacy that could be grieved is the ground of the plea "return for the sake of your servants"; the God who suffered with them and can be grieved by them is the God to whom they cry as Father)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *God afflicted in their affliction ("in all their affliction he was afflicted… he carried them" — the divine interior co-suffering, not detached)* (thread — a gem: God's empathetic entering-into his people's suffering, w/ 63:9, 40:11, Judg 10:16, Heb 4:15); *the Holy Spirit grieved ("they rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit" — a relation intimate enough to be wounded)* (new — a gem: only love can be grieved; rebellion wounds God, w/ 63:10, Eph 4:30, Gen 6:6); *God appealed to as Father ("you are our Father… return" — the estranged interior leaning back on intimacy)* (thread — a gem: the plea grounded in fatherly closeness, w/ 63:16, 64:8, Luke 15:18); *the raw complaint within relationship ("why do you make us wander, harden our heart?" — honest protest addressed to the Father)* (thread — w/ 63:17, Hab 1:2-3).
