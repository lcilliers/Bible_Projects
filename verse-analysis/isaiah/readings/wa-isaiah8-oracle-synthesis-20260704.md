## ISA-fear-him · Isa 8:1–22 · [D, multi] — fear relocated; the sanctuary; waiting for the hiding God

**The reading.** Against the dread of the invading "waters of the River" (v.7) reaching "**even to the neck**", the refrain holds: "**God is with us**" (v.10, *Immanu-el*). Then the decisive inner-being instruction, spoken with God's "strong hand upon" the prophet: "**Do not call conspiracy all that this people calls conspiracy, and do not fear what they fear, nor be in dread. But the LORD of hosts, him you shall honour as holy. Let him be your fear, and let him be your dread**" (vv.12–13). To those who trust him God "**will become a sanctuary**", but to the rest "**a stone of offense and a rock of stumbling**" (v.14). And the prophet's own posture in the dark: "**I will wait for the LORD, who is hiding his face from the house of Jacob, and I will hope in him**" (v.17); over against the temptation to "**inquire of the mediums and the necromancers who chirp and mutter**" instead of God (v.19). The finding is **fear itself *reassigned* — taken off "what they fear" and fixed on the LORD ("let him be your fear") — with God as *sanctuary* to the trusting and *stumbling-stone* to the rest, and the interior resolved to *wait and hope* through God's hiddenness** *(stated)*. The operation is **the interior's *dread* treated as a quantity that must have an object: the cure for wrong fear is not fearlessness but *rightly-placed* fear — to make the Holy One himself one's dread, which paradoxically *disarms* the fear of men and armies; and when God "hides his face", the tested response is *waiting and hoping* rather than seeking the dead** *(inferred: the key move is vv.12–13 — the antidote to the panic that "this people" feels ("do not fear what they fear") is not the absence of fear but its *transfer*: "**let HIM be your fear, and let him be your dread**"; the finding is that a heart *will* fear something, and freedom from the fear of men/circumstances comes precisely by *fearing God more* — the greater dread swallows the lesser (to hold God as holy is to be unafraid of the "conspiracy"); then the same God is experienced two ways by two interiors — "**sanctuary**" to those who take refuge, "**stone of stumbling**" to those who do not (v.14) — the *same reality* is refuge or offence depending on the heart that meets it; and vv.17,19 give the tested posture — when God "**hides his face**", the faithful interior neither despairs nor turns to the occult ("mediums… who chirp and mutter", the counterfeit consolation of the dead) but "**waits**" and "**hopes in him**" — waiting on a God temporarily hidden is itself an act of faith, the refusal to seek false certainty in the dark)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *fear relocated, not removed ("let him be your fear" — the greater dread swallowing the lesser)* (new — a gem: freedom from the fear of men by fearing God more, w/ Matt 10:28, Isa 51:12-13); *the same God as sanctuary or stumbling-stone (one reality met as refuge or offence by two hearts)* (new — a gem: the interior's disposition determines what God is to it, w/ 28:16, 1 Pet 2:7-8); *waiting on the hiding God ("I will wait… who is hiding his face… and I will hope in him")* (thread — faith through divine hiddenness, w/ Ps 13, 30:7, Hab 3:16); *refusing the counterfeit consolation of the dead (inquiring of God, not mediums)* (new — the faithful interior not seeking false certainty in the dark).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** fear relocated; the greater dread swallowing the lesser; God as sanctuary vs stumbling-stone; waiting on the hiding God; refusing the occult. Chapter 8 develops ch.7's fear/faith axis into a *doctrine of fear* — the interior is freed from the fear of men not by becoming fearless but by making the Holy One its dread, and it learns to *wait* when God hides.
- **Threads:** the fear of the LORD displacing the fear of man (→ 11:2-3, the ruler's "delight in the fear of the LORD"; ← 8:12-13); sanctuary vs stumbling-stone (→ 28:16); waiting/hoping through hiddenness (→ 25:9, 30:18, 40:31; w/ Hab 2:1).
