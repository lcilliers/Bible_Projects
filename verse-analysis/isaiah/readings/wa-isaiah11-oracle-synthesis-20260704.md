## ISA-shoot-jesse · Isa 11:1–16 · [D] — the Spirit-rested ruler; delight in the fear of the LORD; the peace of full knowledge

**The reading.** From the felled stump a new life: "**There shall come forth a shoot from the stump of Jesse, and a branch from his roots shall bear fruit**" (v.1). What defines him is an *interior* endowment: "**the Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the LORD. And his delight shall be in the fear of the LORD**" (vv.2–3). He judges not "**by what his eyes see, or… what his ears hear**" but "**with righteousness… for the meek**" (vv.3–4). The fruit of his reign is a healed creation — "**the wolf shall dwell with the lamb**" (v.6) — and its cause is stated: "**they shall not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain; for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea**" (v.9). The finding is **the *ideal interior* portrayed — a ruler on whom the Spirit *rests*, whose defining pleasure is "**delight in the fear of the LORD**", who judges past appearances to the truth — and the *universal peace* that flows from a world *full of the knowledge of God*** *(stated)*. The operation is **the perfected self shown as one *possessed and rested-upon by the Spirit*, whose fear of God is not dread but *delight*, whose judgment is freed from the surface (eyes/ears) to the real — and whose interior condition, multiplied across the earth as "knowledge of the LORD", *is* the ground of peace** *(inferred: the portrait inverts every disordered interior of chs 1–10 — where the woes showed faculties corrupted by autonomy (5:8–23) and Assyria showed strength falsely attributed to self (10:13), here is a self whose every faculty is *Spirit-given* ("wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge", v.2 — the sevenfold Spirit resting, not striving); the inner-being gem is v.3 — "**his delight shall be in the fear of the LORD**": the fear of God, elsewhere a trembling (8:13), is here *pleasure* — the mature interior does not merely submit to God's fear but *enjoys* it, its deepest gladness is reverence; and his judging "**not by what his eyes see**" (v.3) is the healed opposite of the woes' surface-driven values (5:20) — he reaches the real beneath appearance; finally, the peace of vv.6–9 is explicitly *epistemic in cause* — the wolf lies with the lamb "**for** the earth shall be full of the **knowledge of the LORD**" (v.9): the finding is that outer peace is the overflow of a world whose *interior* is filled with the knowledge of God — creation is unhurt because it is God-knowing; the ideal self and the healed world are one continuous thing, both defined by a rightly-filled interior)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the Spirit resting on the ideal self (faculties Spirit-given, not self-generated — the sevenfold Spirit at rest)* (new — a gem: the perfected interior as gift-endowed, the inverse of autonomy, w/ 42:1, 61:1); *delight in the fear of the LORD (reverence as pleasure, not dread — the fear of God enjoyed)* (new — a gem: the mature interior's gladness in reverence, w/ Ps 112:1, 8:13); *judging past appearance to the real (not "by what the eyes see" — the healed opposite of surface-driven values)* (thread — perception freed from the surface, w/ 5:20, 1 Sam 16:7); *peace as the overflow of the knowledge of God ("full of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea")* (thread — a gem: outer peace grounded in an interior filled with God-knowledge, w/ 2:3-4, Hab 2:14).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the Spirit resting on the ideal self; delight in the fear of the LORD; judging past appearance; peace as the overflow of the knowledge of God. Chapter 11 is the *positive* counterpart to the whole indictment of chs 1–10 — every corrupted faculty shown healed in the Spirit-rested ruler, and the disordered world made peaceable by being filled with the knowledge of God; it portrays the interior as God intends it.
- **Threads:** the Spirit-endowed self (→ 42:1, the servant; 61:1, "the Spirit… is upon me"); delight in the fear of the LORD (← 8:13; w/ Ps 112:1); knowledge of God as the ground of peace (← 2:3; → 54:13, "all your children shall be taught by the LORD").
