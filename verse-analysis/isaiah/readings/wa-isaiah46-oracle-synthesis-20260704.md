## ISA-carried-not-carrying · Isa 46:1–13 · [D, multi] — the idols that must be carried vs the God who carries

**The reading.** The Babylonian gods are pictured being hauled away on pack-animals: "**Bel bows down; Nebo stoops; their idols are on beasts and livestock; these things you carry are borne as burdens on weary beasts… they cannot save the burden, but themselves go into captivity**" (vv.1–2). Against this, God's own relation to his people is the exact inverse: "**Listen to me… who have been borne by me from before your birth, carried from the womb; even to your old age I am he, and to gray hairs I will carry you. I have made, and I will bear; I will carry and will save**" (vv.3–4). And his settled purpose: "**My counsel shall stand, and I will accomplish all my purpose**" (v.10). The finding is **the sharp contrast between *a god you must carry* (a burden that cannot save) and *the God who carries you* — "even to your old age… to gray hairs I will carry you"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the reversal of the load-bearing relation: idolatry makes the worshipper the *bearer* of his god (an exhausting burden that gives nothing back), whereas the true God is the *bearer* of the worshipper from womb to gray hairs — the interior's whole security relocated from carrying to being-carried** *(inferred: the image is unforgettable and exact — the idols "**are borne as burdens on weary beasts**" (v.1, the god that must be lugged about, dead weight), while God says "**I have made, and I will bear; I will carry and will save**" (v.4); the finding is that every object of ultimate trust is either something you *carry* or Someone who *carries* you — the false god is a *burden* (it depends on you, exhausts you, and "cannot save the burden", v.2), the true God is a *bearer* (you depend on him, and he sustains you); and the sweep of God's carrying is lifelong — "**from before your birth… to your old age… to gray hairs**" (vv.3–4): the finding is that the interior's security across the whole span of life, and especially into the frailty of age, rests on being *carried* — the same tenderness as the shepherd's bosom (40:11); the deepest folly of idolatry, then, is that it inverts the proper order, making the self the weary porter of what should have borne it; and this carrying God is also the one whose "**counsel shall stand**" (v.10) — the bearer is also the sovereign, so being carried is being carried by One who cannot fail)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the god carried vs the God who carries ("borne as burdens on weary beasts" vs "I will carry you" — every ultimate trust is a burden you bear or a Bearer who holds you)* (thread — a gem: the reversal of the load-bearing relation, w/ 46:1-4, 40:11, Ps 68:19, Matt 11:28-30); *carried from womb to gray hairs (lifelong sustaining, especially into the frailty of age)* (new — a gem: the interior's security across the whole span, w/ 46:3-4, 63:9); *the bearer who is also sovereign ("my counsel shall stand… I will accomplish all my purpose" — carried by One who cannot fail)* (thread — w/ 46:10, 14:24, 55:11).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the god carried vs the God who carries; carried from womb to gray hairs; the bearer who is also sovereign. Chapter 46 crystallises the idol-polemic into one unforgettable inner-being image — you either *carry* your god (a burden that cannot save) or are *carried* by God (from the womb to gray hairs) — and the God who carries is the One whose purpose stands.
- **Threads:** carried by God / the Shepherd's bosom (← 40:11; → 63:9); idols as dead weight (← 44:9-20; → 47); God's counsel that stands (← 14:24, 40:8; → 48:3, 55:11).
