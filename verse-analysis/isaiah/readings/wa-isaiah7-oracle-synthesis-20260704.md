## ISA-immanuel · Isa 7:1–25 · [D, multi] — the shaking heart; faith as the ground of standing; "God with us"

**The reading.** Two kings march on Jerusalem, and the royal house dissolves in fear: "**the heart of Ahaz and the heart of his people shook as the trees of the forest shake before the wind**" (v.2). Into that panic comes the word: "**Be careful, be quiet, do not fear, and do not let your heart be faint**" (v.4), and then the condition on which everything turns: "**If you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all**" (v.9). God even offers Ahaz a sign "**deep as Sheol or high as heaven**" (v.11), but Ahaz declines under a mask of piety — "**I will not ask, and I will not put the LORD to the test**" (v.12) — refusing the very faith he is called to. So the sign is given anyway: "**the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel**" — *God with us* (v.14). The finding is **a *shaking* heart summoned to *faith* as the sole ground of stability — "if you will not believe, you will not be established" — and a king who, offered God's help, *refuses to trust* behind false reverence; against which God pledges his own presence, "God with us"** *(stated)*. The operation is **fear and faith set as the two possible states of the threatened interior — the heart shakes "like trees before the wind" (instability itself), and faith is named not as one virtue but as the very *condition of standing* (ta'aminu/te'amenu — believe/be-established, the same root): to trust is to be made firm; to refuse trust is to have no footing at all** *(inferred: the inner-being crux is the wordplay of v.9 — "**if you are not firm in faith (ta'aminu), you will not be firm at all (te'amenu)**": belief and stability are the *same word* — faith is not a help toward standing, it *is* standing; the finding is that the threatened self has exactly one foundation, and it is trust in God, so that unbelief is not merely a spiritual deficiency but *structural collapse* ("no footing"); the tragedy is Ahaz — offered a sign of any magnitude (v.11, God stooping to prop his faith), he *declines* it and dresses the refusal as piety ("I will not put the LORD to the test", v.12) — the reading exposes *false humility as unbelief in disguise*: Ahaz would rather trust his own political calculation (an alliance with Assyria) than the offered God; and yet the "Immanuel" sign (v.14) answers the shaking heart at its root — the cure for the fear of the two kings is not their removal but *God with us*, the presence that makes the trembling heart able to be still)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the shaking heart (fear as instability — "the heart shook as trees before the wind")* (thread — the trembling interior, w/ Isa 33:14, Ps 55:4-5); *faith as the ground of standing ("if you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all" — believing and being-established one word)* (new — a gem: trust is not a help toward stability but *is* stability, w/ 2 Chr 20:20, Isa 26:3); *false piety as unbelief in disguise (Ahaz refusing the offered sign under a mask of reverence)* (new — a gem: religious-sounding refusal of trust); *trust in one's own calculation vs the offered God (the alliance preferred to Immanuel)* (thread — misplaced reliance, w/ 30:1-2, 31:1); *"God with us" as the answer to the fearful heart (presence, not removal of the threat, stills the interior)* (new — w/ 8:10, 41:10, 43:5).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the shaking heart; faith as the ground of standing; false piety as disguised unbelief; trust in calculation vs the offered God; "God with us." Chapter 7 crystallises the book's fear/faith axis into a single maxim — *believe, or you have no footing* — and diagnoses the subtle sin of refusing trust while sounding devout.
- **Threads:** fear vs faith (→ 8:12-13, "let him be your fear"; 12:2, "I will trust and not be afraid"); "if you are not firm in faith" (→ 28:16, the tested cornerstone, "whoever believes will not be in haste"); God-with-us / presence stilling fear (→ 41:10, 43:2, 43:5).
