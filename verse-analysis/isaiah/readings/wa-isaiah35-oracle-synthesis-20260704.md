## ISA-highway-ransomed · Isa 35:1–10 · [D, multi] — the anxious heart strengthened; the ransomed returning with everlasting joy

**The reading.** The desert blossoms and sings (vv.1–2), and the word turns straight to the fearful interior: "**Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees. Say to those who have an anxious heart, 'Be strong; fear not! Behold, your God will come… He will come and save you'**" (vv.3–4). A "**Way of Holiness**" is opened on which "**even if they are fools, they shall not go astray**" (v.8). And the close, one of the book's great notes of joy: "**the ransomed of the LORD shall return and come to Zion with singing; everlasting joy shall be upon their heads; they shall obtain gladness and joy, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away**" (v.10). The finding is **the *anxious heart* directly addressed and *heartened* — "be strong, fear not, your God will come" — and the ransomed brought home to a joy so full that "sorrow and sighing flee away"** *(stated)*. The operation is **the fearful interior met not with rebuke but with *strengthening* — the drooping hands and feeble knees braced, the "anxious heart" told to "be strong" on the ground that "your God will come and save you" — and the terminus of the whole journey given as *joy overtaking sorrow*: gladness that does not merely comfort grief but puts it to flight** *(inferred: the tenderness of vv.3–4 is the inner-being gem — the word does not scold the frightened but *strengthens* them: "**strengthen the weak hands… say to those who have an anxious heart** (nimharei-lev, the hasty/panicked heart), **'Be strong; fear not!'**"; the finding is that God's response to fear is *encouragement grounded in his coming* — "fear not" is not a bare command but rests on a reason, "**behold, your God will come… and save you**" (v.4): courage is given to the anxious heart by turning its eyes to the approaching Saviour; and the destination redefines the interior's future — "**everlasting joy upon their heads… and sorrow and sighing shall flee away**" (v.10): the finding is that the goal of the redeemed interior is not a neutral relief but a *positive, everlasting joy* so complete that its opposite (sorrow and sighing) actively *flees* — grief is not merely absent but routed; the "Way of Holiness" (v.8) on which "even fools shall not go astray" adds that the path home is *kept* — the frail and foolish are secured on it, so the anxious heart need not fear losing the way; the whole chapter is the answer to the fear that has run through the book — the trembling heart of Ahaz (7:2), the anxious, the weak-kneed — now told "fear not" and led home singing)*.

**Characteristics surfaced:** *the anxious heart strengthened, not rebuked ("say to those who have an anxious heart, Be strong, fear not")* (thread — a gem: God's response to fear is encouragement, w/ 35:3-4, 41:10, 40:29-31); *"fear not" grounded in God's coming ("behold, your God will come and save you" — courage from turning to the approaching Saviour)* (new — a gem: fear answered by a reason, not a bare command, w/ 35:4, 41:10,13-14); *joy that routs sorrow ("everlasting joy… sorrow and sighing shall flee away" — grief not merely absent but put to flight)* (thread — a gem: the redeemed interior's positive everlasting joy, w/ 35:10, 25:8, 51:11, 65:19); *the kept way (even fools shall not go astray on the Way of Holiness — the frail secured on the path home)* (new — the anxious heart need not fear losing the way, w/ 35:8, 30:21).

## Chapter-level notes
- **Surfaced:** the anxious heart strengthened; "fear not" grounded in God's coming; joy that routs sorrow; the kept way. Chapter 35 closes Part I of the book (and batch 4) by answering its long fear-theme with pure encouragement — the anxious heart braced, and the ransomed led home to a joy that puts sorrow to flight.
- **Book-level note (batch 4, chs 28–35):** the woes on Ephraim/Jerusalem are, at heart, a sustained anatomy of *how the interior receives or refuses God*: the offered *rest* declined (28:12); faith as *un-panicked composure* on the tested cornerstone (28:16); worship with the *lips while the heart is far* (29:13); the *clay disputing the Potter* (29:16); salvation refused where it was offered — *"in quietness and trust… but you were unwilling"* (30:15); the *waiting, guiding* God who becomes an inner voice, "this is the way" (30:21); reliance on *flesh, not spirit* (31:3); *peace as the fruit* of a Spirit-given righteousness (32:15-17); *who can dwell with the consuming fire* — integrated cleanness ordered by the fear of the LORD (33:14-16); and finally the *anxious heart heartened* and led home with joy (35). The through-line: **the interior is saved by a trusting, quiet, receptive turn to God — and destroyed by the willful, self-reliant, heart-far refusal of it.**
- **Threads carried forward:** quietness/rest/trust (→ 40:31, 41:10); the heart far / hollow worship (→ 58:1-4); flesh vs spirit (→ 40:6-8); the fear of the LORD as treasure (→ 50:10); "fear not" (→ 41:10,13; 43:1,5 — the refrain of Part II).
