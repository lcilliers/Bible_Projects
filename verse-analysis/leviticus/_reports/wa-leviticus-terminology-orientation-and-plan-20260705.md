# Leviticus — terminology-first orientation & method plan

> **Researcher's steer (2026-07-05):** don't walk the rituals or the prescriptions. Treat Leviticus as a **terminology problem first** — the *state/concept* words (clean, unclean, holy, sin, blessing, curse, redeemed…), not the ritual machinery. Hold open the real question: **are the atonement-ritual words even inner-being words?** Method: (A) a quick **exposition of all the verse-record verses** as the baseline, then (B) **interpretations run as discoveries** on top of it.
>
> **The driving questions (per term):** where does the word come from (root/origin) · what meaning does it carry *for the inner being* · why is the inner being regarded as *unclean* · why does it need an *external, physical* method to be *reset*.

## Part 2 — What the map already tells us (four discovery-seeds)

Before any exposition, the distribution alone yields four findings that bear directly on your questions:

**(a) Clean/unclean is the master-axis — and it is a *state*, not an act.** `tame`/`taher` together dominate Leviticus (~95 + ~69 verse-hits). The book's central inner-being category is not an emotion or a virtue but a **condition of the person** — *clean* or *unclean* — that can be *contracted, transmitted, and reset*. This is the axis your questions target most sharply (*why is the inner being unclean; why an external reset*).

**(b) Atonement is literally "to cover" (kaphar).** The questioned term's own root is *covering*. This bears on the reset-question: atonement does not (lexically) *cleanse* or *remove* — it *covers*. The cleansing word is a *different* term (`taher`). **The Day of Atonement verse holds them together and shows the relationship:** *"on this day shall **atonement** (kipper) be made… to **cleanse** (taher) you; you shall be **clean** (taher) from all your **sins** (chattat)"* (16:30). So — a first hypothesis to test: **the ritual/atonement words are the *mechanism*; the inner-being terminology is the *state* (clean/sin) that the mechanism resets.** Your instinct that "atonement-ritual words may not be inner-being" is supported by the data — they describe *what is done*, while clean/unclean/sin describe *what the person is*.

**(c) The "heart" is nearly absent (levav, 3 verses).** Leviticus does **not** psychologise. Where Genesis/Exodus located the inner being in the *heart* (lev/levav) and its intents, Leviticus locates it in the **nephesh** (the living self/person) held in a **state** (clean/unclean, holy/profane, guilty/cleared). This is a genuine shift: **the inner being in Leviticus is a *status-bearing self*, not a deliberating heart.** That reframes what "inner being" even means in this book — and is itself a finding worth the study.

**(d) The self that bears the state is nephesh + basar (flesh).** The uncleanness attaches to the *nephesh* and the *basar* — the living person and the body. This is why the reset is *physical* (water, blood, time, separation): the state is borne *bodily*, so it is addressed *bodily*. (A hypothesis for the "why external/physical" question.)

## Part 4 — Worked sample (Leviticus 16, the Day of Atonement) — the exposition format

*(This is the Phase-A format applied to one chapter, so you can calibrate. Ritual-only verses marked; inner-being spans in bold.)*

- **16:1–2** — *[ritual/frame]* the LORD speaks after the death of Aaron's sons; Aaron not to enter the Holy Place at any time.
- **16:3, 5** — *[ritual]* Aaron enters with a bull and goats for a **sin-offering** (chattat).
- **16:4** — *[ritual — no inner-being span]* the linen garments put on.
- **16:6, 11** — Aaron offers the bull as a **sin-offering** (chattat) and makes **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) *for himself and his house*.
- **16:10** — the live (chay) goat presented to make **atonement** (kipper) over it, sent to Azazel.
- **16:13–14** — *[ritual — no inner-being span]* the incense cloud covers the mercy seat; blood sprinkled.
- **16:15** — *[ritual]* the people's **sin-offering** (chattat) goat killed, blood brought inside the veil.
- **16:29, 31** — **you shall afflict** (anah) **yourselves** (nephesh) and do no work — *the one interior act the people perform*.
- **16:30** — **atonement** (kipper/kaphar) made **to cleanse** (taher) you; **you shall be clean** (taher) from all your **sins** (chattat) — ***the crux: the ritual serves a state-change (clean-from-sin) in the person***.
- **16:32–34** — *[ritual]* the anointed priest makes **atonement** (kipper) for the sanctuary, tent, altar, priests, people, once a year for their **sins** (chattat).

**What the sample shows:** of ~29 recorded verses, the inner-being *terminology* lives in a handful — the **sin** the person carries, the **cleansing/clean** state, the **nephesh afflicted** — while the bulk is *covering-mechanism* and procedure. The chapter's inner-being *finding* is one verse (16:30): **the person is unclean-by-sin and is reset to clean.** Everything else is the apparatus of that reset. This is exactly the distinction your reframe is after.
