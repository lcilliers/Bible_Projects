# The heart / the inner self — a discovery document (developing)

> **Mode: DISCOVERY, not summary.** The heart is the master-term of the inner being, and the most colour-rich; it also raises the corpus's hardest structural question — how the four **seats** (heart, soul, spirit, flesh) differ. Gather widely, trace the colours, hold the boundaries open. Registry: `ib_characteristic.code = the-heart`.

## Working sense (held open)
The centre of the inner being — but the corpus will not let "heart" settle into one meaning. It is at once an *organ/seat*, the *true self*, a thing to be *guarded, given, weighed, searched, ruled, inclined, cleansed*, and the *place where every other operation happens*. Proverbs is intensely heart-centred; the Psalms distribute the interior across heart/soul/spirit/flesh. The central discovery task: is "the heart" **one characteristic**, or the **whole interior named from its centre** — and how does it differ from the other seats?

## The evidence, gathered by colour

**Colour 1 — the heart is the TRUE self.** "As in water face reflects face, so **the heart of man reflects the man**" (Pro 27:19). Not a faculty the self *has* but the self it *is*. Here "heart" = identity.

**Colour 2 — the heart is the SOURCE of the life, to be GUARDED.** "**Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life**" (Pro 4:23). The heart is the *origin* from which everything issues; hence the object of the most vigilant guarding.

**Colour 3 — the heart is the self's OFFERING, to be GIVEN.** "**My son, give me your heart**" (Pro 23:26); "apply your heart… direct your heart" (Pro 23:12,19). The deepest appeal asks not for behaviour but for *the heart itself*.

**Colour 4 — the heart is WEIGHED and SEARCHED by God.** "The LORD **weighs the heart**" (Pro 21:2; 16:2 "weighs the spirit"); "you who **test the hearts**" (Ps 7:9); "he **knows the secrets of the heart**" (Ps 44:21); "he **fashions the hearts** of them all" (Ps 33:15); "search me… **know my heart**" (Ps 139:23). The heart lies open to God — and can invite that opening.

**Colour 5 — the heart can be HARDENED.** "Whoever **hardens his heart** will fall into calamity" (Pro 28:14); the "**stiffened neck**" broken beyond healing (Pro 29:1); "do not **harden your hearts**" (Ps 95:8). A self-inflicted callousing.

**Colour 6 — the heart cannot self-CLEANSE.** "**Who can say, 'I have made my heart pure'?**" (Pro 20:9); therefore "**create** in me a clean heart, O God" (Ps 51:10) — it must be *made*, not self-washed.

**Colour 7 — the heart DIVIDED vs UNITED / WHOLE / STEADFAST.** "They speak with a **double heart**" (Ps 12:2); against it, "**unite my heart** to fear your name" (Ps 86:11); "with my **whole heart** I seek you" (Ps 9:1; 119:10; 138:1); "my heart is **steadfast/fixed**" (Ps 57:7; 108:1; 112:7 "his heart is firm"). The heart can be split, or gathered and made firm.

**Colour 8 — the heart BROKEN / contrite.** "The LORD is near to the **brokenhearted**" (Ps 34:18); "a **broken and contrite heart** you will not despise" (Ps 51:17); "he **heals the brokenhearted**" (Ps 147:3). Here brokenness is not failure but the acceptable condition.

**Colour 9 — the FELT heart.** "**The heart knows its own bitterness**, and no stranger shares its joy" (Pro 14:10); "a **glad heart** makes a cheerful face, but by sorrow of heart the **spirit is crushed**" (Pro 15:13); "**anxiety in a man's heart weighs him down**" (Pro 12:25). (Fuller treatment: `the-felt-interior`.)

**Colour 10 — the DEEP / opaque-yet-drawable heart.** "**The purpose in a man's heart is like deep water**, but a man of understanding will draw it out" (Pro 20:5); "the heart of kings is **unsearchable**" (Pro 25:3); "the inward mind and heart… are **deep**" (Ps 64:6). Hidden, yet reachable by the discerning (though never by self, Colour 6).

**Colour 11 — the heart INCLINED / directed.** The heart's *direction* can be set: by the self — "**incline your heart** to understanding" (Pro 2:2); by God — "**incline my heart** to your testimonies" (Ps 119:36); guarded against evil — "**let not my heart incline** to any evil" (Ps 141:4). The heart is steerable — but by whom?

**Colour 12 — the heart as WRITING-SURFACE / the place wisdom ENTERS.** "**Write them on the tablet of your heart**" (Pro 3:3; 7:3); "**wisdom will come into your heart**" (Pro 2:10); "your law is **within my heart**" (Ps 40:8). The interior as an inscribable, in-fillable space.

## Where it changes colour — the developing picture
The heart is **simultaneously the most active and the most passive** thing in the inner being. It *guards, gives, inclines, seeks* (the self acting from its centre) — and it is *weighed, searched, fashioned, hardened, broken, cleansed, inscribed* (the self acted upon). The live discovery is this **double agency**:
- The heart can be **inclined by the self** (Pro 2:2 — I incline my own heart) **and by God** (Ps 119:36 — you incline my heart) **and is turned by God even against its grain** (Pro 21:1 — the king's heart a stream in the LORD's hand).
- The heart can be **hardened by the self** (Pro 28:14) but **cannot be cleansed by the self** (Pro 20:9) — the self can *damage* its heart but not *purify* it.

So the heart is the arena where the self's agency and God's meet — the self can steer, harden, offer, and guard it, but only God can weigh, fashion, and cleanse it. **The heart is the one thing the self both owns and cannot control.**

## The seats — the hard structural question
Heart (*leb/lebab*), soul (*nephesh*), spirit (*ruach*), flesh (*basar*) are all tagged as *seats*, and the operations distribute across them unevenly and overlappingly:
- **soul** carries longing/thirst, the self-addressed "O my soul", the fainting/poured-out self (Ps 42, 62, 103, 116);
- **spirit** carries the crushed/faint spirit, the committed spirit, ruling/holding-back the spirit, the lamp searching the innermost (Ps 31:5, 51:10, Pro 16:32, 18:14, 20:27);
- **heart** carries nearly everything above;
- **flesh** carries frailty/dust and the body's register of inner states (Ps 16:9, 63:1, 73:26, 103:14).
**Open:** are these four *different faculties*, or one inner being named from four angles (its centre = heart, its life/appetite = soul, its breath/animating force = spirit, its frailty = flesh)? The data show heavy overlap (the crushed *spirit* 18:14 vs the crushed *heart*-sorrow 15:13) — suggesting angles more than parts. But *ruach* (spirit) clusters distinctly around *ruling/holding* and *committing* — a hint that it is the self's *governing/animating* aspect. Unresolved and central.

## Junctions (boundaries blurring — nearly all of them)
Because the heart is *where the operations happen*, it junctions with almost every characteristic: **being-known** (weighed/searched, Colour 4); **self-mastery** (guarding/ruling/inclining, Colours 2,11); **the-felt-interior** (Colour 9); **forgiveness-confession** (cleansing, Colour 6); **integrity-legibility** (divided vs whole, Colour 7); **self-address** (the heart addressed and exhorted); **teachability** (the hardened heart, Colour 5); **wisdom-formation** (the heart as the place wisdom enters, Colour 12).

## Thin / uncertain evidence (noted, not yet weighed)
- **The ENLARGED heart.** "I will run… when you **enlarge my heart**" (Ps 119:32) — the heart can be *made bigger* (capacity expanded by God). One clear witness; a distinct colour (dilation) worth watching.
- **The heart that ENVIES / rages.** "his heart **rages against the LORD**" (Pro 19:3); envy "makes the bones rot" (Pro 14:30) — the heart as the seat of resentment; thin, junctions with self-toward-others.
- **The heart that MEDITATES / muses.** "the **meditation of my heart**" (Ps 19:14); "I commune with my own heart" (Ps 77:6) — the heart as the organ of inner speech/reflection; under-gathered here.

## Open questions (the debate, left open)
1. Is "the heart" **one characteristic** or the **whole interior named from its centre** — and is it even the right unit, given the four seats?
2. How do **heart / soul / spirit / flesh** differ in *operation* — distinct faculties, or four angles on one self? (Is *ruach* the governing aspect?)
3. Given the **double agency** — the self can incline/harden/offer/guard the heart, but only God can weigh/fashion/cleanse/enlarge it — where exactly is the line between what the self does *to* its heart and what only God does?
4. If Proverbs makes the heart the *source* of the springs of life (4:23) and the *true self* (27:19), is "the heart" the master-characteristic under which the others are *facets* — or a genuine peer among them?
