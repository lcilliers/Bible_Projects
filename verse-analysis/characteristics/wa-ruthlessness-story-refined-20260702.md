## The story
**I. Egypt — dread schemes into ruthless enslavement (Exo 1:7-14).**
A king who *did not know* Joseph (Exo 1:8) plots — *"deal shrewdly"* (1:10) — against a people he casts as **enemies**; out of **dread** (1:12) he **afflicts** and **oppresses** them (1:11-12); and he makes them serve — **ruthlessly** (1:13; the characteristic welds to *enslave*, source = dread) — **embittering** their lives (1:14; effect = *bitter*).

**II. The servant-law — ruthlessness forbidden, the fear of God its restraint (Lev 25:34-53).**
The law **forbids** ruling a brother **ruthlessly** (1:43; characteristic, prohibition) and sets against it the **fear of God** (restrained-by fear-of-God @25:36/43). The poor brother is to be **supported** (25:35), **lent to** without gain (25:37), **not made a slave** (25:39). Ruthlessness recurs as the forbidden **manner of rule** (25:46; 25:53).

**III. The shepherds — ruthlessness as the failure of care (Eze 34:4-6).**
The shepherds do **not strengthen, heal, or seek** (34:4, all under prohibition) but **rule** with **force** and **harshness** (34:4; the characteristic welds to *rule*); and the flock is **scattered** (34:5).

**Across the three:** ruthlessness is the **harsh manner of dominion** (welds to *enslave* and *rule*), **driven** by dread, **restrained** only by the fear of God, **condemned** wherever it appears — its impact the **embittering of life** and the **scattering of the weak**.
