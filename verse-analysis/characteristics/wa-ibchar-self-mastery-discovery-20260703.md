# Self-mastery / ruling one's spirit — a discovery document (developing)

> **Mode: DISCOVERY, not summary.** Gather the self-government evidence; trace the colours; hold open whether self-mastery (the ACT), self-address (the MODE), and stillness (the STATE) are three faces of one operation — and where the *governing* self sits relative to the *governed* spirit. Registry: `ib_characteristic.code = self-mastery`.

## Working sense (held open)
The self governing the self. In Proverbs it is the **supreme strength** (16:32) and the **wall** that makes a self defensible (25:28); in the Psalms it appears mostly as the *stilling* of one's own soul. A self that can hold, quiet, restrain, and steer itself.

## The evidence, gathered by colour

**Colour 1 — ruling / holding the spirit (the governing act).** "**Whoever rules his spirit** is better than **he who takes a city**" (Pro 16:32); "a fool gives **full vent** to his spirit, but a wise man **quietly holds it back**" (Pro 29:11). The conquest of the *self* set above the conquest of a city.

**Colour 2 — the WALL / city (self-control as defensibility).** "**A man without self-control is like a city broken into and left without walls**" (Pro 25:28). The un-governed self is *defenceless*; self-control is the perimeter that makes an interior holdable.

**Colour 3 — slow-to-anger / governing temper.** "Whoever is **slow to anger** has great understanding, but a **hasty temper** exalts folly" (Pro 14:29); "a **soft answer** turns away wrath" (Pro 15:1); "it is his **glory to overlook** an offense" (Pro 19:11). Temper governed — the overlooking a *chosen* honour.

**Colour 4 — appetite-restraint.** "Put a **knife to your throat** if you are given to appetite" (Pro 23:2); "eat only **enough** for you, lest you… vomit it" (Pro 25:16). The self checking its own hunger.

**Colour 5 — tongue-restraint / guarding the mouth.** "Whoever **restrains his lips** is prudent" (Pro 10:19); "whoever **guards his mouth** preserves his life" (Pro 13:3; 21:23); "a **cool spirit**" restrains words (Pro 17:27); "I will **guard my ways**… I will **muzzle my mouth**" (Ps 39:1); "**set a guard**, O LORD, over my mouth" (Ps 141:3). Speech governed — sometimes by the self, sometimes *asked of God* (141:3).

**Colour 6 — self-QUIETING / stilling the soul (the Psalter's mode).** "For God alone, O my soul, **wait in silence**" (Ps 62:1,5); "I have **calmed and quieted** my soul" (Ps 131:2); "**be angry, and do not sin**… **be silent**" (Ps 4:4); "**Be still**, and know that I am God" (Ps 46:10). The self *hushing itself*.

**Colour 7 — guarding the heart's INCLINATION.** "**Let not my heart incline** to any evil" (Ps 141:4); "keep your heart with all vigilance" (Pro 4:23). Governing not just act and word but the heart's *leaning* — and again, sometimes *asked of God* (141:4).

## Where it changes colour — the developing picture
Self-mastery spans **holding, quieting, restraining, cooling, steering** — from the fierce "rule his spirit / knife to the throat" to the tender "calmed and quieted… like a weaned child." The live discoveries:
- **The governed thing is usually the *spirit* (ruach)** — "rules his spirit" (16:32), "holds back his spirit" (29:11), "a cool spirit" (17:27), "the spirit of man is the lamp of the LORD" (20:27). This clusters with the seats-question (see `the-heart`): *ruach* may be the self's *governing/animating* aspect, the thing both ruled and doing the ruling.
- **Sometimes the self masters itself; sometimes it asks God to.** "I will muzzle my mouth" (Ps 39:1, self) vs "set a guard over my mouth" (Ps 141:3, asked of God); "let not my heart incline to evil" (141:4, asked of God). Self-mastery shades into *petition for keeping* — the self that does not trust its own governing asks God to govern it. **Where is the line between self-control and dependence for control?**

## Junctions (boundaries blurring)
- **self-address** — the *mode* of self-mastery: the self commanding its own soul ("O my soul…"). Is self-address how self-mastery is *performed*?
- **rest-stillness** — the *state* self-quieting produces (sweet sleep, the stilled soul). Act → state.
- **the-felt-interior** — self-mastery governs the *felt* states (the tranquil vs raging heart, Pro 14:30).
- **speech-outflow** — tongue-restraint is a large slice of self-mastery.
- **the-compulsive-will** — its *failure*: the un-governed self venting, the captured will unable to hold back.
- **the-heart** — ruling/inclining the heart (Colours 1,7).

## Thin / uncertain evidence (noted, not yet weighed)
- **Overlooking as *glory*.** "It is his **glory** to overlook an offense" (Pro 19:11) — restraint framed as *honour*, not mere suppression. A positive colour worth developing.
- **The "cool spirit."** "He who has a **cool spirit**" (Pro 17:27) — a *temperature* metaphor for self-command, against the "hot-tempered" (15:18). Physiological/temperamental register, lightly attested.
- **Bridling.** "I will **bridle** my tongue" imagery (Ps 39; cf. the horse/mule "without understanding" needing bit and bridle, Ps 32:9) — the self as an animal needing restraint. Suggestive of self-mastery as *self-bridling*.

## Open questions (the debate, left open)
1. Are **self-mastery (act), self-address (mode), and stillness (state)** three faces of one operation, or three operations?
2. Where does the **governing "I"** sit relative to the **governed spirit** — is the ruach both ruler and ruled?
3. Where is the line between **self-control** and **asking God for control** (Ps 141:3–4)? Is mature self-mastery actually *dependence*?
4. Is self-control a **strength the self has** (16:32 — a might greater than a warrior's) or a **gift/fruit** it receives? The corpus leans "strength," but the petitions (141) lean "gift."
