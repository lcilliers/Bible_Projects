## 1. The verse in context (Lev 25:42–46)
- **42 (ground):** "For they are **my servants**, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt; they shall not be sold as slaves." — *Israel belongs to God; redeemed from Egypt.*
- **43:** "You shall **not rule over** [`radah` H7287] him **with ruthlessness** [`be-perek` H6531] **but shall fear** [`yare` H3372] **your God** [`elohim` H430]."
- **46:** "…over your **brothers** the people of Israel you shall not rule ruthlessly."

**Shape:** a prohibition (`radah` `be-perek`) set directly against a positive ground (`yare` `elohim`). Ruthless dominion **vs** the fear of God — the two are opposed in one breath.

## 3. Its own inner-being operations
1. **Ruthless dominion** (`radah` + `be-perek`) — the will to dominate, exercised harshly, over the impoverished/indentured brother (v.39 context). Here it is the **prohibited** operation.
   - *Note:* `perek` here modifies `radah` ("rule"), whereas in Exo 1:13 it modified `abad` ("enslave"). Same manner-word, **different host-verb** → `perek` qualifies *any* domination act.
2. **The fear of God** (`yare elohim`) — an inner posture of reverence that **restrains** cruelty. This is the verse's central contribution: cruelty is checked **from within**, by reverence toward God. The inner being that fears God will not dominate ruthlessly.

## 4. Observations this verse contributes (Lev 25:43-originated)
- **A. The fear of God is the inner restraint on cruelty.** The verse opposes "fear your God" to "rule ruthlessly" — reverence is the brake on the will-to-dominate. *(operation: fear-of-God / D2 source-restraint · resolved on this verse — explicit.)*
- **B. Ruthless dominion over a brother usurps God's ownership.** The ground (v.42) is "they are **my** servants"; to `perek` God's servant is to seize what is God's. The wrong is grounded in **divine ownership**, not merely in cruelty. *(D2/D10 · needs-corroboration.)*
- **C. `perek` is a manner that attaches to dominion (`radah`) as well as enslavement (`abad`).** Same manner-word, different host-verb. *(corroborates Exo 1:13 #50 — perek as a manner-operation.)*
- **D. The redemption-memory grounds the prohibition.** "I brought you out of Egypt" (v.42): because God freed Israel from Egyptian `perek`, Israel must not `perek` the brother — the freed must not become the oppressor. *(D12 · corroborates Exo 1:13 #51.)*

## 6. The verse's own headline
Lev 25:43 does not merely forbid cruelty — it **locates the restraint of cruelty inside the perpetrator**, as the fear of God, and grounds the prohibition in **God's ownership of the victim** and in **Israel's own memory of being the victim**. The inner mechanism: reverence toward God displaces the will-to-dominate.
