Avoid unsubstantiated superlatives — "the most severe," "the clearest," "the strongest linkage," "exceeding every prior chapter" — when the underlying claim hasn't been rigorously compared against every candidate it's being ranked against. State the specific fact (what the text says, what term recurs, what the reading finds) rather than dressing it in comparative/ranking language.

**Why:** flagged directly (2026-07-27) as a recurring habit, specifically in the IBA Daniel passage-debate documents (dan-debate work) — phrases like "the corpus's most striking lexical convergence" or "the passage's clearest stated interior" appeared repeatedly across the Dan 4-8 debates without the comparison being actually substantiated against the full set of prior instances. It reads as inflating the finding's significance rather than reporting it.

**How to apply:** in analytical writing (debate documents, findings, chat summaries) — including but not limited to [[project_iba_passage_debate_no_separate_ai_chat_needed]] work — write the observation directly: "this verse states X using the same root as verse Y" rather than "this is the strongest/clearest instance of X in the corpus." Reserve comparative language only for cases where a genuine, checked comparison across the full candidate set has been done and can be pointed to.
