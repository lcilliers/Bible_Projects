The unit of a corrected-method read is the **passage** — a coherent sense-unit / maximal run of consecutive verses — **not** the whole chapter. "There is no reason to read such a long span in one go. You should read passages, not the whole chapter." (le Roux, on Ps 78, a 72-verse recital.)

**Why:** a long chapter (e.g. Ps 78 = 72 verses, 241 spans) read as one undifferentiated sweep flattens attention — each sense-unit has its own movement and deserves to be read on its own terms. Passage-depth is where the reading actually happens; chapter-depth is too coarse.

**How to apply:**
- Break a long chapter into its natural passages first (Ps 78 → prologue v1-8 / Exodus v9-16 / food-rebellion v17-31 / false-repentance v32-39 / plagues v40-55 / apostasy+Shiloh v56-64 / God-chooses-David v65-72). Read one passage at a time, at full depth.
- A short psalm may itself be a single passage — then the whole psalm is one read. The rule bites for long/narrative-recital chapters.
- Reconciles with "minimise breaks" ([[feedback_proceed_autonomously_on_stable_rules]]): the unit is the PASSAGE, and several passage-units can be processed back-to-back within one turn without stopping — batch passages, don't batch by cramming a whole long chapter into one pass.
- Aligns with the existing passage model ([[project_ve_lexical_is_verse_first]], [[project_term_driven_genre_aware_lexical_method]], [[project_passage_reading_checkback_gate]]): passage = maximal run of consecutive verses; anchor = first verse.

**★ REFINEMENT (2026-07-10, le Roux):** "Read passages based on the START and END of a char, and not a reading of a bunch of verses where the chars are not directly interrelated." The passage is **bounded by the characteristic's own extent** — where a char is introduced, develops, and resolves — together with any chars *directly interrelated* with it (sharing pairs / couplings / a common bearer or movement). Do NOT slice a chapter into arbitrary equal verse-blocks (v1-9 / v10-18…) and call each a passage; a passage is a **char-arc**, so that every within-passage pair is a genuine relation, not an artifact of the slicing. Adjacent verses whose chars do not interconnect belong to *different* passages even if consecutive. This is the char-driven read ([[feedback_char_driven_read_not_span_sweep]]) applied to passage boundaries: the char is the lens, and its start→end is the frame.
