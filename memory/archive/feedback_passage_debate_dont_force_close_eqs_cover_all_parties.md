Two confirmed calibrations for passage-debate work (`report.passage_debate`, [[project_movement_operation_definition_written]]), given after reviewing the Jonah 1 debate (2026-07-28):

1. **Don't push to force-close emergent questions (EQs).** The user is not expecting heavy elaboration on inner-being pre-action states, nor an explanation of *how* the inner being works. Where the text's own operations show something, capture it; where it doesn't, raising the question and leaving it open (per method Part B.1 "consider, don't force-resolve" and B.8 "carried forward") is the correct and sufficient outcome — do not manufacture interpretive content to close a gap the text itself leaves silent.
2. **Cover every party in the passage, not just the main story character.** The Jonah 1 debate's attention to the sailors/mariners/captain (their fear-to-worship arc, individuated at times, distinct from Jonah) was explicitly called out as good — it matches a standing objective: no party in a passage should be left out of sight just because they aren't the book's protagonist.

**Why:** the researcher's method (step 2 note (f) — every human is a presumptive candidate) already implies both of these, but the confirmation sets the *degree*: thoroughness in surfacing is wanted, not thoroughness in resolving.

**How to apply:** in every future passage debate (any book, not just Jonah/Daniel), (a) keep the emergent-questions log genuinely open — a question logged and carried forward is a complete, correct outcome, not a placeholder needing an answer; (b) explicitly check every named or collective human party in the passage for a stated-or-silent inner-being operation, even minor/background ones, before closing out the verse.
