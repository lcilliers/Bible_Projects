The researcher, twice on 2026-07-04: *"it is very easy to gloss over the detail, and group stuff together without asking why are they different"* and *"your natural instinct will be to group together, summarise, generalise — resist the temptation."*

**Why:** the study's yield lives in the fine distinctions between operations, especially between **same-gloss / repeated spans**. Grouping them into one "X operation" destroys exactly what the study exists to surface. Genesis examples: "to know" (yada) appears 52×, "to give" (natan) 89×, "to live" 59× — each occurrence is a *distinct* operation in its verse, and the contrast between occurrences is often the gem (Gen 3: promised god-like *knowing* 3:5 → shame-*knowing* 3:7; gift-*giving* 3:6 → blame-*giving* 3:12; Gen 5: the identical "lived…and died" formula broken by Enoch and by Noah's naming).

**How to apply:**
- For every span, read the operation **off that verse**, never off the gloss/lemma (the gloss is a lemma-constant; e.g. *yare* glossed "revere" but operating as **dread** in Gen 3:10).
- When a gloss repeats in a passage, **do not merge** — write each occurrence's operation separately, then make the **difference between them** an explicit finding.
- Passages are cast wide to hold a whole operation-web, but casting-wide is NOT licence to summarise the web into one theme — surface the individual span-operations AND the interactive ones.
- Prefer depth over throughput; a passage reading that ends in a tidy generalisation has probably failed. Relates to [[feedback_each_chapter_first_principles_find_the_gems]], [[project_extended_lexical_model_refinement]], and the Gen 3 worked example.
