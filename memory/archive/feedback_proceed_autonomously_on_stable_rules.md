Once a method/plan is agreed and the rules are set, **proceed autonomously through the whole task** — do not stop for approval every few minutes. Approve-per-pilot then run the full job (e.g. run the 3 Psalm pilots, then complete the entire book without per-chapter sign-off).

**Why:** the researcher finds constant check-ins and re-litigating settled rules disruptive and slow. "I do not want to stop every 5 minutes for approval… we do not re-invent the rules every 5 minutes" (2026-07-09).

**How to apply:**
- Confirm at genuine decision forks (scope, method choice, a pilot's outcome) — not at every mechanical step.
- Treat the established rules as **stable**. If a rule is genuinely unclear or wrong, **fix it once, record it, and move on** — do not re-open settled rules repeatedly.
- Commit incrementally and report progress, but keep going to completion rather than pausing to ask "shall I continue?".
- This complements [[feedback_interaction_protocols]] (confirm before *non-trivial/irreversible* acts) — the bar for "stop and ask" is real decisions, not routine execution within an agreed plan.
