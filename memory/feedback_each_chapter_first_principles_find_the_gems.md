**Researcher direction, 2026-07-02 (quality gate for the chapter-driven poetic readings).**

**Do every chapter analysis as if it is the first.** Refrain from shortcuts. Do NOT template a psalm against the ones before it or flatten them into a common shape — **the psalms are not all the same; each has a different slant, focus, and key** (Ps 3–7 laments/vindication; Ps 8 a creation-hymn of human dignity; etc.). Each is **rich in meaning with deep HIDDEN GEMS**, and the reading's job is to find *that psalm's* particular treasure.

**Why:** the whole point of the chapter/reading layer is the inference that surfaces **hidden meaning** the lexical cannot carry (see [[project_term_driven_genre_aware_lexical_method]] — "collection transcends into one truth"; hidden-meaning returns as inference at the story layer). Templating kills exactly the thing the method exists to produce. Cross-psalm threads may be *noted* where they genuinely emerge, but never at the cost of the individual psalm's distinct disclosure.

**How to apply:** for each chapter — read the full Phase-1 lexical view carefully, do a genuine per-verse sanity-check (not a rubber-stamp), and write the reading from *this* psalm's movement and emphasis. Slow is fine; depth over throughput. Prefer doing one chapter thoroughly to batching several thinly. The mechanical pipeline (backfill → Phase 1 → sanity → reading → file) is the substrate; the reading is where the gem is dug out. Cf. [[feedback_reusable_engine_scripts_and_continuous_learning]] (reusable mechanics) — but the *reading* is never mechanical.
